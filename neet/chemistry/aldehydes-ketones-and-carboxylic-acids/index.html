  1. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 46
    For the following reaction sequence, choose the correct option.

    1. If P is the sodium salt of a carboxylic acid, Q is a primary alcohol
    2. P and Q are aromatic compounds
    3. If P gives a carboxylic acid on acidification, Q gives a poisonous gas on exposure to air and light
    4. Both P and Q are carbonyl compounds
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    Friedel–Crafts acylation of benzene gives acetophenone, $\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3}$.
    NaOCl brings about the haloform reaction: $\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3 \rightarrow C_6H_5COONa\ (P) + CHCl_3\ (Q)}$.
    P on acidification gives benzoic acid; Q (chloroform) is oxidised by air in light to poisonous phosgene, $\mathrm{COCl_2}$. Hence option (3).

  2. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 82
    The compound that CANNOT be obtained from the aldol condensation reaction shown below, is

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    2,2-Dimethylcyclopentanone can form an enolate only at C-5, its only $\alpha$-$\mathrm{CH_2}$.
    Crossed aldol with PhCHO followed by dehydration gives the 5-benzylidene ketone as E and Z isomers, options (2) and (4).
    Self-condensation gives the conjugated exocyclic enone, option (3).
    Aldol dehydration (E1cB) always puts the C=C between the $\alpha$ and $\beta$ carbons, in conjugation with C=O. In (1) the C=C lies inside the second ring, away from the carbonyl, so (1) cannot be formed.

  3. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 86
    Consider the following reaction, and choose the correct option.

    1. On treating compound P with saturated $\mathrm{NaHCO_3}$ solution, brisk effervescence is observed
    2. Compound P can be prepared by treating benzene with anhydrous $\mathrm{AlCl_3}$ and $\mathrm{CH_3COCl}$
    3. On treatment with bromine water, compound P gives a white precipitate
    4. Compound P is obtained by the hydrogenation of benzoyl chloride with Pd on $\mathrm{BaSO_4}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    The Étard reaction ($\mathrm{CrO_2Cl_2}$, then hydrolysis) oxidises toluene to benzaldehyde, P.
    Benzaldehyde is not an acid, so no $\mathrm{CO_2}$ with $\mathrm{NaHCO_3}$. Benzene with $\mathrm{CH_3COCl}$ gives acetophenone. The white precipitate with bromine water is a test for phenol/aniline.
    Rosenmund reduction ($\mathrm{H_2}$, Pd/$\mathrm{BaSO_4}$) of benzoyl chloride gives benzaldehyde, so (4) is correct.

  4. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 70
    The correct order of decreasing acidity of the following aliphatic acids is :

    1. $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3CCOOH > (CH_3)_2CHCOOH > CH_3COOH > HCOOH}$
    2. $\mathrm{CH_3COOH > (CH_3)_2CHCOOH > (CH_3)_3CCOOH > HCOOH}$
    3. $\mathrm{HCOOH > CH_3COOH > (CH_3)_2CHCOOH > (CH_3)_3CCOOH}$
    4. $\mathrm{HCOOH > (CH_3)_3CCOOH > (CH_3)_2CHCOOH > CH_3COOH}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    Alkyl groups are electron releasing (+I); they destabilise the carboxylate ion and lower acidity. The more alkyl groups, the weaker the acid.
    So $\mathrm{HCOOH > CH_3COOH > (CH_3)_2CHCOOH > (CH_3)_3CCOOH}$.

  5. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 78
    The major product of the following reaction is :

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    Excess $\mathrm{CH_3MgBr}$ adds to both groups. The ketone C=O gives a tertiary alkoxide, which on hydrolysis becomes a tertiary alcohol (Ph, $\mathrm{CH_3}$ and OH on one carbon).
    The nitrile adds one $\mathrm{CH_3MgBr}$ to give an imine salt, which $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$ hydrolyses to a methyl ketone ($-\mathrm{COCH_3}$).
    So the product is option (2).

  6. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 83
    Identify the suitable reagent for the following conversion.

    1. (i) $\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$, (ii) $\mathrm{H^+/H_2O}$
    2. (i) $\mathrm{AlH(iBu)_2}$ (ii) $\mathrm{H_2O}$
    3. (i) $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$, (ii) $\mathrm{H^+/H_2O}$
    4. $\mathrm{H_2}$ / Pd-$\mathrm{BaSO_4}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    An ester is reduced to an aldehyde by DIBAL-H, $\mathrm{AlH(iBu)_2}$, followed by hydrolysis.
    $\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$ reduces the ester all the way to the primary alcohol, $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ does not reduce esters readily, and $\mathrm{H_2}$/Pd-$\mathrm{BaSO_4}$ (Rosenmund) reduces acid chlorides, not esters.
