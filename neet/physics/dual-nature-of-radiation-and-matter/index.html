  1. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 8
    A beam of light falls on a metal surface such that photo-electrons are generated. If power of the light source starts to decrease linearly with time $t$, then variation of the photocurrent $I$ and magnitude of the stopping potential $|V|$ with time is best represented by:

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Photocurrent is proportional to the number of photons per second, i.e. to the power, so $I$ decreases linearly with $t$.
    Stopping potential depends only on frequency ($e|V| = h\nu - \phi$), which is unchanged, so $|V|$ stays constant.
    This matches option (1).

  2. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 18
    A ray of light with wavelength $\lambda$ is incident on three different photoelectric cells namely 1, 2 and 3. The threshold wavelength of these photoelectric cells are $\lambda_1$, $\lambda_2$, and $\lambda_3$, respectively and the magnitude of stopping potentials of these cells are $V_1$, $V_2$ and $V_3$, respectively. The relation between $\lambda$ and threshold wavelengths are $\lambda_1 < \lambda$, $\lambda_2 > \lambda$ and $\lambda_3 >> \lambda$. The correct option is:

    1. $V_1 = 0,\ V_2 < V_3$
    2. $V_1 = 0,\ V_2 > V_3$
    3. $V_1 > V_2,\ V_3 = 0$
    4. $V_1 < V_2,\ V_3 = 0$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Emission needs $\lambda < \lambda_0$. For cell 1, $\lambda > \lambda_1$: no emission, $V_1 = 0$.
    $eV = hc\left(\dfrac{1}{\lambda} - \dfrac{1}{\lambda_0}\right)$ grows with $\lambda_0$; since $\lambda_3 \gg \lambda$ while $\lambda_2$ is only greater than $\lambda$, $\lambda_3 > \lambda_2$ and so $V_3 > V_2$.
    So $V_1 = 0,\ V_2 < V_3$.

  3. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 19
    A photon and an electron, each of 20 eV energy, move in free space. The ratio of linear momentum of electron $p_e$ to that of photon $p_{Ph}$, $\dfrac{p_e}{p_{Ph}}$ is:
    [Take speed of light = $3 \times 10^8$ ms$^{-1}$, charge of electron = $-1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ C and mass of electron = $9 \times 10^{-31}$ kg]

    1. $\dfrac{2}{450}$
    2. $\dfrac{1}{250}$
    3. $225$
    4. $275$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    $E = 20 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} = 3.2 \times 10^{-18}$ J.
    $p_e = \sqrt{2mE} = \sqrt{2 \times 9 \times 10^{-31} \times 3.2 \times 10^{-18}} = 2.4 \times 10^{-24}$ kg m/s; $p_{Ph} = E/c = 1.07 \times 10^{-26}$ kg m/s.
    Ratio $= \dfrac{2.4 \times 10^{-24} \times 3 \times 10^8}{3.2 \times 10^{-18}} = 225$.

  4. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 34
    De-Broglie wavelength of an electron orbiting in the $n = 2$ state of hydrogen atom is close to (Given Bohr radius = 0.052 $nm$)

    1. 0.067 $nm$
    2. 0.67 $nm$
    3. 1.67 $nm$
    4. 2.67 $nm$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    $r_2 = n^2a_0 = 4\times0.052 = 0.208$ nm. Bohr's condition $2\pi r_n = n\lambda$ gives $\lambda = \dfrac{2\pi\times0.208}{2} \approx 0.65$ nm, closest to 0.67 nm.

  5. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 37
    A photon and an electron (mass $m$) have the same energy $E$. The ratio $(\lambda_{\text{photon}}/\lambda_{\text{electron}})$ of their de Broglie wavelengths is: ($c$ is the speed of light)

    1. $\sqrt{\dfrac{E}{2m}}$
    2. $c\sqrt{2mE}$
    3. $c\sqrt{\dfrac{2m}{E}}$
    4. $\dfrac{1}{c}\sqrt{\dfrac{E}{2m}}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    Photon: $\lambda_p = \dfrac{hc}{E}$. Electron: $\lambda_e = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2mE}}$.
    $\dfrac{\lambda_p}{\lambda_e} = \dfrac{c\sqrt{2mE}}{E} = c\sqrt{\dfrac{2m}{E}}$.

  6. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 38
    Which of the following options represent the variation of photoelectric current with property of light shown on the x-axis?

    1. A only
    2. A and C
    3. A and D
    4. B and D
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Photoelectric current is proportional to the intensity of light (above the threshold frequency), so graph A is correct and B is wrong.
    For a fixed intensity, the photoelectric (saturation) current does not depend on the frequency, so graphs C and D are wrong. Hence A only.

  7. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 10
    If $c$ is the velocity of light in free space, the correct statements about photon among the following are :
    A. The energy of a photon is $E = h\nu$.
    B. The velocity of a photon is $c$.
    C. The momentum of a photon, $p = \dfrac{h\nu}{c}$.
    D. In a photon-electron collision, both total energy and total momentum are conserved.
    E. Photon possesses positive charge.
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. A, C and D only
    2. A, B, D and E only
    3. A and B only
    4. A, B, C and D only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    A, B and C are the standard photon relations: $E = h\nu$, speed $c$ in free space, and $p = E/c = h\nu/c$.
    D is true: in a photon-electron collision total energy and total momentum are conserved.
    E is false: a photon is electrically neutral.
    So A, B, C and D only.

  8. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 17
    The graph which shows the variation of $\left(\dfrac{1}{\lambda^2}\right)$ and its kinetic energy, $E$ is (where $\lambda$ is de Broglie wavelength of a free particle) :

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{p} = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2mE}}$, so $\dfrac{1}{\lambda^2} = \dfrac{2m}{h^2}E$.
    $\dfrac{1}{\lambda^2}$ is directly proportional to $E$: a straight line through the origin, graph (2).

  9. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 19
    The minimum wavelength of $X$-rays produced by an electron accelerated through a potential difference of $V$ volts is proportional to:

    1. $\sqrt{V}$
    2. $\dfrac{1}{V}$
    3. $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{V}}$
    4. $V^2$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    The cut-off $\lambda_{\min}$ is reached when the whole kinetic energy $eV$ of an electron goes into one photon: $\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_{\min}} = eV \Rightarrow \lambda_{\min} = \dfrac{hc}{eV} \propto \dfrac{1}{V}$.

  10. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 27
    The work functions of Caesium (Cs), Potassium (K) and Sodium (Na) are 2.14 eV, 2.30 eV and 2.75 eV respectively. If incident electromagnetic radiation has an incident energy of 2.20 eV, which of these photosensitive surfaces may emit photoelectrons?

    1. Cs only
    2. Both Na and K
    3. K only
    4. Na only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Photoemission needs the photon energy to be at least the work function. 2.20 eV exceeds only the work function of caesium (2.14 eV), so only Cs emits photoelectrons.

  11. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 6
    The graph which shows the variation of the de Broglie wavelength ($\lambda$) of a particle and its associated momentum ($p$) is

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    $\lambda = h/p$, so $\lambda$ is inversely proportional to $p$. The graph is a rectangular hyperbola that falls steeply and flattens out, as in graph (4).

  12. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 17
    When two monochromatic lights of frequency, $\nu$ and $\dfrac{\nu}{2}$ are incident on a photoelectric metal, their stopping potential becomes $\dfrac{V_s}{2}$ and $V_s$ respectively. The threshold frequency for this metal is

    1. $2\nu$
    2. $3\nu$
    3. $\dfrac{2}{3}\nu$
    4. $\dfrac{3}{2}\nu$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    Einstein's equation $eV = h\nu - h\nu_0$ for each light:
    $\dfrac{eV_s}{2} = h\nu - h\nu_0$ and $eV_s = \dfrac{h\nu}{2} - h\nu_0$.
    Doubling the first and equating: $2h\nu - 2h\nu_0 = \dfrac{h\nu}{2} - h\nu_0$, so $\nu_0 = \dfrac{3}{2}\nu$.
    (This is the formal result. Physically the data are inconsistent: a threshold above both frequencies would mean no emission at all.)

    Note: The data in this question are not physically consistent: the lower frequency is given the larger stopping potential, and the result $\nu_0 = \frac{3}{2}\nu$ is above both frequencies used. NTA's final key keeps option (4), the value obtained by solving the two equations formally.
