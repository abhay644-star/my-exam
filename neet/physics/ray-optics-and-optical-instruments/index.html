  1. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 20
    Which of the following measurements require ‘index correction’?

    1. Measurement of resistance of a wire using meter bridge
    2. Measurement of gravitational acceleration using simple pendulum
    3. Measurement of focal length of lenses using optical bench
    4. Measurement of speed of sound using resonance tube
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    Index correction is the correction for the difference between the actual distance between the uprights (needles/lens) and the separation read from the optical bench scale.
    It is applied in focal-length measurements on an optical bench.

  2. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 22
    Consider three media $P$, $Q$ and $R$ with refractive indices 1, 1.25, and 1.5 respectively. The medium $Q$ having a thickness of 5 cm is placed between extended media $P$ and $R$ as shown in the figure. An object $O$ is placed at the centre of medium $Q$. If viewed from medium $P$ near the normal direction, the apparent depth of $O$ is $h_1$. For similar observation from medium $R$, the apparent depth is $h_2$. The value of $|h_1 - h_2|$, in cm, is:

    1. $0$
    2. $1$
    3. $2$
    4. $3$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    Object is 2.5 cm from each face. Apparent depth $= d\,\dfrac{n_{\text{observer}}}{n_{\text{object}}}$.
    $h_1 = 2.5 \times \dfrac{1}{1.25} = 2$ cm; $h_2 = 2.5 \times \dfrac{1.5}{1.25} = 3$ cm.
    $|h_1 - h_2| = 1$ cm.

  3. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 29
    The lens combination as shown in the figure, consists of two lenses, $L_1$ and $L_2$, of the focal lengths +10 cm and – 10 cm, respectively. The position of the image formed is:

    1. 20 cm to the left of the concave lens
    2. 60 cm to the left of the concave lens
    3. 30 cm to the right of the concave lens
    4. 60 cm to the right of the concave lens
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    $L_1$: $u = -30$, $f = +10$: $\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{10} - \dfrac{1}{30} \Rightarrow v = 15$ cm, i.e. 12 cm beyond $L_2$ (virtual object, $u = +12$).
    $L_2$: $\dfrac{1}{v} = -\dfrac{1}{10} + \dfrac{1}{12} = -\dfrac{1}{60} \Rightarrow v = -60$ cm.
    Image is 60 cm to the left of the concave lens.

  4. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 2
    A microscope has an objective of focal length 2 cm, eyepiece of focal length 4 cm and the tube length of 40 cm. If the distance of distinct vision of eye is 25 cm, the magnification in the microscope is

    1. 100
    2. 125
    3. 150
    4. 250
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    For a compound microscope (final image at infinity), $m = \dfrac{L}{f_o}\times\dfrac{D}{f_e} = \dfrac{40}{2}\times\dfrac{25}{4} = 20 \times 6.25 = 125$.

  5. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 13
    In a certain camera, a combination of four similar thin convex lenses are arranged axially in contact. Then the power of the combination and the total magnification in comparison to the power ($p$) and magnification ($m$) for each lens will be, respectively –

    1. $4p$ and $4m$
    2. $p^4$ and $4m$
    3. $4p$ and $m^4$
    4. $p^4$ and $m^4$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    For thin lenses in contact the powers add: $P = p + p + p + p = 4p$.
    For a combination the total magnification is the product of the individual magnifications: $m\times m\times m\times m = m^4$.

  6. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 32
    A light ray enters through a right angled prism at point $P$ with the angle of incidence $30^\circ$ as shown in figure. It travels through the prism parallel to its base $BC$ and emerges along the face $AC$. The refractive index of the prism is:

    1. $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$
    2. $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
    3. $\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{4}$
    4. $\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    The prism angle at $A$ is $90^\circ$, so the two refraction angles inside satisfy $r_1 + r_2 = 90^\circ$.
    The ray emerges along face $AC$ (grazing emergence), so $r_2$ is the critical angle: $\sin r_2 = \dfrac{1}{\mu}$, hence $\sin r_1 = \cos r_2$.
    At $P$: $\sin 30^\circ = \mu \sin r_1 = \mu\cos r_2 \Rightarrow \cos r_2 = \dfrac{1}{2\mu}$.
    $\sin^2 r_2 + \cos^2 r_2 = 1 \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{\mu^2} + \dfrac{1}{4\mu^2} = 1 \Rightarrow \mu^2 = \dfrac{5}{4}$, so $\mu = \dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2}$.

  7. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 36
    A small telescope has an objective of focal length 140 cm and an eye piece of focal length 5.0 cm. The magnifying power of telescope for viewing a distant object is:

    1. 17
    2. 32
    3. 34
    4. 28
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    For normal adjustment, $m = \dfrac{f_o}{f_e} = \dfrac{140}{5} = 28$.

  8. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 10
    Light travels a distance $x$ in time $t_1$ in air and $10x$ in time $t_2$ in another denser medium. What is the critical angle for this medium?

    1. $\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{t_2}{t_1}\right)$
    2. $\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{10\,t_2}{t_1}\right)$
    3. $\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{t_1}{10\,t_2}\right)$
    4. $\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{10\,t_1}{t_2}\right)$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    Speed in air $c = x/t_1$; speed in the medium $v = 10x/t_2$.
    Refractive index $n = \dfrac{c}{v} = \dfrac{t_2}{10\,t_1}$.
    $\sin C = \dfrac{1}{n} = \dfrac{10\,t_1}{t_2}$, so $C = \sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{10\,t_1}{t_2}\right)$.

  9. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 36
    Two thin lenses are of same focal lengths ($f$), but one is convex and the other one is concave. When they are placed in contact with each other, the equivalent focal length of the combination will be :

    1. Zero
    2. $f/4$
    3. $f/2$
    4. Infinite
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    $\dfrac{1}{F} = \dfrac{1}{f} + \dfrac{1}{-f} = 0$, so $F$ is infinite (the combination has zero power and behaves like a plane glass plate).

  10. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 41
    In the figure shown here, what is the equivalent focal length of the combination of lenses (Assume that all layers are thin)?

    1. 40 cm
    2. – 40 cm
    3. – 100 cm
    4. – 50 cm
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    The combination is a biconvex lens ($n_1 = 1.5$) between two plano-concave lenses ($n_2 = 1.6$), all of radius 20 cm, in contact.
    Biconvex: $\dfrac{1}{f_1} = (1.5 - 1)\left(\dfrac{2}{20}\right) = \dfrac{1}{20}$. Each plano-concave: $\dfrac{1}{f_2} = (1.6 - 1)\left(-\dfrac{1}{20}\right) = -\dfrac{0.6}{20}$.
    $\dfrac{1}{F} = \dfrac{1}{20} - \dfrac{1.2}{20} = -\dfrac{1}{100}$, so $F = -100$ cm.

  11. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 5
    A biconvex lens has radii of curvature, 20 cm each. If the refractive index of the material of the lens is 1.5, the power of the lens is

    1. +2 D
    2. +20 D
    3. +5 D
    4. Infinity
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    Lens maker's formula: $\dfrac{1}{f} = (\mu - 1)\left(\dfrac{1}{R_1} - \dfrac{1}{R_2}\right) = 0.5\left(\dfrac{1}{0.2} + \dfrac{1}{0.2}\right) = 5\ \text{m}^{-1}$.
    Power $= +5$ D.

  12. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 49
    Two transparent media $A$ and $B$ are separated by a plane boundary. The speed of light in those media are $1.5 \times 10^{8}$ m/s and $2.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s, respectively. The critical angle for a ray of light for these two media is

    1. $\sin^{-1}$ (0.500)
    2. $\sin^{-1}$ (0.750)
    3. $\tan^{-1}$ (0.500)
    4. $\tan^{-1}$ (0.750)
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    Light is slower in $A$, so $A$ is the denser medium. Total internal reflection happens for light going from $A$ to $B$.
    $\sin C = \dfrac{n_B}{n_A} = \dfrac{v_A}{v_B} = \dfrac{1.5}{2.0} = 0.750$, so $C = \sin^{-1}(0.750)$.
