  1. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 33
    A point charge $Q$ is placed inside a cavity within a solid isolated conducting sphere. Consider points $A$, $B$ and $C$ as shown in the figure, where the magnitudes of the electric fields are $E_A$, $E_B$, $E_C$, respectively. The points $B$ and $C$ are at the same distance from the center of the solid sphere. The correct option is :

    1. $E_A = 0,\ E_B = E_C$
    2. $E_A \ne 0,\ E_B = E_C$
    3. $E_A = 0,\ E_B > E_C$
    4. $E_A \ne 0,\ E_B < E_C$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    Inside the cavity the field of $Q$ (and the induced charge on the cavity wall) is present, so $E_A \ne 0$.
    Charge $+Q$ appears on the outer surface and spreads uniformly (the conductor shields the cavity), so outside the field is radial: $E = \dfrac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2}$.
    $B$ and $C$ are equidistant from the centre, so $E_B = E_C$.

  2. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 28
    Two identical charged conducting spheres A and B have their centres separated by a certain distance. Charge on each sphere is q and the force of repulsion between them is F. A third identical uncharged conducting sphere is brought in contact with sphere A first and then with B and finally removed from both. New force of repulsion between spheres A and B (Radii of A and B are negligible compared to the distance of separation so that for calculating force between them they can be considered as point charges) is best given as :

    1. $\dfrac{3F}{5}$
    2. $\dfrac{2F}{3}$
    3. $\dfrac{F}{2}$
    4. $\dfrac{3F}{8}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    After touching A: A and C each have $q/2$. After touching B: B and C share $q + q/2$, so each has $3q/4$.
    $F' \propto \dfrac{q}{2}\cdot\dfrac{3q}{4} = \dfrac{3}{8}q^2$, so $F' = \dfrac{3F}{8}$.

  3. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 35
    An electric dipole with dipole moment $5\times10^{-6}$ Cm is aligned with the direction of a uniform electric field of magnitude $4\times10^{5}$ N/C. The dipole is then rotated through an angle of $60^\circ$ with respect to the electric field. The change in the potential energy of the dipole is :

    1. 0.8 J
    2. 1.0 J
    3. 1.2 J
    4. 1.5 J
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    $U = -pE\cos\theta$, so $\Delta U = pE(1 - \cos60^\circ) = 5\times10^{-6}\times4\times10^{5}\times\tfrac{1}{2} = 1.0$ J.

  4. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 5
    If $\oint_s \vec{E}\cdot\vec{dS} = 0$ over a surface, then :

    1. the number of flux lines entering the surface must be equal to the number of flux lines leaving it.
    2. the magnitude of electric field on the surface is constant.
    3. all the charges must necessarily be inside the surface.
    4. the electric field inside the surface is necessarily uniform.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Zero net flux through a closed surface means the net charge enclosed is zero (Gauss's law). The field need not be constant or uniform; it only means that as many field lines leave the surface as enter it.

  5. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 32
    An electric dipole is placed at an angle of 30° with an electric field of intensity $2\times10^5\ \text{N C}^{-1}$. It experiences a torque equal to 4 N m. Calculate the magnitude of charge on the dipole, if the dipole length is 2 cm.

    1. 8 mC
    2. 6 mC
    3. 4 mC
    4. 2 mC
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    $\tau = pE\sin\theta = q(2a)E\sin\theta$.
    $4 = q \times 0.02 \times 2\times10^5 \times 0.5 = 2000\,q \Rightarrow q = 2\times10^{-3}$ C $= 2$ mC.
