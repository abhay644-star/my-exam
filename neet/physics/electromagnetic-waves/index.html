  1. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 38
    An electromagnetic wave travelling in a lossless dielectric medium having a dielectric constant, $\varepsilon_r = 9$, has the electric field, $E_x = E_0\sin(kz - 2\pi \times 10^6 t)$ Vm$^{-1}$ where $E_0$ is the amplitude and $k$ is the wave vector. Among the following options, the $\textbf{incorrect}$ choice is

    1. The speed of the electromagnetic wave inside the medium is $10^8$ ms$^{-1}$
    2. The wavelength of the electromagnetic wave inside the medium is 300 m
    3. The magnetic field is given by the relation $B_y = \dfrac{B_0}{v}\sin(kz - 2\pi \times 10^6 t)$ where $v$ is the speed of the electromagnetic wave inside the medium
    4. The direction of propagation of the electromagnetic wave is along $+z$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2) or (3) (either is accepted)

    $v = c/\sqrt{\varepsilon_r} = 10^8$ m/s, so (1) is correct; the phase $kz - \omega t$ means travel along $+z$, so (4) is correct.
    $f = 10^6$ Hz, so $\lambda = v/f = 100$ m, not 300 m: (2) is incorrect.
    The amplitude of $B_y$ should be $B_0 = E_0/v$; writing $B_0/v$ is also wrong, so (3) is incorrect. NTA accepts both (2) and (3).

  2. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 43
    The following table presents the part of the electromagnetic spectrum and their corresponding major applications.

    Part of the electromagnetic spectrumApplications
    P.MicrowaveI.For purifying the water
    Q.UV raysII.For warming the food
    R.Gamma raysIII.For AM and FM communication systems
    S.Radio waveIV.For treating the Cancer cells
    The $\textbf{correct}$ option is:

    1. P-I, Q-II, R-III, S-IV
    2. P-I, Q-IV, R-II, S-III
    3. P-II, Q-I, R-IV, S-III
    4. P-II, Q-IV, R-III, S-I
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    Microwaves heat food (P-II); UV kills germs in water purifiers (Q-I); gamma rays are used in cancer radiotherapy (R-IV); radio waves carry AM/FM broadcasts (S-III).

  3. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 9
    The electric field in a plane electromagnetic wave is given by
    $E_z = 60\cos(5x + 1.5\times10^{9}t)$ V/m.
    Then expression for the corresponding magnetic field is (here subscripts denote the direction of the field) :

    1. $B_y = 2\times10^{-7}\cos(5x + 1.5\times10^{9}t)$ T
    2. $B_x = 2\times10^{-7}\cos(5x + 1.5\times10^{9}t)$ T
    3. $B_z = 60\cos(5x + 1.5\times10^{9}t)$ T
    4. $B_y = 60\sin(5x + 1.5\times10^{9}t)$ T
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    $B_0 = E_0/c = 60/(3\times10^{8}) = 2\times10^{-7}$ T, with the same phase as $E$.
    The phase $5x + \omega t$ means travel along $-x$. $\vec{B}$ must be perpendicular to both $\vec{E}$ ($z$) and the direction of travel ($x$), so it lies along $y$ (and $\hat{z}\times\hat{y} = -\hat{x}$ is consistent).
    So $B_y = 2\times10^{-7}\cos(5x + 1.5\times10^{9}t)$ T.

  4. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 26
    A parallel plate capacitor made of circular plates is being charged such that the surface charge density on its plates is increasing at a constant rate with time. The magnetic field arising due to displacement current is :

    1. zero at all places
    2. constant between the plates and zero outside the plates
    3. non-zero everywhere with maximum at the imaginary cylindrical surface connecting peripheries of the plates
    4. zero between the plates and non-zero outside
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    By the Ampère–Maxwell law, at distance $r$ from the axis: for $r < R$, $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0 r}{2}\dfrac{dE}{dt}$ (grows with $r$); for $r > R$, $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0 R^2}{2r}\dfrac{dE}{dt}$ (falls as $1/r$).
    So the field is non-zero (off the axis) both inside and outside, and is greatest at $r = R$, the cylindrical surface joining the edges of the plates.

  5. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 39
    A parallel plate capacitor is charged by connecting it to a battery through a resistor. If I is the current in the circuit, then in the gap between the plates :

    1. displacement current of magnitude equal to I flows in a direction opposite to that of I.
    2. displacement current of magnitude greater than I flows but can be in any direction.
    3. there is no current.
    4. displacement current of magnitude equal to I flows in the same direction as I.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    While the capacitor charges, the changing electric field between the plates gives a displacement current $I_d = \epsilon_0 \dfrac{d\Phi_E}{dt}$. This equals the conduction current I in the wires and is in the same direction, so the total current is continuous through the circuit.

  6. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 45
    The property which is not of an electromagnetic wave travelling in free space is that :

    1. they travel with a speed equal to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \epsilon_0}}$.
    2. they originate from charges moving with uniform speed.
    3. they are transverse in nature.
    4. the energy density in electric field is equal to energy density in magnetic field.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    Electromagnetic radiation is produced by accelerated (or oscillating) charges. A charge moving with uniform speed produces only steady electric and magnetic fields, not radiation.
    The other three are true: speed $c = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}$, the fields are transverse, and the electric and magnetic energy densities are equal.

  7. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 22
    In a plane electromagnetic wave travelling in free space, the electric field component oscillates sinusoidally at a frequency of $2.0\times10^{10}$ Hz and amplitude $48\ \text{V m}^{-1}$. Then the amplitude of oscillating magnetic field is : (Speed of light in free space = $3 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$)

    1. $1.6\times10^{-9}$ T
    2. $1.6\times10^{-8}$ T
    3. $1.6\times10^{-7}$ T
    4. $1.6\times10^{-6}$ T
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    $B_0 = \dfrac{E_0}{c} = \dfrac{48}{3\times10^8} = 1.6\times10^{-7}$ T.
