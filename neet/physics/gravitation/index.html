  1. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 13
    Two planets $P_1$ and $P_2$ with equal mass have radii $R_1$ and $R_2$, respectively, where $R_2 = \dfrac{R_1}{2}$. The escape speeds of $P_1$ and $P_2$ are $v_1$ and $v_2$, respectively. Then $\dfrac{v_2}{v_1}$ is:

    1. $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$
    2. $1$
    3. $\sqrt{2}$
    4. $2$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    $v_e = \sqrt{2GM/R}$; with equal $M$, $v_e \propto 1/\sqrt{R}$.
    $\dfrac{v_2}{v_1} = \sqrt{\dfrac{R_1}{R_2}} = \sqrt{2}$.

  2. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 14
    In a solar system, the time-period of revolution of a planet tracing a circular orbit of radius $R$ is proportional to:

    1. $R^{1/2}$
    2. $R^{3/2}$
    3. $R^2$
    4. $R^3$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    Gravity provides centripetal force: $\dfrac{GMm}{R^2} = m\omega^2 R \Rightarrow T^2 = \dfrac{4\pi^2}{GM}R^3$.
    So $T \propto R^{3/2}$ (Kepler's third law).

  3. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 31
    The radius of Martian orbit around the Sun is about 4 times the radius of the orbit of Mercury. The Martian year is 687 Earth days. Then which of the following is the length of 1 year on Mercury ?

    1. 88 earth days
    2. 225 earth days
    3. 172 earth days
    4. 124 earth days
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Kepler's third law: $T \propto r^{3/2}$. $T_{Mercury} = \dfrac{687}{4^{3/2}} = \dfrac{687}{8} \approx 86$ days, closest to 88 earth days.

  4. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 32
    A body weighs 48 N on the surface of the earth. The gravitational force experienced by the body due to the earth at a height equal to one-third the radius of the earth from its surface is :

    1. 16 N
    2. 27 N
    3. 32 N
    4. 36 N
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    $F \propto \dfrac{1}{(R+h)^2}$. At $h = R/3$: $F = 48\times\left(\dfrac{R}{4R/3}\right)^2 = 48\times\dfrac{9}{16} = 27$ N.

  5. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 8
    The mass of a planet is $\dfrac{1}{10}$th that of the earth and its diameter is half that of the earth. The acceleration due to gravity on that planet is :

    1. $4.9\ \text{m s}^{-2}$
    2. $3.92\ \text{m s}^{-2}$
    3. $19.6\ \text{m s}^{-2}$
    4. $9.8\ \text{m s}^{-2}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    $g = \dfrac{GM}{R^2}$. With $M' = M/10$ and $R' = R/2$: $g' = \dfrac{G(M/10)}{R^2/4} = \dfrac{4}{10}g = 0.4 \times 9.8 = 3.92\ \text{m s}^{-2}$.

  6. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 46
    The minimum energy required to launch a satellite of mass $m$ from the surface of earth of mass $M$ and radius $R$ in a circular orbit at an altitude of $2R$ from the surface of the earth is:

    1. $\dfrac{GmM}{2R}$
    2. $\dfrac{GmM}{3R}$
    3. $\dfrac{5GmM}{6R}$
    4. $\dfrac{2GmM}{3R}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    The orbit radius is $R + 2R = 3R$. Total energy in orbit $= -\dfrac{GMm}{2(3R)} = -\dfrac{GMm}{6R}$. Energy at the surface (ignoring the earth's rotation) $= -\dfrac{GMm}{R}$.
    Energy needed $= -\dfrac{GMm}{6R} + \dfrac{GMm}{R} = \dfrac{5GmM}{6R}$.

  7. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 16
    Two bodies of mass $m$ and $9m$ are placed at a distance $R$. The gravitational potential on the line joining the bodies where the gravitational field equals zero, will be ($G$ = gravitational constant) :

    1. $-\dfrac{8Gm}{R}$
    2. $-\dfrac{12Gm}{R}$
    3. $-\dfrac{16Gm}{R}$
    4. $-\dfrac{20Gm}{R}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    Let the field be zero at distance $x$ from $m$: $\dfrac{Gm}{x^2} = \dfrac{G(9m)}{(R - x)^2} \Rightarrow R - x = 3x \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{R}{4}$.
    Potential there: $V = -\dfrac{Gm}{R/4} - \dfrac{9Gm}{3R/4} = -\dfrac{4Gm}{R} - \dfrac{12Gm}{R} = -\dfrac{16Gm}{R}$.

  8. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 46
    A satellite is orbiting just above the surface of the earth with period $T$. If $d$ is the density of the earth and $G$ is the universal constant of gravitation, the quantity $\dfrac{3\pi}{Gd}$ represents :

    1. $T$
    2. $T^2$
    3. $T^3$
    4. $\sqrt{T}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    For an orbit just above the surface, $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{R^3}{GM}}$ with $M = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3 d$.
    $T^2 = \dfrac{4\pi^2 R^3}{G \cdot \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 d} = \dfrac{3\pi}{Gd}$.

  9. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 28
    A body of mass 60 g experiences a gravitational force of 3.0 N, when placed at a particular point. The magnitude of the gravitational field intensity at that point is

    1. 0.05 N/kg
    2. 50 N/kg
    3. 20 N/kg
    4. 180 N/kg
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    Field intensity = force per unit mass $= \dfrac{3.0}{0.060} = 50$ N/kg.

  10. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 36
    Match List-I with List-II

    List-IList-II
    (a)Gravitational constant (G)(i)[L$^2$T$^{-2}$]
    (b)Gravitational potential energy(ii)[M$^{-1}$L$^3$T$^{-2}$]
    (c)Gravitational potential(iii)[LT$^{-2}$]
    (d)Gravitational intensity(iv)[ML$^2$T$^{-2}$]
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below

    1. (a) - (ii), (b) - (i), (c) - (iv), (d) - (iii)
    2. (a) - (ii), (b) - (iv), (c) - (i), (d) - (iii)
    3. (a) - (ii), (b) - (iv), (c) - (iii), (d) - (i)
    4. (a) - (iv), (b) - (ii), (c) - (i), (d) - (iii)
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    $G = Fr^2/m^2$: [M$^{-1}$L$^3$T$^{-2}$]. Potential energy is an energy: [ML$^2$T$^{-2}$]. Potential is energy per unit mass: [L$^2$T$^{-2}$]. Intensity is force per unit mass: [LT$^{-2}$].
    So (a)-(ii), (b)-(iv), (c)-(i), (d)-(iii).
