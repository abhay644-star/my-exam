  1. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 1
    A particle of mass $M$ moves along a horizontal $x$ axis from $x = 0$ to $x = L$. The coefficient of kinetic friction varies as a function of $x$ as $\mu_k(x) = \mu_0 - \alpha x$, where $\mu_0$, $\alpha$ are constants of appropriate dimensions, so that $\mu_k(L) = 0$. The total work done by the frictional force during the motion is $n\mu_0 MgL$, where $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity. The value of $n$ is:

    1. $3$
    2. $1$
    3. $\dfrac{1}{3}$
    4. $\dfrac{1}{2}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    $\mu_k(L)=0$ gives $\alpha = \mu_0/L$, so $\mu_k$ falls linearly from $\mu_0$ to $0$.
    Magnitude of work $= \int_0^L \mu_k Mg\,dx = Mg\left(\mu_0 L - \dfrac{\alpha L^2}{2}\right) = \dfrac{1}{2}\mu_0 MgL$.
    Hence $n = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

  2. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 44
    Bob $B$ of mass $m$ at rest is hanging vertically from the ceiling via a massless string of length 10 m, as shown in the figure. Point mass $A$ of mass $m$ travelling horizontally with speed 10 ms$^{-1}$ hits bob $B$ elastically. The bob $B$ rises $h$ meter after the collision. Taking the acceleration due to gravity $g = 10$ ms$^{-2}$ and neglecting the size of the bob, the value of $h$ is:

    1. $8$
    2. $7$
    3. $5$
    4. $2.5$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    Elastic head-on collision of equal masses exchanges velocities: $B$ moves off at 10 m/s.
    $h = \dfrac{v^2}{2g} = \dfrac{100}{20} = 5$ m (less than the string length, so the string stays taut).

  3. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 5
    The kinetic energies of two similar cars A and B are 100 J and 225 J respectively. On applying breaks, car A stops after 1000 m and car B stops after 1500 m. If $F_A$ and $F_B$ are the forces applied by the breaks on cars A and B, respectively, then the ratio $F_A/F_B$ is

    1. $\dfrac{3}{2}$
    2. $\dfrac{2}{3}$
    3. $\dfrac{1}{3}$
    4. $\dfrac{1}{2}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    By the work-energy theorem the braking work equals the kinetic energy lost: $F d = K$.
    $F_A = 100/1000 = 0.1$ N and $F_B = 225/1500 = 0.15$ N, so $F_A/F_B = \dfrac{0.1}{0.15} = \dfrac{2}{3}$.

  4. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 7
    A bob of heavy mass $m$ is suspended by a light string of length $l$. The bob is given a horizontal velocity $v_0$ as shown in figure. If the string gets slack at some point $P$ making an angle $\theta$ from the horizontal, the ratio of the speed $v$ of the bob at point $P$ to its initial speed $v_0$ is:

    1. $(\sin\theta)^{1/2}$
    2. $\left(\dfrac{1}{2+3\sin\theta}\right)^{1/2}$
    3. $\left(\dfrac{\cos\theta}{2+3\sin\theta}\right)^{1/2}$
    4. $\left(\dfrac{\sin\theta}{2+3\sin\theta}\right)^{1/2}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    At P the string just goes slack, so tension is zero and gravity's radial component gives the centripetal force: $mg\sin\theta = \dfrac{mv^2}{l} \Rightarrow v^2 = gl\sin\theta$.
    P is at height $l(1+\sin\theta)$ above the lowest point. Energy: $v_0^2 = v^2 + 2gl(1+\sin\theta) = gl(2+3\sin\theta)$.
    $\dfrac{v}{v_0} = \left(\dfrac{\sin\theta}{2+3\sin\theta}\right)^{1/2}$.

  5. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 27
    Two bodies A and B of same mass undergo completely inelastic one dimensional collision. The body A moves with velocity $v_1$ while body B is at rest before collision. The velocity of the system after collision is $v_2$. The ratio $v_1 : v_2$ is :

    1. 4:1
    2. 1:4
    3. 1:2
    4. 2:1
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    In a completely inelastic collision the bodies move together. Conservation of momentum: $mv_1 = (m + m)v_2$, so $v_1 : v_2 = 2 : 1$.

  6. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 33
    At any instant of time $t$, the displacement of any particle is given by $2t - 1$ (SI unit) under the influence of force of $5N$. The value of instantaneous power is (in SI unit):

    1. 7
    2. 6
    3. 10
    4. 5
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    Velocity $v = \dfrac{d}{dt}(2t - 1) = 2\ \text{m s}^{-1}$.
    Power $P = Fv = 5 \times 2 = 10$ W.
