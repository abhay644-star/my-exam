  1. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 35
    In Geiger-Marsden experiment, the number of scattered $\alpha$-particles $N(\theta)$ is plotted as a function of scattering angle $\theta$. Which of the following options represents the correct plot?

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    Rutherford's formula: $N(\theta) \propto \dfrac{1}{\sin^4(\theta/2)}$.
    Most particles go nearly undeflected; the count falls steeply as $\theta$ increases and only a few scatter near $180^\circ$.
    This is graph (3).

  2. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 40
    Consider that an electron is revolving in an excited state of Hydrogen atom with velocity $\sqrt{25.6} \times 10^5$ ms$^{-1}$. The radius of the orbit is $x \times 10^{-9}$ m. The value of $x$ is:
    [Take the mass of electron to the $9 \times 10^{-31}$ kg, charge of electron = $-1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ C and $\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} = 9 \times 10^9$ Nm$^2$C$^{-2}$]

    1. $4$
    2. $3$
    3. $2$
    4. $1$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    Coulomb force gives the centripetal force: $\dfrac{mv^2}{r} = \dfrac{ke^2}{r^2} \Rightarrow r = \dfrac{ke^2}{mv^2}$.
    $r = \dfrac{9 \times 10^9 \times 2.56 \times 10^{-38}}{9 \times 10^{-31} \times 25.6 \times 10^{10}} = 1 \times 10^{-9}$ m.
    $x = 1$.

  3. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 18
    A model for quantized motion of an electron in a uniform magnetic field $B$ states that the flux passing through the orbit of the electron is $n(h/e)$ where $n$ is an integer, $h$ is Planck's constant and $e$ is the magnitude of electron's charge. According to the model, the magnetic moment of an electron in its lowest energy state will be ($m$ is the mass of the electron)

    1. $\dfrac{he}{\pi m}$
    2. $\dfrac{he}{2\pi m}$
    3. $\dfrac{heB}{\pi m}$
    4. $\dfrac{heB}{2\pi m}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    Lowest state $n = 1$: $B\pi r^2 = \dfrac{h}{e} \Rightarrow r^2 = \dfrac{h}{\pi eB}$.
    In the field $r = \dfrac{mv}{eB}$, so $v = \dfrac{eBr}{m}$. Magnetic moment $\mu = IA = \dfrac{ev}{2\pi r}\pi r^2 = \dfrac{evr}{2} = \dfrac{e^2Br^2}{2m}$.
    $\mu = \dfrac{e^2B}{2m}\cdot\dfrac{h}{\pi eB} = \dfrac{he}{2\pi m}$.

  4. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 30
    A particle of mass $m$ is moving around the origin with a constant force $F$ pulling it towards the origin. If Bohr model is used to describe its motion, the radius $r$ of the $n^{\text{th}}$ orbit and the particle's speed $v$ in the orbit depend on $n$ as

    1. $r \propto n^{1/3};\ v \propto n^{1/3}$
    2. $r \propto n^{1/3};\ v \propto n^{2/3}$
    3. $r \propto n^{2/3};\ v \propto n^{1/3}$
    4. $r \propto n^{4/3};\ v \propto n^{-1/3}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    $F = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ gives $v \propto r^{1/2}$. Bohr's condition $mvr = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$ gives $r^{3/2} \propto n$.
    So $r \propto n^{2/3}$ and $v \propto r^{1/2} \propto n^{1/3}$.

  5. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 15
    Match List I with List II.

    List I
    (Spectral Lines of Hydrogen for transitions from)    		List II
    (Wavelengths (nm))
    A.$n_2 = 3$ to $n_1 = 2$I.410.2
    B.$n_2 = 4$ to $n_1 = 2$II.434.1
    C.$n_2 = 5$ to $n_1 = 2$III.656.3
    D.$n_2 = 6$ to $n_1 = 2$IV.486.1
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. A-IV, B-III, C-I, D-II
    2. A-I, B-II, C-III, D-IV
    3. A-II, B-I, C-IV, D-III
    4. A-III, B-IV, C-II, D-I
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    These are Balmer lines. The larger the energy gap, the smaller $\lambda$: $3\to2$ gives the longest $\lambda$ (656.3 nm, $H_\alpha$), then $4\to2$ (486.1 nm), $5\to2$ (434.1 nm) and $6\to2$ (410.2 nm).
    So A-III, B-IV, C-II, D-I.

  6. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 25
    Given below are two statements :
    $\textbf{Statement I :}$ Atoms are electrically neutral as they contain equal number of positive and negative charges.
    $\textbf{Statement II :}$ Atoms of each element are stable and emit their characteristic spectrum.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the $\textit{most appropriate}$ answer from the options given below :

    1. Statement I is correct but Statement II is incorrect.
    2. Statement I is incorrect but Statement II is correct.
    3. Both Statement I and Statement II are correct.
    4. Both Statement I and Statement II are incorrect.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Statement I is correct: an atom has as many electrons as protons, so it is electrically neutral.
    Statement II is not correct as a general statement: not every atom is stable (the nuclei of radioactive elements decay), and an atom emits its characteristic spectrum only when it is excited, not on its own.
    So Statement I is correct but Statement II is incorrect.

    Note: NTA's final key of June 2024 accepted two options for this question. After the Supreme Court's order of 23 July 2024, the key revised on 26 July 2024 accepts only option (1).

  7. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 26
    In hydrogen spectrum, the shortest wavelength in the Balmer series is $\lambda$. The shortest wavelength in the Bracket series is :

    1. $2\lambda$
    2. $4\lambda$
    3. $9\lambda$
    4. $16\lambda$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    The shortest $\lambda$ of a series is the series limit ($n_i \to \infty$): $\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = \dfrac{R}{n_f^2}$.
    Balmer ($n_f = 2$): $\lambda = \dfrac{4}{R}$. Brackett ($n_f = 4$): $\lambda' = \dfrac{16}{R} = 4\lambda$.

  8. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 49
    The radius of inner most orbit of hydrogen atom is $5.3\times10^{-11}$ m. What is the radius of third allowed orbit of hydrogen atom?

    1. 0.53 Å
    2. 1.06 Å
    3. 1.59 Å
    4. 4.77 Å
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    Bohr radius $r_n = n^2 r_1$. For $n = 3$: $r_3 = 9 \times 0.53$ Å $= 4.77$ Å.
