  1. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 27
    An unpolarized light beam travelling in air is incident on a medium of refractive index 1.73 at Brewster's angle. Then-

    1. reflected light is completely polarized and the angle of reflection is close to $60^\circ$
    2. reflected light is partially polarized and the angle of reflection is close to $30^\circ$
    3. both reflected and transmitted light are perfectly polarized with angles of reflection and refraction close to $60^\circ$ and $30^\circ$, respectively.
    4. transmitted light is completely polarized with angle of refraction close to $30^\circ$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Brewster's law: $\tan i_B = \mu = 1.73 = \sqrt{3}$, so $i_B = 60^\circ$, and the angle of reflection equals $60^\circ$.
    At this angle the reflected light is completely plane polarised, while the transmitted (refracted, at $30^\circ$) light is only partially polarised.

  2. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 43
    The intensity of transmitted light when a polaroid sheet, placed between two crossed polaroids at $22.5^\circ$ from the polarization axis of one of the polaroid, is ($I_0$ is the intensity of polarised light after passing through the first polaroid):

    1. $\dfrac{I_0}{2}$
    2. $\dfrac{I_0}{4}$
    3. $\dfrac{I_0}{8}$
    4. $\dfrac{I_0}{16}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    Malus's law twice: the middle sheet is at $22.5^\circ$ to the first and $67.5^\circ$ to the last.
    $I = I_0\cos^2 22.5^\circ\cos^2 67.5^\circ = I_0(\sin22.5^\circ\cos22.5^\circ)^2 = I_0\left(\dfrac{\sin45^\circ}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{I_0}{8}$.

  3. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 16
    If the monochromatic source in Young’s double slit experiment is replaced by white light, then

    1. there will be a central bright white fringe surrounded by a few coloured fringes.
    2. all bright fringes will be of equal width.
    3. interference pattern will disappear.
    4. there will be a central dark fringe surrounded by a few coloured fringes.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    At the centre the path difference is zero for every colour, so all colours reinforce and the central fringe is white. Away from the centre the fringe width ($\propto \lambda$) differs for each colour, so a few coloured fringes are seen before the pattern blurs out.

  4. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 19
    An unpolarised light beam strikes a glass surface at Brewster’s angle. Then

    1. both the reflected and refracted light will be completely polarised.
    2. the reflected light will be completely polarised but the refracted light will be partially polarised.
    3. the reflected light will be partially polarised.
    4. the refracted light will be completely polarised.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    At Brewster's angle the reflected ray is perpendicular to the refracted ray, and the reflected light is completely plane polarised (vibrations perpendicular to the plane of incidence). The refracted light still contains both components, so it is only partially polarised.

  5. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 25
    For Young’s double slit experiment, two statements are given below:
    $\textbf{Statement I :}$ If screen is moved away from the plane of slits, angular separation of the fringes remains constant.
    $\textbf{Statement II :}$ If the monochromatic source is replaced by another monochromatic source of larger wavelength, the angular separation of fringes decreases.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the $\textit{correct}$ answer from the options given below:

    1. Both Statement I and Statement II are true.
    2. Both Statement I and Statement II are false.
    3. Statement I is true but Statement II is false.
    4. Statement I is false but Statement II is true.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    The angular fringe separation is $\theta = \lambda/d$.
    Statement I is true: $\theta$ does not depend on the slit-to-screen distance $D$.
    Statement II is false: a larger $\lambda$ increases $\theta$.

  6. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 24
    In a Young's double slit experiment, a student observes 8 fringes in a certain segment of screen when a monochromatic light of 600 nm wavelength is used. If the wavelength of light is changed to 400 nm, then the number of fringes he would observe in the same region of the screen is

    1. 6
    2. 8
    3. 9
    4. 12
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    Fringe width $\beta = \lambda D/d$, so the number of fringes in a fixed length is inversely proportional to $\lambda$.
    $n_2 = 8 \times \dfrac{600}{400} = 12$.

  7. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 29
    A light ray falls on a glass surface of refractive index $\sqrt{3}$, at an angle 60°. The angle between the refracted and reflected rays would be

    1. 30°
    2. 60°
    3. 90°
    4. 120°
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    Snell's law: $\sin r = \dfrac{\sin 60°}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so $r = 30°$.
    The reflected ray makes 60° with the normal, so the angle between the reflected and refracted rays is $180° - 60° - 30° = 90°$. (Here $\tan 60° = \sqrt{3}$, so 60° is the Brewster angle.)
