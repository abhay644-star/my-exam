  1. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 137
    The inactive form of Bt toxin is converted to the active form in the insect gut ________

    1. due to alkaline pH
    2. due to acidic pH
    3. by proteases
    4. by nucleases
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Bt toxin exists as an inactive protoxin in the bacterium. Once ingested, the alkaline pH of the insect gut solubilises the crystals and converts the protoxin into the active toxin, which binds midgut epithelial cells and causes pores, lysis and death of the insect.

  2. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 114
    Given below are two statements :
    $\textbf{Statement I :}$ Transfer RNAs and ribosomal RNA do not interact with mRNA.
    $\textbf{Statement II :}$ RNA interference (RNAi) takes place in all eukaryotic organisms as a method of cellular defence.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the $\textbf{most appropriate}$ answer from the options given below :

    1. Both Statement I and Statement II are correct
    2. Both Statement I and Statement II are incorrect
    3. Statement I is correct but Statement II is incorrect
    4. Statement I is incorrect but Statement II is correct
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    During translation tRNA anticodons pair with mRNA codons and the ribosomal RNA binds the mRNA, so Statement I is incorrect.
    RNA interference takes place in all eukaryotic organisms as a method of cellular defence (silencing of a specific mRNA by complementary dsRNA), so Statement II is correct.

  3. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 116
    Which of the following genetically engineered organisms was used by Eli Lilly to prepare human insulin?

    1. Bacterium
    2. Yeast
    3. Virus
    4. Phage
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    In 1983 Eli Lilly prepared two DNA sequences for the A and B chains of human insulin and introduced them into plasmids of the bacterium $\textit{E. coli}$, which produced the insulin chains.

  4. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 144
    Silencing of specific mRNA is possible via RNAi because of -

    1. Complementary dsRNA
    2. Inhibitory ssRNA
    3. Complementary tRNA
    4. Non-complementary ssRNA
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    In RNA interference a complementary double-stranded RNA binds to the specific mRNA and prevents its translation, silencing the gene.

  5. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 161
    Why can't insulin be given orally to diabetic patients?

    1. Human body will elicit strong immune response
    2. It will be digested in Gastro-Intestinal (GI) tract
    3. Because of structural variation
    4. Its bioavailability will be increased
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    Insulin is a polypeptide hormone. Taken by mouth it would be digested by proteolytic enzymes in the gastro-intestinal tract before it could be absorbed, so it is given by injection.

  6. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 124
    Given below are two statements:
    $\textbf{Statement I :}$ Bt toxins are insect group specific and coded by a gene $\textit{cry}$ IAc.
    $\textbf{Statement II :}$ Bt toxin exists as inactive protoxin in $\textit{B. thuringiensis}$. However, after ingestion by the insect the inactive protoxin gets converted into active form due to acidic pH of the insect gut.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below:

    1. Statement I is true but Statement II is false
    2. Statement I is false but Statement II is true
    3. Both Statement I and Statement II are true
    4. Both Statement I and Statement II are false
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Bt toxins are specific to insect groups and are coded by $\textit{cry}$ genes; $\textit{cry}$IAc (with $\textit{cry}$IIAb) controls cotton bollworms. So Statement I is true.
    The inactive protoxin is converted into the active toxin in the $\textbf{alkaline}$ pH of the insect gut, not an acidic one, so Statement II is false.

  7. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 168
    Match List I with List II :

    List IList II
    A.$\alpha$-1 antitrypsinI.Cotton bollworm
    B.Cry IAbII.ADA deficiency
    C.Cry IAcIII.Emphysema
    D.Enzyme replacement therapyIV.Corn borer
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. A-III, B-IV, C-I, D-II
    2. A-II, B-IV, C-I, D-III
    3. A-II, B-I, C-IV, D-III
    4. A-III, B-I, C-II, D-IV
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Transgenic animals are used to produce $\alpha$-1 antitrypsin, used to treat emphysema (A-III). The gene $\textit{cry IAb}$ controls the corn borer (B-IV), and $\textit{cry IAc}$ and $\textit{cry IIAb}$ control cotton bollworms (C-I). Enzyme replacement therapy (injection of functional ADA) is one treatment for ADA deficiency (D-II).

  8. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 166
    Which one of the following techniques does not serve the purpose of early diagnosis of a disease for its early treatment?

    1. Recombinant DNA Technology
    2. Serum and Urine analysis
    3. Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) technique
    4. Enzyme Linked Immuno-Sorbent Assay (ELISA) technique
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    Conventional methods such as serum and urine analysis can detect a disease only after symptoms have appeared, when the pathogen numbers are high. Recombinant DNA technology, PCR and ELISA can detect a pathogen at very low concentration, so they allow early diagnosis.

  9. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 136
    Transposons can be used during which one of the following ?

    1. Polymerase Chain Reaction
    2. Gene Silencing
    3. Autoradiography
    4. Gene sequencing
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    In RNA interference (gene silencing), the complementary RNA may come from mobile genetic elements (transposons) that replicate through an RNA intermediate.

  10. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 183
    In gene therapy of Adenosine Deaminase (ADA) deficiency, the patient requires periodic infusion of genetically engineered lymphocytes because :

    1. Retroviral vector is introduced into these lymphocytes.
    2. Gene isolated from marrow cells producing ADA is introduced into cells at embryonic stages
    3. Lymphocytes from patient's blood are grown in culture, outside the body.
    4. Genetically engineered lymphocytes are not immortal cells.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    The lymphocytes given the functional ADA gene are not immortal; they die after a time, so the treatment has to be repeated periodically.

  11. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 188
    Statements related to human Insulin are given below.
    Which statement(s) is/are $\textbf{correct}$ about genetically engineered Insulin?
    (a) Pro-hormone insulin contain extra stretch of C-peptide
    (b) A-peptide and B-peptide chains of insulin were produced separately in $\textit{E.coli}$, extracted and combined by creating disulphide bond between them.
    (c) Insulin used for treating Diabetes was extracted from Cattles and Pigs.
    (d) Pro-hormone Insulin needs to be processed for converting into a mature and functional hormone.
    (e) Some patients develop allergic reactions to the foreign insulin.
    Choose $\textbf{the most appropriate}$ answer from the options given below:

    1. (a), (b) and (d) only
    2. (b) only
    3. (c) and (d) only
    4. (c), (d) and (e) only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    Only (b) describes genetically engineered insulin: Eli Lilly made the A and B chains separately in E. coli and joined them with disulphide bonds, so no pro-hormone is involved.
    (a) and (d) describe natural pro-insulin, and (c) and (e) describe the older insulin taken from cattle and pigs.
