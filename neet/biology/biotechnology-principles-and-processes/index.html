  1. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 162
    During PCR, primers bind to the DNA strands in the ______step.

    1. denaturation
    2. extension
    3. annealing
    4. ligation
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    A PCR cycle has three steps: denaturation (strands separate at high temperature), annealing (the mixture is cooled and primers bind to complementary sequences on the single strands) and extension (Taq polymerase extends the primers). Primer binding is annealing.

  2. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 168
    Given below are two statements :
    Statement I : Plasmids are autonomously replicating DNA.
    Statement II : Plasmids are extrachromosomal DNA.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the most appropriate answer from the options given below :

    1. Both Statement I and Statement II are correct
    2. Both Statement I and Statement II are incorrect
    3. Statement I is correct but Statement II is incorrect
    4. Statement I is incorrect but Statement II is correct
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Plasmids are small circular DNA molecules present outside the bacterial chromosome (extrachromosomal) and they carry their own origin of replication, so they replicate autonomously. Both statements are correct.

  3. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 176
    Given below are two statements : one is labelled as Assertion A and the other is labelled as Reason R.
    Assertion A : In recombinant DNA technology, lysozyme is used for disrupting bacterial cells while cellulase is for plant cells.
    Reason R : Isolation of genetic material needs disruption of cells.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the most appropriate answer from the options given below :

    1. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is correct but R is not correct
    4. A is not correct but R is correct
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    To isolate DNA, the cell must first be broken open to release it. Lysozyme digests the bacterial cell wall and cellulase digests the plant cell wall, so these enzymes are used for cell disruption. R gives the reason for using them, so both are correct and R explains A.

  4. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 180
    Match List-I with List-II.

    List-IList-II
    A.TransformationI.Restriction enzyme
    B.Cloning siteII.Transfer DNA to host bacteria
    C.SelectionIII.Replication
    D.OriIV.Antibiotic
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

    1. A-II, B-I, C-IV, D-III
    2. A-I, B-II, C-IV, D-III
    3. A-III, B-IV, C-II, D-I
    4. A-IV, B-I, C-III, D-II
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Transformation is the introduction of DNA into host bacteria (A-II). A cloning site is a recognition site for a restriction enzyme (B-I). Selection of transformants uses antibiotic-resistance markers (C-IV). Ori is the origin of replication (D-III).

  5. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 92
    Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplifies DNA following the equation.

    1. $\mathrm{N^2}$
    2. $2^n$
    3. $2n + 1$
    4. $\mathrm{2N^2}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    Each PCR cycle doubles the number of DNA copies, so after $n$ cycles one DNA molecule gives $2^n$ copies (exponential amplification).

  6. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 115
    In the above represented plasmid an alien piece of DNA is inserted at EcoRI site. Which of the following strategies will be chosen to select the recombinant colonies?

    1. Using ampicillin & tetracyclin containing medium plate.
    2. Blue color colonies will be selected.
    3. White color colonies will be selected.
    4. Blue color colonies grown on ampicillin plates can be selected.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    The EcoRI site lies within the $\beta$-galactosidase gene. Inserting foreign DNA there inactivates the gene (insertional inactivation).
    On a medium with a chromogenic substrate, recombinant colonies cannot make $\beta$-galactosidase and stay white, while non-recombinants give blue colonies. So white colonies are selected (the amp and tet genes stay intact in both kinds).

  7. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 126
    Identify the part of a bio-reactor which is used as a foam braker from the given figure.

    1. A
    2. B
    3. D
    4. C
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    In a simple stirred-tank bioreactor the motor sits on top (B), the foam braker is the paddle just below the lid at the top of the culture (C), flat-bladed impellers stir the medium (A), and the sterile air sparger is at the bottom (D).
    So the foam braker is C, option (4).

  8. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 136
    Given below are two statements :
    $\textbf{Statement I :}$ The DNA fragments extracted from gel electrophoresis can be used in construction of recombinant DNA.
    $\textbf{Statement II :}$ Smaller size DNA fragments are observed near anode while larger fragments are found near the wells in an agarose gel.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the $\textbf{most appropriate}$ answer from the options given below :

    1. Both statement I and statement II are correct
    2. Both statement I and statement II are incorrect
    3. Statement I is correct but statement II is incorrect
    4. Statement I is incorrect but statement II is correct
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Separated DNA bands are cut out of the agarose gel and extracted (elution); the purified fragments are used to construct recombinant DNA, so Statement I is correct.
    DNA is negatively charged and moves towards the anode; smaller fragments move farther, so they lie near the anode while larger fragments stay near the wells. Statement II is correct.

  9. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 172
    Which of the following enzyme(s) are $\textbf{NOT}$ essential for gene cloning?
    A. Restriction enzymes
    B. DNA ligase
    C. DNA mutase
    D. DNA recombinase
    E. DNA polymerase
    Choose the $\textbf{correct}$ answer from the options given below :

    1. C and D only
    2. A and B only
    3. D and E only
    4. B and C only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Gene cloning needs restriction enzymes to cut DNA, DNA ligase to join the fragments and DNA polymerase (for example in PCR) to amplify the gene.
    'DNA mutase' and 'DNA recombinase' are not among the essential tools, so C and D only.

  10. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 179
    The blue and white selectable markers have been developed which differentiate recombinant colonies from non-recombinant colonies on the basis of their ability to produce colour in the presence of a chromogenic substrate.
    Given below are two statements about this method:
    $\textbf{Statement I :}$ The blue coloured colonies have DNA insert in the plasmid and they are identified as recombinant colonies.
    $\textbf{Statement II :}$ The colonies without blue colour have DNA insert in the plasmid and are identified as recombinant colonies.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the $\textbf{most appropriate}$ answer from the options given below :

    1. Both Statement I and Statement II are correct
    2. Both Statement I and Statement II are incorrect
    3. Statement I is correct but Statement II is incorrect
    4. Statement I is incorrect but Statement II is correct
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    The insert goes into the $\beta$-galactosidase coding region and inactivates it (insertional inactivation).
    Non-recombinant colonies still make the enzyme and turn blue with the chromogenic substrate; recombinant colonies stay colourless (white). So Statement I is incorrect and Statement II is correct.

  11. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 111
    What is the fate of a piece of DNA carrying only gene of interest which is transferred into an alien organism?
    A. The piece of DNA would be able to multiply itself independently in the progeny cells of the organism.
    B. It may get integrated into the genome of the recipient.
    C. It may multiply and be inherited along with the host DNA.
    D. The alien piece of DNA is not an integral part of chromosome.
    E. It shows ability to replicate.
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

    1. B and C only
    2. A and E only
    3. A and B only
    4. D and E only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    A piece of DNA carrying only the gene of interest has no origin of replication, so it cannot multiply on its own in the alien organism (A and E are wrong, and D describes a plasmid-like state it cannot maintain).
    It may, however, get integrated into the recipient's genome (B) and then multiply and be inherited along with the host DNA (C). So B and C only.

  12. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 126
    The capacity to generate a whole plant from any cell of the plant is called:

    1. Differentiation
    2. Somatic hybridization
    3. Totipotency
    4. Micropropagation
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    The capacity of a plant cell (or explant) to generate a whole plant is called totipotency; it is the basis of plant tissue culture and micropropagation.

  13. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 132
    Hind II always cuts DNA molecules at a particular point called recognition sequence and it consists of:

    1. 4 bp
    2. 10 bp
    3. 8 bp
    4. 6 bp
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    $\textit{Hind}$ II, the first restriction endonuclease isolated, always cuts DNA at a particular point by recognising a specific sequence of six base pairs, its recognition sequence.

  14. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 143
    Which of the following are fused in somatic hybridization involving two varieties of plants?

    1. Protoplasts
    2. Pollens
    3. Callus
    4. Somatic embryos
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    In somatic hybridisation the cell walls are digested away and the naked protoplasts of the two plant varieties are fused to give hybrid protoplasts, which are grown into somatic hybrids (for example the pomato).

  15. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 163
    The “Ti plasmid” of $\textit{Agrobacterium tumefaciens}$ stands for

    1. Tumor inducing plasmid
    2. Temperature independent plasmid
    3. Tumour inhibiting plasmid
    4. Tumor independent plasmid
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Ti stands for tumour inducing: this plasmid of $\textit{Agrobacterium tumefaciens}$ transfers a piece of DNA (T-DNA) into dicot plant cells and causes tumours (crown gall). It has been modified into a cloning vector that is no longer pathogenic.

  16. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 172
    Which of the following statements is incorrect?

    1. Bio-reactors are used to produce small scale bacterial cultures.
    2. Bio-reactors have an agitator system, an oxygen delivery system and foam control system.
    3. A bio-reactor provides optimal growth conditions for achieving the desired product.
    4. Most commonly used bio-reactors are of stirring type.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Bioreactors are large vessels (100–1000 litres) in which large volumes of culture are processed; small-scale cultures are grown in flasks in the laboratory. So statement (1) is incorrect. The other statements are correct: stirred-tank bioreactors are the most common, and they have agitator, oxygen delivery and foam control systems that keep growth conditions optimal.

  17. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 183
    The following diagram showing restriction sites in $\textit{E.coli}$ cloning vector pBR322. Find the role of ‘$X$’ and ‘$Y$’ genes :

    1. The gene ‘X’ is for protein involved in replication of Plasmid and ‘$Y$’ for resistance to antibiotics.
    2. Gene ‘$X$’ is responsible for recognition sites and ‘$Y$’ is responsible for antibiotic resistance.
    3. The gene ‘$X$’ is responsible for resistance to antibiotics and ‘$Y$’ for protein involved in the replication of Plasmid.
    4. The gene ‘$X$’ is responsible for controlling the copy number of the linked DNA and ‘$Y$’ for protein involved in the replication of Plasmid.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    In the standard map of pBR322, the two sites next to each other near Pvu II are $\textit{ori}$ and $\textit{rop}$. $X$ is $\textit{ori}$, the origin of replication, which also controls the copy number of the linked DNA. $Y$ is $\textit{rop}$, which codes for the proteins involved in the replication of the plasmid. The antibiotic resistance genes ($\textit{amp}^R$ and $\textit{tet}^R$) are elsewhere on the plasmid.

  19. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 128
    During the purification process for recombinant DNA technology, addition of chilled ethanol precipitates out

    1. RNA
    2. DNA
    3. Histones
    4. Polysaccharides
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    After the cell is broken open and RNA, proteins and other molecules are removed with enzymes (ribonuclease, protease), adding chilled ethanol makes the purified DNA precipitate out as a mass of fine threads.

  21. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 150
    Main steps in the formation of Recombinant DNA are given below. Arrange these steps in a correct sequence.
    A. Insertion of recombinant DNA into the host cell.
    B. Cutting of DNA at specific location by restriction enzyme.
    C. Isolation of desired DNA fragment.
    D. Amplification of gene of interest using PCR.
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. B, C, D, A
    2. C, A, B, D
    3. C, B, D, A
    4. B, D, A, C
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1) or (3) (either is accepted)

    NCERT lists the steps of recombinant DNA technology as isolation of DNA, cutting it at specific locations with restriction enzymes, amplification of the gene of interest by PCR, ligation into a vector and insertion of the recombinant DNA into the host: C, B, D, A (option 3).
    If C is read as isolating the desired fragment after the DNA has been cut (separating it by gel electrophoresis and eluting it), the order becomes B, C, D, A (option 1). Both readings end with amplification and insertion, and NTA accepts both.

    Note: NTA's final key accepts both option (1) and option (3). Statement C can mean isolating the DNA before cutting (C, B, D, A) or isolating the desired fragment after cutting (B, C, D, A).

  22. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 185
    Which of the following is not a cloning vector?

    1. BAC
    2. YAC
    3. pBR322
    4. Probe
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    BAC (bacterial artificial chromosome), YAC (yeast artificial chromosome) and the plasmid pBR322 are all cloning vectors. A probe is a labelled single-stranded piece of DNA or RNA used to find a complementary sequence, not a vector.

  23. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 119
    Given below are two statements : one is labelled as $\textbf{Assertion (A)}$ and the other is labelled as $\textbf{Reason (R)}$.
    $\textbf{Assertion (A) :}$
    Polymerase chain reaction is used in DNA amplification.
    $\textbf{Reason (R) :}$
    The ampicillin resistant gene is used as a selectable marker to check transformation
    In the light of the above statements, choose the $\textbf{correct}$ answer from the options given below :

    1. Both (A) and (R) are correct and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are correct but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is correct but (R) is not correct
    4. (A) is not correct but (R) is correct
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    Both are correct statements, but they are unrelated: PCR amplifies DNA, while an antibiotic resistance gene (ampicillin) is a selectable marker for picking out transformants.

  24. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 130
    Which one of the following statement is $\textbf{not true}$ regarding gel electrophoresis technique?

    1. The process of extraction of separated DNA strands from gel is called elution.
    2. The separated DNA fragments are stained by using ethidium bromide.
    3. The presence of chromogenic substrate gives blue coloured DNA bands on the gel.
    4. Bright orange coloured bands of DNA can be observed in the gel when exposed to UV light.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    DNA in the gel is stained with ethidium bromide and seen as bright orange bands under UV light; cutting out and extracting the bands is elution. A chromogenic substrate (blue colonies) is used in blue-white selection of recombinants, not in gel electrophoresis.

  25. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 144
    In the following palindromic base sequences of DNA, which one can be cut easily by particular restriction enzyme?

    1. 5′GATACT3′; 3′CTATGA5′
    2. 5′GAATTC3′; 3′CTTAAG5′
    3. 5′CTCAGT3′; 3′GAGTCA5′
    4. 5′GTATTC3′; 3′CATAAG5′
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    5′-GAATTC-3′ reads the same on both strands in the 5′→3′ direction (a palindrome) and is the recognition site of EcoRI. The other sequences are not palindromes.

  26. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 158
    Given below are two statements:
    $\textbf{Statement I:}$
    Restriction endonucleases recognise specific sequence to cut DNA known as palindromic nucleotide sequence.
    $\textbf{Statement II}$:
    Restriction endonucleases cut the DNA strand a little away from the centre of the palindromic site.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the $\textbf{most appropriate}$ answer from the options given below:

    1. Both Statement I and Statement II are correct
    2. Both Statement I and Statement II are incorrect
    3. Statement I is correct but Statement II is incorrect
    4. Statement I is incorrect but Statement II is correct
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Both are correct. Each restriction endonuclease recognises a specific palindromic sequence and cuts each strand a little away from the centre of the site, between the same two bases, leaving sticky ends.

  27. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 194
    Which of the following is $\textbf{not}$ a desirable feature of a cloning vector?

    1. Presence of origin of replication
    2. Presence of a marker gene
    3. Presence of single restriction enzyme site
    4. Presence of two or more recognition sites
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    A cloning vector should have a single recognition site for a given restriction enzyme. Two or more sites would cut the vector into several fragments and complicate cloning.
