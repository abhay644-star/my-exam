  1. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 138
    Given below are two statements :
    Statement I : Down’s syndrome is caused by the absence of one of the X-chromosomes.
    Statement II : Turner’s syndrome is caused by the presence of an additional copy of the chromosomes.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. Both Statement I and Statement II are correct
    2. Both Statement I and Statement II are incorrect
    3. Statement I is correct but Statement II is incorrect
    4. Statement I is incorrect but Statement II is correct
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    Down’s syndrome is caused by an additional copy of chromosome 21 (trisomy 21), so Statement I is incorrect. Turner’s syndrome is caused by the absence of one X chromosome (45, XO), so Statement II is also incorrect. The two descriptions are swapped.

  2. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 169
    For a person with blood group ‘O’, which of the following is not a possible combination of parents’ blood group genotypes?

    1. Father : $I^Ai$ and Mother : $I^Bi$
    2. Father : $I^Ai$ and Mother : $I^Ai$
    3. Father : $I^Bi$ and Mother : $I^Bi$
    4. Father : $I^AI^B$ and Mother : $I^Ai$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    A child with blood group O has genotype $ii$ and must receive an $i$ allele from each parent. In options (1)–(3) both parents carry $i$. In option (4) the father is $I^AI^B$ and has no $i$ allele, so an $ii$ child is impossible.

  3. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 175
    Given below are two statements : one is labelled as Assertion A and the other is labelled as Reason R.
    Assertion A : In an experiment, Mendel observed that the F1 progeny plants are all tall and none are dwarf.
    Reason R : Stem height is a contrasting trait, with tall being dominant and dwarf being recessive.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the most appropriate answer from the options given below :

    1. Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are correct but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is correct but R is not correct
    4. A is not correct but R is correct
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    In Mendel’s monohybrid cross ($TT \times tt$) all F1 plants are $Tt$ and tall, none dwarf. This is because tall is dominant over dwarf for the contrasting trait of stem height, so the dwarf allele is not expressed in the heterozygote. R correctly explains A.

  4. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 130
    What is the pattern of inheritance for polygenic trait?

    1. Mendelian inheritance pattern
    2. Non-mendelian inheritance pattern
    3. Autosomal dominant pattern
    4. X-linked recessive inheritance pattern
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    Polygenic traits such as human skin colour are controlled by several genes with additive effects and show continuous variation. They do not give the clear Mendelian ratios, so they follow a non-Mendelian pattern of inheritance.

  5. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 145
    Genes R and Y follow independent assortment. If RRYY produce round yellow seeds and rryy produce wrinkled green seeds, what will be the phenotypic ratio of the F2 generation?

    1. Phenotypic ratio - 1 : 2 : 1
    2. Phenotypic ratio - 3 : 1
    3. Phenotypic ratio - 9 : 3 : 3 : 1
    4. Phenotypic ratio - 9 : 7
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    The F1 (RrYy) is a dihybrid. With independent assortment, selfing gives round yellow : round green : wrinkled yellow : wrinkled green $= 9 : 3 : 3 : 1$ in the F2.

  6. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 152
    With the help of given pedigree, find out the probability for the birth of a child having no disease and being a carrier (has the disease mutation in one allele of the gene) in $\mathrm{F_3}$ generation.

    1. 1/4
    2. 1/2
    3. 1/8
    4. Zero
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Carrier females and affected males point to an X-linked recessive trait. The $\mathrm{F_2}$ cross is a carrier female ($\mathrm{X^AX^a}$) $\times$ an affected male ($\mathrm{X^aY}$).
    Children: $\mathrm{X^AX^a}$ (carrier daughter), $\mathrm{X^aX^a}$ (affected daughter), $\mathrm{X^AY}$ (normal son), $\mathrm{X^aY}$ (affected son), each with probability 1/4.
    The probability of an unaffected carrier child is 1/4.

  7. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 104
    Which one of the following can be explained on the basis of Mendel's Law of Dominance?
    A. Out of one pair of factors one is dominant and the other is recessive.
    B. Alleles do not show any expression and both the characters appear as such in $F_2$ generation.
    C. Factors occur in pairs in normal diploid plants.
    D. The discrete unit controlling a particular character is called factor.
    E. The expression of only one of the parental characters is found in a monohybrid cross.
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

    1. B, C and D only
    2. A, B, C, D and E
    3. A, B and C only
    4. A, C, D and E only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    The Law of Dominance states that characters are controlled by discrete units called factors (D), which occur in pairs (C), and that in a dissimilar pair one factor dominates the other (A). This explains why only one parental character is expressed in the $F_1$ of a monohybrid cross (E).
    B is wrong: alleles do express, and the recessive character reappears in $F_2$ because of segregation, not dominance. So A, C, D and E only.

  8. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 122
    A pink flowered Snapdragon plant was crossed with a red flowered Snapdragon plant. What type of phenotype/s is/are expected in the progeny?

    1. Only pink flowered plants
    2. Red, Pink as well as white flowered plants
    3. Only red flowered plants
    4. Red flowered as well as pink flowered plants
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    Flower colour in snapdragon shows incomplete dominance: RR is red, Rr pink and rr white.
    Pink (Rr) $\times$ red (RR) gives RR and Rr in a 1 : 1 ratio, i.e. red and pink flowered plants only.

  9. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 131
    In a plant, black seed color (BB/Bb) is dominant over white seed color (bb). In order to find out the genotype of the black seed plant, with which of the following genotype will you cross it?

    1. Bb
    2. BB/Bb
    3. BB
    4. bb
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    The genotype of a plant showing the dominant phenotype is found by a test cross with the homozygous recessive (bb). If all the progeny are black the plant is BB; if black and white appear in a 1 : 1 ratio it is Bb.

  10. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 134
    Match List I with List II

    List IList II
    A.Two or more alternative forms of a geneI.Back cross
    B.Cross of $\mathrm{F_1}$ progeny with homozygous recessive parentII.Ploidy
    C.Cross of $\mathrm{F_1}$ progeny with any of the parentsIII.Allele
    D.Number of chromosome sets in plantIV.Test cross
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

    1. A-III, B-IV, C-I, D-II
    2. A-IV, B-III, C-II, D-I
    3. A-I, B-II, C-III, D-IV
    4. A-II, B-I, C-III, D-IV
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Alternative forms of a gene are alleles (A-III). Crossing the $\mathrm{F_1}$ with the homozygous recessive parent is a test cross (B-IV). Crossing the $\mathrm{F_1}$ with either parent is a back cross (C-I). The number of chromosome sets is the ploidy (D-II).

  11. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 151
    Match List I with List II :

    List IList II
    A.Down’s syndromeI.$11^{\text{th}}$ chromosome
    B.$\alpha$-ThalassemiaII.‘X’ chromosome
    C.$\beta$-ThalassemiaIII.$21^{\text{st}}$ chromosome
    D.Klinefelter’s syndromeIV.$16^{\text{th}}$ chromosome
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. A-III, B-IV, C-I, D-II
    2. A-IV, B-I, C-II, D-III
    3. A-I, B-II, C-III, D-IV
    4. A-II, B-III, C-IV, D-I
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Down’s syndrome is trisomy of chromosome 21 (A-III). $\alpha$-Thalassemia is controlled by the genes HBA1 and HBA2 on chromosome 16 (B-IV). $\beta$-Thalassemia is controlled by the single gene HBB on chromosome 11 (C-I). Klinefelter’s syndrome (XXY) results from an extra X chromosome (D-II).

  12. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 199
    As per ABO blood grouping system, the blood group of father is $\mathrm{B^+}$, mother is $\mathrm{A^+}$ and child is $\mathrm{O^+}$. Their respective genotype can be
    A. $\mathrm{I^Bi\ /\ I^Ai\ /\ ii}$
    B. $\mathrm{I^BI^B\ /\ I^AI^A\ /\ ii}$
    C. $\mathrm{I^AI^B\ /\ iI^A\ /\ I^Bi}$
    D. $\mathrm{I^Ai\ /\ I^Bi\ /\ I^Ai}$
    E. $\mathrm{iI^B\ /\ iI^A\ /\ I^AI^B}$
    Choose the most appropriate answer from the options given below :

    1. C & B only
    2. D & E only
    3. A only
    4. B only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    A child of blood group O must be $\mathrm{ii}$, so each parent must carry one $\mathrm{i}$ allele. The father (group B) must be $\mathrm{I^Bi}$ and the mother (group A) must be $\mathrm{I^Ai}$. Only A ($\mathrm{I^Bi\ /\ I^Ai\ /\ ii}$) fits.

  13. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 105
    The phenomenon of pleiotropism refers to

    1. presence of several alleles of a single gene controlling a single crossover.
    2. presence of two alleles, each of the two genes controlling a single trait.
    3. a single gene affecting multiple phenotypic expression.
    4. more than two genes affecting a single character.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    In pleiotropy a single gene has more than one phenotypic effect (for example, the gene for phenylketonuria affects mental development as well as hair and skin pigmentation).
    Several genes affecting one character is polygenic inheritance, not pleiotropy.

  14. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 134
    Frequency of recombination between gene pairs on same chromosome as a measure of the distance between genes to map their position on chromosome, was used for the first time by

    1. Thomas Hunt Morgan
    2. Sutton and Boveri
    3. Alfred Sturtevant
    4. Henking
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    Alfred Sturtevant, a student of Morgan, used the frequency of recombination between gene pairs on the same chromosome as a measure of the distance between them and so mapped their positions on the chromosome.

  15. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 138
    Which of the following statements are correct about Klinefelter’s Syndrome?
    A. This disorder was first described by Langdon Down (1866).
    B. Such an individual has overall masculine development. However, the feminine development is also expressed.
    C. The affected individual is short statured.
    D. Physical, psychomotor and mental development is retarded.
    E. Such individuals are sterile.
    Choose the $\textbf{correct}$ answer from the options given below :

    1. A and B only
    2. C and D only
    3. B and E only
    4. A and E only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    Klinefelter’s syndrome (47, XXY) gives an individual with overall masculine development but some feminine features (such as gynaecomastia), and such individuals are sterile: B and E are correct.
    A, C and D describe Down’s syndrome (described by Langdon Down in 1866): short stature and retarded physical, psychomotor and mental development.

  16. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 154
    Which one of the following symbols represents mating between relatives in human pedigree analysis?

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    In pedigree charts a single horizontal line joins a male and a female in an ordinary mating, while a double line marks consanguineous mating (mating between relatives). Option (2) shows the double line. Option (3) is parents with an affected son and option (4) shows affected individuals.

  17. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 182
    Broad palm with single palm crease is visible in a person suffering from-

    1. Down’s syndrome
    2. Turner’s syndrome
    3. Klinefelter’s syndrome
    4. Thalassemia
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Down's syndrome (trisomy 21) is marked by short stature, a small round head, a furrowed tongue, a partly open mouth and a broad palm with a characteristic palm crease.

  18. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 118
    XO type of sex determination can be found in :

    1. $\textit{Drosophila}$
    2. Birds
    3. Grasshoppers
    4. Monkeys
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    In grasshoppers, males have only one X chromosome (XO) and females have XX. Drosophila and monkeys have XY, and birds have ZW.

  20. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 142
    Which of the following occurs due to the presence of autosome linked dominant trait ?

    1. Sickle cell anaemia
    2. Myotonic dystrophy
    3. Haemophilia
    4. Thalessemia
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    Myotonic dystrophy is an autosomal dominant disorder. Sickle cell anaemia and thalassaemia are autosomal recessive, and haemophilia is X-linked recessive.

  21. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 148
    Given below are two statements : one is labelled as $\textbf{Assertion (A)}$ and the other is labelled as $\textbf{Reason (R)}$.
    $\textbf{Assertion (A) :}$ Mendel’s law of Independent assortment does not hold good for the genes that are located closely on the same chromosome.
    $\textbf{Reason (R) :}$ Closely located genes assort independently.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the $\textbf{correct}$ answer from the options given below:

    1. Both (A) and (R) are correct and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are correct but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is correct but (R) is not correct
    4. (A) is not correct but (R) is correct
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    (A) is correct: closely placed genes on one chromosome are linked and tend to be inherited together. (R) is wrong for the same reason: linked genes do not assort independently.

  22. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 186
    If a colour blind female marries a man whose mother was also colour blind, what are the chances of her progeny having colour blindness?

    1. 25%
    2. 50%
    3. 75%
    4. 100%
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    The woman is X$^c$X$^c$. The man got his X from his colour-blind mother, so he is X$^c$Y.
    Every daughter is X$^c$X$^c$ and every son X$^c$Y: all the children are colour blind (100%).

  23. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 192
    The recombination frequency between the genes a & c is 5%, b & c is 15%, b & d is 9%, a & b is 20%, c & d is 24% and a & d is 29%. What will be the sequence of these genes on a linear chromosome?

    1. a, d, b, c
    2. d, b, a, c
    3. a, b, c, d
    4. a, c, b, d
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    Map distances add up along the chromosome: a–c 5 + c–b 15 = a–b 20; c–b 15 + b–d 9 = c–d 24; a–c 5 + c–b 15 + b–d 9 = a–d 29.
    So the order is a, c, b, d.
