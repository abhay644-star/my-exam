  1. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 150
    Sponges exchange $\mathrm{O_2}$ with $\mathrm{CO_2}$ by __________.

    1. simple diffusion over their entire body surfaces
    2. moist cuticle
    3. tracheal tubes
    4. gills
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    Lower invertebrates such as sponges, coelenterates and flatworms have no special respiratory organs; they exchange $\mathrm{O_2}$ and $\mathrm{CO_2}$ by simple diffusion over their entire body surface. Moist cuticle is used by earthworms, tracheae by insects and gills by most aquatic arthropods and molluscs.

  2. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 101
    Match $\textbf{List - I}$ with $\textbf{List - II}$.

    List - IList - II
    A.EmphysemaI.Rapid spasms in muscle due to low $\mathrm{Ca^{++}}$ in body fluid
    B.Angina PectorisII.Damaged alveolar walls and decreased respiratory surface
    C.Glomerulo-nephritisIII.Acute chest pain when not enough oxygen is reaching to heart muscle
    D.TetanyIV.Inflammation of glomeruli of kidney
    Choose the $\textbf{correct}$ answer from the options given below :

    1. A-III, B-I, C-IV, D-II
    2. A-III, B-I, C-II, D-IV
    3. A-II, B-IV, C-III, D-I
    4. A-II, B-III, C-IV, D-I
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    Emphysema: damaged alveolar walls, so the respiratory surface decreases. Angina pectoris: acute chest pain when too little oxygen reaches the heart muscle. Glomerulonephritis: inflammation of the glomeruli. Tetany: rapid muscle spasms due to low $\mathrm{Ca^{++}}$.
    So A-II, B-III, C-IV, D-I.

  3. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 153
    Which of the following factors are favourable for the formation of oxyhaemoglobin in alveoli?

    1. Low $\mathrm{pCO_2}$ and High $\mathrm{H^+}$ concentration
    2. Low $\mathrm{pCO_2}$ and High temperature
    3. High $\mathrm{pO_2}$ and High $\mathrm{pCO_2}$
    4. High $\mathrm{pO_2}$ and Lesser $\mathrm{H^+}$ concentration
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    In the alveoli, high $\mathrm{pO_2}$, low $\mathrm{pCO_2}$, lesser $\mathrm{H^+}$ concentration (higher pH) and lower temperature all favour the binding of oxygen to haemoglobin. High $\mathrm{H^+}$, high $\mathrm{pCO_2}$ and high temperature favour the release of oxygen in the tissues instead.

  4. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 155
    Match List I with List II :

    List IList II
    A.Expiratory capacityI.Expiratory reserve volume + Tidal volume + Inspiratory reserve volume
    B.Functional residual capacityII.Tidal volume + Expiratory reserve volume
    C.Vital capacityIII.Tidal volume + Inspiratory reserve volume
    D.Inspiratory capacityIV.Expiratory reserve volume + Residual volume
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. A-II, B-I, C-IV, D-III
    2. A-I, B-III, C-II, D-IV
    3. A-II, B-IV, C-I, D-III
    4. A-III, B-II, C-IV, D-I
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    Expiratory capacity = TV + ERV (A-II). Functional residual capacity = ERV + RV (B-IV). Vital capacity = ERV + TV + IRV (C-I). Inspiratory capacity = TV + IRV (D-III).

  5. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 173
    Vital capacity of lung is _______.

    1. IRV + ERV
    2. IRV + ERV + TV + RV
    3. IRV + ERV + TV − RV
    4. IRV + ERV + TV
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    Vital capacity is the maximum volume of air breathed out after a forced inspiration: $\mathrm{VC} = \mathrm{ERV} + \mathrm{TV} + \mathrm{IRV}$. Adding RV gives the total lung capacity.
