  1. NEET (UG) 2026, 21 June (re-exam), Code 50 · Question 144
    Arrange the following in descending order of number of species in the Amazonian rain forest.
    (a) Plants
    (b) Birds
    (c) Fishes
    (d) Invertebrates
    (e) Mammals
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. (c) $>$ (b) $>$ (d) $>$ (e) $>$ (a)
    2. (d) $>$ (a) $>$ (c) $>$ (b) $>$ (e)
    3. (e) $>$ (b) $>$ (a) $>$ (c) $>$ (d)
    4. (b) $>$ (a) $>$ (d) $>$ (c) $>$ (e)
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    The Amazonian rain forest has about 1,25,000 species of invertebrates, 40,000 plants, 3,000 fishes, 1,300 birds and 427 mammals. So the descending order is invertebrates $>$ plants $>$ fishes $>$ birds $>$ mammals, i.e. (d) $>$ (a) $>$ (c) $>$ (b) $>$ (e).

  2. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 99
    Which one of the following is an example of ex-situ conservation?

    1. National Park
    2. Wildlife Sanctuary
    3. Zoos and botanical gardens
    4. Protected areas
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    Ex-situ ('off-site') conservation protects species outside their natural habitat, as in zoos, botanical gardens, wildlife safari parks and seed banks. National parks, wildlife sanctuaries and other protected areas conserve species in their habitats (in-situ).

  3. NEET (UG) 2025, 4 May, Code 45 · Question 164
    Match $\textbf{List I}$ with $\textbf{List II}$ :

    List IList II
    A.The Evil QuartetI.Cryopreservation
    B.Ex situ conservationII.Alien species invasion
    C.$\textit{Lantana camara}$III.Causes of biodiversity losses
    D.DodoIV.Extinction
    Choose the option with all correct matches.

    1. A-III, B-II, C-I, D-IV
    2. A-III, B-I, C-II, D-IV
    3. A-III, B-IV, C-II, D-I
    4. A-III, B-II, C-IV, D-I
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    The 'Evil Quartet' names the four major causes of biodiversity loss; cryopreservation of gametes is an ex situ method; $\textit{Lantana camara}$ is an invasive alien weed; the dodo (Mauritius) is an extinct bird.
    So A-III, B-I, C-II, D-IV.

  4. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 102
    List of endangered species was released by-

    1. FOAM
    2. IUCN
    3. GEAC
    4. WWF
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    The IUCN (International Union for Conservation of Nature and Natural Resources) publishes the Red List (Red Data Book), which lists threatened and endangered species.

  5. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 103
    The type of conservation in which the threatened species are taken out from their natural habitat and placed in special setting where they can be protected and given special care is called;

    1. Semi-conservative method
    2. Sustainable development
    3. $\textit{in-situ}$ conservation
    4. Biodiversity conservation
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    Taking threatened species out of their natural habitat and protecting them in special settings (zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks) is $\textit{ex situ}$ conservation. That option is not offered; of the choices given, it is a form of biodiversity conservation, so option (4) is the intended answer. It is not $\textit{in situ}$ conservation, which protects species within their natural habitat.

  6. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 115
    These are regarded as major causes of biodiversity loss:
    A. Over exploitation
    B. Co-extinction
    C. Mutation
    D. Habitat loss and fragmentation
    E. Migration
    Choose the correct option:

    1. A, B and E only
    2. A, B and D only
    3. A, C and D only
    4. A, B, C and D only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    The four major causes of biodiversity loss (the 'Evil Quartet') are habitat loss and fragmentation, over-exploitation, alien species invasions and co-extinctions. Mutation and migration are not among them. So A, B and D only.

  7. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 128
    Tropical regions show greatest level of species richness because
    A. Tropical latitudes have remained relatively undisturbed for millions of years, hence more time was available for species diversification.
    B. Tropical environments are more seasonal.
    C. More solar energy is available in tropics.
    D. Constant environments promote niche specialization.
    E. Tropical environments are constant and predictable.
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

    1. A, B and E only
    2. A, B and D only
    3. A, C, D and E only
    4. A and B only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    The reasons given in NCERT for high tropical diversity are: the tropics have stayed relatively undisturbed for millions of years, giving more time for species diversification (A); tropical environments are less seasonal, relatively constant and predictable (E), which promotes niche specialisation (D); and more solar energy is available, giving higher productivity (C).
    B is wrong, since tropical environments are less seasonal. So A, C, D and E only.

  8. NEET (UG) 2024, 5 May, Code T1 · Question 139
    Match List I with List II

    List IList II
    A.Robert MayI.Species-Area relationship
    B.Alexander von HumboldtII.Long term ecosystem experiment using out door plots
    C.Paul EhrlichIII.Global species diversity at about 7 million
    D.David TilmanIV.Rivet popper hypothesis
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

    1. A-I, B-III, C-II, D-IV
    2. A-III, B-IV, C-II, D-I
    3. A-II, B-III, C-I, D-IV
    4. A-III, B-I, C-IV, D-II
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (4)

    Robert May estimated global species diversity at about 7 million (A-III). Alexander von Humboldt described the species-area relationship (B-I). Paul Ehrlich proposed the rivet popper hypothesis (C-IV). David Tilman carried out long-term ecosystem experiments using outdoor plots (D-II).

  9. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 107
    Among ‘The Evil Quartet’, which one is considered the most important cause driving extinction of species?

    1. Habitat loss and fragmentation
    2. Over exploitation for economic gain
    3. Alien species invasions
    4. Co-extinctions
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    The four major causes of biodiversity loss (the ‘Evil Quartet’) are habitat loss and fragmentation, over-exploitation, alien species invasions and co-extinctions. Habitat loss and fragmentation is the most important cause driving animals and plants to extinction.

  10. NEET (UG) 2023, 7 May, Code E1 · Question 125
    The historic Convention on Biological Diversity, ‘The Earth Summit’ was held in Rio de Janeiro in the year :

    1. 1985
    2. 1992
    3. 1986
    4. 2002
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    The Earth Summit (Convention on Biological Diversity) was held in Rio de Janeiro in 1992. The World Summit on Sustainable Development followed in Johannesburg in 2002.

  11. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 101
    Which of the following is $\textbf{not}$ a method of $\textit{ex situ}$ conservation?

    1. $\textit{In vitro}$ fertilization
    2. National Parks
    3. Micropropagation
    4. Cryopreservation
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (2)

    National parks protect species in their natural habitat, which is in situ conservation. In vitro fertilisation, micropropagation (tissue culture) and cryopreservation are all ex situ methods.

  12. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 112
    Habitat loss and fragmentation, over exploitation, alien species invasion and co-extinction are causes for:

    1. Population explosion
    2. Competition
    3. Biodiversity loss
    4. Natality
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (3)

    These are the four main causes of biodiversity loss (the 'Evil Quartet').

  13. NEET (UG) 2022, 17 July, Code Q1 · Question 151
    $\textit{In-situ}$ conservation refers to:

    1. Protect and conserve the whole ecosystem
    2. Conserve only high-risk species
    3. Conserve only endangered species
    4. Conserve only extinct species
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (1)

    In situ (on site) conservation protects species in their natural habitat, so the whole ecosystem is protected, as in national parks, sanctuaries and biosphere reserves.
