  1. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 1 · Question 52
    What is the energy (in J atom$^{-1}$) required for the following process ?
    $\mathrm{Li^{2+}(g) \rightarrow Li^{3+}(g) + e^-}$
    (Take the ionization energy for the H atom in the ground state as $2.18 \times 10^{-18}$ J atom$^{-1}$)

    1. $8.72 \times 10^{-18}$
    2. $1.962 \times 10^{-18}$
    3. $1.962 \times 10^{-17}$
    4. $6.54 \times 10^{-17}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $\mathrm{Li^{2+}}$ is hydrogen-like with $Z = 3$, electron in $n = 1$.
    $IE = Z^2 \times IE_\mathrm{H} = 9 \times 2.18 \times 10^{-18}$
    $= 1.962 \times 10^{-17}$ J atom$^{-1}$.

  2. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 71
    The surface of sodium metal is irradiated with radiation of wavelength $x$ nm. The kinetic energy of ejected electrons is $2.8\times10^{-20}$ J. The work function of sodium is 2.3 eV. The value of $x$ is __________ $\times10^2$ nm. (Nearest integer)
    (Given : $h=6.6\times10^{-34}$ J s ; 1 eV $=1.6\times10^{-19}$ J; $c=3.0\times10^8$ m s$^{-1}$)

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 5

    Photon energy $=\phi+K=2.3\times1.6\times10^{-19}+0.28\times10^{-19}=3.96\times10^{-19}$ J.
    $\lambda=\dfrac{hc}{E}=\dfrac{6.6\times10^{-34}\times3\times10^8}{3.96\times10^{-19}}=5\times10^{-7}$ m $=500$ nm.
    $500=5\times10^2$ nm, so the answer is $5$.

  3. JEE Main 2026, 4 April, Shift 1 · Question 52
    What is the ratio of wave number of first line (lowest energy line) of Balmer series of H atomic spectrum to first line of its Brackett series?

    1. $5:1$
    2. $5:0.81$
    3. $5:1.75$
    4. $5:27$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Balmer, first line ($3\to2$): $\bar\nu=R\left(\dfrac14-\dfrac19\right)=\dfrac{5R}{36}$.
    Brackett, first line ($5\to4$): $\bar\nu=R\left(\dfrac1{16}-\dfrac1{25}\right)=\dfrac{9R}{400}$.
    Ratio $=\dfrac{5}{36}\cdot\dfrac{400}{9}=\dfrac{2000}{324}\approx6.17=5:0.81$.

    Which of the following is correct set of $4$ quantum numbers of $19^{\text{th}}$ electron in Chromium (Atomic number $=24$) in accordance with Aufbau principle?

    1. $n=3,\ l=2,\ m=+2,\ s=+\dfrac12$
    2. $n=3,\ l=2,\ m=-2,\ s=+\dfrac12$
    3. $n=4,\ l=1,\ m=0,\ s=+\dfrac12$
    4. $n=4,\ l=0,\ m=0,\ s=+\dfrac12$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Aufbau order: $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^2\,3p^6$ accounts for $18$ electrons; the $19^{\text{th}}$ enters $4s$ (lower $n+l$ than $3d$).
    For $4s$: $n=4$, $l=0$, $m=0$, $s=+\dfrac12$.

    Given below are two statements :
    Statement I : When an electric discharge is passed through gaseous hydrogen, the hydrogen molecules dissociate and the energetically excited hydrogen atoms produce electromagnetic radiation of discrete frequencies.

    In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    3. Statement I is true but Statement II is false
    4. Statement I is false but Statement II is true
    Show answer and solution

    Shell $n$ has subshells $l=0$ to $n-1$ with $2l+1$ orbitals each: total $\sum(2l+1)=n^2$. Statement I is true.
    The orientation of the orbitals is given by $m_l=-l,\ldots,0,\ldots,+l$. Statement II is true.

  31. JEE Main 2025, 24 January, Shift 2 · Question 52
    For hydrogen atom, the orbital/s with lowest energy is/are :
    (A) 4s
    (B) $3p_x$
    (C) $3d_{x^2-y^2}$
    (D) $3d_{z^2}$
    (E) $4p_z$
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. (A) only
    2. (B) only
    3. (B), (C) and (D) only
    4. (A) and (E) only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    In the hydrogen atom the orbital energy depends only on n.
    The n = 3 orbitals ($3p_x$, $3d_{x^2-y^2}$, $3d_{z^2}$) are degenerate and lower than the n = 4 orbitals.
    Lowest: (B), (C) and (D).

  32. JEE Main 2025, 28 January, Shift 1 · Question 51
    In a multielectron atom, which of the following orbitals described by three quantum numbers will have same energy in absence of electric and magnetic fields?
    A. $n=1, l=0, m_l=0$
    B. $n=2, l=0, m_l=0$
    C. $n=2, l=1, m_l=1$
    D. $n=3, l=2, m_l=1$
    E. $n=3, l=2, m_l=0$
    Choose the <b><i>correct</i></b> answer from the options given below:

    1. D and E Only
    2. C and D Only
    3. B and C Only
    4. A and B Only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    In a multielectron atom the orbital energy depends on both $n$ and $l$ (not on $m_l$ without external fields).
    Only D and E share $n=3$, $l=2$ (both 3d orbitals), so they are degenerate. B (2s) and C (2p) differ in $l$.
    Answer: D and E only.

  33. JEE Main 2025, 28 January, Shift 2 · Question 51
    Which of the following is/are not correct with respect to energy of atomic orbitals of hydrogen atom ?
    (A) 1s < 2p < 3d < 4s
    (B) 1s < 2s = 2p < 3s = 3p
    (C) 1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p
    (D) 1s < 2s < 4s < 3d

    2. (C) and (D) only

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

  34. JEE Main 2025, 29 January, Shift 1 · Question 52
    If $a_0$ is denoted as the Bohr radius of hydrogen atom, then what is the de-Broglie wavelength $(\lambda)$ of the electron present in the second orbit of hydrogen atom ? [n : any integer]

    1. $\dfrac{8\pi a_0}{n}$
    2. $\dfrac{4\pi a_0}{n}$
    3. $\dfrac{4n}{\pi a_0}$
    4. $\dfrac{2a_0}{n\pi}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Radius of the second orbit: $r_2=2^2a_0=4a_0$.
    Bohr's quantum condition in de Broglie form is $2\pi r=n\lambda$, so $\lambda=\dfrac{2\pi(4a_0)}{n}=\dfrac{8\pi a_0}{n}$ (with $n=2$ this is $4\pi a_0$).

  35. JEE Main 2025, 29 January, Shift 2 · Question 52
    Given below are two statements :
    <b>Statement (I) :</b> It is impossible to specify simultaneously with arbitrary precision, both the linear momentum and the position of a particle.
    <b>Statement (II) :</b> If the uncertainty in the measurement of position and uncertainty in measurement of momentum are equal for an electron, then the uncertainty in the measurement of velocity is $\ge\sqrt{\dfrac{h}{\pi}}\times\dfrac{1}{2m}$.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the <b>correct</b> answer from the options given below :

    1. Both <b>Statement I</b> and <b>Statement II</b> are true
    2. Both <b>Statement I</b> and <b>Statement II</b> are false
    3. <b>Statement I</b> is true but <b>Statement II</b> is false
    4. <b>Statement I</b> is false but <b>Statement II</b> is true
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    I is Heisenberg's uncertainty principle: correct.
    II: $\Delta x\,\Delta p\ge\dfrac{h}{4\pi}$ with $\Delta x=\Delta p$ gives $\Delta p\ge\dfrac12\sqrt{\dfrac h\pi}$, so $\Delta v=\dfrac{\Delta p}{m}\ge\dfrac{1}{2m}\sqrt{\dfrac h\pi}$: correct.
