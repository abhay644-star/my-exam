Solved questions from this paper

A few questions with their official answers and worked solutions. All 75 are in the online test.

  1. Question 1 · Mathematics · Sets
    Let $A=\{(\alpha, \beta)\in\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R} : |\alpha-1|\le4 \text{ and } |\beta-5|\le6\}$ and $B=\{(\alpha, \beta)\in\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R} : 16(\alpha-2)^2+9(\beta-6)^2\le144\}$. Then

    1. $A\subset B$
    2. $B\subset A$
    3. $A\cup B=\{(x, y) : -4\le x\le4, -1\le y\le11\}$
    4. neither $A\subset B$ nor $B\subset A$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    A is the rectangle $-3\le\alpha\le5$, $-1\le\beta\le11$.
    B is the region inside the ellipse $\dfrac{(\alpha-2)^2}{9}+\dfrac{(\beta-6)^2}{16}\le1$, which lies within $-1\le\alpha\le5$, $2\le\beta\le10$.
    So $B\subset A$ (and $A\not\subset B$, e.g. the corner $(-3, -1)$).

  2. Question 2 · Mathematics · Relations and Functions
    If the range of the function $f(x)=\dfrac{5-x}{x^2-3x+2}$, $x\ne1, 2$, is $(-\infty, \alpha]\cup[\beta, \infty)$, then $\alpha^2+\beta^2$ is equal to :

    1. $188$
    2. $190$
    3. $192$
    4. $194$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    $y=\dfrac{5-x}{x^2-3x+2}\iff yx^2+(1-3y)x+(2y-5)=0$ must have a real root $x\ne1, 2$.
    Discriminant: $(1-3y)^2-4y(2y-5)=y^2+14y+1\ge0$, so $y\le-7-4\sqrt3$ or $y\ge-7+4\sqrt3$ (the excluded $x=1, 2$ do not satisfy the equation, and $y=0$ comes from $x=5$).
    $\alpha^2+\beta^2=(7+4\sqrt3)^2+(7-4\sqrt3)^2=2(49+48)=194$.

  3. Question 26 · Physics · Units and Measurements
    The dimension of $\sqrt{\dfrac{\mu_0}{\epsilon_0}}$ is equal to that of :
    ($\mu_0$ = Vacuum permeability and $\epsilon_0$ = Vacuum permittivity)

    1. Voltage
    2. Resistance
    3. Capacitance
    4. Inductance
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $\mu_0$ has units $\text{V s A}^{-1}\text{m}^{-1}$ and $\epsilon_0$ has units $\text{A s V}^{-1}\text{m}^{-1}$.
    $\dfrac{\mu_0}{\epsilon_0}$ has units $\text{V}^2\text{A}^{-2}=\Omega^2$, so $\sqrt{\mu_0/\epsilon_0}$ (about $377\ \Omega$, the impedance of free space) has the dimensions of resistance.

  4. Question 28 · Physics · Laws of Motion
    An object with mass 500 g moves along $x$-axis with speed $v=4\sqrt x$ m/s. The force acting on the object is :

    1. $5$ N
    2. $4$ N
    3. $8$ N
    4. $6$ N
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $a=v\dfrac{dv}{dx}=4\sqrt x\cdot\dfrac{2}{\sqrt x}=8$ m/s$^2$ (constant).
    $F=ma=0.5\times8=4$ N.

  5. Question 51 · Chemistry · Structure of Atom
    The extra stability of half-filled subshell is due to :
    (A) Symmetrical distribution of electrons
    (B) Smaller coulombic repulsion energy
    (C) The presence of electrons with the same spin in non-degenerate orbitals
    (D) Larger exchange energy
    (E) Relatively smaller shielding of electrons by one another
    Indentify the correct statements :

    1. (A), (B) and (D) only
    2. (B), (C) and (D) only
    3. (A), (B), (D) and (E) only
    4. (B), (D) and (E) only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Half-filled subshells are stable because of the symmetrical distribution of electrons (A) and the large exchange energy of electrons with parallel spins (D); electrons of parallel spin keep apart, lowering the repulsion (B); and electrons in the same subshell shield one another relatively little, so they are held more strongly (E).
    (C) is wrong: the orbitals of a subshell are degenerate.
    (A), (B), (D) and (E).

  6. Question 52 · Chemistry · Thermodynamics
    The correct statement amongst the following is :

    1. The term 'standard state' implies that the temperature is 0$^\circ$C.
    2. The standard state of a pure gas is the pure gas at a pressure of 1 bar and temperature 273 K.
    3. $\Delta_fH^\theta_{298}$ is zero for O(g)
    4. $\Delta_fH^\theta_{500}$ is zero for $\mathrm{O_2(g)}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    The standard state fixes the pressure (1 bar) but not the temperature, so (1) and (2) are wrong.
    The standard enthalpy of formation is zero for an element in its reference state at any temperature: $\mathrm{O_2(g)}$ qualifies (4), atomic O(g) does not (3).
