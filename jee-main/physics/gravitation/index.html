  1. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 1 · Question 30
    A planet ($\mathrm{P_1}$) is moving around the star of mass $2M$ in the orbit of radius $R$. Another planet ($\mathrm{P_2}$) is moving around another star of mass $4M$ in a orbit of radius $2R$. Ratio of time periods of revolution of $\mathrm{P_2}$ and $\mathrm{P_1}$ is ________.

    1. $\dfrac{1}{2}$
    2. $2$
    3. $4$
    4. $\dfrac{1}{4}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{r^3}{GM_s}}$, so $T \propto \sqrt{r^3/M_s}$.
    $\dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = \sqrt{\dfrac{(2R)^3/(4M)}{R^3/(2M)}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{8}{2}} = 2$.

  2. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 31
    If a body of mass 1 kg falls on the earth from infinity, it attains velocity $(v)$ and kinetic energy $(k)$ on reaching the surface of earth. The values of $v$ and $k$ respectively are __________.
    (Take radius of earth to be 6400 km and $g=9.8$ m/s$^2$)

    1. 11.2 km/s; $6.27\times10^7$ J
    2. 11.2 km/s; $12.54\times10^7$ J
    3. 8.8 km/s; $6.27\times10^7$ J
    4. 8.8 km/s; $12.54\times10^7$ J
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Energy conservation: $\dfrac12mv^2=\dfrac{GMm}{R}=mgR$.
    $v=\sqrt{2gR}=\sqrt{2\times9.8\times6.4\times10^6}=\sqrt{1.2544\times10^8}=11.2$ km/s.
    $k=mgR=1\times9.8\times6.4\times10^6=6.27\times10^7$ J.

  3. JEE Main 2026, 4 April, Shift 2 · Question 30
    The height in terms of radius of the earth ($R$), at which the acceleration due to gravity becomes $\dfrac g9$, where $g$ is acceleration due to gravity on earth's surface, is _______.

    1. $\sqrt3R$
    2. $2\sqrt2R$
    3. $2R$
    4. $\dfrac49R$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $g_h=\dfrac{g}{(1+h/R)^2}=\dfrac g9\Rightarrow1+\dfrac hR=3$.
    $h=2R$.

  4. JEE Main 2026, 5 April, Shift 1 · Question 28
    When one moves from a point $16$ km below the earth's surface to a point $16$ km above the earth's surface. The change in g is approximately $\alpha$ %. The value of $\alpha$ is _____.
    (Take radius of the earth $= 6400$ km.)

    1. $0.12$
    2. $0.25$
    3. $0.50$
    4. $0.75$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    At depth $d$: $g_1=g\left(1-\dfrac dR\right)=g(1-0.0025)$.
    At height $h\ll R$: $g_2\approx g\left(1-\dfrac{2h}{R}\right)=g(1-0.005)$.
    Change $=g_1-g_2=0.0025\,g$, i.e. about $0.25\%$.

  5. JEE Main 2026, 5 April, Shift 2 · Question 32
    A body of mass $m$ is taken from the surface of earth to a height equal to twice the radius of earth $(R_e)$. The increase in potential energy will be __________.
    ($g$ is acceleration due to gravity at the surface of earth)

    1. $\dfrac12mgR_e$
    2. $\dfrac34mgR_e$
    3. $\dfrac14mgR_e$
    4. $\dfrac23mgR_e$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    $\Delta U=GMm\left(\dfrac{1}{R_e}-\dfrac{1}{3R_e}\right)=\dfrac23\dfrac{GMm}{R_e}$.
    With $GM=gR_e^2$: $\Delta U=\dfrac23mgR_e$.

  6. JEE Main 2026, 21 January, Shift 1 · Question 33
    Initially a satellite of 100 kg is in a circular orbit of radius $1.5R_E$. This satellite can be moved to a circular orbit of radius $3R_E$ by supplying $\alpha\times10^6$ J of energy The value of $\alpha$ is __________. (Take Radius of Earth $R_E=6\times10^6$ m and $g=10\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$)

    1. $100$
    2. $1000$
    3. $150$
    4. $500$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Orbital energy $E=-\dfrac{GMm}{2r}=-\dfrac{gR_E^2m}{2r}$.
    $\Delta E=\dfrac{gR_E^2m}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{1.5R_E}-\dfrac{1}{3R_E}\right)=\dfrac{gR_Em}{6}$.
    $=\dfrac{10\times6\times10^6\times100}{6}=10^9\ \mathrm{J}=1000\times10^6$ J, so $\alpha=1000$.

  7. JEE Main 2026, 22 January, Shift 1 · Question 28
    The escape velocity from a spherical planet $A$ is 10 km/s. The escape velocity from another planet $B$ whose density and radius are 10% of those of planet $A$, is ______ m/s.

    1. $1000$
    2. $1000\sqrt2$
    3. $100\sqrt{10}$
    4. $200\sqrt5$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $v_e=\sqrt{\dfrac{2GM}{R}}=R\sqrt{\dfrac{8\pi G\rho}{3}}$, so $v_e\propto R\sqrt{\rho}$.
    $v_B=10\ \text{km/s}\times0.1\times\sqrt{0.1}=\dfrac{10^4}{10\sqrt{10}}\ \text{m/s}=100\sqrt{10}$ m/s.

  8. JEE Main 2026, 22 January, Shift 1 · Question 30
    Net gravitational force at the center of a square is found to be $F_1$ when four particles having mass $M$, $2M$, $3M$ and $4M$ are placed at the four corners of the square as shown in figure and it is $F_2$ when the positions of $3M$ and $4M$ are interchanged. The ratio $\dfrac{F_1}{F_2}$ is $\dfrac{\alpha}{\sqrt5}$. The value of $\alpha$ is _______.

    1. $1$
    2. $2$
    3. $2\sqrt5$
    4. $3$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Each mass pulls the centre towards its corner with a force proportional to its mass. Put $4M$ at $(-1,1)$, $3M$ at $(1,1)$, $M$ at $(-1,-1)$, $2M$ at $(1,-1)$.
    $F_1\propto(-4+3-1+2,\ 4+3-1-2)=(0,4)$, magnitude $4$.
    After swapping $3M$ and $4M$: $(-3+4-1+2,\ 3+4-1-2)=(2,4)$, magnitude $2\sqrt5$.
    $\dfrac{F_1}{F_2}=\dfrac{4}{2\sqrt5}=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt5}$, so $\alpha=2$.

  9. JEE Main 2026, 22 January, Shift 2 · Question 31
    Given below are two statements :
    Statement I : A satellite is moving around earth in the orbit very close to the earth surface. The time period of revolution of satellite depends upon the density of earth.
    Statement II : The time period of revolution of the satellite is $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{R_e}{g}}$ (for satellite very close to the earth surface), where $R_e$ radius of earth and $g$ acceleration due to gravity.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. Both Statement I and Statement II are true
    2. Both Statement I and Statement II are false
    3. Statement I is true but Statement II is false
    4. Statement I is false but Statement II is true
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    For an orbit just above the surface, $\dfrac{mv^2}{R_e}=mg$, so $T=\dfrac{2\pi R_e}{v}=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{R_e}{g}}$. Statement II is true.
    With $g=\dfrac43\pi G\rho R_e$, $T=\sqrt{\dfrac{3\pi}{G\rho}}$, which depends on the density. Statement I is true.

  10. JEE Main 2026, 24 January, Shift 1 · Question 29
    Three masses 200 kg, 300 kg and 400 kg are placed at the vertices of an equilateral triangle with sides 20 m. They are rearranged on the vertices of a bigger triangle of side 25 m and with the same centre. The work done in this process __________ J.
    (Gravitational constant $G=6.7\times10^{-11}$ N m$^2$/ kg$^2$)

    1. $4.77\times10^{-7}$
    2. $9.86\times10^{-6}$
    3. $2.85\times10^{-7}$
    4. $1.74\times10^{-7}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    $U=-\dfrac{G}{a}\left(m_1m_2+m_2m_3+m_3m_1\right)$, with $\sum m_im_j=60000+120000+80000=2.6\times10^5$ kg$^2$.
    $W=U_f-U_i=G\times2.6\times10^5\left(\dfrac1{20}-\dfrac1{25}\right)=6.7\times10^{-11}\times2.6\times10^5\times0.01\approx1.74\times10^{-7}$ J.

  11. JEE Main 2025, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 47
    A satellite of mass 1000 kg is launched to revolve around the earth in an orbit at a height of 270 km from the earth's surface. Kinetic energy of the satellite in this orbit is __________ $\times10^{10}$ J.
    (Mass of earth $=6\times10^{24}$ kg, Radius of earth $=6.4\times10^6$ m, Gravitational constant $=6.67\times10^{-11}$ N m$^2$ kg$^{-2}$)

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 3

    Orbit radius $r=6.4\times10^6+0.27\times10^6=6.67\times10^6$ m.
    $KE=\dfrac{GMm}{2r}=\dfrac{6.67\times10^{-11}\times6\times10^{24}\times10^3}{2\times6.67\times10^6}=3\times10^{10}$ J.

  12. JEE Main 2025, 3 April, Shift 1 · Question 46
    Three identical spheres of mass m, are placed at the vertices of an equilateral triangle of length $a$. When released, they interact only through gravitational force and collide after a time T = 4 seconds. If the sides of the triangle are increased to length $2a$ and also the masses of the spheres are made 2m, then they will collide after __________ seconds.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 8

    The only quantities are $G$, $m$ and $a$, so by dimensions $T\propto\sqrt{\dfrac{a^3}{Gm}}$.
    New time: $4\sqrt{\dfrac{8}{2}}=8$ s.

  13. JEE Main 2025, 4 April, Shift 1 · Question 30
    Given below are two statements : one is labelled as Assertion A and the other is labelled as Reason R
    Assertion A: The kinetic energy needed to project a body of mass m from earth surface to infinity is $\dfrac12$mgR, where R is the radius of earth.
    Reason R: The maximum potential energy of a body is zero when it is projected to infinity from earth surface.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true but R is false
    4. A is false but R is true
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    To reach infinity the body needs $\dfrac{GMm}{R}=mgR$ of kinetic energy (its potential energy rises from $-mgR$ to 0), not $\dfrac12mgR$: A is false.
    Gravitational potential energy $-\dfrac{GMm}{r}$ increases with $r$ and reaches its maximum value, zero, at infinity: R is true.

  14. JEE Main 2025, 4 April, Shift 2 · Question 29
    An object is kept at rest at a distance of 3R above the earth's surface where R is earth's radius. The minimum speed with which it must be projected so that it does not return to earth is :
    (Assume M = mass of earth, G = Universal gravitational constant)

    1. $\sqrt{\dfrac{2GM}{R}}$
    2. $\sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{R}}$
    3. $\sqrt{\dfrac{3GM}{R}}$
    4. $\sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{2R}}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    The object is at $r=R+3R=4R$ from the centre.
    To escape, $\dfrac12mv^2=\dfrac{GMm}{4R}$, so $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{2R}}$.

  15. JEE Main 2025, 7 April, Shift 2 · Question 31
    Given below are two statements : one is labelled as Assertion (A) and the other is labelled as Reason (R).
    Assertion (A) : The radius vector from the Sun to a planet sweeps out equal areas in equal intervals of time and thus areal velocity of planet is constant.
    Reason (R) : For a central force field the angular momentum is a constant.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the most appropriate answer from the options given below :

    1. Both (A) and (R) are correct and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are correct but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is correct but (R) is not correct
    4. (A) is not correct but (R) is correct
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    A central force exerts no torque about the centre, so the angular momentum $L$ of the planet is conserved.
    Areal velocity $\dfrac{dA}{dt}=\dfrac{L}{2m}$ is therefore constant (Kepler's second law).
    Both are correct and (R) explains (A).

  16. JEE Main 2025, 22 January, Shift 1 · Question 30
    A small point of mass m is placed at a distance 2R from the centre 'O' of a big uniform solid sphere of mass M and radius R. The gravitational force on 'm' due to M is $F_1$. A spherical part of radius $R/3$ is removed from the big sphere as shown in the figure and the gravitational force on m due to remaining part of M is found to be $F_2$. The value of ratio $F_1 : F_2$ is

    1. $12 : 9$
    2. $12 : 11$
    3. $11 : 10$
    4. $16 : 9$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $F_1=\dfrac{GMm}{4R^2}$.
    The removed sphere has mass $\dfrac{M}{27}$ and (as drawn) touches the surface on the side of m, so its centre is $\dfrac{2R}{3}$ from O and $\dfrac{4R}{3}$ from m.
    Its pull: $\dfrac{G(M/27)m}{16R^2/9}=\dfrac{GMm}{48R^2}$.
    $F_2=\dfrac{GMm}{R^2}\left(\dfrac14-\dfrac1{48}\right)=\dfrac{11}{48}\dfrac{GMm}{R^2}$, so $F_1:F_2=12:11$.

  17. JEE Main 2025, 22 January, Shift 2 · Question 36
    Given below are two statements. One is labelled as Assertion (A) and the other is labelled as Reason (R).
    Assertion (A) : A simple pendulum is taken to a planet of mass and radius, 4 times and 2 times, respectively, than the Earth. The time period of the pendulum remains same on earth and the planet.
    Reason (R) : The mass of the pendulum remains unchanged at Earth and the other planet.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is true but (R) is false
    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $g=\dfrac{GM}{R^2}$: on the planet $g'=\dfrac{4}{2^2}g=g$, so $T=2\pi\sqrt{l/g}$ is unchanged. (A) is true.
    The mass of the bob is indeed unchanged, so (R) is true, but $T$ does not depend on the mass at all; the reason for (A) is the equal value of $g$.

  18. JEE Main 2025, 23 January, Shift 2 · Question 30
    If a satellite orbiting the Earth is 9 times closer to the Earth than the Moon, what is the time period of rotation of the satellite? Given rotational time period of Moon $=27$ days and gravitational attraction between the satellite and the moon is neglected.

    1. 27 days
    2. 3 days
    3. 81 days
    4. 1 day
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Kepler's third law: $T\propto r^{3/2}$.
    $T_s=27\times\left(\dfrac19\right)^{3/2}=\dfrac{27}{27}=1$ day.

  19. JEE Main 2025, 23 January, Shift 2 · Question 46
    A satellite of mass $\dfrac{M}{2}$ is revolving around earth in a circular orbit at a height of $\dfrac{R}{3}$ from earth surface. The angular momentum of the satellite is $M\sqrt{\dfrac{GMR}{x}}$. The value of $x$ is __________, where M and R are the mass and radius of earth, respectively.
    (G is the gravitational constant)

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 3

    Orbit radius $r=R+\dfrac R3=\dfrac{4R}{3}$ and orbital speed $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$.
    $L=mvr=\dfrac M2\sqrt{GMr}=\dfrac M2\sqrt{\dfrac{4GMR}{3}}=M\sqrt{\dfrac{GMR}{3}}$.
    $x=3$.

  20. JEE Main 2025, 24 January, Shift 1 · Question 31
    A satellite is launched into a circular orbit of radius 'R' around the earth. A second satellite is launched into an orbit of radius 1.03 R. The time period of revolution of the second satellite is larger than the first one approximately by

    1. $2.5\%$
    2. $9\%$
    3. $3\%$
    4. $4.5\%$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Kepler's third law: $T\propto R^{3/2}$, so $\dfrac{\Delta T}{T}\approx\dfrac32\cdot\dfrac{\Delta R}{R}=\dfrac32\times3\%=4.5\%$.

  21. JEE Main 2025, 24 January, Shift 2 · Question 47
    Acceleration due to gravity on the surface of earth is 'g'. If the diameter of earth is reduced to one third of its original value and mass remains unchanged, then the acceleration due to gravity on the surface of the earth is __________ g.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 9

    $g=\dfrac{GM}{R^2}$. With $R\to\dfrac R3$ and the same M, $g'=9g$.

  22. JEE Main 2025, 28 January, Shift 2 · Question 29
    Earth has mass 8 times and radius 2 times that of a planet. If the escape velocity from the earth is 11.2 km/s, the escape velocity in km/s from the planet will be :

    1. 2.8
    2. 5.6
    3. 11.2
    4. 8.4
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $v_e=\sqrt{\dfrac{2GM}{R}}\propto\sqrt{\dfrac MR}$.
    $\dfrac{v_{\text{earth}}}{v_{\text{planet}}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{8}{2}}=2$, so $v_{\text{planet}}=5.6$ km/s.

  23. JEE Main 2025, 29 January, Shift 2 · Question 48
    Two planets, A and B are orbiting a common star in circular orbits of radii $R_A$ and $R_B$, respectively, with $R_B=2R_A$. The planet B is $4\sqrt2$ times more massive than planet A. The ratio $\left(\dfrac{L_B}{L_A}\right)$ of angular momentum $(L_B)$ of planet B to that of planet A$(L_A)$ is closest to integer __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 8

    Orbital speed $v=\sqrt{GM/R}$, so $L=mvR=m\sqrt{GMR}$.
    $\dfrac{L_B}{L_A}=4\sqrt2\times\sqrt2=8$.
