  1. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 1 · Question 44
    Angular momentum of an electron in a hydrogen atom is $\dfrac{3h}{\pi}$, then the energy of the electron is ______ eV.

    1. $-1.51$
    2. $-0.85$
    3. $-0.38$
    4. $-0.28$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Bohr: $L = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi} = \dfrac{3h}{\pi} \Rightarrow n = 6$.
    $E_6 = -\dfrac{13.6}{36} \approx -0.38$ eV.

  2. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 47
    Using Bohr's model, calculate the ratio of the magnetic fields generated due to the motion of the electrons in the $2^{\text{nd}}$ and $4^{\text{th}}$ orbits of hydrogen atom __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 32

    Field at the nucleus: $B=\dfrac{\mu_0I}{2r}$ with $I=\dfrac{ev}{2\pi r}$, so $B\propto\dfrac{v}{r^2}$.
    In Bohr's model $v\propto\dfrac1n$ and $r\propto n^2$, so $B\propto\dfrac{1}{n^5}$.
    $\dfrac{B_2}{B_4}=\left(\dfrac42\right)^5=32$.

  3. JEE Main 2026, 4 April, Shift 2 · Question 43
    In Rutherford's alpha-particle scattering experiment, only a few alpha particles rebound back because
    A. The size of gold nucleus is very small as compared to the size of gold atom.
    B. Alpha particle and gold nucleus have equal charge.
    C. The impact parameter is minimum for a few alpha particles.
    D. A few alpha particles have very high kinetic energy.
    E. Only a few alpha particles undergo head-on collision with the nuclei.
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

    1. A, B Only
    2. B, E Only
    3. C, D Only
    4. A, C, E Only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    The nucleus is tiny compared with the atom (A), so only a few alpha particles come with nearly zero impact parameter (C), i.e. nearly head-on (E); only these are turned back.
    B is false (charges $+2e$ and $+79e$); D is irrelevant (all have about the same energy, and higher energy would reduce scattering).
    A, C, E only.

  4. JEE Main 2026, 5 April, Shift 1 · Question 36
    In the hydrogen atom, the electron makes a transition from the higher orbit ($i$) to a lower orbit ($f$). The ratio of the radius of the orbits in given by $r_i : r_f = 16 : 4$. The wavelength of photon emitted due to this transition is _______nm.
    (Given Rydberg constant $= 1.0973 \times 10^7$ /m)

    1. $121$
    2. $242$
    3. $486$
    4. $974$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $r\propto n^2$: taking $n_i^2=16$, $n_f^2=4$ gives $n_i=4$, $n_f=2$.
    $\dfrac1\lambda=R\left(\dfrac14-\dfrac1{16}\right)=\dfrac{3R}{16}$.
    $\lambda=\dfrac{16}{3\times1.0973\times10^7}$ m $\approx4.86\times10^{-7}$ m $=486$ nm.

  5. JEE Main 2026, 6 April, Shift 1 · Question 44
    The ratio of momentum of the photons of the 1$^{\text{st}}$ and 2$^{\text{nd}}$ line of Balmer series of Hydrogen atoms is $\alpha/\beta$. The possible values of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are:-

    1. $27$ and $20$
    2. $3$ and $16$
    3. $5$ and $36$
    4. $20$ and $27$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    $p=\dfrac h\lambda\propto\dfrac1\lambda\propto\left(\dfrac1{2^2}-\dfrac1{n^2}\right)$.
    1st line ($3\to2$): $\dfrac14-\dfrac19=\dfrac5{36}$; 2nd line ($4\to2$): $\dfrac14-\dfrac1{16}=\dfrac3{16}$.
    Ratio $=\dfrac{5/36}{3/16}=\dfrac{20}{27}$.

  6. JEE Main 2026, 21 January, Shift 1 · Question 41
    If an alpha particle with energy 7.7 MeV is bombarded on a thin gold foil, the closest distance from nucleus it can reach is _________m. (Atomic number of gold $=79$ and $\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_o}=9\times10^9$ in SI units)

    1. $3.85\times10^{-16}$
    2. $2.95\times10^{-16}$
    3. $3.85\times10^{-14}$
    4. $2.95\times10^{-14}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    At closest approach, $K=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{(2e)(79e)}{r_0}$.
    $r_0=\dfrac{9\times10^9\times158\times(1.6\times10^{-19})^2}{7.7\times10^6\times1.6\times10^{-19}}=\dfrac{9\times10^9\times158\times1.6\times10^{-19}}{7.7\times10^6}\approx2.95\times10^{-14}$ m.

  7. JEE Main 2026, 21 January, Shift 2 · Question 44
    The energy of an electron in an orbit of the Bohr's atom is $-0.04E_0$ eV where $E_0$ is the ground state energy. If $L$ is the angular momentum of the electron in this orbit and $h$ is the Planck's constant, then $\dfrac{2\pi L}{h}$ is __________ :

    1. $5$
    2. $4$
    3. $2$
    4. $6$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $E_n=\dfrac{E_1}{n^2}$; the energy is $0.04=\dfrac1{25}$ of the ground-state value, so $n=5$.
    $L=\dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$ gives $\dfrac{2\pi L}{h}=n=5$.

  8. JEE Main 2026, 22 January, Shift 1 · Question 43
    7.9 MeV $\alpha$-particle scatters from a target material of atomic number 79. From the given data the estimated diameter of nuclei of the target material is (approximately) ______m.
    $\left[\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}=9\times10^9\ \text{Nm}^2/\text{C}^2\text{ and electron charge}=1.6\times10^{-19}\ \text{C}\right]$

    1. $1.69\times10^{-12}$
    2. $2.88\times10^{-14}$
    3. $1.44\times10^{-13}$
    4. $5.76\times10^{-14}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Distance of closest approach (head-on): $r_0=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\cdot\dfrac{2Ze^2}{K}=\dfrac{9\times10^9\times2\times79\times(1.6\times10^{-19})^2}{7.9\times10^6\times1.6\times10^{-19}}=2.88\times10^{-14}$ m.
    Taking this as the estimate of the nuclear radius, the diameter is about $2r_0=5.76\times10^{-14}$ m.

  9. JEE Main 2026, 22 January, Shift 2 · Question 44
    The smallest wavelength of Lyman series is 91 nm. The difference between the largest wavelengths of Paschen and Balmer series is nearly ______ nm.

    1. $1550$
    2. $1875$
    3. $1784$
    4. $1217$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Lyman limit: $\dfrac1{91\ \text{nm}}=R$.
    Largest Paschen ($4\to3$): $\dfrac1\lambda=R\left(\dfrac19-\dfrac1{16}\right)=\dfrac{7R}{144}\Rightarrow\lambda=\dfrac{144\times91}{7}=1872$ nm.
    Largest Balmer ($3\to2$): $\dfrac1\lambda=\dfrac{5R}{36}\Rightarrow\lambda=\dfrac{36\times91}{5}=655.2$ nm.
    Difference $\approx1217$ nm.

  10. JEE Main 2026, 23 January, Shift 1 · Question 44
    In hydrogen atom spectrum, $(R\rightarrow$ Rydberg's constant$)$
    A. the maximum wavelength of the radiation of Lyman series is $\dfrac{4}{3R}$
    B. the Balmer series lies in the visible region of the spectrum
    C. the minimum wavelength of the radiation of Paschen series is $\dfrac9R$
    D. the minimum wavelength of Lyman series is $\dfrac{5}{4R}$
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. A, B and D Only
    2. A, B Only
    3. A, B and C Only
    4. B, D Only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    A: Lyman longest line, $n=2\to1$: $\dfrac1\lambda=R\left(1-\dfrac14\right)$, $\lambda=\dfrac4{3R}$: true.
    B: the Balmer lines are in the visible region: true.
    C: Paschen shortest line, $\infty\to3$: $\lambda=\dfrac9R$: true.
    D: Lyman shortest line, $\infty\to1$: $\lambda=\dfrac1R$, not $\dfrac5{4R}$: false.
    A, B and C only.

  11. JEE Main 2026, 24 January, Shift 1 · Question 45
    Two electrons are moving in orbits of two hydrogen like atoms with speeds $3\times10^5$ m/s and $2.5\times10^5$ m/s respectively. If the radii of these orbits are nearly same then the possible order of energy states are __________ respectively.

    1. 9 and 8
    2. 10 and 12
    3. 6 and 5
    4. 8 and 10
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Bohr model: $r\propto\dfrac{n^2}{Z}$ and $v\propto\dfrac Zn$.
    Equal radii means $Z\propto n^2$, so $v\propto\dfrac{n^2}{n}=n$.
    $\dfrac{n_1}{n_2}=\dfrac{3}{2.5}=\dfrac65$: the states 6 and 5.

  12. JEE Main 2025, 2 April, Shift 1 · Question 43
    Considering Bohr's atomic model for hydrogen atom :
    (A) the energy of H atom in ground state is same as energy of He$^+$ ion in its first excited state.
    (B) the energy of H atom in ground state is same as that for Li$^{++}$ ion in its second excited state.
    (C) the energy of H atom in its ground state is same as that of He$^+$ ion for its ground state.
    (D) the energy of He$^+$ ion in its first excited state is same as that for Li$^{++}$ ion in its ground state.
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. (A), (B) only
    2. (A), (C) only
    3. (B), (D) only
    4. (A), (D) only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $E_n=-13.6\dfrac{Z^2}{n^2}$ eV, so compare $\dfrac{Z^2}{n^2}$. H ground state: 1.
    (A) He$^+$, $n=2$: $\dfrac44=1$. True.
    (B) Li$^{++}$, $n=3$: $\dfrac99=1$. True.
    (C) He$^+$, $n=1$: 4. False.
    (D) He$^+$, $n=2$: 1, but Li$^{++}$, $n=1$: 9. False.

  13. JEE Main 2025, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 40
    Assuming the validity of Bohr's atomic model for hydrogen like ions the radius of Li$^{++}$ ion in its ground state is given by $\dfrac1X a_0$, where X = __________.
    (Where $a_0$ is the first Bohr's radius.)

    1. $1$
    2. $2$
    3. $3$
    4. $9$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $r_n=a_0\dfrac{n^2}{Z}$. For Li$^{++}$, $Z=3$ and $n=1$: $r=\dfrac{a_0}{3}$, so $X=3$.

  14. JEE Main 2025, 3 April, Shift 2 · Question 44
    Given below are two statements: one is labelled as Assertion A and the other is labelled as Reason R
    Assertion A : The Bohr model is applicable to hydrogen and hydrogen-like atoms only.
    Reason R : The formulation of Bohr model does not include repulsive force between electrons.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true but R is false
    4. A is false but R is true
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Bohr's model treats a single electron moving in the field of the nucleus, so it works only for one-electron (hydrogen-like) species: A is true.
    It has no term for the repulsion between electrons, which is exactly why it fails for many-electron atoms: R is true and explains A.

  15. JEE Main 2025, 3 April, Shift 2 · Question 50
    An electron in the hydrogen atom initially in the fourth excited state makes a transition to n$^{\text{th}}$ energy state by emitting a photon of energy 2.86 eV. The integer value of n will be __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 2

    The fourth excited state is $n=5$: $E_5=-\dfrac{13.6}{25}=-0.544$ eV.
    Final energy $=-0.544-2.86=-3.40$ eV $=-\dfrac{13.6}{4}$, so $n=2$.

  16. JEE Main 2025, 4 April, Shift 1 · Question 43
    Considering the Bohr model of hydrogen like atoms, the ratio of the radius of $5^{\text{th}}$ orbit of the electron in $\mathrm{Li^{2+}}$ and $\mathrm{He^+}$ is

    1. $\dfrac23$
    2. $\dfrac32$
    3. $\dfrac49$
    4. $\dfrac94$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    In the Bohr model $r_n\propto\dfrac{n^2}{Z}$.
    For the same $n$: $\dfrac{r_{Li^{2+}}}{r_{He^+}}=\dfrac{Z_{He}}{Z_{Li}}=\dfrac23$.

  17. JEE Main 2025, 4 April, Shift 2 · Question 41
    Given below are two statements :
    Statement (I) : The dimensions of Planck's constant and angular momentum are same.
    Statement (II) : In Bohr's model electron revolve around the nucleus only in those orbits for which angular momentum is integral multiple of Planck's constant.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the most appropriate answer from the options given below :

    1. Both Statement I and Statement II are correct
    2. Both Statement I and Statement II are incorrect
    3. Statement I is correct but Statement II is incorrect
    4. Statement I is incorrect but Statement II is correct
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $h$ has unit J s $=$ kg m$^2$ s$^{-1}$, the same as angular momentum: Statement I is correct.
    Bohr's condition is $L=n\dfrac{h}{2\pi}$, an integral multiple of $\dfrac{h}{2\pi}$, not of $h$: Statement II is incorrect.

  18. JEE Main 2025, 7 April, Shift 1 · Question 43
    For a hydrogen atom, the ratio of the largest wavelength of Lyman series to that of the Balmer series is

    1. 3 : 4
    2. 5 : 27
    3. 27 : 5
    4. 5 : 36
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Lyman, longest ($2\to1$): $\dfrac1\lambda=R\left(1-\dfrac14\right)=\dfrac{3R}{4}$. Balmer, longest ($3\to2$): $\dfrac1\lambda=R\left(\dfrac14-\dfrac19\right)=\dfrac{5R}{36}$.
    Ratio $=\dfrac{4/3}{36/5}=\dfrac{5}{27}$.

  19. JEE Main 2025, 7 April, Shift 1 · Question 44
    In a hydrogen like ion, the energy difference between the $2^{\text{nd}}$ excitation energy state and ground is 108.8 eV. The atomic number of the ion is:

    1. $1$
    2. $3$
    3. $2$
    4. $4$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    The second excited state is $n=3$: $\Delta E=13.6Z^2\left(1-\dfrac19\right)=\dfrac{108.8}{9}Z^2$ eV.
    $\dfrac{108.8}{9}Z^2=108.8\Rightarrow Z=3$.

  20. JEE Main 2025, 22 January, Shift 1 · Question 44
    An electron in the ground state of the hydrogen atom has the orbital radius of $5.3\times10^{-11}$ m while that for the electron in third excited state is $8.48\times10^{-10}$ m. The ratio of the de Broglie wavelengths of electron in the ground state to that in the excited state is

    1. $3$
    2. $4$
    3. $9$
    4. $16$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $\dfrac{r_n}{r_1}=\dfrac{8.48\times10^{-10}}{5.3\times10^{-11}}=16=n^2$, so $n=4$ (the third excited state).
    Bohr's condition $2\pi r_n=n\lambda_n$ gives $\lambda_n=\dfrac{2\pi r_n}{n}\propto n$.
    So the two wavelengths differ by a factor of 4 (the excited-state wavelength is 4 times the ground-state one); the final key takes this factor, 4, as the answer.

    Note: With $\lambda\propto n$, the ratio ground : excited is strictly $1:4$; NTA's final key gives option (2), 4, i.e. the ratio of the larger to the smaller wavelength.

  21. JEE Main 2025, 22 January, Shift 2 · Question 44
    An electron projected perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field B moves in a circle. If Bohr's quantization is applicable, then the radius of the electronic orbit in the first excited state is :

    1. $\sqrt{\dfrac{h}{\pi eB}}$
    2. $\sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{\pi eB}}$
    3. $\sqrt{\dfrac{h}{2\pi eB}}$
    4. $\sqrt{\dfrac{4h}{\pi eB}}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Circular motion: $mv=eBr$. Bohr's condition: $mvr=\dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$.
    So $eBr^2=\dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$ and $r=\sqrt{\dfrac{nh}{2\pi eB}}$.
    First excited state, $n=2$: $r=\sqrt{\dfrac{h}{\pi eB}}$.

  22. JEE Main 2025, 24 January, Shift 1 · Question 44
    During the transition of electron from state A to state C of a Bohr atom, the wavelength of emitted radiation is 2000 $\mathrm{\mathring{A}}$ and it becomes 6000 $\mathrm{\mathring{A}}$ when the electron jumps from state B to state C. Then the wavelength of the radiation emitted during the transition of electrons from state A to state B is

    1. $2000$ $\mathrm{\mathring{A}}$
    2. $3000$ $\mathrm{\mathring{A}}$
    3. $4000$ $\mathrm{\mathring{A}}$
    4. $6000$ $\mathrm{\mathring{A}}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Energies add: $E_{AC}=E_{AB}+E_{BC}$, so $\dfrac1{\lambda_{AB}}=\dfrac1{2000}-\dfrac1{6000}=\dfrac{1}{3000}$.
    $\lambda_{AB}=3000$ $\mathrm{\mathring{A}}$.

  23. JEE Main 2025, 28 January, Shift 2 · Question 43
    The frequency of revolution of the electron in Bohr's orbit varies with n, the principal quantum number as

    1. $\dfrac{1}{n^3}$
    2. $\dfrac{1}{n^2}$
    3. $\dfrac{1}{n}$
    4. $\dfrac{1}{n^4}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    In Bohr's model $v\propto\dfrac1n$ and $r\propto n^2$.
    Frequency $f=\dfrac{v}{2\pi r}\propto\dfrac{1}{n^3}$.

  24. JEE Main 2025, 29 January, Shift 2 · Question 43
    The number of spectral lines emitted by atomic hydrogen that is in the 4<sup>th</sup> energy level, is

    1. $6$
    2. $3$
    3. $1$
    4. $0$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    From $n=4$, transitions between any two of the levels 4, 3, 2, 1 are possible: $\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}=6$ lines.
