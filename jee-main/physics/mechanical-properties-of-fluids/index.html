  1. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 1 · Question 45
    A liquid drop of diameter 2 mm breaks into 512 droplets. The change in surface energy is $\alpha\times10^{-6}$ J. The value of $\alpha$ is ________. (Take surface tension of liquid $= 0.08$ N/m)

    1. $10$
    2. $7$
    3. $8$
    4. $11$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Volume conserved: $512r^3 = R^3 \Rightarrow r = R/8$, with $R = 1$ mm.
    $\Delta A = 4\pi(512r^2 - R^2) = 4\pi R^2(8-1) = 28\pi\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^2}$.
    $\Delta E = T\Delta A = 0.08\times28\pi\times10^{-6} \approx 7.04\times10^{-6}$ J, so $\alpha \approx 7$.

  2. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 1 · Question 50
    A tub is filled with water and a wooden cube 10 cm $\times$ 10 cm $\times$ 10 cm is placed in the water. The wooden cube is found to float on the water with a part of it submerged in water. When a metal coin is placed on the wooden cube, the submerged part is increased by 3.87 cm. The mass of the metal coin is ________ gram.
    (Take water density as $1\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$ and density of wood as $0.4\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$)

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 387

    Without the coin, 4 cm is submerged ($0.4\times10$). With the coin, 7.87 cm is submerged, which is still less than 10 cm, so the cube still floats.
    Extra buoyancy = weight of coin: $m = \rho_w A\,\Delta h = 1\times100\times3.87 = 387$ g.

  3. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 33
    The surface tension of a soap bubble is 0.03 N/m. The work done in increasing the diameter of bubble from 2 cm to 6 cm is $\alpha\pi\times10^{-4}$ J. The value of $\alpha$ is __________. (Take $\pi=3.14$)

    1. $0.86$
    2. $0.64$
    3. $1.92$
    4. $7.68$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    A soap bubble has two surfaces: $W=T\times2\times4\pi(r_2^2-r_1^2)$.
    $W=0.03\times8\pi\times(9-1)\times10^{-4}=1.92\pi\times10^{-4}$ J.
    $\alpha=1.92$.

  4. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 35
    If an air bubble of diameter 2 mm rises steadily through a liquid of density 2000 kg/m$^3$ at a rate of 0.5 cm/s, then the coefficient of viscosity of liquid is __________ Poise. (Take $g=10$ m/s$^2$)

    1. $0.88$
    2. $8.8$
    3. $88.8$
    4. $0.088$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Terminal velocity (neglecting the density of air): $v=\dfrac{2r^2\rho g}{9\eta}$.
    $\eta=\dfrac{2\times(10^{-3})^2\times2000\times10}{9\times5\times10^{-3}}=\dfrac{0.04}{0.045}=0.89$ Pa s.
    $1$ Pa s $=10$ Poise, so $\eta\approx8.8$ Poise.

    2. $16\pi r^2S$

  10. JEE Main 2026, 8 April, Shift 2 · Question 34
    A spherical liquid drop of radius $R$ acquires the terminal velocity $v_1$ when falls through a gas of viscosity $\eta$. Now the drop is broken into 64 identical droplets and each droplet acquires terminal velocity $v_2$ falling through the same gas. The ratio of terminal velocities $v_1/v_2$ is __________.

    1. $4$
    2. $0.25$
    3. $32$
    4. $16$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Volume is conserved: $64r^3=R^3\Rightarrow r=\dfrac R4$.

  11. JEE Main 2026, 21 January, Shift 1 · Question 30
    Water flows through a horizontal tube as shown in the figure. The difference in height between the water columns in vertical tubes is 5 cm and the area of cross-sections at $A$ and $B$ are 6 cm$^2$ and 3 cm$^2$ respectively. The rate of flow will be _________ cm$^3$/s. (take $g=10\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$)

    1. $100\sqrt{3}$
    2. $\dfrac{200}{\sqrt{3}}$
    3. $200\sqrt{6}$
    4. $200\sqrt{3}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    A spherical body of radius $r$ and density $\sigma$ falls freely through a viscous liquid having density $\rho$ and viscosity $\eta$ and attains a terminal velocity $v_0$. Estimated maximum error in the quantity $\eta$ is : (Ignore errors associated with $\sigma$, $\rho$ and $g$, gravitational acceleration)

    1. $2\left[\dfrac{\Delta r}{r}+\dfrac{\Delta v_o}{v_o}\right]$
    2. $2\left[\dfrac{\Delta r}{r}-\dfrac{\Delta v_o}{v_o}\right]$
    3. $2\dfrac{\Delta r}{r}-\dfrac{\Delta v_o}{v_o}$
    4. $\dfrac{2\Delta r}{r}+\dfrac{\Delta v_o}{v_o}$
    Show answer and solution

  13. JEE Main 2026, 21 January, Shift 2 · Question 33
    Surface tension of two liquids (having same densities), $T_1$ and $T_2$, are measured using capillary rise method utilizing two tubes with inner radii of $r_1$ and $r_2$ where $r_1>r_2$. The measured liquid heights in these tubes are $h_1$ and $h_2$ respectively. [Ignore the weight of the liquid about the lowest point of miniscus]. The heights $h_1$ and $h_2$ and surfaces tensions $T_1$ and $T_2$ satisfy the relation :

    1. $h_1=h_2$ and $T_1=T_2$
    2. $h_1>h_2$ and $T_1<T_2$
    3. $h_1>h_2$ and $T_1=T_2$
    4. $h_1<h_2$ and $T_1=T_2$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Capillary rise $h=\dfrac{2T\cos\theta}{r\rho g}$, so with equal densities $\dfrac{h_1}{h_2}=\dfrac{T_1}{T_2}\cdot\dfrac{r_2}{r_1}$, where $\dfrac{r_2}{r_1}<1$.
    If $T_1=T_2$ then $h_1<h_2$ (option 4 is consistent; options 1 and 3 are not). If $T_1<T_2$ then $h_1<h_2$ as well, so option 2 is impossible.
    The final key takes option (4).

    Statement I: Pressure of a fluid is exerted only on a solid surface in contact as the fluid-pressure does not exist everywhere in a still fluid.
    Statement II: Excess potential energy of the molecules on the surface of a liquid, when compared to interior, results in surface tension.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below

  28. JEE Main 2025, 4 April, Shift 1 · Question 32
    Two liquids A and B have $\theta_A$ and $\theta_B$ as contact angles in a capillary tube. If $K=\cos\theta_A/\cos\theta_B$, then identify the correct statement:

    1. K is negative, then liquid A and liquid B have concave meniscus.
    2. K is negative, then liquid A and liquid B have convex meniscus.

  30. JEE Main 2025, 8 April, Shift 2 · Question 46
    A cube having a side of 10 cm with unknown mass and 200 gm mass were hung at two ends of an uniform rigid rod of 27 cm long.     Show answer and solution

    Answer: 4

    The excess pressures are $\dfrac{4T}{r_1}$ and $\dfrac{4T}{r_2}$; their difference acts across the common film: $\dfrac{4T}{r}=\dfrac{4T}{r_1}-\dfrac{4T}{r_2}$.
    $r=\dfrac{r_1r_2}{r_2-r_1}=\dfrac{2\times4}{2}=4$ cm.

  32. JEE Main 2025, 22 January, Shift 2 · Question 32
    A tube of length L is shown in the figure. The radius of cross section at the point (1) is 2 cm and at the point (2) is 1 cm, respectively. If the velocity of water entering at point (1) is 2 m/s, then velocity of water leaving the point (2) will be

    1. 6 m/s
    2. 8 m/s
    3. 4 m/s
    4. 2 m/s
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Continuity: $A_1v_1=A_2v_2$, so $v_2=v_1\left(\dfrac{r_1}{r_2}\right)^2=2\times4=8$ m/s.

  33. JEE Main 2025, 22 January, Shift 2 · Question 33
    A small rigid spherical ball of mass M is dropped in a long vertical tube containing glycerine. The velocity of the ball becomes constant after some time. If the density of glycerine is half of the density of the ball, then the viscous force acting on the ball will be
    (consider g as acceleration due to gravity)

    1. Mg
    2. $\dfrac{3}{2}$Mg
    3. 2 Mg
    4. $\dfrac{\text{Mg}}{2}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    At terminal velocity the net force is zero: weight $=$ buoyancy $+$ viscous force.
    Buoyancy $=\dfrac{Mg}{2}$ (liquid density is half the ball's), so the viscous force $=Mg-\dfrac{Mg}{2}=\dfrac{Mg}{2}$.

  35. JEE Main 2025, 23 January, Shift 2 · Question 32
    Water flows in a horizontal pipe whose one end is closed with a valve. The reading of the pressure gauge attached to the pipe is $P_1$. The reading of the pressure gauge falls to $P_2$ when the valve is opened. The speed of water flowing in the pipe is proportional to

    1. $P_1-P_2$
    2. $\sqrt{P_1-P_2}$
    3. $(P_1-P_2)^2$
    4. $(P_1-P_2)^4$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Bernoulli's equation for the horizontal pipe: $P_1=P_2+\dfrac12\rho v^2$.
    $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{2(P_1-P_2)}{\rho}}\propto\sqrt{P_1-P_2}$.

  36. JEE Main 2025, 23 January, Shift 2 · Question 47
    An air bubble of radius 1.0 mm is observed at a depth of 20 cm below the free surface of a liquid having surface tension 0.095 J/m$^2$ and density $10^3$ kg/m$^3$. The difference between pressure inside the bubble and atmospheric pressure is __________ N/m$^2$.
    (Take $g=10$ m/s$^2$)

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 2190

    Inside an air bubble in a liquid (one surface): $P_{\text{in}}=P_0+\rho gh+\dfrac{2T}{r}$.
    $\rho gh=10^3\times10\times0.2=2000$ N/m$^2$; $\dfrac{2T}{r}=\dfrac{2\times0.095}{10^{-3}}=190$ N/m$^2$.
    Difference $=2190$ N/m$^2$.

  37. JEE Main 2025, 24 January, Shift 1 · Question 32
    The amount of work done to break a big water drop of radius 'R' into 27 small drops of equal radius is 10 J. The work done required to break the same big drop into 64 small drops of equal radius will be

    1. $5$ J
    2. $10$ J
    3. $15$ J
    4. $20$ J
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Volume is conserved, so $n$ drops have radius $r=Rn^{-1/3}$ and total area $4\pi R^2n^{1/3}$.
    $W=4\pi R^2T\left(n^{1/3}-1\right)$: for $n=27$ the factor is 2, for $n=64$ it is 3.
    $W=10\times\dfrac32=15$ J.

    An air bubble of radius 0.1 cm lies at a depth of 20 cm below the free surface of a liquid of density 1000 kg/m$^3$. If the pressure inside the bubble is 2100 N/m$^2$ greater than the atmospheric pressure, then the surface tension of the liquid in SI unit is (use $g=10$m/s$^2$)

    1. $0.05$
    2. $0.1$
    3. $0.25$
    4. $0.02$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

  39. JEE Main 2025, 28 January, Shift 1 · Question 29
    Consider following statements:
    A. Surface tension arises due to extra energy of the molecules at the interior as compared to the molecules at the surface, of a liquid.
    B. As the temperature of liquid rises, the coefficient of viscosity increases.
    C. As the temperature of gas increases, the coefficient of viscosity increases
    D. The onset of turbulence is determined by Reynold's number.
    E. In a steady flow two stream lines never intersect.
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

    1. A, D, E Only
    2. C, D, E Only
    3. A, B, C Only
    4. B, C, D Only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    A is false: the molecules at the surface (not the interior) have extra energy.
    B is false: the viscosity of a liquid falls as the temperature rises.

  40. JEE Main 2025, 28 January, Shift 1 · Question 30
    In the experiment for measurement of viscosity '$\eta$' of given liquid with a ball having radius R, consider following statements.
    A. Graph between terminal velocity V and R will be a parabola.
    B. The terminal velocities of different diameter balls are constant for a given liquid.
    C. Measurement of terminal velocity is dependent on the temperature.
    D. This experiment can be utilized to assess the density of a given liquid.
    E. If balls are dropped with some initial speed, the value of $\eta$ will change.
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

    1. A, C and D Only
    2. B, D and E Only
    3. C, D and E Only
    4. A, B and E Only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $V=\dfrac{2R^2(\rho-\sigma)g}{9\eta}$.
    A: $V\propto R^2$, a parabola: true. B: $V$ depends on $R$: false.
    C: $\eta$, and hence $V$, depends on temperature: true. D: with $\eta$ known, the same relation gives the liquid density $\sigma$: true.
    E: the terminal velocity, and $\eta$, do not depend on the initial speed: false.
    Answer: A, C and D only.

  41. JEE Main 2025, 28 January, Shift 2 · Question 33
    A 400 g solid cube having an edge of length 10 cm floats in water. How much volume of the cube is outside the water ?
    (Given : density of water $=1000$ kg m$^{-3}$)

    1. 400 cm$^3$
    2. 600 cm$^3$
    3. 1400 cm$^3$
    4. 4000 cm$^3$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Floating: weight of water displaced = weight of cube = 400 g, so the submerged volume is 400 cm$^3$.
    Cube volume $=1000$ cm$^3$, so $1000-400=600$ cm$^3$ is outside the water.

  42. JEE Main 2025, 29 January, Shift 1 · Question 48
    In a hydraulic lift, the surface area of the input piston is 6 cm$^2$ and that of the output piston is 1500 cm$^2$. If 100 N force is applied to the input piston to raise the output piston by 20 cm, then the work done is __________ kJ.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 5

    Output force $=100\times\dfrac{1500}{6}=25000$ N.
    Work $=25000\times0.20=5000$ J $=5$ kJ (the same as the input work, as the input piston moves 250 times further).
