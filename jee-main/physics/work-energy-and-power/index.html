  1. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 36
    A spherical ball of mass 2 kg falls from a height of 10 m and is brought to rest after penetrating 10 cm into sand. The average force exerted by sand on the ball is __________ N.
    (Take $g=10$ m/s$^2$)

    1. $1980$
    2. $2020$
    3. $2000$
    4. $1000$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Work-energy theorem from release to rest: $mg(h+d)-Fd=0$.
    $F=\dfrac{2\times10\times10.1}{0.1}=2020$ N.

  2. JEE Main 2026, 4 April, Shift 1 · Question 46
    A $1$ kg block subjected to two simultaneous forces $(2\hat{i}+3\hat{j}+4\hat{k})$ N and $(3\hat{i}-\hat{j}-2\hat{k})$ N is moved a distance of $25$ m along $(3\hat{i}-4\hat{j})$ direction. The work done in this process is ________ J.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 35

    Net force $\vec F=5\hat i+2\hat j+2\hat k$ N.
    Displacement $\vec d=25\cdot\dfrac{3\hat i-4\hat j}{5}=15\hat i-20\hat j$ m.
    $W=\vec F\cdot\vec d=75-40=35$ J.

  3. JEE Main 2026, 4 April, Shift 1 · Question 49
    A body of mass $2$ kg begins to move under the influence of time dependent force $\vec{F}=(2t\hat{i}+6t^2\hat{j})$ N, where $\hat{i}$ and $\hat{j}$ are unit vectors along $x$ and $y$-axis respectively. The power produced by the force at $t=2$ s is ____ W.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 200

    $\vec a=\dfrac{\vec F}{m}=t\hat i+3t^2\hat j$; starting from rest, $\vec v=\dfrac{t^2}{2}\hat i+t^3\hat j$.
    At $t=2$ s: $\vec F=4\hat i+24\hat j$ N, $\vec v=2\hat i+8\hat j$ m/s.
    $P=\vec F\cdot\vec v=8+192=200$ W.

  4. JEE Main 2026, 5 April, Shift 2 · Question 28
    A mass of 1 kg is kept on a inclined plane with $30^\circ$ inclination with respect to horizontal plane and it is at rest initially. Then the whole assembly is moved up with constant velocity of 4 m/s. The work done by the frictional force in time 2 s is __________ J. (Take $g=10\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$)

    1. $20$
    2. $25$
    3. $30$
    4. $10$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    The block stays at rest relative to the incline, and the assembly moves with constant velocity, so static friction balances the component of gravity along the incline: $f=mg\sin30^\circ=5$ N, directed up the incline.
    In 2 s the block moves $4\times2=8$ m vertically upward.
    Angle between the friction (along the incline, up) and the vertical displacement is $60^\circ$.
    $W_f=5\times8\times\cos60^\circ=20$ J.

  5. JEE Main 2026, 6 April, Shift 1 · Question 28
    The rain drop of mass 1 g, starts with zero velocity from a height of 1 km. It hits the ground with a speed of 5 m/s. The work done by the unknown resistive force is ________ J.
    (take $g=10$ m/s$^2$)

    1. $-8.75$
    2. $-8.35$
    3. $-9.55$
    4. $-9.98$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Work-energy theorem: $W_g+W_r=\Delta K$.
    $W_g=mgh=10^{-3}\times10\times1000=10$ J; $\Delta K=\dfrac12\times10^{-3}\times25=0.0125$ J.
    $W_r=0.0125-10=-9.9875\approx-9.98$ J.

  6. JEE Main 2026, 6 April, Shift 1 · Question 32
    A smooth inclined plane ends in a vertical circular loop, as shown in the figure. A small body is released from height $h$ as shown. If the body exerts a force of three times its weight on the plane at the highest point of circle then the height $h=\alpha R$. The value of $\alpha$ is ________.

    1. $2$
    2. $4$
    3. $3$
    4. $6$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    At the top: $N+mg=\dfrac{mv^2}{R}$ with $N=3mg\Rightarrow v^2=4gR$.
    Energy: $mgh=mg(2R)+\dfrac12mv^2=2mgR+2mgR$.
    $h=4R$, so $\alpha=4$.

  7. JEE Main 2026, 6 April, Shift 2 · Question 29
    A body of mass 1 kg moves along a straight line with a velocity $v=2x^2$. The work done by the body during displacement from $x=0$ to 5 m is _________ J.

    1. $0$
    2. $250$
    3. $1250$
    4. $1000$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    At $x=0$, $v=0$; at $x=5$ m, $v=2(25)=50$ m/s.
    By the work-energy theorem, $W=\Delta K=\dfrac12(1)(50)^2-0=1250$ J.

  8. JEE Main 2026, 21 January, Shift 1 · Question 27
    Potential energy $(V)$ versus distance $(x)$ is given by the graph. Rank various regions as per the magnitudes of the force (F) acting on a particle from high to low.

    1. $F_{CD}>F_{AB}>F_{BC}>F_{DE}$
    2. $F_{BC}>F_{AB}>F_{DE}>F_{CD}$
    3. $F_{CD}>F_{DE}>F_{AB}>F_{BC}$
    4. $F_{BC}>F_{CD}>F_{DE}>F_{AB}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $|F|=\left|\dfrac{dV}{dx}\right|$, the magnitude of the slope of each segment.
    AB: $1/1=1$; BC: $3/1=3$; CD: $0$; DE: $1/2=0.5$.
    So $F_{BC}>F_{AB}>F_{DE}>F_{CD}$.

  9. JEE Main 2026, 21 January, Shift 2 · Question 31
    A body of mass 2 kg is moving along $x$-direction such that its displacement as function of time is given by $x(t)=\alpha t^2+\beta t+\gamma$ m, where $\alpha=1\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, $\beta=1$ m/s and $\gamma=1$ m. The work done on the body during the time interval $t=2$ s to $t=3$ s, is _________ J.

    1. $24$
    2. $49$
    3. $12$
    4. $42$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $v=2\alpha t+\beta=2t+1$: $v(2)=5$ m/s, $v(3)=7$ m/s.
    Work-energy theorem: $W=\dfrac12\times2\times(49-25)=24$ J.

  10. JEE Main 2026, 22 January, Shift 2 · Question 29
    Given below are two statements :
    Statement I : An object moves from position $r_1$ to position $r_2$ under a conservative force field $\vec{F}$. The work done by the force is $W=-\displaystyle\int_{r_1}^{r_2}\vec{F}\cdot\vec{dr}$.
    Statement II : Any object moving from one location to another location can follow infinite number of paths. Therefore, the amount of work done by the object changes with the path it follows for a conservative force.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. Both Statement I and Statement II are true
    2. Both Statement I and Statement II are false
    3. Statement I is true but Statement II is false
    4. Statement I is false but Statement II is true
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Statement I: the work done by the force is $+\displaystyle\int_{r_1}^{r_2}\vec F\cdot d\vec r$; the expression with the minus sign is the change in potential energy. False.
    Statement II: for a conservative force the work depends only on the end points, not on the path. False.

  11. JEE Main 2026, 23 January, Shift 1 · Question 30
    A small bob $A$ of mass $m$ is attached to a massless rigid rod of length 1 m pivoted at point $P$ and kept at an angle of $60^\circ$ with vertical as shown in figure. At distance of 1 m below point $P$, an identical bob $B$ is kept at rest on a smooth horizontal surface that extends to a circular track of radius $R$ as shown in figure. If bob $B$ just manages to complete the circular path of radius $R$ upto a point $Q$ after being hit elastically by bob $A$, then radius $R$ is __________m.

    1. $\dfrac35$
    2. $\dfrac15$
    3. $\dfrac{2-\sqrt3}{5}$
    4. $\dfrac{2+\sqrt3}{5}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    A falls through $1-\cos60^\circ=0.5$ m, so it reaches B with $v^2=2g(0.5)=g$.
    An elastic collision between equal masses passes this speed fully to B.
    To just get over the top of the circle, $v^2=5gR$ at the bottom: $g=5gR$, so $R=\dfrac15$ m.

  12. JEE Main 2026, 23 January, Shift 1 · Question 31
    Two blocks with masses 100 g and 200 g are attached to the ends of springs $A$ and $B$ as shown in figure. The energy stored in $A$ is $E$. The energy stored in $B$, when spring constants $k_A$, $k_B$ of $A$ and $B$, respectively satisfy the relation $4k_A=3k_B$, is :

    1. $4E$
    2. $3E$
    3. $2E$
    4. $\dfrac43E$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    At equilibrium $kx=mg$, so the stored energy is $\dfrac12kx^2=\dfrac{(mg)^2}{2k}$.
    $\dfrac{E_B}{E_A}=\left(\dfrac{m_B}{m_A}\right)^2\dfrac{k_A}{k_B}=4\times\dfrac34=3$.
    $E_B=3E$.

  13. JEE Main 2026, 23 January, Shift 2 · Question 28
    A bead $P$ sliding on a frictionless semi-circular string $(ACB)$ and it is at point $S$ at $t=0$ and at this instant the horizontal component of its velocity is $v$. Another bead $Q$ of the same mass as $P$ is ejected from point $A$ at $t=0$ along the horizontal string $AB$, with the speed $v$, friction between the beads and the respective strings may be neglected in both cases. Let $t_P$ and $t_Q$ be the respective times taken by beads $P$ and $Q$ to reach the point $B$, then the relation between $t_P$ and $t_Q$ is

    1. $t_P<t_Q$
    2. $t_P>t_Q$
    3. $t_P=t_Q$
    4. $t_P>1.25t_Q$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Q moves at constant speed $v$ over $AB=2R$: $t_Q=\dfrac{2R}{v}$.
    At S the tangent is at $45^\circ$, so P's speed there is $\sqrt2v$, and it falls further to C before rising to B, which speeds it up over most of the path.
    P only has the arc from S to B, $\dfrac{3\pi R}{4}\approx2.36R$, to cover at a speed of about $\sqrt2v$ or more: about $\dfrac{1.67R}{v}$, less than $\dfrac{2R}v$. So $t_P<t_Q$.

  14. JEE Main 2026, 24 January, Shift 2 · Question 30
    In case of vertical circular motion of a particle by a thread of length $r$ if the tension in the thread is zero at an angle $30^\circ$ shown in figure, the velocity at the bottom point $(A)$ of the circular path is
    ($g$ = gravitational acceleration)

    1. $\sqrt{\dfrac52gr}$
    2. $\sqrt{5gr}$
    3. $\sqrt{\dfrac72gr}$
    4. $\sqrt{4gr}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    The point is $30^\circ$ above the horizontal, i.e. $r\sin30^\circ=\dfrac r2$ above the centre.
    With zero tension, the radial component of gravity supplies the centripetal force: $mg\sin30^\circ=\dfrac{mv^2}{r} \Rightarrow v^2=\dfrac{gr}2$.
    Energy from A (height $\dfrac{3r}2$ below that point): $v_A^2=v^2+2g\cdot\dfrac{3r}2=\dfrac{7gr}2$.

  15. JEE Main 2025, 22 January, Shift 1 · Question 28
    A bob of mass m is suspended at a point O by a light string of length $l$ and left to perform vertical motion (circular) as shown in figure. Initially, by applying horizontal velocity $v_0$ at the point 'A', the string becomes slack when, the bob reaches at the point 'D'. The ratio of the kinetic energy of the bob at the points B and C is __________.

    1. $1$
    2. $2$
    3. $3$
    4. $4$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    At D (the top) the string just becomes slack, so $T=0$: $mg=\dfrac{mv_D^2}{l}$ and $K_D=\dfrac{mgl}{2}$.
    Heights above A: B is at $l(1-\cos60^\circ)=\dfrac l2$, C is at $l(1+\cos60^\circ)=\dfrac{3l}{2}$, D is at $2l$.
    $K_B=K_D+mg\left(2l-\dfrac l2\right)=2mgl$ and $K_C=K_D+mg\left(2l-\dfrac{3l}{2}\right)=mgl$.
    $K_B:K_C=2$.

  16. JEE Main 2025, 22 January, Shift 2 · Question 30
    A body of mass 100 g is moving in circular path of radius 2 m on vertical plane as shown in figure. The velocity of the body at point A is 10 m/s. The ratio of its kinetic energies at point B and C is :
    (Take acceleration due to gravity as 10 m/s$^2$)

    1. $\dfrac{3-\sqrt{2}}{2}$
    2. $\dfrac{2+\sqrt{3}}{3}$
    3. $\dfrac{3+\sqrt{3}}{2}$
    4. $\dfrac{2+\sqrt{2}}{3}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    OB makes $30^\circ$ with the downward vertical, so B is $2(1-\cos30^\circ)=2-\sqrt3$ m above A. OC is $90^\circ$ beyond OB, i.e. $60^\circ$ from the upward vertical, so C is $2(1+\cos60^\circ)=3$ m above A.
    $v^2=v_A^2-2gh$: $v_B^2=100-20(2-\sqrt3)=60+20\sqrt3$, $v_C^2=100-60=40$.
    $K_B:K_C=\dfrac{60+20\sqrt3}{40}=\dfrac{3+\sqrt3}{2}$.

  17. JEE Main 2025, 22 January, Shift 2 · Question 31
    A force $\vec{F}=2\hat{i}+b\hat{j}+\hat{k}$ is applied on a particle and it undergoes a displacement $\hat{i}-2\hat{j}-\hat{k}$. What will be the value of b, if work done on the particle is zero.

    1. $\dfrac{1}{2}$
    2. $0$
    3. $\dfrac{1}{3}$
    4. $2$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $W=\vec F\cdot\vec d=2-2b-1=0$, so $b=\dfrac12$.

  18. JEE Main 2025, 23 January, Shift 1 · Question 47
    A force $\vec f=x^2y\hat i+y^2\hat j$ acts on a particle in a plane $x+y=10$. The work done by this force during a displacement from $(0,0)$ to $(4\text{m},2\text{m})$ is _____ Joule (round off to the nearest integer)

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 152

    Following the intended method, use $x+y=10$ to write $f_x=x^2(10-x)$ and integrate each component over its own range.
    $W=\displaystyle\int_0^4x^2(10-x)\,dx+\int_0^2y^2\,dy=\left(\frac{640}{3}-64\right)+\frac83=\frac{448}{3}+\frac83=152$ J.

    Note: As printed the data do not fit together: $(0,0)$ and $(4,2)$ do not lie on $x+y=10$, and the force is not conservative, so the work depends on the path. The official answer 152 comes from putting $y=10-x$ in the $x$-component only, as shown in the solution; along the straight line from $(0,0)$ to $(4,2)$ the work would be about 35 J.

  19. JEE Main 2025, 23 January, Shift 2 · Question 31
    A massless spring gets elongated by amount $x_1$ under a tension of 5 N. Its elongation is $x_2$ under the tension of 7 N. For the elongation of $(5x_1-2x_2)$, the tension in the spring will be,

    1. 39 N
    2. 11 N
    3. 15 N
    4. 20 N
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    With $F=kx$: $x_1=\dfrac5k$, $x_2=\dfrac7k$.
    $5x_1-2x_2=\dfrac{25-14}{k}=\dfrac{11}{k}$, so the tension is $11$ N.

  20. JEE Main 2025, 24 January, Shift 1 · Question 29
    A force $F=\alpha+\beta x^2$ acts on an object in the x-direction. The work done by the force is 5 J when the object is displaced by 1 m. If the constant $\alpha=1$N then $\beta$ will be

    1. $10$ N/m$^2$
    2. $12$ N/m$^2$
    3. $15$ N/m$^2$
    4. $8$ N/m$^2$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $W=\displaystyle\int_0^1(\alpha+\beta x^2)\,dx=\alpha+\dfrac{\beta}{3}=5$.
    With $\alpha=1$: $\beta=12$ N/m$^2$.

  21. JEE Main 2025, 28 January, Shift 1 · Question 27
    A bead of mass 'm' slides without friction on the wall of a vertical circular hoop of radius 'R' as shown in figure. The bead moves under the combined action of gravity and a massless spring (k) attached to the bottom of the hoop. The equilibrium length of the spring is 'R'. If the bead is released from top of the hoop with (negligible) zero initial speed, velocity of bead, when the length of spring becomes 'R', would be (spring constant is 'k', g is accleration due to gravity)

    1. $2\sqrt{gR+\dfrac{kR^2}{m}}$
    2. $\sqrt{2Rg+\dfrac{4kR^2}{m}}$
    3. $\sqrt{2Rg+\dfrac{kR^2}{m}}$
    4. $\sqrt{3Rg+\dfrac{kR^2}{m}}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    At the top the spring length is $2R$, so its extension is $R$; when its length is $R$ the extension is zero.
    A chord of length $R$ from the bottom of the circle reaches a height $\dfrac{R^2}{2R}=\dfrac{R}{2}$, so the bead falls $2R-\dfrac{R}{2}=\dfrac{3R}{2}$.
    Energy: $\dfrac12kR^2+mg\cdot\dfrac{3R}{2}=\dfrac12mv^2 \Rightarrow v=\sqrt{3Rg+\dfrac{kR^2}{m}}$.

  22. JEE Main 2025, 28 January, Shift 1 · Question 28
    Given below are two statements: one is labelled as <b>Assertion A</b> and the other is labelled as <b>Reason R</b>
    <b>Assertion A:</b> In a central force field, the work done is independent of the path chosen.
    <b>Reason R:</b> Every force encountered in mechanics does not have an associated potential energy.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the <b><i>most appropriate</i></b> answer from the options given below

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true but R is false
    4. A is false but R is true
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    A central force $F(r)\hat{r}$ is conservative, so the work done between two points does not depend on the path: A is true.
    Not every force has a potential energy (friction, for example, is non-conservative): R is true.
    R is a general remark and does not explain why a central force is conservative, so it is not the correct explanation of A.

  23. JEE Main 2025, 28 January, Shift 2 · Question 31
    A body of mass 4 kg is placed on a plane at a point P having coordinate (3, 4) m. Under the action of force $\vec{F}=\left(2\hat{i}+3\hat{j}\right)$N, it moves to a new point Q having coordinates (6, 10)m in 4 sec. The average power and instantaneous power at the end of 4 sec are in the ratio of :

    1. 13 : 6
    2. 1 : 2
    3. 4 : 3
    4. 6 : 13
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Work $=\vec F\cdot\vec s=(2,3)\cdot(3,6)=24$ J, so average power $=24/4=6$ W.
    Starting from rest, $\vec a=\vec F/m=(0.5,\,0.75)$ m s$^{-2}$ and $\vec v(4)=(2,3)$ m s$^{-1}$.
    Instantaneous power $=\vec F\cdot\vec v=4+9=13$ W. Ratio $=6:13$.

  24. JEE Main 2025, 29 January, Shift 1 · Question 29
    A body of mass 'm' connected to a massless and unstretchable string goes in verticle circle of radius 'R'under gravity g. The other end of the string is fixed at the center of circle. If velocity at top of circular path is $n\sqrt{gR}$, where, $n\ge1$, then ratio of kinetic energy of the body at bottom to that at top of the circle is

    1. $\dfrac{n^2}{n^2+4}$
    2. $\dfrac{n^2+4}{n^2}$
    3. $\dfrac{n+4}{n}$
    4. $\dfrac{n}{n+4}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Energy conservation over a drop of $2R$: $v_b^2=v_t^2+4gR=(n^2+4)gR$.
    $\dfrac{K_b}{K_t}=\dfrac{v_b^2}{v_t^2}=\dfrac{n^2+4}{n^2}$.

  25. JEE Main 2025, 29 January, Shift 1 · Question 30
    As shown below, bob A of a pendulum having massless string of length 'R' is released from 60° to the vertical. It hits another bob B of half the mass that is at rest on a friction less table in the center. Assuming elastic collision, the magnitude of the velocity of bob A after the collision will be (take g as acceleration due to gravity.)

    1. $\dfrac43\sqrt{Rg}$
    2. $\dfrac13\sqrt{Rg}$
    3. $\dfrac23\sqrt{Rg}$
    4. $\sqrt{Rg}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Speed of A at the bottom: $v=\sqrt{2gR(1-\cos60^\circ)}=\sqrt{gR}$.
    Elastic head-on collision of $m$ with $m/2$ at rest: $v_A'=\dfrac{m-m/2}{m+m/2}v=\dfrac v3$.
    So $|v_A'|=\dfrac13\sqrt{Rg}$.

  26. JEE Main 2025, 29 January, Shift 2 · Question 28
    Given below are two statements. One is labelled as <b>Assertion (A)</b> and the other is labelled as <b>Reason (R)</b>.
    <b>Assertion (A) :</b> Three identical spheres of same mass undergo one dimensional motion as shown in figure with initial velocities $v_A=5$ m/s, $v_B=2$ m/s, $v_C=4$ m/s. If we wait sufficiently long for elastic collision to happen, then $v_A=4$ m/s, $v_B=2$ m/s, $v_C=5$ m/s will be the final velocities.
    <b>Reason (R) :</b> In an elastic collision between identical masses, two objects exchange their velocities.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the <b>correct</b> answer from the options given below :

    1. Both <b>(A)</b> and <b>(R)</b> are true and <b>(R)</b> is the correct explanation of <b>(A)</b>
    2. Both <b>(A)</b> and <b>(R)</b> are true but <b>(R)</b> is <b>NOT</b> the correct explanation of <b>(A)</b>
    3. <b>(A)</b> is true but <b>(R)</b> is false
    4. <b>(A)</b> is false but <b>(R)</b> is true
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    R is true for a head-on elastic collision of equal masses.
    A (5) catches B (2): they swap, giving $v_A=2$, $v_B=5$. B (5) catches C (4): they swap, giving $v_B=4$, $v_C=5$. Now $2<4<5$, so no more collisions.
    Final velocities are $v_A=2$, $v_B=4$, $v_C=5$ m/s, not those stated, so A is false.
