  1. JEE Main 2026, 4 April, Shift 2 · Question 34
    The temperature of a metal strip having coefficient of linear expansion $\alpha$ is increased from $T_1$ to $T_2$ resulting in increase of its length by $\Delta L_1$. The temperature is further increased from $T_2$ to $T_3$ such that the increase in its length is $\Delta L_2$.
    Given $T_3+T_1=2T_2$ and $T_2-T_1=\Delta T$, the value of $\Delta L_2$ is _______.

    1. $\Delta L_1[1+2\alpha^2(\Delta T)^2]$
    2. $\Delta L_1[1+\alpha^2(\Delta T)^2]$
    3. $\Delta L_1[1+2\alpha\Delta T]$
    4. $\Delta L_1[1+\alpha\Delta T]$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    $T_3-T_2=T_2-T_1=\Delta T$.
    With length $L_1$ at $T_1$: $\Delta L_1=L_1\alpha\Delta T$, and the length at $T_2$ is $L_2=L_1(1+\alpha\Delta T)$.
    $\Delta L_2=L_2\alpha\Delta T=L_1\alpha\Delta T(1+\alpha\Delta T)=\Delta L_1[1+\alpha\Delta T]$.

  2. JEE Main 2026, 5 April, Shift 2 · Question 34
    An ideal gas at pressure $P$ and temperature $T$ is expanding such that $PT^3$ = constant. The coefficient of volume expansion of the gas is __________.

    1. $\dfrac2T$
    2. $\dfrac1T$
    3. $\dfrac4T$
    4. $\dfrac3T$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $PV=nRT$ and $P\propto T^{-3}$, so $V\propto\dfrac{T}{P}\propto T^4$.
    $\gamma=\dfrac1V\dfrac{dV}{dT}=\dfrac4T$.

  3. JEE Main 2026, 5 April, Shift 2 · Question 47
    The heat extracted out of $x$ gram of water initially at $50\ {}^\circ$C to cool it down to $0\ {}^\circ$C is sufficient to evaporate $(1000-x)$ gram of water also initially at $50\ {}^\circ$C. The value of $x$ (closest integer) is __________.
    (Take latent heat of water 2256 kJ/kg. K, specific heat capacity of water 4200 J/kg. K)

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 922

    Heat released by $x$ g cooling $50\to0\ {}^\circ$C: $x\times4.2\times50=210x$ J.
    Heat to take $(1000-x)$ g from $50$ to $100\ {}^\circ$C and evaporate it: $(1000-x)(4.2\times50+2256)=2466(1000-x)$ J.
    $210x=2466(1000-x) \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{2466000}{2676}\approx921.5$.
    Closest integer: $922$.

  4. JEE Main 2026, 21 January, Shift 1 · Question 29
    An aluminium and steel rods having same lengths and cross-sections are joined to make total length of 120 cm at $30^\circ$C. The coefficient of linear expansion of aluminium and steel are $24\times10^{-6}/^\circ$C and $1.2\times10^{-5}/^\circ$C, respectively. The length of this composite rod when its temperature is raised to $100^\circ$C, is _______ cm.

    1. $120.15$
    2. $120.06$
    3. $120.03$
    4. $120.20$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Each rod is 60 cm long and $\Delta T=70^\circ$C.
    $\Delta L=60\times70\times(24+12)\times10^{-6}=0.1512$ cm.
    New length $\approx120.15$ cm.

  5. JEE Main 2026, 21 January, Shift 1 · Question 42
    A gas based geyser heats water flowing at the rate of 5.0 litres per minute from $27^\circ$C to $87^\circ$C. The rate of consumption of the gas is _________g/s.
    (Take heat of combustion of gas $=5.0\times10^4$ J/g) specific heat capacity of water $=4200$ J/kg.$^\circ$C

    1. $2.1$
    2. $4.2$
    3. $0.21$
    4. $0.42$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Mass flow $=5$ kg/min $=\dfrac{1}{12}$ kg/s.
    Heat needed per second $=\dfrac{1}{12}\times4200\times60=21000$ J/s.
    Gas used $=\dfrac{21000}{5\times10^4}=0.42$ g/s.

  6. JEE Main 2026, 22 January, Shift 1 · Question 32
    Rods $x$ and $y$ of equal dimensions but of different materials are joined as shown in figure. Temperatures of end points $A$ and $F$ are maintained at 100 °C and 40 °C respectively. Given the thermal conductivity of rod $x$ is three times of that of rod $y$, the temperature at junction points $B$ and $E$ are (close to):

    1. 80 °C and 60 °C respectively
    2. 60 °C and 45 °C respectively
    3. 89 °C and 73 °C respectively
    4. 80 °C and 70 °C respectively
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Let the thermal resistance of one rod $x$ be $r$; then a rod $y$ has $3r$.
    B to E: path BCE $=6r$ in parallel with path BDE $=2r$, giving $\dfrac{6r\cdot2r}{8r}=1.5r$.
    Total A to F: $r+1.5r+3r=5.5r$, so the heat current is $\dfrac{60}{5.5r}$.
    $T_B=100-\dfrac{60}{5.5}\approx89$ °C; $T_E=40+3\cdot\dfrac{60}{5.5}\approx73$ °C.

  7. JEE Main 2026, 23 January, Shift 1 · Question 33
    In a perfectly inelastic collision, two spheres made of the same material with masses 15 kg and 25 kg, moving in opposite directions with speeds of 10 m/s and 30 m/s, respectively, strike each other and stick together. The rise in temperature (in $^\circ$C), if all the heat produced during the collision is retained by these spheres, is :
    (specific heat of sphere material 31 cal/kg.$^\circ$C and 1 cal $=$ 4.2 J)

    1. $1.15$
    2. $1.44$
    3. $1.75$
    4. $1.95$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Loss of kinetic energy $=\dfrac12\dfrac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}v_{\text{rel}}^2=\dfrac12\times\dfrac{15\times25}{40}\times40^2=7500$ J.
    Heat capacity $=40\times31\times4.2=5208$ J/$^\circ$C.
    $\Delta T=\dfrac{7500}{5208}\approx1.44\ ^\circ$C.

  8. JEE Main 2026, 24 January, Shift 1 · Question 33
    A brass wire of length 2 m and radius 1 mm at 27 $^\circ$C is held taut between two rigid supports. Initially it was cooled to a temperature of $-43$ $^\circ$C creating a tension $T$ in the wire. The temperature to which the wire has to be cooled in order to increase the tension in it to $1.4T$, is __________ $^\circ$C.

    1. $-86$
    2. $-71$
    3. $-80$
    4. $-65$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Thermal tension $T=YA\alpha\,\Delta\theta$, so $T\propto\Delta\theta$.
    Cooling by $70^\circ$C gives $T$, so $1.4T$ needs $98^\circ$C of cooling: $27-98=-71^\circ$C.

  9. JEE Main 2026, 28 January, Shift 1 · Question 33
    10 kg of ice at $-10\ ^\circ$C is added to 100 kg of water to lower its temperature from $25\ ^\circ$C. Consider no heat exchange to surroundings. The decrement to the temperature of water is __________$^\circ$C.
    (specific heat of ice = 2100 J/Kg.$^\circ$C, specific heat of water = 4200 J/Kg.$^\circ$C, latent heat of fusion of ice = $3.36\times10^5$ J/Kg)

    1. $11.6$
    2. $15$
    3. $6.67$
    4. $10$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Heat to bring the ice to $0\ ^\circ$C and melt it: $10(2100)(10)+10(3.36\times10^5)=3.57\times10^6$ J (less than the $1.05\times10^7$ J the water can give up, so all the ice melts).
    Final temperature $T$: $100(4200)(25-T)=3.57\times10^6+10(4200)T$, so $462000\,T=6.93\times10^6$ and $T=15\ ^\circ$C.
    Fall in temperature of the water $=25-15=10\ ^\circ$C.

  10. JEE Main 2026, 28 January, Shift 1 · Question 35
    Which of the following best represents the temperature versus heat supplied graph for water, in the range of $-20\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $120\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$ ?

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Heating ice from $-20\ ^\circ$C: the temperature rises to $0\ ^\circ$C, stays constant while the ice melts, rises to $100\ ^\circ$C, stays constant while the water boils, then rises again as steam is heated.
    Only graph (1) shows exactly these two horizontal steps, at $0\ ^\circ$C and $100\ ^\circ$C. Graph (2) has no steps, (3) has no melting step, and (4) has an extra step.

  11. JEE Main 2025, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 26
    Match List - I with List - II.

    List - IList - II
    (A) Heat capacity of body(I) J kg$^{-1}$
    (B) Specific heat capacity of body(II) J K$^{-1}$
    (C) Latent heat(III) J kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$
    (D) Thermal conductivity(IV) J m$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. (A)-(IV), (B)-(III), (C)-(II), (D)-(I)
    2. (A)-(III), (B)-(IV), (C)-(I), (D)-(II)
    3. (A)-(III), (B)-(I), (C)-(II), (D)-(IV)
    4. (A)-(II), (B)-(III), (C)-(I), (D)-(IV)
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    (A) Heat capacity $=\dfrac{Q}{\Delta T}$: J K$^{-1}$ (II).
    (B) Specific heat capacity $=\dfrac{Q}{m\Delta T}$: J kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ (III).
    (C) Latent heat $=\dfrac Qm$: J kg$^{-1}$ (I).
    (D) Thermal conductivity, from $\dfrac{Q}{t}=KA\dfrac{\Delta T}{L}$: J m$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$ (IV).

  12. JEE Main 2025, 4 April, Shift 2 · Question 31
    Consider a rectangular sheet of solid material of length $l=9$ cm and width $d=4$ cm. The coefficient of linear expansion is $\alpha=3.1\times10^{-5}$ K$^{-1}$ at room temperature and one atmospheric pressure. The mass of sheet $m=0.1$ kg and the specific heat capacity $C_v=900$ J kg$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$. If the amount of heat supplied to the material is $8.1\times10^2$ J then change in area of the rectangular sheet is :

    1. $2.0\times10^{-6}$ m$^2$
    2. $4.0\times10^{-7}$ m$^2$
    3. $6.0\times10^{-7}$ m$^2$
    4. $3.0\times10^{-7}$ m$^2$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $\Delta T=\dfrac{Q}{mC}=\dfrac{810}{0.1\times900}=9$ K.
    $\Delta A=2\alpha A\Delta T=2\times3.1\times10^{-5}\times(36\times10^{-4})\times9\approx2.0\times10^{-6}$ m$^2$.

  13. JEE Main 2025, 7 April, Shift 1 · Question 47
    A wire of length 10 cm and diameter 0.5 mm is used in a bulb. The temperature of the wire is 1727$^\circ$C and power radiated by the wire is 94.2 W. Its emissivity is $\dfrac x8$ where $x$ = ______.
    (Given $\sigma=6.0\times10^{-8}$ W m$^{-2}$ K$^{-4}$, $\pi=3.14$ and assume that the emissivity of wire material is same at all wavelength.)

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 5

    Radiating area (curved surface) $A=\pi dL=3.14\times0.5\times10^{-3}\times0.1=1.57\times10^{-4}$ m$^2$; $T=2000$ K, $T^4=1.6\times10^{13}$ K$^4$.
    $P=e\sigma AT^4=e\times6.0\times10^{-8}\times1.57\times10^{-4}\times1.6\times10^{13}=150.72\,e$ W.
    $e=\dfrac{94.2}{150.72}=0.625=\dfrac58$, so $x=5$.

  14. JEE Main 2025, 7 April, Shift 2 · Question 47
    Two cylindrical rods A and B made of different materials, are joined in a straight line. The ratios of lengths, radii and thermal conductivites of these rods are: $\dfrac{L_A}{L_B}=\dfrac12$, $\dfrac{r_A}{r_B}=2$ and $\dfrac{K_A}{K_B}=\dfrac12$. The free ends of rods A and B are maintained at 400 K, 200 K, respectively. The temperature of rods interface is ______ K, when equilibrium is established.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 360

    Thermal resistance $R=\dfrac{L}{KA}$: $\dfrac{R_A}{R_B}=\dfrac{L_A}{L_B}\cdot\dfrac{K_B}{K_A}\cdot\left(\dfrac{r_B}{r_A}\right)^2=\dfrac12\cdot2\cdot\dfrac14=\dfrac14$.
    In series the 200 K drop divides as 1 : 4, so 40 K across A.
    Interface temperature $=400-40=360$ K.

  15. JEE Main 2025, 8 April, Shift 2 · Question 32
    Water falls from a height of 200 m into a pool. Calculate the rise in temperature of the water assuming no heat dissipation from the water in the pool.
    (Take $g=10$ m/s$^2$, specific heat of water $=4200$ J/(kg K))

    1. 0.23 K
    2. 0.48 K
    3. 0.14 K
    4. 0.36 K
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    All the potential energy becomes heat: $mgh=mc\,\Delta T$, so $\Delta T=\dfrac{gh}{c}=\dfrac{2000}{4200}\approx0.48$ K.

  16. JEE Main 2025, 22 January, Shift 1 · Question 31
    Two spherical bodies of same materials having radii 0.2 m and 0.8 m are placed in same atmosphere. The temperature of the smaller body is 800 K and temperature of the bigger body is 400 K. If the energy radiated from the smaller body is E, the energy radiated from the bigger body is (assume, effect of the surrounding temperature to be negligible),

    1. $256$ E
    2. $64$ E
    3. $16$ E
    4. E
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Stefan's law: radiated power $\propto AT^4\propto r^2T^4$ (same emissivity).
    Ratio (bigger/smaller) $=\left(\dfrac{0.8}{0.2}\right)^2\left(\dfrac{400}{800}\right)^4=16\times\dfrac1{16}=1$.
    So the bigger body radiates E.

  17. JEE Main 2025, 22 January, Shift 1 · Question 32
    An amount of ice of mass $10^{-3}$ kg and temperature $-10^\circ$C is transformed to vapour of temperature $110^\circ$C by applying heat. The total amount of work required for this conversion is,
    (Take, specific heat of ice $=2100$ J kg$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$, specific heat of water $=4180$ J kg$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$, specific heat of steam $=1920$ J kg$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$, Latent heat of ice $=3.35\times10^5$ J kg$^{-1}$ and Latent heat of steam $=2.25\times10^6$ J kg$^{-1}$)

    1. $3043$ J
    2. $3024$ J
    3. $3022$ J
    4. $3003$ J
    Show answer and solution

    Total per kg $=3043200$ J, so for $10^{-3}$ kg the energy needed is about $3043$ J.

  18. JEE Main 2025, 22 January, Shift 1 · Question 49
    Three conductors of same length having thermal conductivity $k_1$, $k_2$ and $k_3$ are connected as shown in figure.
    Area of cross sections of 1$^{\text{st}}$ and 2$^{\text{nd}}$ conductor are same and for 3$^{\text{rd}}$ conductor it is double of the 1$^{\text{st}}$ conductor. The temperatures are given in the figure. In steady state condition, the value of $\theta$ is __________ $^\circ$C.
    (Given : $k_1=60$ J s$^{-1}$m$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$, $k_2=120$ J s$^{-1}$m$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$, $k_3=135$ J s$^{-1}$m$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$)

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 40

    Conductors 1 and 2 are in parallel between 100$^\circ$C and $\theta$; conductor 3 (area $2A$) carries the same heat current from $\theta$ to 0$^\circ$C.
    $(k_1+k_2)A(100-\theta)=k_3(2A)\theta \Rightarrow 180(100-\theta)=270\theta$.
    $\theta=40^\circ$C.

  19. JEE Main 2025, 23 January, Shift 1 · Question 32
    A gun fires a lead bullet of temperature 300 K into a wooden block. The bullet having melting temperature of 600 K penetrates into the block and melts down. If the total heat required for the process is 625 J, then the mass of the bullet is _____grams.
    (Latent heat of fusion of lead $=2.5\times10^4$ JKg$^{-1}$ and specific heat capacity of lead $=125$ JKg$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$)

    1. $5$
    2. $10$
    3. $15$
    4. $20$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Heat to warm from 300 K to 600 K and then melt: $Q=m(c\Delta T+L)=m(125\times300+25000)=62500\,m$.
    $62500\,m=625 \Rightarrow m=0.01$ kg $=10$ g.

  21. JEE Main 2025, 24 January, Shift 2 · Question 31
    The temperature of a body in air falls from $40^\circ$C to $24^\circ$C in 4 minutes. The temperature of the air is $16^\circ$C. The temperature of the body in the next 4 minutes will be :

    1. $\dfrac{28}{3}\ ^\circ$C
    2. $\dfrac{14}{3}\ ^\circ$C
    3. $\dfrac{42}{3}\ ^\circ$C
    4. $\dfrac{56}{3}\ ^\circ$C
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Newton's law of cooling (average form): $\dfrac{T_1-T_2}{t}=k\left(\dfrac{T_1+T_2}{2}-T_0\right)$.
    First interval: $\dfrac{16}{4}=k(32-16) \Rightarrow k=\dfrac14$ per minute.
    Second: $\dfrac{24-T}{4}=\dfrac14\left(\dfrac{24+T}{2}-16\right) \Rightarrow 48-2T=T-8 \Rightarrow T=\dfrac{56}{3}\ ^\circ$C.

  22. JEE Main 2025, 29 January, Shift 2 · Question 30
    A cup of coffee cools from 90°C to 80°C in t minutes when the room temperature is 20°C. The time taken by the similar cup of coffee to cool from 80°C to 60°C at the same room temperature is :

    1. $\dfrac{10}{13}t$
    2. $\dfrac{13}{5}t$
    3. $\dfrac{5}{13}t$
    4. $\dfrac{13}{10}t$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Newton's law of cooling with average temperatures: $\dfrac{10}{t}=k(85-20)$ and $\dfrac{20}{t'}=k(70-20)$.
    $\dfrac{t'}{t}=\dfrac{20/50}{10/65}=\dfrac{13}{5}$, so $t'=\dfrac{13}{5}t$.
