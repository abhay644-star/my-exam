  1. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 44
    The binding energy per nucleon of ${}^{209}_{83}Bi$ is __________ MeV.
    [Take $m\left({}^{209}_{83}Bi\right)=208.980388$ u, $m_p=1.007825$ u, $m_n=1.008665$ u, $1$ u $=931$ MeV/c$^2$]

    1. $7.48$
    2. $7.84$
    3. $8.79$
    4. $6.94$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Mass defect $=83\times1.007825+126\times1.008665-208.980388$.
    $=83.649475+127.091790-208.980388=1.760877$ u.
    Binding energy $=1.760877\times931\approx1639.4$ MeV.
    Per nucleon $=\dfrac{1639.4}{209}\approx7.84$ MeV.

  2. JEE Main 2026, 4 April, Shift 1 · Question 29
    Two nuclei of mass number $3$ combine with another nucleus of mass number $4$ to yield a nucleus of mass number $10$. If the binding energy per nucleon for the mass numbers $3$, $4$ and $10$ are $5.6$ MeV, $7.4$ MeV and $6.1$ MeV, respectively, then in the process, $\Delta Mc^2=$ ______ MeV.

    1. $6.9$
    2. $7.9$
    3. $2.2$
    4. $4.3$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Binding energy of the reactants: $2\times3\times5.6+4\times7.4=33.6+29.6=63.2$ MeV.
    Binding energy of the product: $10\times6.1=61$ MeV.
    The product is less tightly bound, so the mass increases: $\Delta Mc^2=63.2-61=2.2$ MeV.

  3. JEE Main 2026, 5 April, Shift 2 · Question 44
    Assuming the experimental mass of ${}^{12}_{6}C$ as 12 u, the mass defect of ${}^{12}_{6}C$ atom is __________ $\mathrm{MeV/c^2}$.
    (Mass of proton = 1.00727 u. mass of neutron = 1.00866 u, 1 u = 931.5 $\mathrm{MeV/c^2}$ and c is the speed of the light in vacuum).

    1. $127.5$
    2. $89.03$
    3. $272.0$
    4. $92.0$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Mass of 6 protons and 6 neutrons $=6(1.00727)+6(1.00866)=6.04362+6.05196=12.09558$ u.
    Mass defect $=12.09558-12=0.09558$ u.
    $0.09558\times931.5\approx89.03\ \mathrm{MeV/c^2}$.

  4. JEE Main 2026, 6 April, Shift 1 · Question 48
    The energy released when $\dfrac{7}{17.13}$ kg of $^7_3$Li is converted into $^4_2$He by proton bombardment is $\alpha\times10^{32}$ eV. The value of $\alpha$ is ____. (Nearest integer)
    (Mass of $^7_3$Li $=7.0183$ u, mass of $^4_2$He $=4.004$ u, mass of proton $=1.008$ u and 1 u $=931$ MeV/c$^2$ and Avogadro number $=6.0\times10^{23}$)

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 6

    $^7_3\mathrm{Li}+{}^1_1\mathrm{H}\to2\,{}^4_2\mathrm{He}$: $\Delta m=7.0183+1.008-8.008=0.0183$ u, $Q=0.0183\times931\approx17.04$ MeV per reaction.
    Number of Li nuclei $=\dfrac{7000/17.13}{7}\times6\times10^{23}\approx3.50\times10^{25}$.
    Energy $\approx3.50\times10^{25}\times17.04\times10^6$ eV $\approx5.97\times10^{32}$ eV.
    $\alpha\approx6$.

  5. JEE Main 2026, 6 April, Shift 2 · Question 41
    The energy released if hydrogen atoms are combined to form $_2^4\mathrm{He}$ is ________ MeV. (Take binding energies per nucleon of $_1^2\mathrm{H}$ and $_2^4\mathrm{He}$ as 1.1 MeV and 7.2 MeV, respectively)

    1. $6.1$
    2. $24.4$
    3. $26.6$
    4. $5$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $2\,{}_1^2\mathrm H\to{}_2^4\mathrm{He}$.
    Binding energy of He $=4\times7.2=28.8$ MeV; of two deuterons $=2\times2\times1.1=4.4$ MeV.
    Energy released $=28.8-4.4=24.4$ MeV.

  6. JEE Main 2026, 8 April, Shift 2 · Question 44
    Two radioactive substances A and B of mass numbers 200 and 212 respectively, shows spontaneous $\alpha$-decay with same $Q$ value of 1 MeV. The ratio of energies of $\alpha$-rays produced by A and B is __________.

    1. $\dfrac{2548}{2650}$
    2. $\dfrac{2706}{2646}$
    3. $\dfrac{2597}{2600}$
    4. $\dfrac{2862}{2499}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Momentum conservation shares $Q$ so that $K_\alpha=Q\,\dfrac{A-4}{A}$.
    $K_A=\dfrac{196}{200}$ MeV, $K_B=\dfrac{208}{212}$ MeV.
    $\dfrac{K_A}{K_B}=\dfrac{196\times212}{200\times208}=\dfrac{41552}{41600}=\dfrac{2597}{2600}$.

  7. JEE Main 2026, 22 January, Shift 1 · Question 44
    The minimum frequency of photon required to break a particle of mass 15.348 amu into 4 $\alpha$ particles is ________ kHz.
    [mass of He nucleus = 4.002 amu, 1 amu = $1.66\times10^{-27}$ kg, h = $6.6\times10^{-34}$ J.s and c = $3\times10^8$ m/s]

    1. $9\times10^{20}$
    2. $14.94\times10^{19}$
    3. $14.94\times10^{20}$
    4. $9\times10^{19}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Mass to be supplied: $\Delta m=4(4.002)-15.348=0.660$ amu.
    $E=\Delta m\,c^2=0.66\times1.66\times10^{-27}\times9\times10^{16}\approx9.86\times10^{-11}$ J.
    $\nu=\dfrac{E}{h}=\dfrac{9.86\times10^{-11}}{6.6\times10^{-34}}\approx1.494\times10^{23}$ Hz $=14.94\times10^{19}$ kHz.

  8. JEE Main 2026, 23 January, Shift 2 · Question 44
    Which of the following pair of nuclei are isobars of the element?

    1. ${}^2_1\mathrm{H}$ and ${}^3_1\mathrm{H}$
    2. ${}^{236}_{92}\mathrm{U}$ and ${}^{238}_{92}\mathrm{U}$
    3. ${}^3_1\mathrm{H}$ and ${}^3_2\mathrm{He}$
    4. ${}^{198}_{80}\mathrm{Hg}$ and ${}^{197}_{79}\mathrm{Au}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Isobars have the same mass number A but different atomic numbers.
    ${}^3_1\mathrm{H}$ and ${}^3_2\mathrm{He}$ both have $A=3$. (The H and U pairs are isotopes; Hg-198 and Au-197 have the same number of neutrons, 118, so they are isotones.)

  9. JEE Main 2026, 23 January, Shift 2 · Question 50
    The average energy released per fission for the nucleus of ${}^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U}$ is 190 MeV. When all the atoms of 47 g pure ${}^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U}$ undergo fission process, the energy released is $\alpha\times10^{23}$ MeV. The value of $\alpha$ is ______.
    (Avogadro Number $=6\times10^{23}$ per mole)

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 228

    Number of nuclei $=\dfrac{47}{235}\times6\times10^{23}=1.2\times10^{23}$.
    Energy $=1.2\times10^{23}\times190=228\times10^{23}$ MeV, so $\alpha=228$.

  10. JEE Main 2026, 24 January, Shift 1 · Question 44
    Given below are two statements:
    Statement I: For all elements, greater the mass of the nucleus, greater is the binding energy per nucleon.
    Statement II: For all elements, nuclei with less binding energy per nucleon transforms to nuclei with greater binding energy per nucleon.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below

    1. Both Statement I and Statement II are true
    2. Both Statement I and Statement II are false
    3. Statement I is true but Statement II is false
    4. Statement I is false but Statement II is true
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Statement I is false: binding energy per nucleon peaks near $A\approx56$ and falls for heavier nuclei.
    Statement II is true: nuclear changes (fission of heavy nuclei, fusion of light ones) release energy by moving towards higher binding energy per nucleon.

  11. JEE Main 2026, 24 January, Shift 2 · Question 43
    The binding energy for the following nuclear reactions are expressed in MeV.
    $\mathrm{{}_2He^3+{}_0n^1\rightarrow{}_2He^4+20\ MeV}$
    $\mathrm{{}_2He^4+{}_0n^1\rightarrow{}_2He^5-0.9\ MeV}$
    If $X_3$, $X_4$, $X_5$ denote the stability of $\mathrm{{}_2He^3}$, $\mathrm{{}_2He^4}$ and $\mathrm{{}_2He^5}$, respectively, then the correct order is :

    1. $X_4<X_5<X_3$
    2. $X_4>X_5>X_3$
    3. $X_4=X_5=X_3$
    4. $X_4>X_5<X_3$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Adding a neutron to He-3 releases 20 MeV, so $BE(\mathrm{He^4})=BE(\mathrm{He^3})+20$ MeV.
    Adding a neutron to He-4 absorbs 0.9 MeV, so $BE(\mathrm{He^5})=BE(\mathrm{He^4})-0.9$ MeV $=BE(\mathrm{He^3})+19.1$ MeV.
    Higher binding energy means greater stability: $X_4>X_5>X_3$.

  12. JEE Main 2026, 28 January, Shift 1 · Question 44
    An atom ${}^{8}_{3}X$ is bombarded by shower of fundamental particles and in 10 s this atom absorbed 10 electrons, 10 protons and 9 neutrons. The percentage growth in the surface area of the nucleons is recorded by:

    1. $150\ \%$
    2. $900\%$
    3. $225\%$
    4. $250\%$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Electrons do not change the nucleus. Mass number: $8\to8+10+9=27$.
    $R\propto A^{1/3}$: the radius goes from $2R_0$ to $3R_0$, so the surface area becomes $\left(\tfrac32\right)^2=2.25$ times the original, i.e. $225\%$ of it (the increase itself is $125\%$).

  13. JEE Main 2026, 28 January, Shift 2 · Question 44
    A nucleus has mass number $\alpha$ and radius $R_\alpha$. Another nucleus has mass number $\beta$ and radius $R_\beta$. If $\beta=8\alpha$ then $R_\alpha/R_\beta$ is :

    1. $0.5$
    2. $1$
    3. $8$
    4. $2$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $R\propto A^{1/3}$, so $\dfrac{R_\alpha}{R_\beta}=\left(\dfrac18\right)^{1/3}=0.5$.

  14. JEE Main 2025, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 38
    Energy released when two deuterons $\left({}_1\mathrm{H}^2\right)$ fuse to form a helium nucleus $\left({}_2\mathrm{He}^4\right)$ is :
    (Given : Binding energy per nucleon of ${}_1\mathrm{H}^2=1.1$ MeV and binding energy per nucleon of ${}_2\mathrm{He}^4=7.0$ MeV)

    1. 8.1 MeV
    2. 5.9 MeV
    3. 23.6 MeV
    4. 26.8 MeV
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Binding energy of He-4 $=4\times7.0=28.0$ MeV; of two deuterons $=2\times2\times1.1=4.4$ MeV.
    Energy released $=28.0-4.4=23.6$ MeV.

  15. JEE Main 2025, 3 April, Shift 1 · Question 44
    Match the LIST-I with LIST-II

    LIST-ILIST-II
    A. $\mathrm{{}_0^1n+{}_{92}^{235}U\rightarrow{}_{54}^{140}Xe+{}_{38}^{94}Sr+2\,{}_0^1n}$I. Chemical reaction
    B. $\mathrm{2H_2+O_2\rightarrow2H_2O}$II. Fusion with +ve Q value
    C. $\mathrm{{}_1^2H+{}_1^2H\rightarrow{}_2^3He+{}_0^1n}$III. Fission
    D. $\mathrm{{}_1^1H+{}_1^3H\rightarrow{}_1^2H+{}_1^2H}$IV. Fusion with -ve Q value
    Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

    1. A-III, B-I, C-II, D-IV
    2. A-II, B-I, C-IV, D-III
    3. A-III, B-I, C-IV, D-II
    4. A-II, B-I, C-III, D-IV
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    A: a heavy nucleus splits after neutron capture: fission (III). B: only electrons rearrange: chemical reaction (I).
    C: two deuterons join to give helium-3: fusion releasing energy (II).
    D: the binding energy of two deuterons ($2\times2.22$ MeV) is less than that of $\mathrm{^3H}$ (8.48 MeV), so energy must be supplied: negative Q (IV).

  17. JEE Main 2025, 7 April, Shift 2 · Question 44
    Given below are two statements : one is labelled as Assertion (A) and the other is labelled as Reason (R).
    Assertion (A) : The density of the copper $\left({}^{64}_{29}\text{Cu}\right)$ nucleus is greater than that of the carbon $\left({}^{12}_{6}\text{C}\right)$ nucleus.
    Reason (R) : The nucleus of mass number A has a radius proportional to $A^{1/3}$.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the most appropriate answer from the options given below :

    1. Both (A) and (R) are correct and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are correct but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is correct but (R) is not correct
    4. (A) is not correct but (R) is correct
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    $R=R_0A^{1/3}$ (R correct), so the volume is proportional to $A$, as is the mass. Nuclear density is therefore the same for all nuclei: (A) is not correct.

  18. JEE Main 2025, 8 April, Shift 2 · Question 44
    For a nucleus of mass number A and radius R, the mass density of nucleus can be represented as

    1. $A^3$
    2. $A^{\frac23}$
    3. $A^{\frac13}$
    4. Independent of A
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    $R=R_0A^{1/3}$, so the volume is proportional to $A$, as is the mass. Their ratio, the density, does not depend on $A$.

  19. JEE Main 2025, 23 January, Shift 1 · Question 44
    A radioactive nucleus $n_2$ has 3 times the decay constant as compared to the decay constant of another radioactive nucleus $n_1$. If initial number of both nuclei are the same, what is the ratio of number of nuclei of $n_2$ to the number of nuclei of $n_1$, after one half-life of $n_1$?

    1. $4$
    2. $1/4$
    3. $8$
    4. $1/8$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    After one half-life of $n_1$, $N_1=\dfrac{N_0}{2}$.
    $n_2$ decays three times as fast, so in the same time it passes through three of its own half-lives: $N_2=\dfrac{N_0}{8}$.
    $\dfrac{N_2}{N_1}=\dfrac14$.

  20. JEE Main 2025, 23 January, Shift 2 · Question 44
    Given below are two statements. One is labelled as Assertion (A) and the other is labelled as Reason (R).
    Assertion (A) : The binding energy per nucleon is found to be practically independent of the atomic number A, for nuclei with mass numbers between 30 and 170.
    Reason (R) : Nuclear force is long range.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A)
    3. (A) is true but (R) is false
    4. (A) is false but (R) is true
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    The binding energy per nucleon is nearly constant (about 8 MeV) for mass numbers 30 to 170, so (A) is true.
    This saturation happens because the nuclear force is short range, so (R) is false.

  21. JEE Main 2025, 28 January, Shift 1 · Question 44
    Choose the correct nuclear process from the below options
    [p: proton, n: neutron, e$^-$: electron, e$^+$: positron, $\nu$ : neutrino, $\bar{\nu}$ : antineutrino]

    1. $\mathrm{n}\to\mathrm{p}+\mathrm{e}^-+\nu$
    2. $\mathrm{n}\to\mathrm{p}+\mathrm{e}^++\bar{\nu}$
    3. $\mathrm{n}\to\mathrm{p}+\mathrm{e}^-+\bar{\nu}$
    4. $\mathrm{n}\to\mathrm{p}+\mathrm{e}^++\nu$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Charge conservation needs an electron (not a positron) when a neutron becomes a proton.
    Lepton number: the electron has $+1$, so an antineutrino ($-1$) must be emitted.
    $\mathrm{n}\to\mathrm{p}+\mathrm{e}^-+\bar\nu$ ($\beta^-$ decay).
