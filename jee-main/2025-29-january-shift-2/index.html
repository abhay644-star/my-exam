Solved questions from this paper

A few questions with their official answers and worked solutions. All 75 are in the online test.

  1. Question 1 · Mathematics · Relations and Functions
    If the domain of the function $\log_5(18x-x^2-77)$ is $(\alpha, \beta)$ and the domain of the function $\log_{(x-1)}\left(\dfrac{2x^2+3x-2}{x^2-3x-4}\right)$ is $(\gamma, \delta)$, then $\alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2$ is equal to :

    1. $174$
    2. $186$
    3. $179$
    4. $195$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $18x-x^2-77>0\iff(x-7)(x-11)<0$: $(\alpha,\beta)=(7,11)$.
    Base: $x>1$, $x\neq2$. Argument $\dfrac{(2x-1)(x+2)}{(x-4)(x+1)}>0$; for $x>1$ this needs $x>4$. So $\gamma=4$.
    $\alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2=49+121+16=186$.

  2. Question 3 · Mathematics · Complex Numbers
    If the set of all $a\in\mathbf{R}$, for which the equation $2x^2+(a-5)x+15=3a$ has no real root, is the interval $(\alpha, \beta)$, and $X=\{x\in\mathbf{Z}:\alpha<x<\beta\}$, then $\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}x^2$ is equal to :

    1. $2119$
    2. $2129$
    3. $2109$
    4. $2139$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    No real root: $(a-5)^2-8(15-3a)<0\Rightarrow a^2+14a-95<0\Rightarrow-19<a<5$.
    $X=\{-18,\ldots,4\}$, so $\sum x^2=\sum_{k=1}^{18}k^2+\sum_{k=1}^{4}k^2=2109+30=2139$.

  3. Question 26 · Physics · Units and Measurements
    Match <b>List - I</b> with <b>List - II</b>.

    List - IList - II
    (A) Young's Modulus(I) $ML^{-1}T^{-1}$
    (B) Torque(II) $ML^{-1}T^{-2}$
    (C) Coefficient of Viscosity(III) $M^{-1}L^3T^{-2}$
    (D) Gravitational Constant(IV) $ML^2T^{-2}$
    Choose the <b>correct</b> answer from the options given below :

    1. (A)-(I), (B)-(III), (C)-(II), (D)-(IV)
    2. (A)-(IV), (B)-(II), (C)-(III), (D)-(I)
    3. (A)-(II), (B)-(IV), (C)-(I), (D)-(III)
    4. (A)-(II), (B)-(I), (C)-(IV), (D)-(III)
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Young's modulus (stress): $ML^{-1}T^{-2}$ (II). Torque: $ML^2T^{-2}$ (IV).
    Viscosity coefficient ($F/(A\,dv/dx)$): $ML^{-1}T^{-1}$ (I). $G=Fr^2/m^2$: $M^{-1}L^3T^{-2}$ (III).

  4. Question 27 · Physics · Laws of Motion
    A sand dropper drops sand of mass m(t) on a conveyer belt at a rate proportional to the square root of speed $(v)$ of the belt, i.e. $\dfrac{dm}{dt}\propto\sqrt{v}$. If P is the power delivered to run the belt at constant speed then which of the following relationship is true ?

    1. $P\propto v$
    2. $P^2\propto v^3$
    3. $P^2\propto v^5$
    4. $P\propto\sqrt{v}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Force needed to bring the falling sand up to the belt speed: $F=v\dfrac{dm}{dt}\propto v^{3/2}$.
    $P=Fv\propto v^{5/2}$, so $P^2\propto v^5$.

  5. Question 51 · Chemistry · Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry
    0.1 M solution of KI reacts with excess of $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ and $\mathrm{KIO_3}$ solutions. According to equation
    $\mathrm{5I^-+IO_3^-+6H^+\rightarrow3I_2+3H_2O}$
    Identify the <b>correct</b> statements :
    (A) 200 mL of KI solution reacts with 0.004 mol of $\mathrm{KIO_3}$
    (B) 200 mL of KI solution reacts with 0.006 mol of $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$
    (C) 0.5 L of KI solution produced 0.005 mol of $\mathrm{I_2}$
    (D) Equivalent weight of $\mathrm{KIO_3}$ is equal to $\left(\dfrac{\text{Molecular weight}}{5}\right)$
    Choose the <b>correct</b> answer from the options given below :

    1. (A) and (B) only
    2. (B) and (C) only
    3. (A) and (D) only
    4. (C) and (D) only
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    200 mL of 0.1 M KI is 0.02 mol $\mathrm{I^-}$: it needs 0.004 mol $\mathrm{IO_3^-}$ (A true) and 0.024 mol $\mathrm{H^+}$, i.e. 0.012 mol $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ (B false).
    0.5 L is 0.05 mol $\mathrm{I^-}$, giving 0.03 mol $\mathrm{I_2}$ (C false).
    In $\mathrm{IO_3^-\rightarrow I_2}$ iodine goes from +5 to 0, so the equivalent weight is $M/5$ (D true).

  6. Question 52 · Chemistry · Structure of Atom
    Given below are two statements :
    <b>Statement (I) :</b> It is impossible to specify simultaneously with arbitrary precision, both the linear momentum and the position of a particle.
    <b>Statement (II) :</b> If the uncertainty in the measurement of position and uncertainty in measurement of momentum are equal for an electron, then the uncertainty in the measurement of velocity is $\ge\sqrt{\dfrac{h}{\pi}}\times\dfrac{1}{2m}$.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the <b>correct</b> answer from the options given below :

    1. Both <b>Statement I</b> and <b>Statement II</b> are true
    2. Both <b>Statement I</b> and <b>Statement II</b> are false
    3. <b>Statement I</b> is true but <b>Statement II</b> is false
    4. <b>Statement I</b> is false but <b>Statement II</b> is true
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    I is Heisenberg's uncertainty principle: correct.
    II: $\Delta x\,\Delta p\ge\dfrac{h}{4\pi}$ with $\Delta x=\Delta p$ gives $\Delta p\ge\dfrac12\sqrt{\dfrac h\pi}$, so $\Delta v=\dfrac{\Delta p}{m}\ge\dfrac{1}{2m}\sqrt{\dfrac h\pi}$: correct.
