  1. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 1 · Question 6
    The number of seven-digit numbers, that can be formed by using the digits $1, 2, 3, 5$ and $7$ such that each digit is used at least once, is :

    1. $15400$
    2. $17800$
    3. $16800$
    4. $29400$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Five distinct digits fill seven places, so the extra two places give pattern $(3,1,1,1,1)$ or $(2,2,1,1,1)$.
    Pattern $(3,1,1,1,1)$: $5\cdot\dfrac{7!}{3!}=4200$.
    Pattern $(2,2,1,1,1)$: $\binom{5}{2}\cdot\dfrac{7!}{2!\,2!}=12600$.
    Total $=16800$.

  2. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 1 · Question 7
    The number of elements in the set $S=\left\{(r,k)\ :\ k \in \mathbb{Z} \text{ and } {}^{36}C_{r+1}=\dfrac{6\left({}^{35}C_r\right)}{\left(k^2-3\right)}\right\}$, is :

    1. $2$
    2. $4$
    3. $8$
    4. $16$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    ${}^{36}C_{r+1}=\dfrac{36}{r+1}\,{}^{35}C_r$, so $\dfrac{36}{r+1}=\dfrac{6}{k^2-3}$, i.e. $r+1=6(k^2-3)$.
    Need $0\le r\le35$, so $1\le 6(k^2-3)\le36 \Rightarrow 4\le k^2\le 9$.
    $k^2=4$: $r=5$, $k=\pm2$; $k^2=9$: $r=35$, $k=\pm3$.
    So $S$ has $4$ elements.

  3. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 7
    Let $p_n$ denote the total number of triangles formed by joining the vertices of an $n$-side regular polygon. If $p_{n+1}-p_n=66$, then the sum of all distinct prime divisors of $n$ is :

    1. $7$
    2. $8$
    3. $5$
    4. $6$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $p_n={}^nC_3$, so $p_{n+1}-p_n={}^{n+1}C_3-{}^nC_3={}^nC_2$.
    $\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}=66 \Rightarrow n=12$.
    $12=2^2\cdot 3$; sum of distinct prime divisors $=2+3=5$.

  4. JEE Main 2026, 4 April, Shift 1 · Question 8
    The number of ways, of forming a queue of $4$ boys and $3$ girls such that all the girls are not together, is:

    1. $5040$
    2. $3050$
    3. $3410$
    4. $4320$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    All arrangements: $7!=5040$.
    All three girls together: treat them as one unit, $5!\times3!=720$.
    Required: $5040-720=4320$.

  5. JEE Main 2026, 4 April, Shift 2 · Question 17
    Let $A=\{(a,b,c): a,b,c$ are non-negative integers and $a+b+2c=22\}$. Then $n(A)$ is equal to:

    1. $121$
    2. $124$
    3. $144$
    4. $169$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    For $c=0,1,\ldots,11$: $a+b=22-2c$ has $23-2c$ solutions.
    $n(A)=\sum_{c=0}^{11}(23-2c)=12\times23-2\times66=144$.

  6. JEE Main 2026, 5 April, Shift 1 · Question 21
    Let $A = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$. The number of one-one functions $f : A \to A$ such that $f(1) \ge 3$, $f(3) \le 4$ and $f(2) + f(3) = 5$, is __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 72

    $(f(2),f(3))\in\{(1,4),(2,3),(3,2),(4,1)\}$, all with $f(3)\le4$.
    In each case $f(1)\ge3$ must avoid the two used values: $(1,4)$: $f(1)\in\{3,5,6\}$; $(2,3)$: $\{4,5,6\}$; $(3,2)$: $\{4,5,6\}$; $(4,1)$: $\{3,5,6\}$ — $3$ choices each.
    The remaining $3$ elements map one-one onto the remaining $3$ values in $3!=6$ ways.
    Total $=4\times3\times6=72$.

  7. JEE Main 2026, 5 April, Shift 1 · Question 22
    Two players A and B play a series of games of badminton. The player, who wins $5$ games first, wins the series. Assuming that no game ends in a draw, the number of ways, in which player A wins the series is________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 126

    A wins the last game and exactly $4$ of the earlier games; B wins $k$ games, $k=0,1,2,3,4$.
    The earlier $4+k$ games can be arranged in $\binom{4+k}{k}$ ways.
    Total $=\binom40+\binom51+\binom62+\binom73+\binom84=1+5+15+35+70=126$.

  8. JEE Main 2026, 5 April, Shift 2 · Question 9
    A box contains 5 blue, 6 yellow and 4 red balls. The number of ways, of drawing 8 balls containing at least two balls of each colour, is :

    1. $4100$
    2. $4140$
    3. $4230$
    4. $4290$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    (blue, yellow, red) with each $\ge2$ and total 8: $(4,2,2),(2,4,2),(2,2,4),(3,3,2),(3,2,3),(2,3,3)$.
    $(4,2,2)$: $5\cdot15\cdot6=450$; $(2,4,2)$: $10\cdot15\cdot6=900$; $(2,2,4)$: $10\cdot15\cdot1=150$.
    $(3,3,2)$: $10\cdot20\cdot6=1200$; $(3,2,3)$: $10\cdot15\cdot4=600$; $(2,3,3)$: $10\cdot20\cdot4=800$.
    Total $=4100$.

  9. JEE Main 2026, 6 April, Shift 1 · Question 7
    The number of 4-letter words, with or without meaning, each consisting of two vowels and two consonants that can be formed from the letters of the word INCONSEQUENTIAL, without repeating any letter, is:

    1. $2670$
    2. $2840$
    3. $2920$
    4. $3600$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Distinct vowels: I, O, E, U, A (5). Distinct consonants: N, C, S, Q, T, L (6).
    Choose 2 vowels and 2 consonants, all different, and arrange: $\binom52\binom62\,4!=10\times15\times24=3600$.

  10. JEE Main 2026, 6 April, Shift 2 · Question 7
    A building has ground floor and 10 more floors. Nine persons enter in a lift at the ground floor. The lift goes up to the $10^{\text{th}}$ floor. The number of ways, in which any 4 persons exit at a floor and the remaining 5 persons exit at a different floor, if the lift does not stop at the first and the second floors, is equal to :

    1. $2184$
    2. $3064$
    3. $7056$
    4. $11340$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Available floors: 3rd to 10th, i.e. 8 floors.
    Choose the group of 4: $\binom94=126$ ways.
    Floor for the group of 4 and a different floor for the group of 5: $8\times7=56$ ways.
    Total $=126\times56=7056$.

  11. JEE Main 2026, 8 April, Shift 2 · Question 9
    A person has three different bags and four different books. The number of ways, in which he can put these books in the bags so that no bag is empty, is :

    1. $18$
    2. $36$
    3. $39$
    4. $72$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Count onto maps from 4 books to 3 bags: $3^4-\binom31 2^4+\binom32 1^4=81-48+3=36$.
    (Equivalently: choose the pair of books that share a bag, $\binom42=6$, then arrange 3 groups in 3 bags, $3!=6$: $36$.)

  12. JEE Main 2026, 21 January, Shift 1 · Question 6
    The number of strictly increasing functions $f$ from the set $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ to the set $\{1, 2, 3, \ldots, 9\}$ such that $f(i)\neq i$ for $1\le i\le 6$, is equal to :

    1. $21$
    2. $22$
    3. $27$
    4. $28$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    For a strictly increasing $f$ into $\{1,\ldots,9\}$, $i\le f(i)\le i+3$. Let $g(i)=f(i)-i$; then $g$ is non-decreasing with values in $\{0,1,2,3\}$.
    $f(i)\neq i$ means $g(i)\ge1$, so $g$ is a non-decreasing sequence of length 6 from $\{1,2,3\}$.
    Number of such sequences $=\binom{6+2}{2}=28$.

  13. JEE Main 2026, 21 January, Shift 1 · Question 23
    Let $S=\{(m, n): m, n\in\{1, 2, 3, \ldots, 50\}\}$. If the number of elements $(m, n)$ in S such that $6^m+9^n$ is a multiple of 5 is p and the number of elements $(m, n)$ in S such that $m+n$ is a square of a prime number is q, then $p+q$ is equal to __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 1333

    Modulo 5: $6^m\equiv1$ and $9^n\equiv(-1)^n$, so $6^m+9^n\equiv0$ exactly when $n$ is odd: $p=50\times25=1250$.
    $m+n\in[2,100]$ is a prime square for $m+n=4,9,25,49$; the number of pairs with sum $s\le51$ is $s-1$.
    $q=3+8+24+48=83$.
    $p+q=1333$.

  14. JEE Main 2026, 21 January, Shift 2 · Question 9
    The largest $n\in\mathbf{N}$, for which $7^n$ divides $101!$, is :

    1. $19$
    2. $18$
    3. $16$
    4. $15$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Exponent of 7 in $101!$: $\left\lfloor\dfrac{101}{7}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\dfrac{101}{49}\right\rfloor=14+2=16$.

  15. JEE Main 2026, 22 January, Shift 1 · Question 23
    Let ABC be a triangle. Consider four points $p_1, p_2, p_3, p_4$ on the side AB, five points $p_5, p_6, p_7, p_8, p_9$ on the side BC, and four points $p_{10}, p_{11}, p_{12}, p_{13}$ on the side AC. None of these points is a vertex of the triangle ABC. Then the total number of pentagons, that can be formed by taking all the vertices from the points $p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_{13}$, is ________

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 660

    Five vertices with no three collinear means at most 2 from each side, so the split (AB, BC, AC) is $(2,2,1)$, $(2,1,2)$ or $(1,2,2)$.
    ${}^4C_2\,{}^5C_2\,{}^4C_1+{}^4C_2\,{}^5C_1\,{}^4C_2+{}^4C_1\,{}^5C_2\,{}^4C_2=240+180+240=660$.

  16. JEE Main 2026, 22 January, Shift 2 · Question 22
    Let S be the set of the first 11 natural numbers. Then the number of elements in $A=\{B\subseteq S: n(B)\ge2$ and the product of all elements of B is even$\}$ is ___________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 1979

    Subsets with at least 2 elements: $2^{11}-1-11=2036$.
    The product is odd only if all elements are odd; with 6 odd numbers, such subsets of size at least 2 number $2^6-1-6=57$.
    $2036-57=1979$.

  17. JEE Main 2026, 23 January, Shift 1 · Question 22
    The number of 4-letter words, with or without meaning, which can be formed using the letters PQRPQRSTUVP, is __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 1422

    Letters: P three times, Q and R twice each, S, T, U, V once each (7 different letters).
    All different: $^7P_4=840$.
    One pair and two others: $3\times\binom62\times\dfrac{4!}{2!}=540$.
    Two pairs: $\binom32\times\dfrac{4!}{2!2!}=18$.
    Three P and one other: $6\times4=24$.
    Total $=840+540+18+24=1422$.

  18. JEE Main 2026, 23 January, Shift 2 · Question 7
    The number of ways, in which 16 oranges can be distributed to four children such that each child gets at least one orange, is

    1. $384$
    2. $403$
    3. $455$
    4. $429$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Identical oranges, each child at least one: the number of positive integer solutions of $x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4=16$.
    $\binom{15}{3}=455$.

  19. JEE Main 2026, 23 January, Shift 2 · Question 22
    Let S denote the set of 4-digit numbers $abcd$ such that $a>b>c>d$ and P denote the set of 5-digit numbers having product of its digits equal to 20. Then $n(S)+n(P)$ is equal to ____

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 260

    S: any 4 different digits from 0-9 arranged in decreasing order (the first digit is then never 0): $\binom{10}{4}=210$.
    P: $20=2^2\times5$ with digits from $\{1,2,4,5\}$: $\{5,4,1,1,1\}$ gives $\dfrac{5!}{3!}=20$; $\{5,2,2,1,1\}$ gives $\dfrac{5!}{2!2!}=30$. So $n(P)=50$.
    $n(S)+n(P)=260$.

  20. JEE Main 2026, 24 January, Shift 1 · Question 22
    The number of numbers greater than 5000, less than 9000 and divisible by 3, that can be formed using the digits 0, 1, 2, 5, 9, if the repetition of the digits is allowed, is __________

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 42

    The numbers have 4 digits and must start with 5 (a leading 9 gives at least 9000).
    Since $5\equiv2 \pmod 3$, the last three digits must have sum $\equiv1 \pmod 3$. Residues: $\{0,9\}\to0$, $\{1\}\to1$, $\{2,5\}\to2$.
    Count triples by residues $(r_1,r_2,r_3)$ with sum $\equiv1$: patterns $\{0,0,1\}$: $3\times2\times2\times1=12$; $\{1,1,2\}$: $3\times1\times1\times2=6$; $\{0,2,2\}$: $3\times2\times2\times2=24$.
    Total $=12+6+24=42$.

  21. JEE Main 2026, 24 January, Shift 2 · Question 7
    The largest value of $n$, for which $40^n$ divides $60!$, is

    1. $12$
    2. $13$
    3. $14$
    4. $11$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $40^n=2^{3n}5^n$.
    Power of 2 in $60!$: $30+15+7+3+1=56$, allowing $3n\le56$, $n\le18$.
    Power of 5 in $60!$: $12+2=14$, so $n\le14$.
    Largest $n=14$.

  22. JEE Main 2026, 24 January, Shift 2 · Question 8
    The letters of the word "UDAYPUR" are written in all possible ways with or without meaning and these words are arranged as in a dictionary. The rank of the word "UDAYPUR" is

    1. $1578$
    2. $1579$
    3. $1580$
    4. $1581$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Letters in order: A, D, P, R, U, U, Y.
    Starting with A, D, P or R: $4\times\dfrac{6!}{2!}=1440$.
    U then A: $5!=120$ (total 1560). UDA then P, R or U: $3\times3!=18$ (total 1578).
    UDAYP then R: $1$ word (total 1579). The next word is UDAYPUR itself, so its rank is 1580.

  23. JEE Main 2026, 28 January, Shift 1 · Question 9
    Let $\mathrm{S}=\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9\}$. Let $x$ be the number of 9-digit numbers formed using the digits of the set S such that only one digit is repeated and it is repeated exactly twice. Let $y$ be the number of 9-digit numbers formed using the digits of the set S such that only two digits are repeated and each of these is repeated exactly twice. Then,

    1. $21x=4y$
    2. $45x=7y$
    3. $56x=9y$
    4. $29x=5y$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $x$: choose the repeated digit (9 ways) and 7 of the other 8 digits (8 ways); arrangements $9!/2!$. So $x=36\cdot9!$.
    $y$: choose the two repeated digits $\binom92=36$ and 5 of the other 7 digits $\binom75=21$; arrangements $9!/(2!2!)$. So $y=189\cdot9!$.
    $x/y=36/189=4/21$, i.e. $21x=4y$.

  24. JEE Main 2026, 28 January, Shift 2 · Question 23
    Three persons enter in a lift at the ground floor. The lift will go upto $10^{\text{th}}$ floor. The number of ways, in which the three persons can exit the lift at three different floors, if the lift does not stop at first, second and third floors, is equal to __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 210

    The available floors are the 4th to the 10th, i.e. 7 floors.
    Three persons at three different floors: $7\times6\times5=210$.
