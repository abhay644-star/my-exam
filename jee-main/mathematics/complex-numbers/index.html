  1. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 1 · Question 1
    Let $\alpha, \alpha+2, \alpha \in \mathbb{Z}$, be the roots of the quadratic equation

    $\displaystyle x(x+2)+(x+1)(x+3)+(x+2)(x+4)+\ldots+(x+n-1)(x+n+1)=4n \quad \text{for some } n \in \mathbb{N}.$
    Then $n+\alpha$ is equal to :

    1. $0$
    2. $1$
    3. $2$
    4. $3$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    The LHS is $\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(x+k)(x+k+2) = nx^2 + n(n+1)x + \dfrac{(n-1)n(2n-1)}{6} + n(n-1)$.
    Dividing by $n$: $x^2+(n+1)x+\dfrac{(n-1)(2n-1)}{6}+n-5=0$.
    Roots differ by $2$, so discriminant $=4$: $(n+1)^2-\dfrac{2(2n^2-3n+1)}{3}-4n+20=4$.
    This simplifies to $n^2=49$, so $n=7$.
    Sum of roots $2\alpha+2=-(n+1)=-8 \Rightarrow \alpha=-5$.
    Hence $n+\alpha=2$.

    Let $x$ and $y$ be real numbers such that $50\left(\dfrac{2x}{1+3i}-\dfrac{y}{1-2i}\right)=31+17i,\ i=\sqrt{-1}$. Then the value of $10(x-3y)$ is :

    1. $20$
    2. $31$
    3. $35$
    4. $75$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    $\dfrac{2x}{1+3i}=\dfrac{x(1-3i)}{5}$ and $\dfrac{y}{1-2i}=\dfrac{y(1+2i)}{5}$.
    So LHS $=10(x-y)+10i(-3x-2y)$.
    Comparing: $x-y=3.1$ and $-3x-2y=1.7$.
    Solving: $y=-2.2,\ x=0.9$.
    $10(x-3y)=10(0.9+6.6)=75$.

  3. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 1
    Let $\alpha, \beta$ be the roots of the equation $x^2-3x+r=0$, and $\dfrac{\alpha}{2}, 2\beta$ be the roots of the equation $x^2+3x+r=0$. If the roots of the equation $x^2+6x=m$ are $2\alpha+\beta+2r$ and $\alpha-2\beta-\dfrac{r}{2}$, then $m$ is equal to :

    1. $-135$
    2. $-567$
    3. $135$
    4. $567$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    From the first equation: $\alpha+\beta=3$, $\alpha\beta=r$. From the second: $\dfrac{\alpha}{2}+2\beta=-3$ (and the product is again $\alpha\beta=r$).
    $\alpha=3-\beta \Rightarrow \dfrac{3-\beta}{2}+2\beta=-3 \Rightarrow \beta=-3,\ \alpha=6,\ r=-18$.
    Roots of $x^2+6x-m=0$: $2\alpha+\beta+2r=12-3-36=-27$ and $\alpha-2\beta-\dfrac r2=6+6+9=21$ (sum $-6$, as required).
    Product $=-m \Rightarrow -m=(-27)(21)=-567 \Rightarrow m=567$.

    Let the circles $C_1: |z|=r$ and $C_2: |z-3-4i|=5$, $z \in \mathbb{C}$, be such that $C_2$ lies within $C_1$. If $z_1$ moves on $C_1$, $z_2$ moves on $C_2$ and $\min|z_1-z_2|=2$, then $\max|z_1-z_2|$ is equal to :

    1. $12$
    2. $17$
    3. $22$
    4. $24$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $C_2$ has centre $3+4i$ (at distance $5$ from the origin) and radius $5$.

    Let $z$ be a complex number such that $|z+2|=|z-2|$ and $\arg\left(\dfrac{z+3}{z-i}\right)=\dfrac{\pi}{4}$. Then $|z|^2$ is equal to:

    1. $9$
    2. $4$
    3. $5$
    4. $1$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $|z+2|=|z-2|$ means $z$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $-2$ and $2$, the imaginary axis: $z=iy$.
    $\dfrac{z+3}{z-i}=\dfrac{3+iy}{i(y-1)}=\dfrac{(3+iy)(-i)}{y-1}=\dfrac{y-3i}{y-1}$.
    Argument $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$: real part $=$ imaginary part $>0$, so $\dfrac{y}{y-1}=\dfrac{-3}{y-1} \Rightarrow y=-3$ (then both parts equal $\dfrac34>0$).
    $z=-3i$, so $|z|^2=9$.

    1. $42$
    2. $23$
    3. $27$
    4. $38$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    $z_2+\sqrt3 i=-2-\sqrt3 i$: $|\,\cdot\,|^2=4+3=7$.

  7. JEE Main 2026, 4 April, Shift 2 · Question 5
    If the quadratic equation $(\lambda+2)x^2-3\lambda x+4\lambda=0$, $\lambda\neq-2$, has two positive roots, then the number of possible integral values of $\lambda$ is:

    1. $1$
    2. $2$
    3. $3$
    4. $4$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Real roots: $9\lambda^2-16\lambda(\lambda+2)\ge0\iff\lambda(7\lambda+32)\le0\iff-\dfrac{32}{7}\le\lambda\le0$.
    Both roots positive: product $\dfrac{4\lambda}{\lambda+2}>0$ and sum $\dfrac{3\lambda}{\lambda+2}>0$, i.e. $\lambda>0$ or $\lambda<-2$.
    Together: $-\dfrac{32}{7}\le\lambda<-2$ (at $\lambda=0$ the roots are $0$, not positive).
    Integral values: $\lambda=-4,-3$, i.e. $2$ values.

    Let $a, b \in \mathbb{C}$. Let $\alpha, \beta$ be the roots of the equation $x^2 + ax + b = 0$. If $\beta - \alpha = \sqrt{11}$ and $\beta^2 - \alpha^2 = 3i\sqrt{11}$, then $(\beta^3 - \alpha^3)^2$ is equal to:

    1. $160$
    2. $176$
    3. $194$
    4. $187$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $\beta^2-\alpha^2=(\beta-\alpha)(\beta+\alpha)=3i\sqrt{11}$, so $\alpha+\beta=3i$.
    $(\beta-\alpha)^2=(\alpha+\beta)^2-4\alpha\beta \Rightarrow 11=-9-4\alpha\beta \Rightarrow \alpha\beta=-5$.
    $\beta^3-\alpha^3=(\beta-\alpha)\left[(\alpha+\beta)^2-\alpha\beta\right]=\sqrt{11}\,(-9+5)=-4\sqrt{11}$.
    $(\beta^3-\alpha^3)^2=16\times11=176$.

  10. JEE Main 2026, 6 April, Shift 1 · Question 2
    Let one root of the quadratic equation in $x$:
    $(k^2-15k+27)x^2+9(k-1)x+18=0$
    be twice the other. Then the length of the latus rectum of the parabola $y^2=6kx$ is equal to:

    1. $4$
    2. $6$
    3. $8$
    4. $12$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Let $A=k^2-15k+27$ and the roots be $r,2r$.
    Sum: $3r=-\dfrac{9(k-1)}{A}$; product: $2r^2=\dfrac{18}{A}\Rightarrow r^2=\dfrac{9}{A}$.
    From the sum, $9r^2=\dfrac{81(k-1)^2}{A^2}$, so $\dfrac{81}{A}=\dfrac{81(k-1)^2}{A^2}\Rightarrow A=(k-1)^2$.
    $k^2-15k+27=k^2-2k+1\Rightarrow 13k=26\Rightarrow k=2$.
    Parabola $y^2=12x$: latus rectum $=12$.

    Let $e_1$ and $e_2$ be two distinct roots of the equation $x^2-ax+2=0$. Let the sets
    $\{a\in\mathbb{R}: e_1 \text{ and } e_2 \text{ are the eccentricities of hyperbolas}\}=(\alpha,\beta)$, and
    $\{a\in\mathbb{R}: e_1 \text{ and } e_2 \text{ are the eccentricities of an ellipse and a hyperbola, respectively}\}=(\gamma,\infty)$.

    1. $18$
    2. $22$
    3. $26$
    4. $34$
    Show answer and solution

    Let the set of all values of $k\in\mathbb{R}$ such that the equation $z(\bar z+2+i)+k(2+3i)=0$, $z\in\mathbb{C}$, has at least one solution, be the interval $[\alpha,\beta]$. Then $9(\alpha+\beta)$ is equal to:

    1. $-10$
    2. $-8$
    3. $10\sqrt{13}$
    4. $8\sqrt{13}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Put $z=x+iy$: $|z|^2+(2+i)z+k(2+3i)=0$, and $(2+i)z=(2x-y)+i(x+2y)$.

    So $\alpha+\beta$ is the sum of the roots $=-\dfrac{40}{36}=-\dfrac{10}{9}$, and $9(\alpha+\beta)=-10$.
