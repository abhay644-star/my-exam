  1. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 8
    A man throws a fair coin repeatedly. He gets 10 points for each head he throws and 5 points for each tail he throws. If the probability that he gets exactly 30 points is $\dfrac{m}{n}$, $\gcd(m, n)=1$, then $m+n$ is equal to :

    1. $53$
    2. $55$
    3. $107$
    4. $105$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Count in units of 5 points: a tail adds 1, a head adds 2. Let $P_k$ be the probability that the score is ever exactly $k$ units.
    $P_k=\dfrac12P_{k-1}+\dfrac12P_{k-2}$, with $P_0=1$, $P_1=\dfrac12$.
    $P_2=\dfrac34$, $P_3=\dfrac58$, $P_4=\dfrac{11}{16}$, $P_5=\dfrac{21}{32}$, $P_6=\dfrac{43}{64}$.
    30 points $=6$ units, so $\dfrac mn=\dfrac{43}{64}$ and $m+n=107$.

  2. JEE Main 2026, 4 April, Shift 1 · Question 21
    A coin is tossed $8$ times. If the probability that exactly $4$ heads appear in the first six tosses and exactly $3$ heads appear in the last five tosses is $p$, then $96p$ is equal to ______.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 9

    First six tosses: 1–6; last five: 4–8. Tosses 4, 5, 6 are common. Let $k$ heads fall in tosses 4–6.
    Then tosses 1–3 have $4-k$ heads and tosses 7–8 have $3-k$ heads, so $1\le k\le3$.
    Count $=\sum_k\binom3k\binom3{4-k}\binom2{3-k}$: $k=1$: $3\cdot1\cdot1=3$; $k=2$: $3\cdot3\cdot2=18$; $k=3$: $1\cdot3\cdot1=3$. Total $24$.
    $p=\dfrac{24}{2^8}=\dfrac{3}{32}$, so $96p=9$.

  3. JEE Main 2026, 5 April, Shift 1 · Question 7
    A letter is known to have arrived by post either from KANPUR or from ANANTPUR. On the envelope just two consecutive letters AN are visible. The probability, that the letter came from ANANTPUR, is:

    1. $\dfrac{7}{10}$
    2. $\dfrac{10}{17}$
    3. $\dfrac{12}{19}$
    4. $\dfrac{7}{19}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    KANPUR has $5$ pairs of consecutive letters (KA, AN, NP, PU, UR); AN occurs once: $P(\text{AN}\mid K)=\dfrac15$.
    ANANTPUR has $7$ pairs (AN, NA, AN, NT, TP, PU, UR); AN occurs twice: $P(\text{AN}\mid A)=\dfrac27$.
    By Bayes' theorem with equal priors: $P(A\mid \text{AN})=\dfrac{\frac27}{\frac15+\frac27}=\dfrac{2/7}{17/35}=\dfrac{10}{17}$.

  4. JEE Main 2026, 5 April, Shift 2 · Question 8
    The probabilities that players A and B of a team are selected for the captaincy for a tournament are 0.6 and 0.4, respectively. If A is selected the captain, the probability that the team wins the tournament is 0.8 and if B is selected the captain, the probability that the team wins the tournament is 0.7. Then the probability, that the team wins the tournament, is :

    1. $0.74$
    2. $0.76$
    3. $0.72$
    4. $0.78$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    By the theorem of total probability: $P(W)=0.6\times0.8+0.4\times0.7=0.48+0.28=0.76$.

  5. JEE Main 2026, 6 April, Shift 1 · Question 10
    A bag contains $(N+1)$ coins $-$ $N$ fair coins, and one coin with 'Head' on both sides. A coin is selected at random and tossed. If the probability of getting 'Head' is $\dfrac{9}{16}$, then $N$ is equal to:

    1. $5$
    2. $7$
    3. $8$
    4. $9$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $P(H)=\dfrac{N}{N+1}\cdot\dfrac12+\dfrac{1}{N+1}\cdot1=\dfrac{N+2}{2(N+1)}$.
    $\dfrac{N+2}{2(N+1)}=\dfrac9{16}\Rightarrow16N+32=18N+18\Rightarrow N=7$.

  6. JEE Main 2026, 6 April, Shift 2 · Question 9
    A bag contains 6 blue and 6 green balls. Pairs of balls are drawn without replacement until the bag is empty. The probability that each drawn pair consists of one blue and one green ball is :

    1. $\dfrac{63}{925}$
    2. $\dfrac{17}{231}$
    3. $\dfrac{16}{231}$
    4. $\dfrac{64}{925}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Think of the 12 draws as a colour sequence; all $\binom{12}{6}=924$ placements of the blue balls are equally likely.
    Each of the 6 pairs must be one blue and one green: in each pair the blue can be first or second, giving $2^6=64$ sequences.
    Probability $=\dfrac{64}{924}=\dfrac{16}{231}$.

  7. JEE Main 2026, 8 April, Shift 2 · Question 6
    A candidate has to go to the examination centre to appear in an examination. The candidate uses only one means of transportation for the entire distance out of bus, scooter and car. The probabilities of the candidate going by bus, scooter and car, respectively, are $\dfrac{2}{5}$, $\dfrac{1}{5}$ and $\dfrac{2}{5}$. The probabilities that the candidate reaches late at the examination centre are $\dfrac{1}{5}$, $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\dfrac{1}{4}$ if the candidate uses bus, scooter and car, respectively. Given that the candidate reached late at the examination centre, the probability that the candidate travelled by bus is :

    1. $\dfrac{11}{37}$
    2. $\dfrac{12}{37}$
    3. $\dfrac{13}{37}$
    4. $\dfrac{14}{37}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $P(\text{late})=\dfrac25\cdot\dfrac15+\dfrac15\cdot\dfrac13+\dfrac25\cdot\dfrac14=\dfrac{12+10+15}{150}=\dfrac{37}{150}$.
    By Bayes' theorem, $P(\text{bus}\mid\text{late})=\dfrac{12/150}{37/150}=\dfrac{12}{37}$.
