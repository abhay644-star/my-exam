  1. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 1 · Question 13
    If the point of intersection of the lines $\dfrac{x+1}{3}=\dfrac{y+a}{5}=\dfrac{z+b+1}{7}$ and $\dfrac{x-2}{1}=\dfrac{y-b}{4}=\dfrac{z-2a}{7}$ lies on $xy$-plane, then the value of $a+b$ is :

    1. $2$
    2. $5$
    3. $7$
    4. $9$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Points: $(3t-1,\,5t-a,\,7t-b-1)$ and $(s+2,\,4s+b,\,7s+2a)$.
    $z=0$: $t=\dfrac{b+1}{7}$, $s=-\dfrac{2a}{7}$.
    Equal $x$: $3(b+1)-7=-2a+14 \Rightarrow 2a+3b=18$.
    Equal $y$: $5(b+1)-7a=-8a+7b \Rightarrow a=2b-5$.
    So $b=4,\ a=3$ and $a+b=7$.

  2. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 1 · Question 15
    Let a line $L$ passing through the point $(1,1,1)$ be perpendicular to both the vectors $2\hat{i}+2\hat{j}+\hat{k}$ and $\hat{i}+2\hat{j}+2\hat{k}$. If $P(a,b,c)$ is the foot of perpendicular from the origin on the line $L$, then the value of $34(a+b+c)$ is :

    1. $50$
    2. $80$
    3. $100$
    4. $120$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Direction of $L$: $(2,2,1)\times(1,2,2)=(2,-3,2)$.
    General point $P=(1+2t,\,1-3t,\,1+2t)$.
    $\overrightarrow{OP}\perp L$: $2(1+2t)-3(1-3t)+2(1+2t)=1+17t=0 \Rightarrow t=-\dfrac1{17}$.
    $P=\left(\dfrac{15}{17},\dfrac{20}{17},\dfrac{15}{17}\right)$, so $34(a+b+c)=34\cdot\dfrac{50}{17}=100$.

    Let the point A be the foot of perpendicular drawn from the point $P(a, b, 0)$ on the line $\dfrac{x-1}{2}=\dfrac{y-2}{1}=\dfrac{z-\alpha}{3}$. If the midpoint of the line segment PA is $\left(0, \dfrac34, \dfrac{-1}{4}\right)$, then the value of $a^2+b^2+\alpha^2$ is equal to :

    1. $1$
    2. $2$
    3. $6$
    4. $9$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Let $A=(1+2t,\ 2+t,\ \alpha+3t)$.
    Midpoint: $a=-1-2t$, $b=-\dfrac12-t$, $\alpha=-\dfrac12-3t$.
    $\overrightarrow{PA}=\left(2+4t,\ \dfrac52+2t,\ -\dfrac12\right)$ is perpendicular to $(2,1,3)$: $4+8t+\dfrac52+2t-\dfrac32=0 \Rightarrow t=-\dfrac12$.
    $a=0$, $b=0$, $\alpha=1$, so $a^2+b^2+\alpha^2=1$.

  4. JEE Main 2026, 4 April, Shift 1 · Question 14
    A line with direction ratios $1,-1,2$ intersects the lines $\dfrac{x}{2}=\dfrac{y}{3}=\dfrac{z+1}{3}$ and $\dfrac{x+1}{-1}=\dfrac{y-2}{1}=\dfrac{z}{4}$ at the points $P$ and $Q$, respectively. If the length of the line segment $PQ$ is $\alpha$, then $225\alpha^2$ is equal to:

    1. $1024$
    2. $1014$
    3. $1104$
    4. $1204$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $P=(2s,3s,3s-1)$, $Q=(-1-t,\ 2+t,\ 4t)$, and $\overrightarrow{PQ}=\lambda(1,-1,2)$.
    $-1-t-2s=\lambda$ and $2+t-3s=-\lambda$; adding gives $1-5s=0$, so $s=\dfrac15$.
    Then $\lambda=-t-\dfrac75$, and $4t-3s+1=2\lambda$ gives $4t+\dfrac25=-2t-\dfrac{14}{5} \Rightarrow t=-\dfrac{8}{15}$, $\lambda=-\dfrac{13}{15}$.
    $\alpha=|\lambda|\sqrt6$, so $\alpha^2=\dfrac{169\times6}{225}$ and $225\alpha^2=1014$.

    If $\left(2\alpha+1,\ \alpha^2-3\alpha,\ \dfrac{\alpha-1}{2}\right)$ is the image of $(\alpha,2\alpha,1)$ in the line $\dfrac{x-2}{3}=\dfrac{y-1}{2}=\dfrac z1$, then the possible value(s) of $\alpha$ is (are)

    1. Only $3$
    2. Only $3$ and $-1$
    3. Only $3$, $\dfrac14$ and $-1$
    4. Only $3$ and $\dfrac14$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $PP'=\left(\alpha+1,\ \alpha^2-5\alpha,\ \dfrac{\alpha-3}{2}\right)$ must be perpendicular to $(3,2,1)$:
    $3(\alpha+1)+2(\alpha^2-5\alpha)+\dfrac{\alpha-3}{2}=0\Rightarrow4\alpha^2-13\alpha+3=0\Rightarrow\alpha=3$ or $\dfrac14$.
    Midpoint $\left(\dfrac{3\alpha+1}{2},\dfrac{\alpha^2-\alpha}{2},\dfrac{\alpha+1}{4}\right)$ must lie on the line: $\dfrac{x-2}{3}=\dfrac{\alpha-1}{2}$ and $z=\dfrac{\alpha+1}{4}$.
    $\dfrac{\alpha-1}{2}=\dfrac{\alpha+1}{4}\Rightarrow\alpha=3$ (then $\dfrac{y-1}{2}=\dfrac{3-1}{2}=1$ also matches); $\alpha=\dfrac14$ fails.
    So only $\alpha=3$.

    1. $10\sqrt{13}$
    2. $5\sqrt{13}$
    3. $20\sqrt{13}$
    4. $15\sqrt{13}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    The line is $\dfrac x1=\dfrac{y-1}{2}=\dfrac{z-2}{3}$, through $P(0,1,2)$ with direction $\vec d=(1,2,3)$. (B lies on it with $\alpha=14$.)
    Height = distance of A from the line: $\overrightarrow{PA}=(1,5,1)$, $\overrightarrow{PA}\times\vec d=(13,-2,-3)$, so the distance is $\dfrac{\sqrt{182}}{\sqrt{14}}=\sqrt{13}$.

    Let the direction cosines of two lines satisfy the equations : $4l+m-n=0$ and $2mn+10nl+3lm=0$. Then the cosine of the acute angle between these lines is :

    1. $\dfrac{20}{3\sqrt{38}}$
    2. $\dfrac{10}{7\sqrt{38}}$
    3. $\dfrac{10}{\sqrt{38}}$
    4. $\dfrac{10}{3\sqrt{38}}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    $m=-8l$: $n=-4l$, direction $(1,-8,-4)$, length 9. $m=-\dfrac52l$: direction $(2,-5,3)$, length $\sqrt{38}$.

    If the image of the point $P(a, 2, a)$ in the line $\dfrac x2=\dfrac{y+a}{1}=\dfrac z1$ is Q and the image of Q in the line $\dfrac{x-2b}{2}=\dfrac{y-a}{1}=\dfrac{z+2b}{-5}$ is P, then $a+b$ is equal to ____.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 3

    The midpoint M of PQ is the foot of the perpendicular on both lines, so M is their common point, and PQ is perpendicular to both directions $(2,1,1)$ and $(2,1,-5)$, i.e. parallel to $(-1,2,0)$.
    Common point: $(2s,-a+s,s)=(2b+2t,a+t,-2b-5t)$ gives $b=2a$ and $M=(2a,0,a)$.
    $\overrightarrow{MP}=(-a,2,0)$ must be parallel to $(-1,2,0)$, so $a=1$, $b=2$, and $a+b=3$.

  27. JEE Main 2026, 24 January, Shift 1 · Question 16
    Let the lines $L_1:\vec{r}=\hat{i}+2\hat{j}+3\hat{k}+\lambda\left(2\hat{i}+3\hat{j}+4\hat{k}\right), \lambda\in\mathbb{R}$ and
    $L_2:\vec{r}=\left(4\hat{i}+\hat{j}\right)+\mu\left(5\hat{i}+2\hat{j}+\hat{k}\right), \mu\in\mathbb{R}$, intersect at the point R. Let P and Q be the points lying on lines $L_1$ and $L_2$, respectively, such that $\left|\overline{PR}\right|=\sqrt{29}$ and $\left|\overline{PQ}\right|=\sqrt{\dfrac{47}{3}}$. If the point P lies in the first octant, then $27(QR)^2$ is equal to

    1. $320$
    2. $340$
    3. $348$
    4. $360$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Equating: $1+2\lambda=4+5\mu$, $2+3\lambda=1+2\mu$, $3+4\lambda=\mu$ give $\lambda=\mu=-1$, so $R=(-1,-1,-1)$.
    $|\overrightarrow{PR}|=\sqrt{29}=|(2,3,4)|$, so $P=R\pm(2,3,4)$; first octant gives $P=(1,2,3)$.

Then the square of the area of the parallelogram PQRS is equal to __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 6

    The sum of all values of $\alpha$, for which the shortest distance between the lines
    $\dfrac{x+1}{\alpha}=\dfrac{y-2}{-1}=\dfrac{z-4}{-\alpha}$ and $\dfrac{x}{\alpha}=\dfrac{y-1}{2}=\dfrac{z-1}{2\alpha}$ is $\sqrt{2}$, is

    1. $-6$
    2. $6$
    3. $-8$
    4. $8$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

  30. JEE Main 2026, 28 January, Shift 1 · Question 13
    If the distances of the point $(1, 2, a)$ from the line $\dfrac{x-1}{1}=\dfrac{y}{2}=\dfrac{z-1}{1}$ along the lines $\mathrm{L_1}:\dfrac{x-1}{3}=\dfrac{y-2}{4}=\dfrac{z-a}{b}$ and $\mathrm{L_2}:\dfrac{x-1}{1}=\dfrac{y-2}{4}=\dfrac{z-a}{c}$ are equal, then $a+b+c$ is equal to

    1. $7$
    2. $6$
    3. $5$
    4. $4$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    A point of the given line is $(1+t,2t,1+t)$.
    Along $\mathrm{L_1}$: $(1+3s,2+4s,a+bs)$. Then $t=3s$ and $2+4s=6s$, so $s=1$, $t=3$ and $a+b=4$. Distance $=\sqrt{25+b^2}$.
    Along $\mathrm{L_2}$: $(1+u,2+4u,a+cu)$. Then $t=u$ and $2+4u=2u$, so $u=-1$ and $a-c=0$. Distance $=\sqrt{17+c^2}$.
    Equal distances: $c^2-b^2=8$, i.e. $a^2-(4-a)^2=8$, so $a=3$, $b=1$, $c=3$ and $a+b+c=7$.

    If the distance of the point $\mathrm P(43, \alpha, \beta)$, $\beta<0$, from the line $\vec r=4\hat i-\hat k+\mu\left(2\hat i+3\hat k\right)$, $\mu\in\mathbf{R}$ along a line with direction ratios $3, -1, 0$ is $13\sqrt{10}$, then $\alpha^2+\beta^2$ is equal to ________
