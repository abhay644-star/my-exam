  1. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 6
    If for $3 \le r \le 30$, $\left({}^{30}C_{30-r}\right)+3\left({}^{30}C_{31-r}\right)+3\left({}^{30}C_{32-r}\right)+\left({}^{30}C_{33-r}\right)={}^{m}C_r$, then $m$ equals :

    1. $31$
    2. $32$
    3. $33$
    4. $34$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Using ${}^nC_k={}^nC_{n-k}$, the sum is ${}^{30}C_r+3\,{}^{30}C_{r-1}+3\,{}^{30}C_{r-2}+{}^{30}C_{r-3}$.
    This is the coefficient of $x^r$ in $(1+x)^{30}(1+x)^3=(1+x)^{33}$, i.e. ${}^{33}C_r$.
    So $m=33$.

  2. JEE Main 2026, 4 April, Shift 1 · Question 9
    Let the smallest value of $k\in\mathbb{N}$, for which the coefficient of $x^3$ in

    $\displaystyle (1+x)^3+(1+x)^4+(1+x)^5+\ldots+(1+x)^{99}+(1+kx)^{100},\ x\neq0,$
    is $\left(43n+\dfrac{101}{4}\right)\left({}^{100}C_3\right)$ for some $n\in\mathbb{N}$, be $p$. Then the value of $p+n$ is:

    1. $10$
    2. $11$
    3. $12$
    4. $13$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Coefficient of $x^3$: $\sum_{r=3}^{99}{}^rC_3+k^3\,{}^{100}C_3={}^{100}C_4+k^3\,{}^{100}C_3$ (hockey-stick identity).
    ${}^{100}C_4=\dfrac{97}{4}\,{}^{100}C_3$, so the coefficient is $\left(k^3+\dfrac{97}{4}\right){}^{100}C_3$.
    Equating: $k^3+\dfrac{97}{4}=43n+\dfrac{101}{4} \Rightarrow k^3=43n+1$.
    $k=1$ gives $n=0\notin\mathbb N$; $k=2,3,4,5$ give $k^3-1=7,26,63,124$, none divisible by $43$.
    $k=6$: $215=43\times5$, so $p=6$, $n=5$ and $p+n=11$.

  3. JEE Main 2026, 4 April, Shift 2 · Question 9
    In the expansion of $\left(9x-\dfrac{1}{3\sqrt x}\right)^{18}$, $x>0$, if the term independent of $x$ is $(221)k$, then $k$ is equal to:

    1. $84$
    2. $78$
    3. $168$
    4. $198$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $T_{r+1}={}^{18}C_r(9x)^{18-r}\left(-\dfrac{1}{3\sqrt x}\right)^r$; power of $x$: $18-r-\dfrac r2=0\Rightarrow r=12$.
    $T_{13}={}^{18}C_{12}\cdot\dfrac{9^6}{3^{12}}={}^{18}C_{6}=18564$.
    $18564=221\times84$, so $k=84$.

  4. JEE Main 2026, 5 April, Shift 1 · Question 23
    If the sum of the coefficients of $x^7$ and $x^{14}$ in the expansion of $\left(\dfrac{1}{x^3}-x^4\right)^n$, $x \neq 0$, is zero, then the value of $n$ is ________ .

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 21

    $T_{r+1}=\binom nr(x^{-3})^{n-r}(-x^4)^r=(-1)^r\binom nr x^{7r-3n}$.
    $x^7$: $7r-3n=7$; $x^{14}$: $7r'-3n=14$, so $r'=r+1$, and $7\mid 3n$, i.e. $n=7m$, $r=3m+1$.
    Sum zero: $(-1)^r\left[\binom nr-\binom n{r+1}\right]=0 \Rightarrow \binom nr=\binom n{r+1} \Rightarrow n=2r+1$.
    $7m=6m+3 \Rightarrow m=3$, so $n=21$.

  5. JEE Main 2026, 5 April, Shift 2 · Question 7
    The coefficient of $x^2$ in the expansion of $\left(2x^2+\dfrac1x\right)^{10}$, $x\neq0$, is :

    1. $3240$
    2. $3360$
    3. $3480$
    4. $3600$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $T_{r+1}=\binom{10}{r}(2x^2)^{10-r}x^{-r}=\binom{10}{r}2^{10-r}x^{20-3r}$.
    $20-3r=2 \Rightarrow r=6$.
    Coefficient $=\binom{10}{6}2^4=210\times16=3360$.

  6. JEE Main 2026, 6 April, Shift 1 · Question 8
    If the coefficients of the middle terms in the binomial expansions of $(1+\alpha x)^{26}$ and $(1-\alpha x)^{28}$, $\alpha\ne0$, are equal, then the value of $\alpha$ is:

    1. $1$
    2. $\dfrac{14}{13}$
    3. $\dfrac{27}{7}$
    4. $\dfrac{7}{27}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Middle term of $(1+\alpha x)^{26}$: $T_{14}$, coefficient $\binom{26}{13}\alpha^{13}$.
    Middle term of $(1-\alpha x)^{28}$: $T_{15}$, coefficient $\binom{28}{14}\alpha^{14}$.
    Equal: $\alpha=\dfrac{\binom{26}{13}}{\binom{28}{14}}=\dfrac{14\cdot14}{28\cdot27}=\dfrac{7}{27}$.

  7. JEE Main 2026, 6 April, Shift 2 · Question 22
    If $\left(1-x^3\right)^{10}=\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{10}a_rx^r(1-x)^{30-2r}$, then $\dfrac{9a_9}{a_{10}}$ is equal to __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 30

    $(1-x^3)^{10}=(1-x)^{10}(1+x+x^2)^{10}$ and $1+x+x^2=(1-x)^2+3x$.
    So $(1-x^3)^{10}=(1-x)^{10}\left((1-x)^2+3x\right)^{10}=\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{10}\binom{10}{r}3^rx^r(1-x)^{30-2r}$.
    $a_r=\binom{10}{r}3^r$: $a_9=10\cdot3^9$, $a_{10}=3^{10}$.
    $\dfrac{9a_9}{a_{10}}=\dfrac{90}{3}=30$.

  8. JEE Main 2026, 8 April, Shift 2 · Question 8
    If $26\left(\dfrac{2^3}{3}\left({}^{12}C_2\right)+\dfrac{2^5}{5}\left({}^{12}C_4\right)+\dfrac{2^7}{7}\left({}^{12}C_6\right)+\cdots+\dfrac{2^{13}}{13}\left({}^{12}C_{12}\right)\right)=3^{13}-\alpha$, then $\alpha$ is equal to :

    1. $45$
    2. $48$
    3. $51$
    4. $54$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Use $\dfrac{{}^{12}C_r}{r+1}=\dfrac{{}^{13}C_{r+1}}{13}$. The bracket is $S=\dfrac1{13}\sum_{k=1}^{6}2^{2k+1}\,{}^{13}C_{2k+1}$.
    $\sum_{j\ \text{odd}}2^j\,{}^{13}C_j=\dfrac{(1+2)^{13}-(1-2)^{13}}{2}=\dfrac{3^{13}+1}{2}$; the term $j=1$ is $2\cdot13=26$.
    So $S=\dfrac1{13}\left(\dfrac{3^{13}+1}{2}-26\right)$ and $26S=3^{13}+1-52=3^{13}-51$.
    $\alpha=51$.

  9. JEE Main 2026, 21 January, Shift 1 · Question 7
    If the coefficient of $x$ in the expansion of $(ax^2+bx+c)(1-2x)^{26}$ is $-56$ and the coefficients of $x^2$ and $x^3$ are both zero, then $a+b+c$ is equal to :

    1. $1300$
    2. $1403$
    3. $1483$
    4. $1500$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    In $(1-2x)^{26}$: coefficients of $1, x, x^2, x^3$ are $1, -52, 1300, -20800$.
    $x$: $b-52c=-56$.
    $x^2$: $a-52b+1300c=0$.
    $x^3$: $-52a+1300b-20800c=0$, i.e. $a-25b+400c=0$.
    Subtracting the last two: $-27b+900c=0 \Rightarrow b=\dfrac{100c}{3}$. Then $\dfrac{100c}{3}-52c=-56 \Rightarrow c=3$, $b=100$, $a=25b-400c=1300$.
    $a+b+c=1403$.

  10. JEE Main 2026, 21 January, Shift 2 · Question 21
    If $\left(\dfrac{1}{{}^{15}C_0}+\dfrac{1}{{}^{15}C_1}\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{{}^{15}C_1}+\dfrac{1}{{}^{15}C_2}\right)\cdots\left(\dfrac{1}{{}^{15}C_{12}}+\dfrac{1}{{}^{15}C_{13}}\right)=\dfrac{\alpha^{13}}{{}^{14}C_0\,{}^{14}C_1\cdots{}^{14}C_{12}}$, then $30\alpha$ is equal to __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 32

    $\dfrac{1}{{}^{n}C_r}+\dfrac{1}{{}^{n}C_{r+1}}=\dfrac{r!(n-r-1)!\,[(n-r)+(r+1)]}{n!}=\dfrac{n+1}{n}\cdot\dfrac{1}{{}^{n-1}C_r}$.
    With $n=15$, each factor is $\dfrac{16}{15}\cdot\dfrac{1}{{}^{14}C_r}$, for $r=0,\ldots,12$ (13 factors).
    So $\alpha=\dfrac{16}{15}$ and $30\alpha=32$.

  11. JEE Main 2026, 22 January, Shift 1 · Question 6
    The coefficient of $x^{48}$ in $(1+x)+2(1+x)^2+3(1+x)^3+\ldots+100(1+x)^{100}$ is equal to

    1. $100\cdot{}^{100}C_{49}-{}^{100}C_{50}$
    2. ${}^{100}C_{50}+{}^{101}C_{49}$
    3. $100\cdot{}^{101}C_{49}-{}^{101}C_{50}$
    4. $100\cdot{}^{100}C_{49}-{}^{100}C_{48}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Coefficient $=\sum_{k=48}^{100}k\,{}^{k}C_{48}$. Write $k\,{}^kC_{48}=(k+1)\,{}^kC_{48}-{}^kC_{48}=49\,{}^{k+1}C_{49}-{}^kC_{48}$.
    Hockey-stick sums: $\sum_{k=48}^{100}{}^{k+1}C_{49}={}^{102}C_{50}$ and $\sum_{k=48}^{100}{}^kC_{48}={}^{101}C_{49}$.
    Coefficient $=49\,{}^{102}C_{50}-{}^{101}C_{49}=49\,{}^{101}C_{50}+48\,{}^{101}C_{49}$.
    Since ${}^{101}C_{50}=\dfrac{52}{50}\,{}^{101}C_{49}$, this equals $98.96\,{}^{101}C_{49}$, and $100\,{}^{101}C_{49}-{}^{101}C_{50}=(100-1.04)\,{}^{101}C_{49}$ is the same.

  12. JEE Main 2026, 22 January, Shift 2 · Question 9
    Let $C_r$ denote the coefficient of $x^r$ in the binomial expansion of $(1+x)^n$, $n\in\mathbf{N}$, $0\le r\le n$. If $P_n=C_0-C_1+\dfrac{2^2}{3}C_2-\dfrac{2^3}{4}C_3+\ldots+\dfrac{(-2)^n}{n+1}C_n$, then the value of $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{25}\dfrac{1}{P_{2n}}$ equals.

    1. $675$
    2. $525$
    3. $650$
    4. $580$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $\sum_{r=0}^n\dfrac{C_r}{r+1}t^r=\dfrac{(1+t)^{n+1}-1}{(n+1)t}$. With $t=-2$: $P_n=\dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}-1}{-2(n+1)}$.
    For even index: $P_{2n}=\dfrac{-2}{-2(2n+1)}=\dfrac{1}{2n+1}$.
    $\sum_{n=1}^{25}(2n+1)=2\cdot325+25=675$.

  13. JEE Main 2026, 23 January, Shift 1 · Question 6
    The sum of all possible values of $n\in\mathbf{N}$, so that the coefficients of $x$, $x^2$ and $x^3$ in the expansion of $(1+x^2)^2(1+x)^n$, are in arithmetic progression is :

    1. $3$
    2. $7$
    3. $9$
    4. $12$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $(1+x^2)^2=1+2x^2+x^4$. Coefficients: $x$: $n$; $x^2$: $\binom n2+2$; $x^3$: $\binom n3+2n$.
    A.P. condition: $2\left(\binom n2+2\right)=n+\binom n3+2n$. Multiplying by 6 and simplifying: $n^3-9n^2+26n-24=0$.
    $(n-2)(n-3)(n-4)=0$, so $n=2,3,4$ and the sum is $9$.

  14. JEE Main 2026, 23 January, Shift 1 · Question 7
    The value of $\dfrac{{}^{100}C_{50}}{51}+\dfrac{{}^{100}C_{51}}{52}+\ldots\ldots+\dfrac{{}^{100}C_{100}}{101}$ is :

    1. $\dfrac{2^{100}}{100}$
    2. $\dfrac{2^{100}}{101}$
    3. $\dfrac{2^{101}}{100}$
    4. $\dfrac{2^{101}}{101}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $\dfrac{{}^{100}C_r}{r+1}=\dfrac{{}^{101}C_{r+1}}{101}$.
    Sum $=\dfrac1{101}\left({}^{101}C_{51}+{}^{101}C_{52}+\ldots+{}^{101}C_{101}\right)$. By symmetry this is half of $2^{101}$.
    Sum $=\dfrac{2^{100}}{101}$.

  15. JEE Main 2026, 24 January, Shift 1 · Question 7
    Let $S=\dfrac{1}{25!}+\dfrac{1}{3!\,23!}+\dfrac{1}{5!\,21!}+\ldots$ up to 13 terms. If $13S=\dfrac{2^k}{n!}$, $k\in\mathbb{N}$, then
    $n+k$ is equal to

    1. $49$
    2. $50$
    3. $51$
    4. $52$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    The terms are $\dfrac{1}{(2r-1)!\,(27-2r)!}$, $r=1,\ldots,13$, so $S=\dfrac{1}{26!}\left(\binom{26}{1}+\binom{26}{3}+\ldots+\binom{26}{25}\right)=\dfrac{2^{25}}{26!}$.
    $13S=\dfrac{13\cdot2^{25}}{26\cdot25!}=\dfrac{2^{24}}{25!}$, so $k=24$, $n=25$ and $n+k=49$.

  16. JEE Main 2026, 28 January, Shift 2 · Question 6
    The sum of the coefficients of $x^{499}$ and $x^{500}$ in $(1+x)^{1000}+x(1+x)^{999}+x^2(1+x)^{998}+\ldots+x^{1000}$ is :

    1. ${}^{1002}\mathrm{C}_{501}$
    2. ${}^{1002}\mathrm{C}_{500}$
    3. ${}^{1000}\mathrm{C}_{501}$
    4. ${}^{1001}\mathrm{C}_{501}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Coefficient of $x^r$ in $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{1000}x^k(1+x)^{1000-k}$ is $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{r}\binom{1000-k}{1000-r}=\sum_{n=1000-r}^{1000}\binom{n}{1000-r}=\binom{1001}{1001-r}=\binom{1001}{r}$.
    Required sum $=\binom{1001}{499}+\binom{1001}{500}=\binom{1002}{500}$.

  17. JEE Main 2026, 28 January, Shift 2 · Question 7
    Given below are two statements :
    Statement I : $25^{13}+20^{13}+8^{13}+3^{13}$ is divisible by 7.
    Statement II : The integral part of $\left(7+4\sqrt3\right)^{25}$ is an odd number.
    In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below :

    1. Both Statement I and Statement II are true
    2. Both Statement I and Statement II are false
    3. Statement I is true but Statement II is false
    4. Statement I is false but Statement II is true
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Statement I: for odd $n$, $a^n+b^n$ is divisible by $a+b$. $25^{13}+3^{13}$ is divisible by 28 and $20^{13}+8^{13}$ by 28, so the sum is divisible by 7. True.
    Statement II: let $(7+4\sqrt3)^{25}=I+f$ with $0\le f<1$ and $f'=(7-4\sqrt3)^{25}\in(0,1)$. Then $I+f+f'$ is an even integer (the irrational terms cancel and the rest doubles), and $0<f+f'<2$ forces $f+f'=1$. So $I$ is odd. True.
