  1. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 1 · Question 10
    Let an ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1$, $a<b$, pass through the point $(4,3)$ and have eccentricity $\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{3}$. Then the length of its latus rectum is :

    1. $\dfrac{4\sqrt{5}}{3}$
    2. $2\sqrt{5}$
    3. $\dfrac{7\sqrt{5}}{3}$
    4. $\dfrac{8\sqrt{5}}{3}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Since $a<b$, the major axis is along $y$: $a^2=b^2(1-e^2)=\dfrac{4b^2}{9}$.
    Through $(4,3)$: $\dfrac{16\cdot9}{4b^2}+\dfrac{9}{b^2}=\dfrac{45}{b^2}=1 \Rightarrow b^2=45,\ a^2=20$.
    Latus rectum $=\dfrac{2a^2}{b}=\dfrac{40}{3\sqrt5}=\dfrac{8\sqrt5}{3}$.

  2. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 1 · Question 24
    Let a circle $C$ have its centre in the first quadrant, intersect the coordinate axes at exactly three points and cut off equal intercepts from the coordinate axes. If the length of the chord of $C$ on the line $x+y=1$ is $\sqrt{14}$, then the square of the radius of $C$ is __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 8

    Equal intercepts give centre $(h,h)$, $h>0$; meeting the axes at exactly three points means the circle passes through the origin, so $r^2=2h^2$.
    Distance from centre to $x+y=1$: $d=\dfrac{|2h-1|}{\sqrt2}$.
    Half chord: $r^2-d^2=\dfrac{14}{4}$, so $2h^2-\dfrac{(2h-1)^2}{2}=\dfrac72 \Rightarrow 4h-1=7 \Rightarrow h=2$.
    $r^2=8$.

    Let a circle pass through the origin and its centre be the point of intersection of two mutually perpendicular lines $x+(k-1)y+3=0$ and $2x+k^2y-4=0$. If the line $x-y+2=0$ intersects the circle at the points A and B, then $(AB)^2$ is equal to :

    1. $10$
    2. $27$
    3. $18$
    4. $34$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Perpendicular: $1\cdot2+(k-1)k^2=0 \Rightarrow k^3-k^2+2=0 \Rightarrow k=-1$.
    Lines $x-2y+3=0$ and $2x+y-4=0$ meet at $(1,2)$: centre $(1,2)$, radius$^2=1+4=5$.
    Distance of the centre from $x-y+2=0$: $\dfrac{|1-2+2|}{\sqrt2}=\dfrac1{\sqrt2}$.
    $(AB)^2=4\left(5-\dfrac12\right)=18$.

    Let O be the origin, and P and Q be two points on the rectangular hyperbola $xy=12$ such that the mid point of the line segment PQ is $\left(\dfrac12, -\dfrac12\right)$. Then the area of the triangle OPQ equals :

    1. $\dfrac32$
    2. $\dfrac52$
    3. $\dfrac72$
    4. $\dfrac92$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Let $P\left(x_1,\dfrac{12}{x_1}\right)$, $Q\left(x_2,\dfrac{12}{x_2}\right)$: $x_1+x_2=1$ and $\dfrac{12}{x_1}+\dfrac{12}{x_2}=-1 \Rightarrow x_1x_2=-12$.
    Area $=\dfrac12\left|x_1\cdot\dfrac{12}{x_2}-x_2\cdot\dfrac{12}{x_1}\right|=\dfrac{6|x_1^2-x_2^2|}{|x_1x_2|}=\dfrac12|x_1-x_2||x_1+x_2|$.
    $(x_1-x_2)^2=1+48=49 \Rightarrow |x_1-x_2|=7$.
    Area $=\dfrac72$.

    2. $4$
    3. $5$

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 3

    $e^2=3-2\sqrt2$, so $e^2+2\sqrt2=3$.

    Latus rectum $=\dfrac{16}{3}$; three times it is $16$.

If d denotes the distance between A and B, and a denotes the area of $\Delta SAB$, where S is the focus of the parabola $y^2=4x$, then the value of $(a+d)$ is __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 14

    Reflection in $x+y+4=0$ sends $(x,y)$ to $(-y-4,\,-x-4)$. So $(x,y)$ is on the image when $(-y-4,-x-4)$ is on $y^2=4x$: $(x+4)^2=-4(y+4)$.
    With $y=-5$: $(x+4)^2=4$, so $A(-2,-5)$, $B(-6,-5)$ and $d=4$.
    $S=(1,0)$ is at height 5 above $AB$: $a=\dfrac12\cdot4\cdot5=10$.
    $a+d=14$.

    Let the line $x+y=1$ meet the circle $x^2+y^2=4$ at the points A and B. If the line perpendicular to AB and passing through the mid point of the chord AB intersects the circle at C and D, then the area of the quadrilateral ADBC is equal to :

    1. $\sqrt{14}$
    2. $2\sqrt{14}$
    3. $3\sqrt7$
    4. $5\sqrt7$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    The distance of the centre from $x+y=1$ is $\dfrac1{\sqrt2}$, so $AB=2\sqrt{4-\tfrac12}=\sqrt{14}$.

    The diagonals AB and CD are perpendicular, so area $=\tfrac12\cdot\sqrt{14}\cdot4=2\sqrt{14}$.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Let the ellipse $E_1:\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1$, $a>b$ and $E_2:\dfrac{x^2}{A^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{B^2}=1$, $A<B$ have same eccentricity $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$. Let the product of their lengths of latus rectums be $\dfrac{32}{\sqrt3}$, and the distance between the foci of $E_1$ be 4. If $E_1$ and $E_2$ meet at A, B, C and D, then the area of the quadrilateral ABCD equals :

    1. $\dfrac{12\sqrt6}{5}$
    2. $\dfrac{18\sqrt6}{5}$
    3. $\dfrac{24\sqrt6}{5}$
    4. $6\sqrt6$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $E_1$: $2ae=4\Rightarrow a=2\sqrt3$, $b^2=a^2(1-e^2)=8$; latus rectum $=\dfrac{2b^2}{a}=\dfrac{8}{\sqrt3}$.

  111. JEE Main 2025, 29 January, Shift 2 · Question 23
    Let $y^2=12x$ be the parabola and S be its focus. Let PQ be a focal chord of the parabola such that $(SP)(SQ)=\dfrac{147}{4}$. Let C be the circle described taking PQ as a diameter. If the equation of a circle C is $64x^2+64y^2-\alpha x-64\sqrt3y=\beta$, then $\beta-\alpha$ is equal to __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 1328

    Take $P(3t^2,6t)$ and $Q\left(\tfrac{3}{t^2},-\tfrac6t\right)$. $SP\cdot SQ=9\left(t+\tfrac1t\right)^2=\tfrac{147}{4}$, so $t^2+\tfrac1{t^2}=\tfrac{25}{12}$ and $\left(t-\tfrac1t\right)^2=\tfrac1{12}$.

    $\times64$: $64x^2+64y^2-400x-64\sqrt3y=1728$, so $\beta-\alpha=1728-400=1328$.
