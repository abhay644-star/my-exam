  1. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 1 · Question 10
    Let an ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1$, $a<b$, pass through the point $(4,3)$ and have eccentricity $\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{3}$. Then the length of its latus rectum is :

    1. $\dfrac{4\sqrt{5}}{3}$
    2. $2\sqrt{5}$
    3. $\dfrac{7\sqrt{5}}{3}$
    4. $\dfrac{8\sqrt{5}}{3}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Since $a<b$, the major axis is along $y$: $a^2=b^2(1-e^2)=\dfrac{4b^2}{9}$.
    Through $(4,3)$: $\dfrac{16\cdot9}{4b^2}+\dfrac{9}{b^2}=\dfrac{45}{b^2}=1 \Rightarrow b^2=45,\ a^2=20$.
    Latus rectum $=\dfrac{2a^2}{b}=\dfrac{40}{3\sqrt5}=\dfrac{8\sqrt5}{3}$.

  2. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 1 · Question 24
    Let a circle $C$ have its centre in the first quadrant, intersect the coordinate axes at exactly three points and cut off equal intercepts from the coordinate axes. If the length of the chord of $C$ on the line $x+y=1$ is $\sqrt{14}$, then the square of the radius of $C$ is __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 8

    Equal intercepts give centre $(h,h)$, $h>0$; meeting the axes at exactly three points means the circle passes through the origin, so $r^2=2h^2$.
    Distance from centre to $x+y=1$: $d=\dfrac{|2h-1|}{\sqrt2}$.
    Half chord: $r^2-d^2=\dfrac{14}{4}$, so $2h^2-\dfrac{(2h-1)^2}{2}=\dfrac72 \Rightarrow 4h-1=7 \Rightarrow h=2$.
    $r^2=8$.

  3. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 10
    Let a circle pass through the origin and its centre be the point of intersection of two mutually perpendicular lines $x+(k-1)y+3=0$ and $2x+k^2y-4=0$. If the line $x-y+2=0$ intersects the circle at the points A and B, then $(AB)^2$ is equal to :

    1. $10$
    2. $27$
    3. $18$
    4. $34$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Perpendicular: $1\cdot2+(k-1)k^2=0 \Rightarrow k^3-k^2+2=0 \Rightarrow k=-1$.
    Lines $x-2y+3=0$ and $2x+y-4=0$ meet at $(1,2)$: centre $(1,2)$, radius$^2=1+4=5$.
    Distance of the centre from $x-y+2=0$: $\dfrac{|1-2+2|}{\sqrt2}=\dfrac1{\sqrt2}$.
    $(AB)^2=4\left(5-\dfrac12\right)=18$.

  4. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 11
    Let O be the origin, and P and Q be two points on the rectangular hyperbola $xy=12$ such that the mid point of the line segment PQ is $\left(\dfrac12, -\dfrac12\right)$. Then the area of the triangle OPQ equals :

    1. $\dfrac32$
    2. $\dfrac52$
    3. $\dfrac72$
    4. $\dfrac92$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Let $P\left(x_1,\dfrac{12}{x_1}\right)$, $Q\left(x_2,\dfrac{12}{x_2}\right)$: $x_1+x_2=1$ and $\dfrac{12}{x_1}+\dfrac{12}{x_2}=-1 \Rightarrow x_1x_2=-12$.
    Area $=\dfrac12\left|x_1\cdot\dfrac{12}{x_2}-x_2\cdot\dfrac{12}{x_1}\right|=\dfrac{6|x_1^2-x_2^2|}{|x_1x_2|}=\dfrac12|x_1-x_2||x_1+x_2|$.
    $(x_1-x_2)^2=1+48=49 \Rightarrow |x_1-x_2|=7$.
    Area $=\dfrac72$.

  5. JEE Main 2026, 2 April, Shift 2 · Question 12
    Let the parabola $y=x^2+px+q$ passing through the point $(1, -1)$ be such that the distance between its vertex and the $x$-axis is minimum. Then the value of $p^2+q^2$ is :

    1. $2$
    2. $4$
    3. $5$
    4. $8$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $1+p+q=-1 \Rightarrow q=-2-p$.
    Vertex ordinate $=q-\dfrac{p^2}{4}=-\left(\dfrac{p^2}{4}+p+2\right)=-\left[\left(\dfrac p2+1\right)^2+1\right]$.
    Its distance from the $x$-axis is least ($=1$) when $p=-2$, so $q=0$.
    $p^2+q^2=4$.

    Let A be the point $(3, 0)$ and circles with variable diameter AB touch the circle $x^2+y^2=36$ internally. Let the curve C be the locus of the point B. If the eccentricity of C is $e$, then $72e^2$ is equal to __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 18

    Let M be the midpoint of AB (centre of the variable circle). Internal touching: $OM=6-\dfrac{AB}{2}$.
    With $A'=(-3,0)$, $2\,OM=|\overrightarrow{OA}+\overrightarrow{OB}|=BA'$, so $BA'+BA=12$.
    Locus of B: ellipse with foci $(\pm3,0)$, $2a=12$: $a=6$, $c=3$, $e=\dfrac12$.
    $72e^2=18$.

    Suppose that two chords, drawn from the point $(1,2)$ on the circle $x^2+y^2+x-3y=0$ are bisected by the $y$-axis. If the other ends of these chords are $R$ and $S$, and the mid point of the line segment $RS$ is $(\alpha,\beta)$, then $6(\alpha+\beta)$ is equal to:

    1. $1$
    2. $3$
    3. $4$
    4. $6$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Let a chord's mid-point be $(0,k)$; its other end is $(-1,\ 2k-2)$.
    This point lies on the circle: $1+(2k-2)^2-1-3(2k-2)=0 \Rightarrow (2k-2)(2k-2-3)=0$.
    So $2k-2=0$ or $3$: $R=(-1,0)$, $S=(-1,3)$.
    Mid-point of $RS$: $\left(-1,\dfrac32\right)$, so $6(\alpha+\beta)=6\cdot\dfrac12=3$.

  8. JEE Main 2026, 4 April, Shift 1 · Question 22
    Consider the parabola $P: y^2=4kx$ and the ellipse $E: \dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1$. Let the line segment joining the points of intersection of $P$ and $E$, be their latus rectums. If the eccentricity of $E$ is $e$, then $e^2+2\sqrt2$ is equal to ______.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 3

    The common latus rectum is $x=k$ for $P$ (ends $(k,\pm2k)$) and $x=ae$ for $E$ (ends $\left(ae,\pm\dfrac{b^2}{a}\right)$).
    So $ae=k$ and $\dfrac{b^2}{a}=2k=2ae \Rightarrow b^2=2a^2e$.
    $b^2=a^2(1-e^2) \Rightarrow 1-e^2=2e \Rightarrow e^2+2e-1=0 \Rightarrow e=\sqrt2-1$.
    $e^2=3-2\sqrt2$, so $e^2+2\sqrt2=3$.

    Let $P(3\cos\alpha,2\sin\alpha)$, $\alpha\neq0$, be a point on the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{9}+\dfrac{y^2}{4}=1$, Q be a point on the circle $x^2+y^2-14x-14y+82=0$ and R be a point on the line $x+y=5$ such that the centroid of the triangle PQR is $\left(2+\cos\alpha,\ 3+\dfrac23\sin\alpha\right)$. Then the sum of the ordinates of all possible points R is:

    1. $6$
    2. $2$
    3. $4$
    4. $8$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Centroid: $3\cos\alpha+x_Q+x_R=6+3\cos\alpha\Rightarrow x_Q+x_R=6$; $2\sin\alpha+y_Q+y_R=9+2\sin\alpha\Rightarrow y_Q+y_R=9$.

    $Q=(3,7)$ or $(7,3)$, giving $R=(3,2)$ or $(-1,6)$.
    Sum of ordinates $=2+6=8$.

    Let $H:\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}-\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1$ be a hyperbola such that the distance between its foci is 6 and the distance between its directrices is $\dfrac83$. If the line $x=\alpha$ intersects the hyperbola H at the points A and B such that the area of the triangle AOB is $4\sqrt{15}$, where O is the origin, then $\alpha^2$ equals

    1. $12$
    2. $16$
    3. $24$
    4. $25$
    Show answer and solution

  11. JEE Main 2026, 4 April, Shift 2 · Question 24
    Let A, B and C be the vertices of a variable right angled triangle inscribed in the parabola $y^2=16x$. Let the vertex B containing the right angle be $(4,8)$ and the locus of the centroid of $\triangle ABC$ be a conic $C_0$. Then three times the length of latus rectum of $C_0$ is __________

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 16

    Points of $y^2=16x$: $(4t^2,8t)$; $B$ has $t=1$. Let $A$, $C$ have $t_1,t_2$.
    Slopes $BA=\dfrac{2}{t_1+1}$, $BC=\dfrac{2}{t_2+1}$; perpendicular $\Rightarrow(t_1+1)(t_2+1)=-4$, i.e. $t_1t_2=-5-s$ where $s=t_1+t_2$.
    Centroid: $3k=8+8s$, $3h=4+4(t_1^2+t_2^2)=4+4(s^2+2s+10)=4(s+1)^2+40$.
    With $s+1=\dfrac{3k}{8}$: $3h=\dfrac{9k^2}{16}+40\Rightarrow k^2=\dfrac{16}{3}\left(h-\dfrac{40}{3}\right)$.
    Latus rectum $=\dfrac{16}{3}$; three times it is $16$.

    Then $l_1=2\sqrt2$, $l_2=\sqrt6$, $e_1e_2=\sqrt{3/2}\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, and the value is $\dfrac{4\sqrt3}{\sqrt3/2}\cdot1=8$.

  53. JEE Main 2026, 28 January, Shift 2 · Question 9
    An ellipse has its center at $(1, -2)$, one focus at $(3, -2)$ and one vertex at $(5, -2)$. Then the length of its latus rectum is :

    1. $6\sqrt3$
    2. $\dfrac{16}{\sqrt3}$
    3. $4\sqrt3$
    4. $6$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    $c=2$ and $a=4$, so $b^2=a^2-c^2=12$.
    Latus rectum $=\dfrac{2b^2}{a}=\dfrac{24}{4}=6$.

  54. JEE Main 2026, 28 January, Shift 2 · Question 10
    Let the circle $x^2+y^2=4$ intersect $x$-axis at the points $\mathrm A(a, 0)$, $a>0$ and $\mathrm B(b, 0)$. Let $\mathrm P(2\cos\alpha, 2\sin\alpha)$, $0<\alpha<\dfrac\pi2$ and $\mathrm Q(2\cos\beta, 2\sin\beta)$ be two points such that $(\alpha-\beta)=\dfrac\pi2$. Then the point of intersection of AQ and BP lies on :

    1. $x^2+y^2-4x-4=0$
    2. $x^2+y^2-4y-4=0$
    3. $x^2+y^2-4x-4y-4=0$
    4. $x^2+y^2-4x-4y=0$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    A $=(2,0)$, B $=(-2,0)$. The lines AQ and BP meet at a point R outside the circle, and the angle between two secants is half the difference of the intercepted arcs: arc BQ $=90^\circ+\alpha$ and arc AP $=\alpha$, so $\angle$ARB $=\tfrac12(90^\circ)=45^\circ$.
    So R lies on a circle through A and B on which AB subtends $45^\circ$: its centre is on the y-axis with $\angle$AOB at the centre $=90^\circ$, i.e. centre $(0,2)$ and radius $2\sqrt2$: $x^2+(y-2)^2=8$, i.e. $x^2+y^2-4y-4=0$.
    Check with $\alpha=\pi/4$: R $=(2\sqrt2,2)$ satisfies it.

  55. JEE Main 2026, 28 January, Shift 2 · Question 12
    Let the ellipse $\mathrm{E}:\dfrac{x^2}{144}+\dfrac{y^2}{169}=1$ and the hyperbola $\mathrm{H}:\dfrac{x^2}{16}-\dfrac{y^2}{\lambda^2}=-1$ have the same foci. If e and L respectively denote the eccentricity and the length of the latus rectum of H, then the value of $24(\mathrm e+\mathrm L)$ is :

    1. $67$
    2. $126$
    3. $148$
    4. $296$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    E has foci $(0,\pm5)$ since $169-144=25$.
    H is $\dfrac{y^2}{\lambda^2}-\dfrac{x^2}{16}=1$ with foci $(0,\pm\sqrt{\lambda^2+16})$, so $\lambda^2=9$.
    $e=\dfrac53$ and $L=\dfrac{2\cdot16}{3}=\dfrac{32}{3}$, so $24(e+L)=24\cdot\dfrac{37}{3}=296$.

  56. JEE Main 2026, 28 January, Shift 2 · Question 13
    Let A be the focus of the parabola $y^2=8x$. Let the line $y=mx+c$ intersect the parabola at two distinct points B and C. If the centroid of the triangle ABC is $\left(\dfrac73,\dfrac43\right)$, then $(\mathrm{BC})^2$ is equal to :

    1. $32$
    2. $41$
    3. $80$
    4. $89$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    A $=(2,0)$, so B + C $=(7-2,\ 4)=(5,4)$. Take B, C as $(2t^2,4t)$: $t_1+t_2=1$ and $2(t_1^2+t_2^2)=5$, so $t_1t_2=-\tfrac34$ and $(t_1-t_2)^2=1+3=4$.
    $\mathrm{BC}^2=4(t_1^2-t_2^2)^2+16(t_1-t_2)^2=(t_1-t_2)^2\left[4(t_1+t_2)^2+16\right]=4\times20=80$.
