A few questions from this chapter. Open each one to see the answer.

  1. The structural and functional unit of the kidney is the ______.

    1. alveolus
    2. glomerulus
    3. neuron
    4. nephron
    Show answer

    Answer: nephron

    Each human kidney has nearly one million nephrons, the functional units of the kidney.

  2. What is the glomerular filtration rate (GFR) in a healthy human, in ml per minute?

    1. 250 ml/min
    2. 180 ml/min
    3. 90 ml/min
    4. 125 ml/min
    Show answer

    Answer: 125 ml/min

    On average about 125 ml of filtrate is formed per minute by the kidneys, i.e. about 180 litres per day.

  3. About how many litres of glomerular filtrate are formed in human kidneys per day?

    1. 180 L
    2. 125 L
    3. 250 L
    4. 1.5 L
    Show answer

    Answer: 180 L

    125 ml/min × 1440 min ≈ 180 litres per day; only about 1.5 litres is excreted as urine, so nearly 99% is reabsorbed.

  4. The counter-current mechanism produces an osmolarity gradient from 300 mOsmolL⁻¹ in the cortex to about how many mOsmolL⁻¹ in the inner medulla?

    1. 600 mOsmol/L
    2. 1200 mOsmol/L
    3. 300 mOsmol/L
    4. 2400 mOsmol/L
    Show answer

    Answer: 1200 mOsmol/L

    The gradient rises from 300 mOsmolL⁻¹ in the cortex to about 1200 mOsmolL⁻¹ in the inner medulla, allowing concentrated urine.

  5. Reptiles, birds, land snails and insects excrete nitrogenous wastes mainly as ______.

    1. creatinine
    2. urea
    3. uric acid
    4. ammonia
    Show answer

    Answer: uric acid

    These animals are uricotelic, excreting uric acid as pellets or paste with minimum loss of water.

  6. The juxtaglomerular apparatus is formed by cellular modifications in the distal convoluted tubule and the ______ at their point of contact.

    1. collecting duct
    2. vasa recta
    3. afferent arteriole
    4. efferent arteriole
    Show answer

    Answer: afferent arteriole

    JGA is a sensitive region where the DCT contacts the afferent arteriole; it regulates GFR by releasing renin.

  7. Besides NaCl, which substance, partly re-entering the thin ascending limb from the collecting tubule, helps maintain the concentration gradient in the medullary interstitium?

    1. ammonia
    2. uric acid
    3. urea
    4. glucose
    Show answer

    Answer: urea

    Small amounts of urea enter the thin segment of the ascending limb and are transported back to the interstitium by the collecting tubule, helping maintain the gradient.

  8. An increase in blood flow to the atria of the heart causes release of ______, which causes vasodilation and acts as a check on the renin–angiotensin mechanism.

    1. renin
    2. angiotensin II
    3. ANF
    4. aldosterone
    Show answer

    Answer: ANF

    Atrial natriuretic factor decreases blood pressure by vasodilation, opposing the renin–angiotensin system.
