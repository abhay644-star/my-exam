A few questions from this chapter. Open each one to see the answer.

  1. The commonest simple permanent tissue in plants, made of living cells with thin cellulose walls, is ______.

    1. parenchyma
    2. collenchyma
    3. xylem
    4. sclerenchyma
    Show answer

    Answer: parenchyma

    Parenchyma forms the major part of plant organs and does functions such as photosynthesis, storage and secretion.

  2. The meristem found at the base of leaves or internodes in grasses, which regenerates parts removed by grazing herbivores, is the ______ meristem.

    1. apical
    2. intercalary
    3. secondary
    4. lateral
    Show answer

    Answer: intercalary

    Apical and intercalary meristems are primary meristems: they appear early in life and contribute to the formation of the primary plant body.

  3. Of the four elements of xylem (tracheids, vessels, xylem fibres and xylem parenchyma), which is the only one made of living cells?

    1. tracheids
    2. xylem parenchyma
    3. vessels
    4. xylem fibres
    Show answer

    Answer: xylem parenchyma

    Xylem parenchyma cells store food materials such as starch or fat, and substances like tannins; radial conduction of water is done by ray parenchyma cells.

  4. In old trees, the dark-coloured central wood, which is highly lignified, does not conduct water and gives mechanical support, is called heartwood. What is its other name?

    1. alburnum
    2. duramen
    3. phelloderm
    4. phellem
    Show answer

    Answer: duramen

    The peripheral, lighter-coloured wood that conducts water and minerals from root to leaf is the sapwood or alburnum.

  5. The outer surface of the epidermis of stems and leaves is covered by a waxy layer, which prevents loss of water. What is it called?

    1. pericycle
    2. cuticle
    3. cork
    4. bark
    Show answer

    Answer: cuticle

    The cuticle is absent in roots, which take in water from the soil.

  6. Which simple permanent tissue has cells thickened at the corners by deposits of cellulose, hemicellulose and pectin, and gives mechanical support to growing parts such as young stems?

    1. sclerenchyma
    2. phloem
    3. parenchyma
    4. collenchyma
    Show answer

    Answer: collenchyma

    Collenchyma occurs in layers below the epidermis in dicotyledonous plants; its cells often contain chloroplasts.

  7. In stems, the protoxylem lies towards the centre (pith) and the metaxylem towards the periphery. What is this arrangement of primary xylem called?

    1. polyarch
    2. endarch
    3. mesarch
    4. exarch
    Show answer

    Answer: endarch

    In roots the arrangement is the opposite: the protoxylem lies towards the periphery and the metaxylem towards the centre, which is called exarch.

  8. A monocot root usually has more than six xylem bundles. What is this condition called?

    1. diarch
    2. tetrarch
    3. monarch
    4. polyarch
    Show answer

    Answer: polyarch

    A dicot root usually has two to four xylem bundles (diarch to tetrarch); monocot roots have more than six (polyarch), and they do not undergo secondary growth.
