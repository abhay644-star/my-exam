A few questions from this chapter. Open each one to see the answer.

  1. The simplest alkyne is ______.

    1. ethyne
    2. methane
    3. ethene
    4. propyne
    Show answer

    Answer: ethyne

    Ethyne (HC≡CH), commonly called acetylene, has the C≡C triple bond with just two carbons.

  2. Addition of HBr to propene giving 2-bromopropane as the major product follows ______ rule.

    1. Saytzeff
    2. Huckel
    3. anti-Markovnikov
    4. Markovnikov
    Show answer

    Answer: Markovnikov

    H adds to the carbon with more H atoms via the more stable secondary carbocation, so Br goes to the middle carbon.

  3. Ozonolysis of but-2-ene (CH3CH=CHCH3) followed by Zn/H2O gives two molecules of ______.

    1. methanal
    2. ethanoic acid
    3. propanone
    4. ethanal
    Show answer

    Answer: ethanal

    The C=C bond is cleaved in the middle, each half becoming CH3CHO (ethanal).

  4. How many structural (chain) isomers are possible for the alkane C5H12?

    1. 4
    2. 5
    3. 3
    4. 2
    Show answer

    Answer: 3

    They are n-pentane, 2-methylbutane (isopentane) and 2,2-dimethylpropane (neopentane).

  5. Alkanes are called ______ hydrocarbons because they contain only single bonds.

    1. unsaturated
    2. cyclic
    3. aromatic
    4. saturated
    Show answer

    Answer: saturated

    Every carbon atom in an alkane is joined to four other atoms by single bonds.

  6. How many π electrons are present in a benzene ring?

    1. 4
    2. 3
    3. 6
    4. 12
    Show answer

    Answer: 6

    Benzene has three C=C double bonds, i.e. 6 delocalised π electrons, satisfying Huckel's (4n + 2) rule with n = 1.

  7. What is the hybridisation of each carbon atom in benzene?

    1. sp
    2. sp3
    3. sp3d
    4. sp2
    Show answer

    Answer: sp2

    Each carbon forms three σ bonds in a plane; the unhybridised p orbitals form the π electron cloud.

  8. What is the general formula of alkenes?

    1. CnHn
    2. CnH2n+2
    3. CnH2n-2
    4. CnH2n
    Show answer

    Answer: CnH2n

    Ethene is C2H4 and propene is C3H6.
