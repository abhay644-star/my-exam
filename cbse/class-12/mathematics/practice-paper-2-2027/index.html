General instructions

  1. This question paper contains 38 questions. All questions are compulsory.
  2. This question paper is divided into five Sections - A, B, C, D and E.
  3. In Section A, Questions no. 1 to 18 are multiple choice questions (MCQs) and Questions no. 19 and 20 are Assertion-Reason based questions of 1 mark each.
  4. In Section B, Questions no. 21 to 25 are Very Short Answer (VSA) type questions, carrying 2 marks each.
  5. In Section C, Questions no. 26 to 31 are Short Answer (SA) type questions, carrying 3 marks each.
  6. In Section D, Questions no. 32 to 35 are Long Answer (LA) type questions, carrying 5 marks each.
  7. In Section E, Questions no. 36 to 38 are case study based questions carrying 4 marks each. The first two case study questions have three sub-parts (i), (ii), (iii) of 1, 1 and 2 marks; the third has two sub-parts of 2 marks each.

Section A: 1 mark each

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Relations and Functions
    Let $R=\{(1,1),(2,2),(3,3),(1,2),(2,3)\}$ be a relation on the set $\{1,2,3\}$. Then $R$ is

    1. reflexive, but neither symmetric nor transitive
    2. reflexive and transitive, but not symmetric
    3. an equivalence relation
    4. symmetric and transitive, but not reflexive
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) reflexive, but neither symmetric nor transitive

    $(1,1),(2,2),(3,3)\in R$, so $R$ is reflexive. $(1,2)\in R$ but $(2,1)\notin R$, so not symmetric. $(1,2),(2,3)\in R$ but $(1,3)\notin R$, so not transitive.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Inverse Trigonometric Functions
    The principal value of $\cos^{-1}\left(\cos\frac{4\pi}{3}\right)$ is

    1. $\frac{4\pi}{3}$
    2. $\frac{2\pi}{3}$
    3. $\frac{\pi}{3}$
    4. $-\frac{\pi}{3}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) $\frac{2\pi}{3}$

    $\cos\frac{4\pi}{3}=-\frac{1}{2}$, and the principal value of $\cos^{-1}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)$ in $[0,\pi]$ is $\frac{2\pi}{3}$. ($\frac{4\pi}{3}$ is not in $[0,\pi]$.)

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Inverse Trigonometric Functions
    The range of the function $f(x)=2\sin^{-1}x+\frac{\pi}{2}$, $x\in[-1,1]$, is

    1. $\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3\pi}{2}\right]$
    2. $[0, \pi]$
    3. $[-\pi, \pi]$
    4. $\left[\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3\pi}{2}\right]$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3\pi}{2}\right]$

    $-\frac{\pi}{2}\le\sin^{-1}x\le\frac{\pi}{2} \Rightarrow -\pi\le 2\sin^{-1}x\le\pi \Rightarrow -\frac{\pi}{2}\le f(x)\le\frac{3\pi}{2}$.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Matrices
    If $A=\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2\\ 2 & 1\end{bmatrix}$ and $A^{2}-2A=kI$, then the value of $k$ is

    1. $3$
    2. $-3$
    3. $5$
    4. $1$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $3$

    $A^{2}=\begin{bmatrix}5 & 4\\ 4 & 5\end{bmatrix}$, so $A^{2}-2A=\begin{bmatrix}3 & 0\\ 0 & 3\end{bmatrix}=3I$. Hence $k=3$.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Determinants
    If $\begin{vmatrix}x & 2\\ 3 & x\end{vmatrix}=\begin{vmatrix}4 & 1\\ 2 & 3\end{vmatrix}$, then $x$ is equal to

    1. $\pm 4$
    2. $4$
    3. $\pm 2$
    4. $16$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\pm 4$

    $x^{2}-6=12-2=10 \Rightarrow x^{2}=16 \Rightarrow x=\pm 4$.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Continuity and Differentiability
    The derivative of $\tan^{-1}(e^{x})$ with respect to $x$ is

    1. $\frac{e^{x}}{1+e^{2x}}$
    2. $\frac{1}{1+e^{2x}}$
    3. $\frac{e^{x}}{1+e^{x}}$
    4. $\frac{e^{x}}{\sqrt{1-e^{2x}}}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\frac{e^{x}}{1+e^{2x}}$

    By the chain rule, $\frac{d}{dx}\tan^{-1}(e^{x})=\frac{1}{1+(e^{x})^{2}}\cdot e^{x}=\frac{e^{x}}{1+e^{2x}}$.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Continuity and Differentiability
    The function $f(x)=|x-3|$, $x\in\mathbb{R}$, is

    1. continuous everywhere but not differentiable at $x=3$
    2. differentiable everywhere
    3. not continuous at $x=3$
    4. neither continuous nor differentiable at $x=3$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) continuous everywhere but not differentiable at $x=3$

    $|x-3|$ is continuous everywhere. At $x=3$, the left-hand derivative is $-1$ and the right-hand derivative is $1$, so it is not differentiable there.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Application of Derivatives
    The minimum value of $f(x)=x^{2}+\frac{16}{x^{2}}$, $x\neq 0$, is

    1. $8$
    2. $4$
    3. $16$
    4. $0$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $8$

    $f'(x)=2x-\frac{32}{x^{3}}=0 \Rightarrow x^{4}=16 \Rightarrow x=\pm 2$; $f''(x)=2+\frac{96}{x^{4}}\gt 0$. Minimum value $=4+4=8$.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Integrals
    $\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{9-4x^{2}}}$ is equal to

    1. $\frac{1}{2}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{2x}{3}\right)+C$
    2. $\frac{1}{3}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{2x}{3}\right)+C$
    3. $\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{2x}{3}\right)+C$
    4. $\frac{1}{2}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{3x}{2}\right)+C$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\frac{1}{2}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{2x}{3}\right)+C$

    Put $2x=u$, $dx=\frac{du}{2}$: $\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{du}{\sqrt{3^{2}-u^{2}}}=\frac{1}{2}\sin^{-1}\frac{u}{3}+C=\frac{1}{2}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{2x}{3}\right)+C$.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Integrals
    The value of $\int_{0}^{2}|x-1|\,dx$ is

    1. $0$
    2. $1$
    3. $2$
    4. $\frac{1}{2}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) $1$

    $\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)dx+\int_{1}^{2}(x-1)dx=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=1$.

  11. Question 11 · 1 mark · Differential Equations
    The integrating factor of the differential equation $x\frac{dy}{dx}+2y=x^{3}$, $x\gt 0$, is

    1. $x^{2}$
    2. $2x$
    3. $e^{x^{2}}$
    4. $\log x$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $x^{2}$

    Dividing by $x$: $\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac{2}{x}y=x^{2}$. I.F. $=e^{\int\frac{2}{x}dx}=e^{2\log x}=x^{2}$.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Application of Integrals
    The area of the region bounded by the line $y=2x$, the $x$-axis and the line $x=3$ is

    1. $9$ sq. units
    2. $6$ sq. units
    3. $18$ sq. units
    4. $3$ sq. units
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $9$ sq. units

    Area $=\int_{0}^{3}2x\,dx=\left[x^{2}\right]_{0}^{3}=9$ sq. units.

  13. Question 13 · 1 mark · Vector Algebra
    If the vectors $\hat{i}+2\hat{j}-\hat{k}$ and $3\hat{i}+\lambda\hat{j}+\hat{k}$ are perpendicular, then the value of $\lambda$ is

    1. $-1$
    2. $1$
    3. $-2$
    4. $2$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $-1$

    Dot product $=3+2\lambda-1=0 \Rightarrow \lambda=-1$.

  14. Question 14 · 1 mark · Vector Algebra
    The position vectors of points $P$ and $Q$ are $3\vec{a}-2\vec{b}$ and $\vec{a}+\vec{b}$ respectively. The position vector of the point $R$ which divides $PQ$ internally in the ratio $2:1$ is

    1. $\frac{5}{3}\vec{a}$
    2. $\frac{7\vec{a}-3\vec{b}}{3}$
    3. $\frac{4\vec{a}-\vec{b}}{2}$
    4. $-\vec{a}+4\vec{b}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\frac{5}{3}\vec{a}$

    $\vec{r}=\frac{2(\vec{a}+\vec{b})+1(3\vec{a}-2\vec{b})}{2+1}=\frac{5\vec{a}}{3}$.

  15. Question 15 · 1 mark · Three Dimensional Geometry
    A line makes angles of $90^{\circ}$ and $60^{\circ}$ with the positive directions of the $x$-axis and $y$-axis respectively. The angle it makes with the positive direction of the $z$-axis is

    1. $30^{\circ}$ or $150^{\circ}$
    2. $45^{\circ}$
    3. $60^{\circ}$
    4. $90^{\circ}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $30^{\circ}$ or $150^{\circ}$

    $\cos^{2}90^{\circ}+\cos^{2}60^{\circ}+\cos^{2}\gamma=1 \Rightarrow \cos^{2}\gamma=\frac{3}{4} \Rightarrow \cos\gamma=\pm\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, so $\gamma=30^{\circ}$ or $150^{\circ}$.

  16. Question 16 · 1 mark · Linear Programming
    The corner points of the bounded feasible region of a linear programming problem are $(0,0)$, $(6,0)$, $(4,3)$ and $(0,5)$. The maximum value of $Z=4x+3y$ is

    1. $25$
    2. $24$
    3. $15$
    4. $28$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $25$

    $Z(0,0)=0$, $Z(6,0)=24$, $Z(4,3)=25$, $Z(0,5)=15$. Maximum $=25$.

  17. Question 17 · 1 mark · Linear Programming
    If the feasible region of a linear programming problem is unbounded, then the objective function

    1. may or may not have an optimal value
    2. always has a maximum value
    3. always has a minimum value
    4. never has a maximum or a minimum value
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) may or may not have an optimal value

    For an unbounded feasible region, a maximum or minimum may or may not exist; it has to be checked using the open half-plane test.

  18. Question 18 · 1 mark · Probability
    If $A$ and $B$ are independent events with $P(A)=0.5$ and $P(B)=0.4$, then $P(A\cup B)$ is

    1. $0.7$
    2. $0.9$
    3. $0.2$
    4. $0.3$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $0.7$

    $P(A\cap B)=0.5\times 0.4=0.2$; $P(A\cup B)=0.5+0.4-0.2=0.7$.

  19. Question 19 · 1 mark · Application of Derivatives
    Assertion (A): The function $f(x)=e^{x}$ is strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$.
    Reason (R): If $f'(x)\gt 0$ for every $x$ in an interval, then $f$ is strictly increasing on that interval.
    Select the correct option.

    1. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    2. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    3. Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.
    4. Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

    $f'(x)=e^{x}\gt 0$ for all real $x$, so by the stated result $f$ is strictly increasing. Both are true and R explains A.

  20. Question 20 · 1 mark · Probability
    Assertion (A): If $P(A)=0.3$, $P(B)=0.4$ and $A$, $B$ are mutually exclusive events, then $A$ and $B$ are independent.
    Reason (R): Two events $A$ and $B$ are independent if and only if $P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)$.
    Select the correct option.

    1. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    2. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    3. Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.
    4. Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.

    Mutually exclusive means $P(A\cap B)=0$, but $P(A)P(B)=0.12\neq 0$, so $A$ and $B$ are not independent. The Assertion is false; the Reason is true.

Section B: 2 marks each

  1. Question 21 · 2 marks · Integrals
    Find $\displaystyle\int\frac{dx}{x^{2}+6x+13}$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $x^{2}+6x+13=(x+3)^{2}+2^{2}$
    $\int\frac{dx}{(x+3)^{2}+2^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x+3}{2}\right)+C$

    Marking scheme:

    • Writing the denominator as $(x+3)^{2}+2^{2}$ (1 mark)
    • Correct answer $\frac{1}{2}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x+3}{2}\right)+C$ (1 mark)

  2. Question 22 · 2 marks · Continuity and Differentiability
    If $x^{2}y+y^{3}=10$, find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ at the point $(1, 2)$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Differentiating with respect to $x$: $2xy+x^{2}\frac{dy}{dx}+3y^{2}\frac{dy}{dx}=0$
    $\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{2xy}{x^{2}+3y^{2}}$
    At $(1,2)$: $\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{4}{1+12}=-\frac{4}{13}$

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct implicit differentiation: $2xy+x^{2}y'+3y^{2}y'=0$ (1 mark)
    • $\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{4}{13}$ at $(1,2)$ (1 mark)

  3. Question 23 · 2 marks · Vector Algebra
    Find a unit vector perpendicular to both the vectors $\vec{a}=2\hat{i}-2\hat{j}+\hat{k}$ and $\vec{b}=\hat{i}+2\hat{j}+2\hat{k}$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $\vec{a}\times\vec{b}=\begin{vmatrix}\hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k}\\ 2 & -2 & 1\\ 1 & 2 & 2\end{vmatrix}=-6\hat{i}-3\hat{j}+6\hat{k}$
    $|\vec{a}\times\vec{b}|=\sqrt{36+9+36}=9$
    Required unit vector $=\pm\frac{1}{3}(-2\hat{i}-\hat{j}+2\hat{k})$

    Marking scheme:

    • $\vec{a}\times\vec{b}=-6\hat{i}-3\hat{j}+6\hat{k}$ (1 mark)
    • Unit vector $\pm\frac{1}{3}(-2\hat{i}-\hat{j}+2\hat{k})$ (1 mark)

  4. Question 24 · 2 marks · Three Dimensional Geometry
    Find the angle between the lines $\frac{x-1}{2}=\frac{y+2}{2}=\frac{z-3}{1}$ and $\frac{x+1}{4}=\frac{y}{1}=\frac{z-2}{8}$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Direction ratios: $2, 2, 1$ and $4, 1, 8$.
    $\cos\theta=\frac{|2(4)+2(1)+1(8)|}{\sqrt{4+4+1}\sqrt{16+1+64}}=\frac{18}{3\times 9}=\frac{2}{3}$
    $\theta=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)$

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct formula with direction ratios $2,2,1$ and $4,1,8$ (1 mark)
    • $\theta=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)$ (1 mark)

  5. Question 25 · 2 marks · Probability
    For two events $A$ and $B$, $P(A)=0.6$, $P(B|A)=0.5$ and $P(A\cup B)=0.8$. Find $P(B)$ and $P(A|B)$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B|A)=0.6\times 0.5=0.3$
    $P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B) \Rightarrow 0.8=0.6+P(B)-0.3 \Rightarrow P(B)=0.5$
    $P(A|B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}=\frac{0.3}{0.5}=0.6$

    Marking scheme:

    • $P(A\cap B)=0.3$ (½ marks)
    • $P(B)=0.5$ (½ marks)
    • $P(A|B)=0.6$ (1 mark)

Section C: 3 marks each

  1. Question 26 · 3 marks · Matrices
    Express the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}2 & 4 & -6\\ 0 & 1 & 2\\ 8 & -2 & 3\end{bmatrix}$ as the sum of a symmetric matrix and a skew-symmetric matrix.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $A'=\begin{bmatrix}2 & 0 & 8\\ 4 & 1 & -2\\ -6 & 2 & 3\end{bmatrix}$
    $P=\frac{1}{2}(A+A')=\begin{bmatrix}2 & 2 & 1\\ 2 & 1 & 0\\ 1 & 0 & 3\end{bmatrix}$, and $P'=P$, so $P$ is symmetric.
    $Q=\frac{1}{2}(A-A')=\begin{bmatrix}0 & 2 & -7\\ -2 & 0 & 2\\ 7 & -2 & 0\end{bmatrix}$, and $Q'=-Q$, so $Q$ is skew-symmetric.
    $A=P+Q=\begin{bmatrix}2 & 2 & 1\\ 2 & 1 & 0\\ 1 & 0 & 3\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}0 & 2 & -7\\ -2 & 0 & 2\\ 7 & -2 & 0\end{bmatrix}$

    Marking scheme:

    • Symmetric part $P=\frac{1}{2}(A+A')=\begin{bmatrix}2 & 2 & 1\\ 2 & 1 & 0\\ 1 & 0 & 3\end{bmatrix}$ (1 mark)
    • Skew-symmetric part $Q=\frac{1}{2}(A-A')=\begin{bmatrix}0 & 2 & -7\\ -2 & 0 & 2\\ 7 & -2 & 0\end{bmatrix}$ (1 mark)
    • Showing $P'=P$, $Q'=-Q$ and $A=P+Q$ (1 mark)

  2. Question 27 · 3 marks · Continuity and Differentiability
    If $y=(1+x)^{\log x}$, $x\gt 0$, find $\frac{dy}{dx}$. Also find its value at $x=1$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Taking logarithms: $\log y=\log x\cdot\log(1+x)$
    Differentiating: $\frac{1}{y}\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{\log(1+x)}{x}+\frac{\log x}{1+x}$
    $\frac{dy}{dx}=(1+x)^{\log x}\left[\frac{\log(1+x)}{x}+\frac{\log x}{1+x}\right]$
    At $x=1$: $y=2^{0}=1$, so $\frac{dy}{dx}=1\left[\log 2+0\right]=\log 2$

    Marking scheme:

    • $\log y=\log x\cdot\log(1+x)$ (½ marks)
    • Correct differentiation $\frac{1}{y}\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{\log(1+x)}{x}+\frac{\log x}{1+x}$ (1 mark)
    • $\frac{dy}{dx}=(1+x)^{\log x}\left[\frac{\log(1+x)}{x}+\frac{\log x}{1+x}\right]$ (½ marks)
    • Value at $x=1$ is $\log 2$ (1 mark)

  3. Question 28 · 3 marks · Application of Derivatives
    Find the local maximum and local minimum values of the function $f(x)=x^{3}-3x^{2}-9x+4$, using the second derivative test.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $f'(x)=3x^{2}-6x-9=3(x-3)(x+1)$; $f'(x)=0 \Rightarrow x=-1, 3$
    $f''(x)=6x-6$
    $f''(-1)=-12\lt 0$: local maximum at $x=-1$, value $f(-1)=-1-3+9+4=9$
    $f''(3)=12\gt 0$: local minimum at $x=3$, value $f(3)=27-27-27+4=-23$

    Marking scheme:

    • Critical points $x=-1$ and $x=3$ (1 mark)
    • $f''(x)=6x-6$ with correct signs at both points (1 mark)
    • Local maximum value 9 (at $x=-1$) and local minimum value $-23$ (at $x=3$) (1 mark)

  4. Question 29 · 3 marks · Differential Equations
    Find the particular solution of the differential equation $x\frac{dy}{dx}-y=xe^{\frac{y}{x}}$, $x\gt 0$, given that $y=0$ when $x=1$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{y}{x}+e^{\frac{y}{x}}$, a homogeneous equation. Put $y=vx$, $\frac{dy}{dx}=v+x\frac{dv}{dx}$.
    $v+x\frac{dv}{dx}=v+e^{v} \Rightarrow e^{-v}dv=\frac{dx}{x}$
    Integrating: $-e^{-v}=\log x+C$, i.e. $-e^{-\frac{y}{x}}=\log x+C$
    $y(1)=0$: $-1=0+C \Rightarrow C=-1$
    Particular solution: $e^{-\frac{y}{x}}=1-\log x$

    Marking scheme:

    • Substitution $y=vx$ giving $x\frac{dv}{dx}=e^{v}$ (1 mark)
    • Separating and integrating: $-e^{-\frac{y}{x}}=\log x+C$ (1 mark)
    • $C=-1$ and particular solution $e^{-\frac{y}{x}}=1-\log x$ (1 mark)

  5. Question 30 · 3 marks · Three Dimensional Geometry
    Find the coordinates of the foot of the perpendicular drawn from the point $P(0, 0, -2)$ to the line $\frac{x-1}{1}=\frac{y+1}{2}=\frac{z-2}{-2}$. Also find the length of this perpendicular.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: A general point on the line: $Q(1+\lambda, -1+2\lambda, 2-2\lambda)$
    $\overrightarrow{PQ}=(1+\lambda)\hat{i}+(-1+2\lambda)\hat{j}+(4-2\lambda)\hat{k}$
    $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ is perpendicular to the line: $1(1+\lambda)+2(-1+2\lambda)-2(4-2\lambda)=0 \Rightarrow 9\lambda-9=0 \Rightarrow \lambda=1$
    Foot of the perpendicular: $Q(2, 1, 0)$
    Length $PQ=\sqrt{(2-0)^{2}+(1-0)^{2}+(0+2)^{2}}=\sqrt{9}=3$ units

    Marking scheme:

    • General point $(1+\lambda, -1+2\lambda, 2-2\lambda)$ and perpendicularity condition (1 mark)
    • $\lambda=1$ and foot $(2, 1, 0)$ (1 mark)
    • Length of perpendicular $=3$ units (1 mark)

  6. Question 31 · 3 marks · Linear Programming
    Solve the following linear programming problem graphically:
    Minimise $Z=2x+3y$
    subject to the constraints $x+y\ge 4$, $x+3y\ge 6$, $x\ge 0$, $y\ge 0$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Draw $x+y=4$ (through $(4,0)$, $(0,4)$) and $x+3y=6$ (through $(6,0)$, $(0,2)$). The feasible region lies on the side away from the origin of both lines, in the first quadrant; it is unbounded.
    Intersection: subtracting, $2y=2 \Rightarrow y=1, x=3$.

    Corner point$Z=2x+3y$
    $(6,0)$12
    $(3,1)$9
    $(0,4)$12
    The smallest value is 9. As the region is unbounded, draw $2x+3y\lt 9$: this open half-plane has no point in common with the feasible region.
    So the minimum value of $Z$ is 9 at $x=3, y=1$.

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct graph showing the unbounded feasible region (1 mark)
    • Corner points $(6,0), (3,1), (0,4)$ with values of $Z$ (1 mark)
    • Half-plane $2x+3y\lt 9$ checked; minimum $Z=9$ at $(3,1)$ (1 mark)

Section D: 5 marks each

  1. Question 32 · 5 marks · Relations and Functions
    Let $A=\mathbb{R}-\{2\}$ and $B=\mathbb{R}-\{3\}$. Show that the function $f: A\to B$ defined by $f(x)=\frac{3x+1}{x-2}$ is one-one and onto.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: First, $f(x)\neq 3$ for any $x\in A$: if $\frac{3x+1}{x-2}=3$ then $3x+1=3x-6$, which is impossible. So $f$ maps $A$ into $B$.
    One-one: Let $f(x_1)=f(x_2)$: $\frac{3x_1+1}{x_1-2}=\frac{3x_2+1}{x_2-2}$
    $\Rightarrow (3x_1+1)(x_2-2)=(3x_2+1)(x_1-2) \Rightarrow -6x_1+x_2=-6x_2+x_1 \Rightarrow 7x_2=7x_1 \Rightarrow x_1=x_2$. So $f$ is one-one.
    Onto: Let $y\in B$ and $y=\frac{3x+1}{x-2}$. Then $xy-2y=3x+1 \Rightarrow x=\frac{2y+1}{y-3}$, which is a real number as $y\neq 3$.
    Also $x\neq 2$: if $\frac{2y+1}{y-3}=2$ then $2y+1=2y-6$, impossible. So $x\in A$ and $f(x)=y$.
    Every element of $B$ has a pre-image in $A$, so $f$ is onto.
    Hence $f$ is one-one and onto.

    Marking scheme:

    • Showing $f(x_1)=f(x_2)\Rightarrow 7x_1=7x_2$ (1½ marks)
    • Conclusion: $f$ is one-one (½ marks)
    • Solving $y=\frac{3x+1}{x-2}$ to get $x=\frac{2y+1}{y-3}$ (1½ marks)
    • Showing $x\neq 2$ (so $x\in A$) and $f(x)=y$ (1 mark)
    • Conclusion: $f$ is onto (hence one-one and onto) (½ marks)

  2. Question 33 · 5 marks · Determinants
    If $A=\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 1\\ 1 & -1 & 2\\ 2 & 1 & -1\end{bmatrix}$, find $A^{-1}$. Hence, solve the system of equations:
    $x+y+z=6$, $x-y+2z=5$, $2x+y-z=1$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $|A|=1(1-2)-1(-1-4)+1(1+2)=-1+5+3=7\neq 0$, so $A^{-1}$ exists.
    Cofactors: $C_{11}=-1, C_{12}=5, C_{13}=3$; $C_{21}=2, C_{22}=-3, C_{23}=1$; $C_{31}=3, C_{32}=-1, C_{33}=-2$
    $\operatorname{adj}A=\begin{bmatrix}-1 & 2 & 3\\ 5 & -3 & -1\\ 3 & 1 & -2\end{bmatrix}$, $A^{-1}=\frac{1}{7}\begin{bmatrix}-1 & 2 & 3\\ 5 & -3 & -1\\ 3 & 1 & -2\end{bmatrix}$
    The system is $AX=B$ with $B=\begin{bmatrix}6\\ 5\\ 1\end{bmatrix}$, so $X=A^{-1}B=\frac{1}{7}\begin{bmatrix}-6+10+3\\ 30-15-1\\ 18+5-2\end{bmatrix}=\frac{1}{7}\begin{bmatrix}7\\ 14\\ 21\end{bmatrix}$
    $x=1$, $y=2$, $z=3$

    Marking scheme:

    • $|A|=7$ (1 mark)
    • Correct cofactors / $\operatorname{adj}A=\begin{bmatrix}-1 & 2 & 3\\ 5 & -3 & -1\\ 3 & 1 & -2\end{bmatrix}$ (2 marks)
    • $A^{-1}=\frac{1}{7}\operatorname{adj}A$ and $X=A^{-1}B$ (1 mark)
    • $x=1$, $y=2$, $z=3$ (1 mark)

  3. Question 34 · 5 marks · Integrals
    Evaluate: $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{x\sin x}{3+\cos^{2}x}\,dx$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Let $I=\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{x\sin x}{3+\cos^{2}x}dx$. Using $\int_{0}^{a}f(x)dx=\int_{0}^{a}f(a-x)dx$:
    $I=\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{(\pi-x)\sin x}{3+\cos^{2}x}dx$
    Adding: $2I=\pi\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{\sin x}{3+\cos^{2}x}dx$
    Put $\cos x=t$, $-\sin x\,dx=dt$; $x=0\Rightarrow t=1$, $x=\pi\Rightarrow t=-1$:
    $2I=\pi\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dt}{3+t^{2}}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{3}}\left[\tan^{-1}\frac{t}{\sqrt{3}}\right]_{-1}^{1}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\frac{\pi}{6}+\frac{\pi}{6}\right)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3\sqrt{3}}$
    $I=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6\sqrt{3}}$ (or $\frac{\sqrt{3}\pi^{2}}{18}$)

    Marking scheme:

    • Using the property: $I=\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{(\pi-x)\sin x}{3+\cos^{2}x}dx$ (1 mark)
    • $2I=\pi\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{\sin x}{3+\cos^{2}x}dx$ (1 mark)
    • Substitution $\cos x=t$ with limits $1$ to $-1$, giving $\pi\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dt}{3+t^{2}}$ (1 mark)
    • $\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{3}}\left[\tan^{-1}\frac{t}{\sqrt{3}}\right]_{-1}^{1}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3\sqrt{3}}$ (1 mark)
    • $I=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6\sqrt{3}}$ (1 mark)

  4. Question 35 · 5 marks · Application of Integrals
    Using integration, find the area of the smaller region bounded by the circle $x^{2}+y^{2}=16$ and the line $x=2$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Sketch: circle with centre $O(0,0)$, radius 4; the line $x=2$ cuts it at $(2, 2\sqrt{3})$ and $(2, -2\sqrt{3})$. The smaller region lies between $x=2$ and $x=4$ and is symmetric about the $x$-axis.
    Area $=2\int_{2}^{4}\sqrt{16-x^{2}}\,dx$
    $=2\left[\frac{x}{2}\sqrt{16-x^{2}}+\frac{16}{2}\sin^{-1}\frac{x}{4}\right]_{2}^{4}$
    $=2\left[\left(0+8\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}\right)-\left(\sqrt{12}+8\cdot\frac{\pi}{6}\right)\right]=2\left[4\pi-2\sqrt{3}-\frac{4\pi}{3}\right]$
    $=\frac{16\pi}{3}-4\sqrt{3}$ sq. units

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct sketch with the smaller region shaded (1 mark)
    • Area $=2\int_{2}^{4}\sqrt{16-x^{2}}\,dx$ (1 mark)
    • Correct integration $\left[\frac{x}{2}\sqrt{16-x^{2}}+8\sin^{-1}\frac{x}{4}\right]$ (1 mark)
    • Correct substitution of limits (1 mark)
    • Area $=\frac{16\pi}{3}-4\sqrt{3}$ sq. units (1 mark)

Section E: case-based, 4 marks each

  1. Question 36 · 4 marks · Probability
    In an inter-school quiz, the last question is given to two students, Meera and Kabir, who work on it independently. The probability that Meera answers it correctly is $\frac{1}{2}$ and the probability that Kabir answers it correctly is $\frac{1}{3}$.
    Based on the given information, answer the following questions:

    (i) Find the probability that both of them answer the question correctly. [1]

    (ii) Find the probability that the question is answered correctly by at least one of them. [1]

    (iii) Given that the question is answered correctly by at least one of them, find the probability that exactly one of them answered it correctly. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Find the probability that both of them answer the question correctly.

    Model answer: $P(M\cap K)=P(M)P(K)=\frac{1}{2}\times\frac{1}{3}=\frac{1}{6}$

    Marking scheme:

    • $\frac{1}{6}$ (1 mark)

    (ii) Find the probability that the question is answered correctly by at least one of them.

    Model answer: $1-P(M')P(K')=1-\frac{1}{2}\times\frac{2}{3}=\frac{2}{3}$

    Marking scheme:

    • $\frac{2}{3}$ (1 mark)

    (iii) Given that the question is answered correctly by at least one of them, find the probability that exactly one of them answered it correctly.

    Model answer: P(exactly one) $=P(M)P(K')+P(M')P(K)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{3}=\frac{1}{2}$
    Required probability $=\frac{1/2}{2/3}=\frac{3}{4}$

    Marking scheme:

    • P(exactly one) $=\frac{1}{2}$ (1 mark)
    • Conditional probability $=\frac{1/2}{2/3}=\frac{3}{4}$ (1 mark)

  2. Question 37 · 4 marks · Differential Equations
    In a laboratory, a culture of bacteria grows at a rate proportional to the number of bacteria present. If $N$ is the number of bacteria at time $t$ hours, then $\frac{dN}{dt}=kN$, where $k$ is a positive constant. At the start ($t=0$) there are 1000 bacteria, and the number doubles in 3 hours.
    Based on the given information, answer the following questions:

    (i) Write the order and degree of the differential equation $\frac{dN}{dt}=kN$. [1]

    (ii) Find the general solution of the differential equation. [1]

    (iii) Find the number of bacteria after 9 hours. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Write the order and degree of the differential equation $\frac{dN}{dt}=kN$.

    Model answer: Order 1, degree 1

    Marking scheme:

    • Order 1 and degree 1 (1 mark)

    (ii) Find the general solution of the differential equation.

    Model answer: $\frac{dN}{N}=k\,dt \Rightarrow \log N=kt+c \Rightarrow N=Ce^{kt}$

    Marking scheme:

    • $N=Ce^{kt}$ (or $\log N=kt+c$) (1 mark)

    (iii) Find the number of bacteria after 9 hours.

    Model answer: $t=0$: $N=1000 \Rightarrow C=1000$. $t=3$: $2000=1000e^{3k} \Rightarrow e^{3k}=2$
    At $t=9$: $N=1000e^{9k}=1000(e^{3k})^{3}=1000\times 8=8000$

    Marking scheme:

    • $C=1000$ and $e^{3k}=2$ (1 mark)
    • $N(9)=8000$ (1 mark)

  3. Question 38 · 4 marks · Vector Algebra
    A park designer marks three points $A(1, 1, 0)$, $B(3, 4, 6)$ and $C(4, -1, 0)$ (in metres, with respect to a fixed origin) for a triangular canopy over a seating area.
    Based on the given information, answer the following questions:

    (i) Find $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$, and show that the angle of the triangle at $A$ is a right angle. [2]

    (ii) Using the vector product, find the area of the canopy (triangle $ABC$). [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Find $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$, and show that the angle of the triangle at $A$ is a right angle.

    Model answer: $\overrightarrow{AB}=2\hat{i}+3\hat{j}+6\hat{k}$, $\overrightarrow{AC}=3\hat{i}-2\hat{j}+0\hat{k}$
    $\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AC}=6-6+0=0$, so $\angle A=90^{\circ}$

    Marking scheme:

    • $\overrightarrow{AB}=2\hat{i}+3\hat{j}+6\hat{k}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}=3\hat{i}-2\hat{j}$ (1 mark)
    • $\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AC}=0$, hence right angle at $A$ (1 mark)

    (ii) Using the vector product, find the area of the canopy (triangle $ABC$).

    Model answer: $\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}=\begin{vmatrix}\hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k}\\ 2 & 3 & 6\\ 3 & -2 & 0\end{vmatrix}=12\hat{i}+18\hat{j}-13\hat{k}$
    $|\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}|=\sqrt{144+324+169}=\sqrt{637}=7\sqrt{13}$
    Area $=\frac{1}{2}\times 7\sqrt{13}=\frac{7\sqrt{13}}{2}\ \mathrm{m^{2}}$

    Marking scheme:

    • $\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}=12\hat{i}+18\hat{j}-13\hat{k}$ (1 mark)
    • Area $=\frac{7\sqrt{13}}{2}\ \mathrm{m^{2}}$ (1 mark)
