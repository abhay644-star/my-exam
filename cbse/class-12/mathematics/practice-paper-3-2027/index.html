General instructions

  1. This question paper contains 38 questions. All questions are compulsory.
  2. This question paper is divided into five Sections - A, B, C, D and E.
  3. In Section A, Questions no. 1 to 18 are multiple choice questions (MCQs) and Questions no. 19 and 20 are Assertion-Reason based questions of 1 mark each.
  4. In Section B, Questions no. 21 to 25 are Very Short Answer (VSA) type questions, carrying 2 marks each.
  5. In Section C, Questions no. 26 to 31 are Short Answer (SA) type questions, carrying 3 marks each.
  6. In Section D, Questions no. 32 to 35 are Long Answer (LA) type questions, carrying 5 marks each.
  7. In Section E, Questions no. 36 to 38 are case study based questions carrying 4 marks each. The first two case study questions have three sub-parts (i), (ii), (iii) of 1, 1 and 2 marks; the third has two sub-parts of 2 marks each.
  8. There is no overall choice. However, an internal choice has been provided in 2 questions in Section B, 3 questions in Section C, 2 questions in Section D and one sub-part each in 2 questions of Section E.

Section A: 1 mark each

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Inverse Trigonometric Functions
    The principal value of $\cot^{-1}(-\sqrt{3})$ is

    1. $-\frac{\pi}{6}$
    2. $\frac{\pi}{6}$
    3. $\frac{5\pi}{6}$
    4. $\frac{2\pi}{3}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $\frac{5\pi}{6}$

    The principal value branch of $\cot^{-1}$ is $(0,\pi)$. $\cot\frac{5\pi}{6}=-\cot\frac{\pi}{6}=-\sqrt{3}$ and $\frac{5\pi}{6}\in(0,\pi)$, so the value is $\frac{5\pi}{6}$.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Inverse Trigonometric Functions
    The principal value branch of $\operatorname{cosec}^{-1}x$ is

    1. $[0,\pi]-\left\{\frac{\pi}{2}\right\}$
    2. $\left[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right]-\{0\}$
    3. $\left(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right)$
    4. $(0,\pi)$
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) $\left[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right]-\{0\}$

    $\operatorname{cosec}^{-1}x$ takes values in $\left[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right]$ except 0, since $\operatorname{cosec}$ is not defined at 0.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Inverse Trigonometric Functions
    The value of $\cos\left(\sin^{-1}\frac{3}{5}\right)$ is

    1. $\frac{3}{5}$
    2. $\frac{3}{4}$
    3. $\frac{5}{4}$
    4. $\frac{4}{5}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) $\frac{4}{5}$

    Let $\sin^{-1}\frac{3}{5}=\theta$, so $\sin\theta=\frac{3}{5}$ with $\theta\in\left[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right]$; here $\theta$ is in the first quadrant. $\cos\theta=\sqrt{1-\frac{9}{25}}=\frac{4}{5}$.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Matrices
    If $A$ is a matrix of order $3\times 2$ and $B$ is a matrix of order $2\times 4$, then the order of $(AB)'$ is

    1. $3\times 4$
    2. $2\times 2$
    3. $4\times 3$
    4. not defined
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $4\times 3$

    $AB$ is defined and has order $3\times 4$. Its transpose has order $4\times 3$.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Matrices
    If $A=\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1\\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$, then $A^{2}$ is

    1. $O$, the zero matrix
    2. $I$, the identity matrix
    3. $A$
    4. $\begin{bmatrix}0 & 0\\ 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $O$, the zero matrix

    $A^{2}=\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1\\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1\\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0 & 0\\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}=O$, although $A\neq O$.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Determinants
    The cofactor of the element $a_{23}$ in the determinant $\begin{vmatrix}1 & 4 & -2\\ 3 & 0 & 5\\ -1 & 2 & 6\end{vmatrix}$ is

    1. $-6$
    2. 6
    3. $-2$
    4. 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $-6$

    $M_{23}=\begin{vmatrix}1 & 4\\ -1 & 2\end{vmatrix}=2+4=6$ and $A_{23}=(-1)^{2+3}M_{23}=-6$.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Continuity and Differentiability
    The number of points at which the function $f(x)=|x|+|x-2|$, $x\in\mathbb{R}$, is not differentiable is

    1. 0
    2. 1
    3. 2
    4. 3
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) 2

    $|x|$ is not differentiable at $x=0$ and $|x-2|$ is not differentiable at $x=2$; at each of these points the left and right derivatives of $f$ differ by 2. So $f$ is not differentiable at exactly 2 points.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Continuity and Differentiability
    If $y=Ae^{3x}+Be^{-3x}$, where $A$ and $B$ are constants, then $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}$ is equal to

    1. $3y$
    2. $-9y$
    3. $9y$
    4. $6y$
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $9y$

    $\frac{dy}{dx}=3Ae^{3x}-3Be^{-3x}$ and $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}=9Ae^{3x}+9Be^{-3x}=9y$.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Application of Derivatives
    The function $f(x)=xe^{-x}$ is strictly increasing in the interval

    1. $(-\infty,1)$
    2. $(1,\infty)$
    3. $(0,\infty)$
    4. $(-\infty,0)$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $(-\infty,1)$

    $f'(x)=e^{-x}-xe^{-x}=e^{-x}(1-x)$. Since $e^{-x}\gt 0$, $f'(x)\gt 0$ exactly when $x\lt 1$.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Integrals
    $\displaystyle\int\frac{dx}{x\log x}$, $x\gt 1$, is equal to

    1. $\frac{1}{\log x}+C$
    2. $\frac{(\log x)^{2}}{2}+C$
    3. $x\log x-x+C$
    4. $\log|\log x|+C$
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) $\log|\log x|+C$

    Put $t=\log x$, $dt=\frac{dx}{x}$: $\int\frac{dt}{t}=\log|t|+C=\log|\log x|+C$.

  11. Question 11 · 1 mark · Integrals
    The value of $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}xe^{x}\,dx$ is

    1. $e$
    2. 1
    3. $e-1$
    4. 0
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) 1

    By parts: $\int xe^{x}dx=xe^{x}-e^{x}$. So $\left[xe^{x}-e^{x}\right]_{0}^{1}=(e-e)-(0-1)=1$.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Differential Equations
    The general solution of the differential equation $\frac{dy}{dx}=e^{x-y}$ is

    1. $e^{y}=e^{x}+C$
    2. $e^{-y}=e^{x}+C$
    3. $e^{x}+e^{y}=C$
    4. $y=e^{x}+C$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $e^{y}=e^{x}+C$

    $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{e^{x}}{e^{y}}\Rightarrow e^{y}dy=e^{x}dx$. Integrating: $e^{y}=e^{x}+C$.

  13. Question 13 · 1 mark · Vector Algebra
    The vectors $2\hat{i}-3\hat{j}+4\hat{k}$ and $\lambda\hat{i}+6\hat{j}-8\hat{k}$ are collinear if $\lambda$ is equal to

    1. 4
    2. $-2$
    3. 2
    4. $-4$
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) $-4$

    Collinear vectors have proportional components: $\frac{\lambda}{2}=\frac{6}{-3}=\frac{-8}{4}=-2$, so $\lambda=-4$.

  14. Question 14 · 1 mark · Vector Algebra
    If $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ are non-zero vectors such that $|\vec{a}+\vec{b}|=|\vec{a}-\vec{b}|$, then $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ are

    1. parallel
    2. perpendicular
    3. equal
    4. collinear and opposite in direction
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) perpendicular

    Squaring: $|\vec{a}|^{2}+2\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}+|\vec{b}|^{2}=|\vec{a}|^{2}-2\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}+|\vec{b}|^{2}\Rightarrow\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}=0$, so the vectors are perpendicular.

  15. Question 15 · 1 mark · Linear Programming
    The maximum value of $Z=3x+2y$ subject to the constraints $x+y\le 4$, $x\le 3$, $x\ge 0$, $y\ge 0$ is

    1. 8
    2. 9
    3. 11
    4. 12
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) 11

    Corner points of the feasible region: $(0,0)$, $(3,0)$, $(3,1)$, $(0,4)$. $Z$ = 0, 9, 11, 8 respectively. Maximum $Z=11$ at $(3,1)$.

  16. Question 16 · 1 mark · Linear Programming
    Which of the following points lies in the feasible region determined by the constraints $x+2y\le 8$, $3x+y\le 9$, $x\ge 0$, $y\ge 0$?

    1. $(3,1)$
    2. $(2,2)$
    3. $(0,5)$
    4. $(1,4)$
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) $(2,2)$

    $(2,2)$: $2+4=6\le 8$ and $6+2=8\le 9$, so it satisfies all the constraints. $(3,1)$ gives $3x+y=10\gt 9$; $(0,5)$ gives $x+2y=10\gt 8$; $(1,4)$ gives $x+2y=9\gt 8$.

  17. Question 17 · 1 mark · Probability
    A bag contains 4 red and 6 black balls. Two balls are drawn at random one after the other without replacement. The probability that both balls are red is

    1. $\frac{2}{15}$
    2. $\frac{4}{25}$
    3. $\frac{4}{15}$
    4. $\frac{1}{5}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\frac{2}{15}$

    By the multiplication theorem: $P=\frac{4}{10}\times\frac{3}{9}=\frac{12}{90}=\frac{2}{15}$.

  18. Question 18 · 1 mark · Continuity and Differentiability
    If the function $f(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{\sin 5x}{3x}, & x\neq 0\\ k, & x=0\end{cases}$ is continuous at $x=0$, then the value of $k$ is

    1. $\frac{3}{5}$
    2. 1
    3. 5
    4. $\frac{5}{3}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) $\frac{5}{3}$

    $\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin 5x}{3x}=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{5}{3}\cdot\frac{\sin 5x}{5x}=\frac{5}{3}$. For continuity, $k=f(0)=\frac{5}{3}$.

  19. Question 19 · 1 mark · Differential Equations
    Assertion (A): $y=\sin x$ is a solution of the differential equation $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}-y=0$.
    Reason (R): $y=e^{x}$ is a solution of the differential equation $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}-y=0$.
    Select the correct option.

    1. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    2. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    3. Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.
    4. Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.

    For $y=\sin x$: $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}-y=-\sin x-\sin x=-2\sin x$, which is not zero for all $x$, so A is false. For $y=e^{x}$: $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}-y=e^{x}-e^{x}=0$, so R is true.

  20. Question 20 · 1 mark · Vector Algebra
    Assertion (A): For the vectors $\vec{a}=\hat{i}+\hat{j}$ and $\vec{b}=\hat{i}-\hat{j}$, $|\vec{a}\times\vec{b}|=2$.
    Reason (R): $\vec{a}\times\vec{b}=-(\vec{b}\times\vec{a})$ for any two vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$.
    Select the correct option.

    1. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    2. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    3. Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.
    4. Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

    $\vec{a}\times\vec{b}=\begin{vmatrix}\hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k}\\ 1 & 1 & 0\\ 1 & -1 & 0\end{vmatrix}=-2\hat{k}$, so $|\vec{a}\times\vec{b}|=2$: A is true. R is a true property of the vector product, but it does not give the value in A.

Section B: 2 marks each

  1. Question 21 · 2 marks · Integrals
    Find $\displaystyle\int x\sec^{2}x\,dx$.

    OR Find $\displaystyle\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{5-4x-x^{2}}}$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Integrating by parts, taking $x$ as the first function:
    $\int x\sec^{2}x\,dx=x\tan x-\int 1\cdot\tan x\,dx=x\tan x-\log|\sec x|+C$
    $=x\tan x+\log|\cos x|+C$

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct use of integration by parts: $x\tan x-\int\tan x\,dx$ (1 mark)
    • Answer $x\tan x+\log|\cos x|+C$ (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: $5-4x-x^{2}=5-(x^{2}+4x+4)+4=9-(x+2)^{2}$
    $\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{3^{2}-(x+2)^{2}}}=\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x+2}{3}\right)+C$

    Marking scheme:

    • Writing $5-4x-x^{2}=3^{2}-(x+2)^{2}$ (1 mark)
    • Answer $\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x+2}{3}\right)+C$ (1 mark)

  2. Question 22 · 2 marks · Continuity and Differentiability
    If $y=\log\left(x+\sqrt{x^{2}+4}\right)$, show that $(x^{2}+4)\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^{2}=1$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{1}{x+\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}\left(1+\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}\right)=\frac{1}{x+\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}+x}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}$
    So $(x^{2}+4)\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^{2}=(x^{2}+4)\cdot\frac{1}{x^{2}+4}=1$.

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct derivative using the chain rule: $\frac{1}{x+\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}\left(1+\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}\right)$ (1 mark)
    • Simplifying to $\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}$ and showing the result (1 mark)

  3. Question 23 · 2 marks · Vector Algebra
    For any two vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ with $|\vec{a}|=|\vec{b}|$, show that $\vec{a}+\vec{b}$ is perpendicular to $\vec{a}-\vec{b}$. Verify this for $\vec{a}=2\hat{i}+\hat{j}-2\hat{k}$ and $\vec{b}=\hat{i}+2\hat{j}+2\hat{k}$.

    OR Find the direction cosines of the line passing through the points $A(1, 2, -3)$ and $B(3, -4, 0)$. Also show that this line is perpendicular to a line whose direction ratios are $3, 1, 0$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $(\vec{a}+\vec{b})\cdot(\vec{a}-\vec{b})=|\vec{a}|^{2}-\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}+\vec{b}\cdot\vec{a}-|\vec{b}|^{2}=|\vec{a}|^{2}-|\vec{b}|^{2}=0$, so $\vec{a}+\vec{b}\perp\vec{a}-\vec{b}$ (when both are non-zero).
    Verification: $|\vec{a}|=\sqrt{4+1+4}=3$ and $|\vec{b}|=\sqrt{1+4+4}=3$.
    $\vec{a}+\vec{b}=3\hat{i}+3\hat{j}$, $\vec{a}-\vec{b}=\hat{i}-\hat{j}-4\hat{k}$, and $(\vec{a}+\vec{b})\cdot(\vec{a}-\vec{b})=3-3+0=0$.

    Marking scheme:

    • $(\vec{a}+\vec{b})\cdot(\vec{a}-\vec{b})=|\vec{a}|^{2}-|\vec{b}|^{2}=0$ (1 mark)
    • Verification: $|\vec{a}|=|\vec{b}|=3$ and $(3\hat{i}+3\hat{j})\cdot(\hat{i}-\hat{j}-4\hat{k})=0$ (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: Direction ratios of AB: $3-1, -4-2, 0+3$, i.e. $2, -6, 3$. $\sqrt{4+36+9}=7$.
    Direction cosines: $\frac{2}{7}, -\frac{6}{7}, \frac{3}{7}$
    $2(3)+(-6)(1)+3(0)=0$, so the two lines are perpendicular.

    Marking scheme:

    • Direction cosines $\frac{2}{7}, -\frac{6}{7}, \frac{3}{7}$ (1 mark)
    • $2(3)+(-6)(1)+3(0)=0$, so the lines are perpendicular (1 mark)

  4. Question 24 · 2 marks · Vector Algebra
    If $|\vec{a}|=3$, $|\vec{b}|=2$ and $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}=3$, find $|2\vec{a}-3\vec{b}|$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $|2\vec{a}-3\vec{b}|^{2}=4|\vec{a}|^{2}-12\,\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}+9|\vec{b}|^{2}=4(9)-12(3)+9(4)=36-36+36=36$
    $|2\vec{a}-3\vec{b}|=6$

    Marking scheme:

    • Expansion $4|\vec{a}|^{2}-12\,\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}+9|\vec{b}|^{2}$ (1 mark)
    • $|2\vec{a}-3\vec{b}|=6$ (1 mark)

  5. Question 25 · 2 marks · Probability
    A coin is tossed three times. Let $E$ be the event 'exactly two heads are obtained' and $F$ be the event 'the first toss shows a head'. Find $P(E|F)$. Are the events $E$ and $F$ independent? Give a reason.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Sample space: 8 equally likely outcomes. $E=\{HHT, HTH, THH\}$, so $P(E)=\frac{3}{8}$. $F=\{HHH, HHT, HTH, HTT\}$, so $P(F)=\frac{4}{8}=\frac{1}{2}$.
    $E\cap F=\{HHT, HTH\}$, so $P(E\cap F)=\frac{2}{8}=\frac{1}{4}$.
    $P(E|F)=\frac{P(E\cap F)}{P(F)}=\frac{1/4}{1/2}=\frac{1}{2}$
    Since $P(E|F)=\frac{1}{2}\neq\frac{3}{8}=P(E)$ (equivalently $P(E\cap F)=\frac{1}{4}\neq P(E)P(F)=\frac{3}{16}$), $E$ and $F$ are not independent.

    Marking scheme:

    • $P(E|F)=\frac{1}{2}$ (1 mark)
    • Not independent, since $P(E|F)\neq P(E)=\frac{3}{8}$ (or $P(E\cap F)\neq P(E)P(F)$) (1 mark)

Section C: 3 marks each

  1. Question 26 · 3 marks · Determinants
    Using determinants, find the area of the triangle whose vertices are $(1, 2)$, $(4, 6)$ and $(-2, 5)$. Also, using determinants, find the equation of the line joining the points $(1, 2)$ and $(4, 6)$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Area $=\frac{1}{2}\left|\begin{vmatrix}1 & 2 & 1\\ 4 & 6 & 1\\ -2 & 5 & 1\end{vmatrix}\right|=\frac{1}{2}\left|1(6-5)-2(4+2)+1(20+12)\right|=\frac{1}{2}|1-12+32|=\frac{21}{2}$ square units
    Let $(x, y)$ be any point on the line. The three points are collinear, so $\begin{vmatrix}x & y & 1\\ 1 & 2 & 1\\ 4 & 6 & 1\end{vmatrix}=0$
    $x(2-6)-y(1-4)+1(6-8)=0\Rightarrow -4x+3y-2=0$
    Equation of the line: $4x-3y+2=0$

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct determinant for the area (½ marks)
    • Area $=\frac{21}{2}$ square units (1 mark)
    • Collinearity condition $\begin{vmatrix}x & y & 1\\ 1 & 2 & 1\\ 4 & 6 & 1\end{vmatrix}=0$ (½ marks)
    • Equation $4x-3y+2=0$ (1 mark)

  2. Question 27 · 3 marks · Application of Derivatives
    Find the intervals in which the function $f(x)=x^{4}-8x^{2}$ is (i) strictly increasing, (ii) strictly decreasing.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $f'(x)=4x^{3}-16x=4x(x-2)(x+2)$; $f'(x)=0\Rightarrow x=-2, 0, 2$
    Sign of $f'(x)$: negative on $(-\infty,-2)$, positive on $(-2,0)$, negative on $(0,2)$, positive on $(2,\infty)$.
    (i) $f$ is strictly increasing on $(-2,0)\cup(2,\infty)$.
    (ii) $f$ is strictly decreasing on $(-\infty,-2)\cup(0,2)$.

    Marking scheme:

    • $f'(x)=4x(x-2)(x+2)$ and the points $x=-2, 0, 2$ (1 mark)
    • Strictly increasing on $(-2,0)\cup(2,\infty)$ (1 mark)
    • Strictly decreasing on $(-\infty,-2)\cup(0,2)$ (1 mark)

  3. Question 28 · 3 marks · Continuity and Differentiability
    If $y=e^{2x}(a+bx)$, where $a$ and $b$ are constants, show that $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}-4\frac{dy}{dx}+4y=0$.

    OR If $x=t+\frac{1}{t}$ and $y=t-\frac{1}{t}$, where $t\neq 0, \pm 1$, show that $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}=-\frac{4}{y^{3}}$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $\frac{dy}{dx}=2e^{2x}(a+bx)+be^{2x}=2y+be^{2x}$ ...(1)
    Differentiating again: $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}=2\frac{dy}{dx}+2be^{2x}$
    From (1), $be^{2x}=\frac{dy}{dx}-2y$, so $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}=2\frac{dy}{dx}+2\left(\frac{dy}{dx}-2y\right)=4\frac{dy}{dx}-4y$
    Hence $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}-4\frac{dy}{dx}+4y=0$.

    Marking scheme:

    • $\frac{dy}{dx}=2y+be^{2x}$ (1 mark)
    • $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}=2\frac{dy}{dx}+2be^{2x}$ (1 mark)
    • Eliminating $be^{2x}$ to get the result (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: $\frac{dx}{dt}=1-\frac{1}{t^{2}}$ and $\frac{dy}{dt}=1+\frac{1}{t^{2}}$, so $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{t^{2}+1}{t^{2}-1}=\frac{x}{y}$ (since $x=\frac{t^{2}+1}{t}$ and $y=\frac{t^{2}-1}{t}$).
    $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}=\frac{y-x\frac{dy}{dx}}{y^{2}}=\frac{y-\frac{x^{2}}{y}}{y^{2}}=\frac{y^{2}-x^{2}}{y^{3}}$
    Also $x^{2}-y^{2}=\left(t+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{2}-\left(t-\frac{1}{t}\right)^{2}=4$, so $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}=-\frac{4}{y^{3}}$.

    Marking scheme:

    • $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{t^{2}+1}{t^{2}-1}=\frac{x}{y}$ (1 mark)
    • $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}=\frac{y^{2}-x^{2}}{y^{3}}$ (quotient rule) (1 mark)
    • Using $x^{2}-y^{2}=4$ to get $-\frac{4}{y^{3}}$ (1 mark)

  4. Question 29 · 3 marks · Differential Equations
    Find the particular solution of the differential equation $(x+1)\frac{dy}{dx}-y=e^{x}(x+1)^{2}$, $x\gt -1$, given that $y=1$ when $x=0$.

    OR Find the particular solution of the differential equation $2xy\frac{dy}{dx}=x^{2}+3y^{2}$, $x\gt 0$, given that $y=1$ when $x=1$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Dividing by $(x+1)$: $\frac{dy}{dx}-\frac{1}{x+1}y=e^{x}(x+1)$, a linear equation with $P=-\frac{1}{x+1}$, $Q=e^{x}(x+1)$.
    I.F. $=e^{-\int\frac{dx}{x+1}}=e^{-\log(x+1)}=\frac{1}{x+1}$
    Solution: $y\cdot\frac{1}{x+1}=\int e^{x}(x+1)\cdot\frac{1}{x+1}\,dx=e^{x}+C$, i.e. $y=(x+1)(e^{x}+C)$
    $y(0)=1$: $1=1\cdot(1+C)\Rightarrow C=0$
    Particular solution: $y=(x+1)e^{x}$

    Marking scheme:

    • I.F. $=\frac{1}{x+1}$ (1 mark)
    • General solution $\frac{y}{x+1}=e^{x}+C$ (1 mark)
    • $C=0$ and particular solution $y=(x+1)e^{x}$ (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{x^{2}+3y^{2}}{2xy}$ is homogeneous. Put $y=vx$, $\frac{dy}{dx}=v+x\frac{dv}{dx}$.
    $v+x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{1+3v^{2}}{2v}\Rightarrow x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{1+v^{2}}{2v}\Rightarrow\frac{2v}{1+v^{2}}dv=\frac{dx}{x}$
    Integrating: $\log(1+v^{2})=\log x+\log C\Rightarrow 1+v^{2}=Cx\Rightarrow x^{2}+y^{2}=Cx^{3}$
    $y(1)=1$: $2=C$
    Particular solution: $x^{2}+y^{2}=2x^{3}$

    Marking scheme:

    • Substitution $y=vx$ giving $\frac{2v}{1+v^{2}}dv=\frac{dx}{x}$ (1 mark)
    • Integrating: $x^{2}+y^{2}=Cx^{3}$ (1 mark)
    • $C=2$ and particular solution $x^{2}+y^{2}=2x^{3}$ (1 mark)

  5. Question 30 · 3 marks · Three Dimensional Geometry
    Show that the lines $\frac{x-1}{2}=\frac{y+1}{3}=\frac{z-2}{1}$ and $\frac{x-5}{2}=\frac{y-1}{-1}=\frac{z-6}{3}$ intersect. Also find their point of intersection.

    OR Find the vector and cartesian equations of the line passing through the point $(2, -1, 3)$ and perpendicular to both the lines $\vec{r}=\lambda(\hat{i}+2\hat{j}-\hat{k})$ and $\vec{r}=\mu(3\hat{i}-\hat{j}+2\hat{k})$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: A general point on the first line: $(2\lambda+1, 3\lambda-1, \lambda+2)$; on the second line: $(2\mu+5, -\mu+1, 3\mu+6)$.
    For a common point: $2\lambda+1=2\mu+5\Rightarrow\lambda-\mu=2$ and $3\lambda-1=-\mu+1\Rightarrow 3\lambda+\mu=2$.
    Solving: $\lambda=1$, $\mu=-1$.
    Check the z-coordinates: $\lambda+2=3$ and $3\mu+6=3$. They are equal, so the lines intersect.
    Point of intersection: $(3, 2, 3)$

    Marking scheme:

    • General points on both lines (1 mark)
    • $\lambda=1$, $\mu=-1$ with the third coordinates checked (1 mark)
    • Point of intersection $(3, 2, 3)$ (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: The required line is parallel to $\vec{b}=(\hat{i}+2\hat{j}-\hat{k})\times(3\hat{i}-\hat{j}+2\hat{k})=\begin{vmatrix}\hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k}\\ 1 & 2 & -1\\ 3 & -1 & 2\end{vmatrix}=3\hat{i}-5\hat{j}-7\hat{k}$
    Vector equation: $\vec{r}=(2\hat{i}-\hat{j}+3\hat{k})+t(3\hat{i}-5\hat{j}-7\hat{k})$
    Cartesian equation: $\frac{x-2}{3}=\frac{y+1}{-5}=\frac{z-3}{-7}$

    Marking scheme:

    • Direction $3\hat{i}-5\hat{j}-7\hat{k}$ (cross product) (1½ marks)
    • Vector equation $\vec{r}=(2\hat{i}-\hat{j}+3\hat{k})+t(3\hat{i}-5\hat{j}-7\hat{k})$ (1 mark)
    • Cartesian equation $\frac{x-2}{3}=\frac{y+1}{-5}=\frac{z-3}{-7}$ (½ marks)

  6. Question 31 · 3 marks · Linear Programming
    Solve the following linear programming problem graphically:
    Maximise $Z=3x+4y$
    subject to the constraints $2x+y\le 10$, $x+2y\le 8$, $x\ge 0$, $y\ge 0$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Draw $2x+y=10$ (through $(5,0)$ and $(0,10)$) and $x+2y=8$ (through $(8,0)$ and $(0,4)$). The feasible region is the bounded region on the origin side of both lines in the first quadrant.
    Intersection of the lines: $x=8-2y\Rightarrow 16-4y+y=10\Rightarrow y=2$, $x=4$.

    Corner point$Z=3x+4y$
    $(0,0)$0
    $(5,0)$15
    $(4,2)$20
    $(0,4)$16
    Maximum $Z=20$ at $x=4$, $y=2$.

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct graph with the bounded feasible region shaded (1 mark)
    • Corner points $(0,0), (5,0), (4,2), (0,4)$ with values of $Z$ (1 mark)
    • Maximum $Z=20$ at $(4,2)$ (1 mark)

Section D: 5 marks each

  1. Question 32 · 5 marks · Relations and Functions
    Let $A=\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}$, where $\mathbb{N}$ is the set of natural numbers, and let $R$ be the relation on $A$ defined by $(a,b)\,R\,(c,d)$ if and only if $a+2d=c+2b$. Show that $R$ is an equivalence relation. Also find the equivalence class of $(3,1)$.

    OR Let $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be defined by $f(x)=\frac{x}{x^{2}+1}$. Show that $f$ is neither one-one nor onto. Also find the range of $f$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Reflexive: for every $(a,b)\in A$, $a+2b=a+2b$, so $(a,b)\,R\,(a,b)$.
    Symmetric: if $(a,b)\,R\,(c,d)$, then $a+2d=c+2b$, i.e. $c+2b=a+2d$, so $(c,d)\,R\,(a,b)$.
    Transitive: if $(a,b)\,R\,(c,d)$ and $(c,d)\,R\,(e,f)$, then $a+2d=c+2b$ and $c+2f=e+2d$. Adding: $a+2d+c+2f=c+2b+e+2d\Rightarrow a+2f=e+2b$, so $(a,b)\,R\,(e,f)$.
    Hence $R$ is an equivalence relation.
    Equivalence class of $(3,1)$: $(c,d)\,R\,(3,1)\iff c+2=3+2d\iff c=2d+1$.
    $[(3,1)]=\{(2d+1,d): d\in\mathbb{N}\}=\{(3,1),(5,2),(7,3),\ldots\}$

    Marking scheme:

    • Reflexive shown (1 mark)
    • Symmetric shown (1 mark)
    • Transitive shown (adding the two equations) (1½ marks)
    • Conclusion: $R$ is an equivalence relation (½ marks)
    • $[(3,1)]=\{(2d+1,d): d\in\mathbb{N}\}$ (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: Not one-one: $f(2)=\frac{2}{5}$ and $f\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{1/2}{5/4}=\frac{2}{5}$. So $f(2)=f\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)$ but $2\neq\frac{1}{2}$.
    Range: let $y=\frac{x}{x^{2}+1}$. Then $yx^{2}-x+y=0$.
    If $y=0$, $x=0$. If $y\neq 0$, this quadratic in $x$ has a real root only when $1-4y^{2}\ge 0$, i.e. $-\frac{1}{2}\le y\le\frac{1}{2}$.
    So the range of $f$ is $\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]$.
    Not onto: the range is not the whole co-domain $\mathbb{R}$; for example, 1 has no pre-image.

    Marking scheme:

    • Not one-one, with an example such as $f(2)=f\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)$ (1½ marks)
    • Writing $yx^{2}-x+y=0$ (1 mark)
    • Discriminant condition $1-4y^{2}\ge 0$ (1 mark)
    • Range $\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]$ (1 mark)
    • Not onto (e.g. 1 has no pre-image) (½ marks)

  2. Question 33 · 5 marks · Integrals
    Evaluate: $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{\sin 2x}{(1+\sin^{2}x)(3+\sin^{2}x)}\,dx$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Put $t=\sin^{2}x$, so $dt=2\sin x\cos x\,dx=\sin 2x\,dx$. When $x=0$, $t=0$; when $x=\frac{\pi}{2}$, $t=1$.
    $I=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{(1+t)(3+t)}$
    Partial fractions: $\frac{1}{(1+t)(3+t)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{1+t}-\frac{1}{3+t}\right)$
    $I=\frac{1}{2}\left[\log(1+t)-\log(3+t)\right]_{0}^{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\log\frac{1+t}{3+t}\right]_{0}^{1}$
    $=\frac{1}{2}\left(\log\frac{2}{4}-\log\frac{1}{3}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{3}{2}$

    Marking scheme:

    • Substitution $t=\sin^{2}x$, $dt=\sin 2x\,dx$ (1 mark)
    • New limits 0 and 1 (½ marks)
    • Partial fractions $\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{1+t}-\frac{1}{3+t}\right)$ (1½ marks)
    • Integrating: $\frac{1}{2}\left[\log\frac{1+t}{3+t}\right]_{0}^{1}$ (1 mark)
    • Value $\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{3}{2}$ (1 mark)

  3. Question 34 · 5 marks · Application of Integrals
    Using integration, find the area of the triangle whose vertices are $A(-1, 1)$, $B(1, 5)$ and $C(3, 3)$.

    OR Using integration, find the area of the region in the first quadrant bounded by the ellipse $\frac{x^{2}}{16}+\frac{y^{2}}{9}=1$, the x-axis and the lines $x=0$ and $x=2$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Equations of the sides:
    AB: $y-1=\frac{5-1}{1+1}(x+1)\Rightarrow y=2x+3$
    BC: $y-5=\frac{3-5}{3-1}(x-1)\Rightarrow y=6-x$
    AC: $y-1=\frac{3-1}{3+1}(x+1)\Rightarrow y=\frac{x+3}{2}$
    Area $=\int_{-1}^{1}\left[(2x+3)-\frac{x+3}{2}\right]dx+\int_{1}^{3}\left[(6-x)-\frac{x+3}{2}\right]dx$
    $=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{3x+3}{2}\,dx+\int_{1}^{3}\frac{9-3x}{2}\,dx$
    $=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{3x^{2}}{2}+3x\right]_{-1}^{1}+\frac{1}{2}\left[9x-\frac{3x^{2}}{2}\right]_{1}^{3}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{9}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{27}{2}-\frac{15}{2}\right)=3+3$
    Area $=6$ square units

    Marking scheme:

    • Equations of AB, BC and AC: $y=2x+3$, $y=6-x$, $y=\frac{x+3}{2}$ (1½ marks)
    • Correct integrals with limits $-1$ to 1 and 1 to 3 (with sketch) (1½ marks)
    • Values of the integrals: 3 and 3 (1½ marks)
    • Area $=6$ square units (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: In the first quadrant, $y=\frac{3}{4}\sqrt{16-x^{2}}$.
    Area $=\int_{0}^{2}\frac{3}{4}\sqrt{16-x^{2}}\,dx=\frac{3}{4}\left[\frac{x}{2}\sqrt{16-x^{2}}+\frac{16}{2}\sin^{-1}\frac{x}{4}\right]_{0}^{2}$
    $=\frac{3}{4}\left[\frac{2}{2}\sqrt{12}+8\sin^{-1}\frac{1}{2}\right]=\frac{3}{4}\left[2\sqrt{3}+\frac{4\pi}{3}\right]$
    $=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}+\pi$ square units

    Marking scheme:

    • Sketch and $y=\frac{3}{4}\sqrt{16-x^{2}}$ (1 mark)
    • Area $=\int_{0}^{2}\frac{3}{4}\sqrt{16-x^{2}}\,dx$ (1 mark)
    • Correct use of $\int\sqrt{a^{2}-x^{2}}\,dx=\frac{x}{2}\sqrt{a^{2}-x^{2}}+\frac{a^{2}}{2}\sin^{-1}\frac{x}{a}$ (1 mark)
    • Substituting the limits: $\frac{3}{4}\left[2\sqrt{3}+\frac{4\pi}{3}\right]$ (1½ marks)
    • Area $=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}+\pi$ square units (½ marks)

  4. Question 35 · 5 marks · Probability
    Bag I contains 4 white and 2 black balls, and Bag II contains 3 white and 5 black balls. Two balls are drawn at random from Bag I, without replacement, and put into Bag II. Then one ball is drawn at random from Bag II.
    (i) Find the probability that the ball drawn from Bag II is white.
    (ii) If the ball drawn from Bag II is white, find the probability that both the balls transferred were white.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Let $E_1$: both transferred balls are white; $E_2$: one white and one black; $E_3$: both black. Let $W$: the ball drawn from Bag II is white.
    $P(E_1)=\frac{^{4}C_{2}}{^{6}C_{2}}=\frac{6}{15}$, $P(E_2)=\frac{4\times 2}{15}=\frac{8}{15}$, $P(E_3)=\frac{1}{15}$
    After the transfer Bag II has 10 balls: $P(W|E_1)=\frac{5}{10}$, $P(W|E_2)=\frac{4}{10}$, $P(W|E_3)=\frac{3}{10}$
    (i) $P(W)=\frac{6}{15}\cdot\frac{5}{10}+\frac{8}{15}\cdot\frac{4}{10}+\frac{1}{15}\cdot\frac{3}{10}=\frac{30+32+3}{150}=\frac{65}{150}=\frac{13}{30}$
    (ii) By Bayes' theorem, $P(E_1|W)=\frac{P(E_1)P(W|E_1)}{P(W)}=\frac{30/150}{65/150}=\frac{30}{65}=\frac{6}{13}$

    Marking scheme:

    • $P(E_1)=\frac{6}{15}$, $P(E_2)=\frac{8}{15}$, $P(E_3)=\frac{1}{15}$ (1½ marks)
    • $P(W|E_1)=\frac{5}{10}$, $P(W|E_2)=\frac{4}{10}$, $P(W|E_3)=\frac{3}{10}$ (1 mark)
    • (i) $P(W)=\frac{13}{30}$ (1 mark)
    • (ii) $P(E_1|W)=\frac{6}{13}$ (Bayes' theorem) (1½ marks)

  1. Question 36 · 4 marks · Three Dimensional Geometry
    In an adventure park, a straight zip-line cable runs from the top of a platform at the point $A(1, 2, 8)$ to an anchor at the point $B(5, 6, 0)$ on the ground. Distances are in metres, with respect to a fixed origin. The top of a lamp post is at the point $P(6, 1, 4)$, and a second straight cable passes through the points $C(0, 1, 2)$ and $D(2, 5, 4)$.
    Based on the given information, answer the following questions:

    (i) Find the length of the cable AB. [1]

    (ii) Write the cartesian equation of the line AB. [1]

    (iii) Find the foot of the perpendicular from the top of the lamp post P to the cable AB, and the length of this perpendicular. [2]

    OR (iii) Find the angle between the cable AB and the second cable CD.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Find the length of the cable AB.

    Model answer: $AB=\sqrt{(5-1)^{2}+(6-2)^{2}+(0-8)^{2}}=\sqrt{16+16+64}=\sqrt{96}=4\sqrt{6}$ m

    Marking scheme:

    • $AB=4\sqrt{6}$ m (1 mark)

    (ii) Write the cartesian equation of the line AB.

    Model answer: Direction ratios of AB: $4, 4, -8$, i.e. $1, 1, -2$. Equation: $\frac{x-1}{1}=\frac{y-2}{1}=\frac{z-8}{-2}$

    Marking scheme:

    • $\frac{x-1}{1}=\frac{y-2}{1}=\frac{z-8}{-2}$ (1 mark)

    (iii) Find the foot of the perpendicular from the top of the lamp post P to the cable AB, and the length of this perpendicular.

    Model answer: A general point on AB: $Q(\lambda+1, \lambda+2, 8-2\lambda)$. $\overrightarrow{PQ}=(\lambda-5)\hat{i}+(\lambda+1)\hat{j}+(4-2\lambda)\hat{k}$.
    $\overrightarrow{PQ}\perp AB$: $(\lambda-5)+(\lambda+1)-2(4-2\lambda)=0\Rightarrow 6\lambda-12=0\Rightarrow\lambda=2$
    Foot of the perpendicular: $Q(3, 4, 4)$; length $PQ=\sqrt{(3-6)^{2}+(4-1)^{2}+(4-4)^{2}}=3\sqrt{2}$ m

    Marking scheme:

    • Perpendicularity condition giving $\lambda=2$ (1 mark)
    • Foot $(3, 4, 4)$ and length $3\sqrt{2}$ m (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: Direction ratios of CD: $2-0, 5-1, 4-2$, i.e. $2, 4, 2$, or $1, 2, 1$. Direction ratios of AB: $1, 1, -2$.
    $\cos\theta=\frac{|1(1)+1(2)+(-2)(1)|}{\sqrt{6}\sqrt{6}}=\frac{1}{6}$, so $\theta=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)$

    Marking scheme:

    • Direction ratios of CD: $1, 2, 1$ (1 mark)
    • $\theta=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)$ (1 mark)

  2. Question 37 · 4 marks · Application of Derivatives
    A drone is programmed so that its height above the ground, $t$ seconds after a timer starts, is $h(t)=t^{3}-12t^{2}+36t+10$ metres, for $0\le t\le 7$.
    Based on the given information, answer the following questions:

    (i) Find the rate at which the height is changing at $t=1$ s. [1]

    (ii) At what times is the vertical speed of the drone zero? [1]

    (iii) Using the second derivative test, find the local maximum and local minimum heights of the drone. [2]

    OR (iii) Find the time intervals in which the drone is rising and in which it is coming down.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Find the rate at which the height is changing at $t=1$ s.

    Model answer: $h'(t)=3t^{2}-24t+36$, so $h'(1)=3-24+36=15$ m/s (the drone is rising).

    Marking scheme:

    • $h'(1)=15$ m/s (1 mark)

    (ii) At what times is the vertical speed of the drone zero?

    Model answer: $h'(t)=3(t^{2}-8t+12)=3(t-2)(t-6)=0\Rightarrow t=2$ s and $t=6$ s.

    Marking scheme:

    • $t=2$ s and $t=6$ s (1 mark)

    (iii) Using the second derivative test, find the local maximum and local minimum heights of the drone.

    Model answer: $h''(t)=6t-24$
    $h''(2)=-12\lt 0$: local maximum, $h(2)=8-48+72+10=42$ m
    $h''(6)=12\gt 0$: local minimum, $h(6)=216-432+216+10=10$ m

    Marking scheme:

    • $h''(t)=6t-24$ with the correct signs at $t=2$ and $t=6$ (1 mark)
    • Local maximum height 42 m and local minimum height 10 m (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: $h'(t)=3(t-2)(t-6)$. For $0\lt t\lt 2$, $h'(t)\gt 0$; for $2\lt t\lt 6$, $h'(t)\lt 0$; for $6\lt t\lt 7$, $h'(t)\gt 0$.
    The drone is rising during $(0, 2)$ and $(6, 7)$ and coming down during $(2, 6)$.

    Marking scheme:

    • Sign of $h'(t)$ in each interval (1 mark)
    • Rising in $(0, 2)$ and $(6, 7)$; coming down in $(2, 6)$ (1 mark)

  3. Question 38 · 4 marks · Matrices
    Three snack stalls X, Y and Z at a school fair sell cups of tea, samosas and sandwiches. The numbers sold in one day are:
    Stall X: 30 cups of tea, 20 samosas, 10 sandwiches
    Stall Y: 25 cups of tea, 15 samosas, 20 sandwiches
    Stall Z: 40 cups of tea, 30 samosas, 5 sandwiches
    The selling prices are ₹10 for a cup of tea, ₹15 for a samosa and ₹40 for a sandwich. The cost prices are ₹6, ₹10 and ₹30 respectively.
    Based on the given information, answer the following questions:

    (i) Write the sales matrix $S$ (rows X, Y, Z; columns tea, samosa, sandwich) and the column matrix $P$ of selling prices, and find the revenue of each stall using matrix multiplication. [2]

    (ii) Using matrices, find the profit of each stall and the total profit of the three stalls. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Write the sales matrix $S$ (rows X, Y, Z; columns tea, samosa, sandwich) and the column matrix $P$ of selling prices, and find the revenue of each stall using matrix multiplication.

    Model answer: $S=\begin{bmatrix}30 & 20 & 10\\ 25 & 15 & 20\\ 40 & 30 & 5\end{bmatrix}$, $P=\begin{bmatrix}10\\ 15\\ 40\end{bmatrix}$
    $SP=\begin{bmatrix}300+300+400\\ 250+225+800\\ 400+450+200\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1000\\ 1275\\ 1050\end{bmatrix}$
    Revenue: X ₹1000, Y ₹1275, Z ₹1050

    Marking scheme:

    • Matrices $S$ and $P$ written correctly (1 mark)
    • $SP$: revenues ₹1000, ₹1275 and ₹1050 (1 mark)

    (ii) Using matrices, find the profit of each stall and the total profit of the three stalls.

    Model answer: Profit per item: $P-C=\begin{bmatrix}10-6\\ 15-10\\ 40-30\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}4\\ 5\\ 10\end{bmatrix}$
    $S(P-C)=\begin{bmatrix}120+100+100\\ 100+75+200\\ 160+150+50\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}320\\ 375\\ 360\end{bmatrix}$
    Profit: X ₹320, Y ₹375, Z ₹360; total profit = ₹1055

    Marking scheme:

    • Profit per item matrix $\begin{bmatrix}4\\ 5\\ 10\end{bmatrix}$ (or cost matrix $SC$) (1 mark)
    • Profits ₹320, ₹375, ₹360 and total ₹1055 (1 mark)
