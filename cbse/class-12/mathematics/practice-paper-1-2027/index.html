General instructions

  1. This question paper contains 38 questions. All questions are compulsory.
  2. This question paper is divided into five Sections - A, B, C, D and E.
  3. In Section A, Questions no. 1 to 18 are multiple choice questions (MCQs) and Questions no. 19 and 20 are Assertion-Reason based questions of 1 mark each.
  4. In Section B, Questions no. 21 to 25 are Very Short Answer (VSA) type questions, carrying 2 marks each.
  5. In Section C, Questions no. 26 to 31 are Short Answer (SA) type questions, carrying 3 marks each.
  6. In Section D, Questions no. 32 to 35 are Long Answer (LA) type questions, carrying 5 marks each.
  7. In Section E, Questions no. 36 to 38 are case study based questions carrying 4 marks each. The first two case study questions have three sub-parts (i), (ii), (iii) of 1, 1 and 2 marks; the third has two sub-parts of 2 marks each.
  8. Internal choices of the board paper are not included: each question has one version.
  9. Draw neat and clean figures wherever required. Take π = 22/7 wherever required, if not stated.
  10. Use of calculators is not allowed.

Section A: 1 mark each

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Inverse Trigonometric Functions
    The value of $\tan^{-1}(\sqrt{3}) - \sec^{-1}(-2)$ is

    1. $-\frac{\pi}{3}$
    2. $\pi$
    3. $\frac{\pi}{3}$
    4. $-\frac{2\pi}{3}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $-\frac{\pi}{3}$

    $\tan^{-1}\sqrt{3}=\frac{\pi}{3}$. The principal value branch of $\sec^{-1}$ is $[0,\pi]-\{\frac{\pi}{2}\}$, so $\sec^{-1}(-2)=\frac{2\pi}{3}$. Value $=\frac{\pi}{3}-\frac{2\pi}{3}=-\frac{\pi}{3}$.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Inverse Trigonometric Functions
    The domain of the function $f(x)=\sin^{-1}(3x-2)$ is

    1. $\left[\frac{1}{3}, 1\right]$
    2. $[-1, 1]$
    3. $[0, 1]$
    4. $\left[\frac{1}{3}, 1\right)$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\left[\frac{1}{3}, 1\right]$

    $-1\le 3x-2\le 1 \Rightarrow 1\le 3x\le 3 \Rightarrow \frac{1}{3}\le x\le 1$.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Inverse Trigonometric Functions
    If $\cos^{-1}x=\frac{2\pi}{3}$, then the value of $\sin^{-1}x$ is

    1. $-\frac{\pi}{6}$
    2. $\frac{\pi}{6}$
    3. $\frac{5\pi}{6}$
    4. $-\frac{\pi}{3}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $-\frac{\pi}{6}$

    $x=\cos\frac{2\pi}{3}=-\frac{1}{2}$. The principal value of $\sin^{-1}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)$ lies in $\left[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right]$ and equals $-\frac{\pi}{6}$.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Matrices
    If $A=\begin{bmatrix}0 & a & 3\\ -2 & 0 & b\\ c & 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}$ is a skew-symmetric matrix, then the value of $a+b+c$ is

    1. $-2$
    2. $2$
    3. $0$
    4. $-4$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $-2$

    For a skew-symmetric matrix $a_{ij}=-a_{ji}$: $a=-(-2)=2$, $b=-1$, $c=-3$. So $a+b+c=2-1-3=-2$.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Determinants
    If $A$ is a square matrix of order 3 and $|A|=5$, then $|\operatorname{adj}(2A)|$ is equal to

    1. $100$
    2. $200$
    3. $1600$
    4. $3200$
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $1600$

    For a matrix $B$ of order 3, $|\operatorname{adj}B|=|B|^{2}$. Here $|2A|=2^{3}|A|=40$, so $|\operatorname{adj}(2A)|=40^{2}=1600$.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Matrices
    If $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2\\ 3 & 4\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x\\ 1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}4\\ y\end{bmatrix}$, then the value of $x+y$ is

    1. $10$
    2. $12$
    3. $14$
    4. $6$
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) $12$

    $x+2=4 \Rightarrow x=2$; $y=3x+4=10$. So $x+y=12$.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Continuity and Differentiability
    The derivative of $\log(\sec x+\tan x)$ with respect to $x$ is

    1. $\sec x$
    2. $\tan x$
    3. $\sec x\tan x$
    4. $\sec^{2}x$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\sec x$

    $\frac{d}{dx}\log(\sec x+\tan x)=\frac{\sec x\tan x+\sec^{2}x}{\sec x+\tan x}=\frac{\sec x(\tan x+\sec x)}{\sec x+\tan x}=\sec x$.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Continuity and Differentiability
    If the function $f(x)=\begin{cases}kx^{2}+2, & x\le 2\\ 3x-2, & x\gt 2\end{cases}$ is continuous at $x=2$, then the value of $k$ is

    1. $\frac{1}{2}$
    2. $1$
    3. $2$
    4. $-\frac{1}{2}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\frac{1}{2}$

    Continuity at $x=2$: $f(2)=4k+2$ and $\lim_{x\to 2^{+}}(3x-2)=4$. So $4k+2=4 \Rightarrow k=\frac{1}{2}$.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Application of Derivatives
    The function $f(x)=x^{3}-6x^{2}+9x+5$ is strictly decreasing in the interval

    1. $(1, 3)$
    2. $(-\infty, 1)$
    3. $(3, \infty)$
    4. $(-\infty, 1)\cup(3,\infty)$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $(1, 3)$

    $f'(x)=3x^{2}-12x+9=3(x-1)(x-3)$, which is negative only for $1\lt x\lt 3$.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Application of Derivatives
    The rate of change of the volume of a sphere with respect to its radius $r$, when $r=3$ cm, is

    1. $36\pi\ \mathrm{cm^{3}/cm}$
    2. $12\pi\ \mathrm{cm^{3}/cm}$
    3. $108\pi\ \mathrm{cm^{3}/cm}$
    4. $27\pi\ \mathrm{cm^{3}/cm}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $36\pi\ \mathrm{cm^{3}/cm}$

    $V=\frac{4}{3}\pi r^{3} \Rightarrow \frac{dV}{dr}=4\pi r^{2}=4\pi(9)=36\pi\ \mathrm{cm^{3}/cm}$.

  11. Question 11 · 1 mark · Differential Equations
    The order and degree of the differential equation $\left(\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}\right)^{3}+\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^{4}+y=0$ are respectively

    1. $2, 3$
    2. $3, 2$
    3. $2, 4$
    4. $4, 2$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $2, 3$

    The highest order derivative is $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}$, so the order is 2. The equation is a polynomial in the derivatives and the highest power of $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}$ is 3, so the degree is 3.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Integrals
    $\int e^{x}(\sin x+\cos x)\,dx$ is equal to

    1. $e^{x}\sin x+C$
    2. $e^{x}\cos x+C$
    3. $-e^{x}\sin x+C$
    4. $e^{x}(\sin x-\cos x)+C$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $e^{x}\sin x+C$

    Using $\int e^{x}\{f(x)+f'(x)\}dx=e^{x}f(x)+C$ with $f(x)=\sin x$, $f'(x)=\cos x$, the integral is $e^{x}\sin x+C$.

  13. Question 13 · 1 mark · Vector Algebra
    The unit vector in the direction of the vector $2\hat{i}-\hat{j}+2\hat{k}$ is

    1. $\frac{1}{3}(2\hat{i}-\hat{j}+2\hat{k})$
    2. $\frac{1}{9}(2\hat{i}-\hat{j}+2\hat{k})$
    3. $\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}(2\hat{i}-\hat{j}+2\hat{k})$
    4. $2\hat{i}-\hat{j}+2\hat{k}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\frac{1}{3}(2\hat{i}-\hat{j}+2\hat{k})$

    Magnitude $=\sqrt{4+1+4}=3$, so the unit vector is $\frac{1}{3}(2\hat{i}-\hat{j}+2\hat{k})$.

  14. Question 14 · 1 mark · Vector Algebra
    If $|\vec{a}|=2$, $|\vec{b}|=3$ and $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}=3$, then the angle between $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ is

    1. $\frac{\pi}{6}$
    2. $\frac{\pi}{3}$
    3. $\frac{\pi}{4}$
    4. $\frac{\pi}{2}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) $\frac{\pi}{3}$

    $\cos\theta=\frac{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|}=\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2} \Rightarrow \theta=\frac{\pi}{3}$.

  15. Question 15 · 1 mark · Vector Algebra
    The area of the parallelogram whose adjacent sides are given by the vectors $\hat{i}+\hat{j}$ and $\hat{j}+\hat{k}$ is

    1. $\sqrt{3}$ sq. units
    2. $3$ sq. units
    3. $\sqrt{2}$ sq. units
    4. $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ sq. units
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\sqrt{3}$ sq. units

    $(\hat{i}+\hat{j})\times(\hat{j}+\hat{k})=\hat{i}-\hat{j}+\hat{k}$, whose magnitude is $\sqrt{3}$.

  16. Question 16 · 1 mark · Probability
    If $P(A)=0.6$, $P(B)=0.5$ and $P(A\cap B)=0.2$, then $P(A|B)$ is

    1. $0.4$
    2. $0.2$
    3. $0.6$
    4. $0.5$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $0.4$

    $P(A|B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}=\frac{0.2}{0.5}=0.4$.

  17. Question 17 · 1 mark · Probability
    A die is thrown twice. The probability that the sum of the numbers is 8, given that the first throw shows an even number, is

    1. $\frac{1}{6}$
    2. $\frac{5}{36}$
    3. $\frac{1}{12}$
    4. $\frac{1}{3}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\frac{1}{6}$

    First throw even: 18 outcomes. Of these, sum 8 occurs for $(2,6),(4,4),(6,2)$: 3 outcomes. Probability $=\frac{3}{18}=\frac{1}{6}$.

  18. Question 18 · 1 mark · Probability
    $A$ and $B$ are independent events with $P(A)=\frac{1}{3}$ and $P(B)=\frac{1}{4}$. The probability that neither $A$ nor $B$ occurs is

    1. $\frac{1}{2}$
    2. $\frac{1}{12}$
    3. $\frac{7}{12}$
    4. $\frac{1}{4}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\frac{1}{2}$

    $P(A'\cap B')=P(A')P(B')=\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{3}{4}=\frac{1}{2}$.

  19. Question 19 · 1 mark · Integrals
    Assertion (A): $\int_{-1}^{1}x|x|\,dx=0$.
    Reason (R): $f(x)=x|x|$ is an even function.
    Select the correct option.

    1. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    2. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    3. Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.
    4. Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.

    $f(-x)=(-x)|-x|=-x|x|=-f(x)$, so $x|x|$ is an odd function and its integral over $[-1,1]$ is 0. The Assertion is true; the Reason is false.

  20. Question 20 · 1 mark · Differential Equations
    Assertion (A): The differential equation $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2xy}$ is a homogeneous differential equation.
    Reason (R): $F(x,y)=\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2xy}$ satisfies $F(\lambda x,\lambda y)=\lambda^{0}F(x,y)$ for every non-zero $\lambda$.
    Select the correct option.

    1. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    2. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    3. Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.
    4. Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

    $F(\lambda x,\lambda y)=\frac{\lambda^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})}{\lambda^{2}\cdot 2xy}=F(x,y)$, so $F$ is homogeneous of degree zero, which is exactly why the equation is homogeneous. Both are true and R explains A.

Section B: 2 marks each

  1. Question 21 · 2 marks · Integrals
    Find $\displaystyle\int\frac{\sec^{2}x}{\tan^{2}x+4}\,dx$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Put $\tan x=t$, so $\sec^{2}x\,dx=dt$.
    $\int\frac{dt}{t^{2}+2^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\tan^{-1}\frac{t}{2}+C$
    $=\frac{1}{2}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\tan x}{2}\right)+C$

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct substitution $\tan x=t$, $\sec^{2}x\,dx=dt$, giving $\int\frac{dt}{t^{2}+4}$ (1 mark)
    • Correct answer $\frac{1}{2}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\tan x}{2}\right)+C$ (1 mark)

  2. Question 22 · 2 marks · Continuity and Differentiability
    If $x=a(\theta+\sin\theta)$ and $y=a(1-\cos\theta)$, find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ at $\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $\frac{dx}{d\theta}=a(1+\cos\theta)$, $\frac{dy}{d\theta}=a\sin\theta$
    $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{\sin\theta}{1+\cos\theta}=\frac{2\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}}{2\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}}=\tan\frac{\theta}{2}$
    At $\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}$: $\frac{dy}{dx}=\tan\frac{\pi}{4}=1$

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct $\frac{dx}{d\theta}=a(1+\cos\theta)$ and $\frac{dy}{d\theta}=a\sin\theta$ (1 mark)
    • $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{\sin\theta}{1+\cos\theta}$ (or $\tan\frac{\theta}{2}$) (½ marks)
    • Value at $\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}$ is 1 (½ marks)

  3. Question 23 · 2 marks · Three Dimensional Geometry
    Find the value of $k$ for which the lines $\frac{x-2}{3}=\frac{y+1}{k}=\frac{z-4}{-2}$ and $\frac{x+1}{2}=\frac{y-3}{4}=\frac{z}{5}$ are perpendicular to each other.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Direction ratios of the lines are $3, k, -2$ and $2, 4, 5$.
    For perpendicular lines $a_1a_2+b_1b_2+c_1c_2=0$:
    $3(2)+k(4)+(-2)(5)=0 \Rightarrow 4k-4=0 \Rightarrow k=1$

    Marking scheme:

    • Direction ratios $3, k, -2$ and $2, 4, 5$ with condition $a_1a_2+b_1b_2+c_1c_2=0$ (1 mark)
    • $6+4k-10=0$ (½ marks)
    • $k=1$ (½ marks)

  4. Question 24 · 2 marks · Vector Algebra
    Find the projection of the vector $\vec{a}=3\hat{i}-\hat{j}+2\hat{k}$ on the vector $\vec{b}=2\hat{i}+2\hat{j}+\hat{k}$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Projection of $\vec{a}$ on $\vec{b}$ $=\frac{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|}$
    $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}=6-2+2=6$, $|\vec{b}|=\sqrt{4+4+1}=3$
    Projection $=\frac{6}{3}=2$

    Marking scheme:

    • $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}=6$ (½ marks)
    • $|\vec{b}|=3$ (½ marks)
    • Projection $=\frac{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|}=2$ (1 mark)

  5. Question 25 · 2 marks · Linear Programming
    The corner points of the bounded feasible region of a linear programming problem are $(0,0)$, $(5,0)$, $(4,3)$ and $(0,6)$. The objective function is $Z=px+qy$, where $p, q\gt 0$. Find the relation between $p$ and $q$ so that the maximum value of $Z$ occurs at both the points $(5,0)$ and $(4,3)$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Since the maximum occurs at both $(5,0)$ and $(4,3)$, $Z(5,0)=Z(4,3)$.
    $5p=4p+3q \Rightarrow p=3q$
    (Check: with $p=3q$, $Z(5,0)=Z(4,3)=15q$, which is more than $Z(0,6)=6q$ and $Z(0,0)=0$.)

    Marking scheme:

    • Writing $Z(5,0)=Z(4,3)$, i.e. $5p=4p+3q$ (1 mark)
    • $p=3q$ (1 mark)

Section C: 3 marks each

  1. Question 26 · 3 marks · Matrices
    For the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}2 & 1\\ 1 & 3\end{bmatrix}$, find the value of $k$ such that $A^{2}-5A+kI=O$. Hence, find $A^{-1}$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $A^{2}=\begin{bmatrix}2 & 1\\ 1 & 3\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}2 & 1\\ 1 & 3\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}5 & 5\\ 5 & 10\end{bmatrix}$
    $A^{2}-5A=\begin{bmatrix}5-10 & 5-5\\ 5-5 & 10-15\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-5 & 0\\ 0 & -5\end{bmatrix}=-5I$
    So $A^{2}-5A+5I=O$, i.e. $k=5$.
    $|A|=5\neq 0$, so $A^{-1}$ exists. Multiplying by $A^{-1}$: $A-5I+5A^{-1}=O$
    $A^{-1}=\frac{1}{5}(5I-A)=\frac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix}3 & -1\\ -1 & 2\end{bmatrix}$

    Marking scheme:

    • $A^{2}=\begin{bmatrix}5 & 5\\ 5 & 10\end{bmatrix}$ (1 mark)
    • $k=5$ (1 mark)
    • $A^{-1}=\frac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix}3 & -1\\ -1 & 2\end{bmatrix}$ (1 mark)

  2. Question 27 · 3 marks · Application of Derivatives
    A water tank is in the shape of an inverted right circular cone of height 9 m and radius of the top 3 m, with its axis vertical. Water is pumped into the tank at the constant rate of $2\pi\ \mathrm{m^{3}}$ per minute. Find the rate at which the water level is rising when the depth of water in the tank is 6 m.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Let $h$ be the depth and $r$ the radius of the water surface at time $t$. By similar triangles, $\frac{r}{h}=\frac{3}{9} \Rightarrow r=\frac{h}{3}$.
    $V=\frac{1}{3}\pi r^{2}h=\frac{1}{3}\pi\frac{h^{2}}{9}h=\frac{\pi h^{3}}{27}$
    $\frac{dV}{dt}=\frac{\pi h^{2}}{9}\frac{dh}{dt}$
    At $h=6$: $2\pi=\frac{\pi(36)}{9}\frac{dh}{dt}=4\pi\frac{dh}{dt} \Rightarrow \frac{dh}{dt}=\frac{1}{2}$
    The water level is rising at $\frac{1}{2}$ m/min (0.5 m per minute).

    Marking scheme:

    • $r=\frac{h}{3}$ and $V=\frac{\pi h^{3}}{27}$ (1 mark)
    • $\frac{dV}{dt}=\frac{\pi h^{2}}{9}\frac{dh}{dt}$ (1 mark)
    • $\frac{dh}{dt}=\frac{1}{2}$ m/min (1 mark)

  3. Question 28 · 3 marks · Continuity and Differentiability
    If $x^{2}+xy+y^{2}=7$, find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ and $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}$ at the point $(1, 2)$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Differentiating: $2x+y+x\frac{dy}{dx}+2y\frac{dy}{dx}=0 \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{2x+y}{x+2y}$
    At $(1,2)$: $\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{4}{5}$
    Differentiating $2x+y+xy'+2yy'=0$ again: $2+2y'+xy''+2(y')^{2}+2yy''=0$
    $\Rightarrow (x+2y)y''=-\left[2+2y'+2(y')^{2}\right]$
    At $(1,2)$: $5y''=-\left[2-\frac{8}{5}+\frac{32}{25}\right]=-\frac{42}{25} \Rightarrow \frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}=-\frac{42}{125}$

    Marking scheme:

    • $\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{2x+y}{x+2y}$ (1 mark)
    • $\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{4}{5}$ at $(1,2)$ (½ marks)
    • Correct second differentiation: $2+2y'+xy''+2(y')^{2}+2yy''=0$ (1 mark)
    • $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}=-\frac{42}{125}$ (½ marks)

  4. Question 29 · 3 marks · Differential Equations
    Find the particular solution of the differential equation $\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac{y}{x}=x^{2}$, $x\gt 0$, given that $y=1$ when $x=1$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: This is linear with $P=\frac{1}{x}$, $Q=x^{2}$.
    I.F. $=e^{\int\frac{1}{x}dx}=e^{\log x}=x$
    Solution: $xy=\int x\cdot x^{2}\,dx=\frac{x^{4}}{4}+C$
    $y(1)=1$: $1=\frac{1}{4}+C \Rightarrow C=\frac{3}{4}$
    Particular solution: $xy=\frac{x^{4}}{4}+\frac{3}{4}$, i.e. $y=\frac{x^{3}}{4}+\frac{3}{4x}$

    Marking scheme:

    • I.F. $=x$ (1 mark)
    • General solution $xy=\frac{x^{4}}{4}+C$ (1 mark)
    • $C=\frac{3}{4}$ and particular solution $y=\frac{x^{3}}{4}+\frac{3}{4x}$ (1 mark)

  5. Question 30 · 3 marks · Three Dimensional Geometry
    Find the shortest distance between the lines $\vec{r}=(\hat{i}-\hat{k})+\lambda(\hat{i}+2\hat{j}+2\hat{k})$ and $\vec{r}=(2\hat{i}+3\hat{j}+\hat{k})+\mu(2\hat{i}+\hat{j}-2\hat{k})$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $\vec{a}_1=\hat{i}-\hat{k}$, $\vec{b}_1=\hat{i}+2\hat{j}+2\hat{k}$, $\vec{a}_2=2\hat{i}+3\hat{j}+\hat{k}$, $\vec{b}_2=2\hat{i}+\hat{j}-2\hat{k}$
    $\vec{a}_2-\vec{a}_1=\hat{i}+3\hat{j}+2\hat{k}$
    $\vec{b}_1\times\vec{b}_2=\begin{vmatrix}\hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k}\\ 1 & 2 & 2\\ 2 & 1 & -2\end{vmatrix}=-6\hat{i}+6\hat{j}-3\hat{k}$, $|\vec{b}_1\times\vec{b}_2|=\sqrt{36+36+9}=9$
    $(\vec{a}_2-\vec{a}_1)\cdot(\vec{b}_1\times\vec{b}_2)=-6+18-6=6$
    Shortest distance $=\frac{|6|}{9}=\frac{2}{3}$ units

    Marking scheme:

    • $\vec{b}_1\times\vec{b}_2=-6\hat{i}+6\hat{j}-3\hat{k}$ with magnitude 9 (1 mark)
    • $(\vec{a}_2-\vec{a}_1)\cdot(\vec{b}_1\times\vec{b}_2)=6$ (1 mark)
    • Shortest distance $=\frac{2}{3}$ units (1 mark)

  6. Question 31 · 3 marks · Linear Programming
    Solve the following linear programming problem graphically:
    Maximise $Z=2x+5y$
    subject to the constraints $x+y\le 9$, $x+3y\le 15$, $x\ge 0$, $y\ge 0$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Draw the lines $x+y=9$ (through $(9,0)$, $(0,9)$) and $x+3y=15$ (through $(15,0)$, $(0,5)$). The feasible region is the bounded region $OABC$ on the origin side of both lines in the first quadrant.
    Intersection of $x+y=9$ and $x+3y=15$: $2y=6 \Rightarrow y=3, x=6$.
    Corner points and values of $Z$:

    Corner point$Z=2x+5y$
    $O(0,0)$0
    $A(9,0)$18
    $B(6,3)$27
    $C(0,5)$25
    Maximum value of $Z$ is 27 at $x=6, y=3$.

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct graph and bounded feasible region (1 mark)
    • Corner points $(0,0), (9,0), (6,3), (0,5)$ (1 mark)
    • Maximum $Z=27$ at $(6,3)$ (1 mark)

Section D: 5 marks each

  1. Question 32 · 5 marks · Relations and Functions
    A relation $R$ is defined on the set $\mathbb{Z}$ of integers as $R=\{(a,b): 2a+3b \text{ is divisible by } 5\}$. Show that $R$ is an equivalence relation. Also write the equivalence class $[0]$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Reflexive: For any $a\in\mathbb{Z}$, $2a+3a=5a$ is divisible by 5, so $(a,a)\in R$.
    Symmetric: Let $(a,b)\in R$, so $2a+3b=5k$ for some $k\in\mathbb{Z}$. Then $2b+3a=5(a+b)-(2a+3b)=5(a+b-k)$, which is divisible by 5. So $(b,a)\in R$.
    Transitive: Let $(a,b)\in R$ and $(b,c)\in R$: $2a+3b=5k$, $2b+3c=5m$. Adding, $2a+5b+3c=5(k+m)$, so $2a+3c=5(k+m-b)$, divisible by 5. So $(a,c)\in R$.
    Hence $R$ is an equivalence relation.
    Class [0]: $[0]=\{b\in\mathbb{Z}: 3b \text{ is divisible by } 5\}=\{b: b \text{ is a multiple of } 5\}=\{\ldots,-10,-5,0,5,10,\ldots\}$

    Marking scheme:

    • Reflexive: $2a+3a=5a$ divisible by 5 (1 mark)
    • Symmetric: $2b+3a=5(a+b)-(2a+3b)$ shown divisible by 5 (1½ marks)
    • Transitive: $2a+3c=5(k+m)-5b$ shown divisible by 5 (1½ marks)
    • $[0]=\{\ldots,-10,-5,0,5,10,\ldots\}$ (multiples of 5) (1 mark)

  2. Question 33 · 5 marks · Integrals
    Evaluate: $\displaystyle\int_{3}^{5}\frac{x^{2}+8x-2}{(x+1)^{2}(x-2)}\,dx$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Let $\frac{x^{2}+8x-2}{(x+1)^{2}(x-2)}=\frac{A}{x-2}+\frac{B}{x+1}+\frac{C}{(x+1)^{2}}$
    $x^{2}+8x-2=A(x+1)^{2}+B(x-2)(x+1)+C(x-2)$
    $x=2$: $18=9A \Rightarrow A=2$; $x=-1$: $-9=-3C \Rightarrow C=3$; coefficient of $x^{2}$: $1=A+B \Rightarrow B=-1$
    $\int\left[\frac{2}{x-2}-\frac{1}{x+1}+\frac{3}{(x+1)^{2}}\right]dx=2\log|x-2|-\log|x+1|-\frac{3}{x+1}$
    $I=\left[2\log|x-2|-\log|x+1|-\frac{3}{x+1}\right]_{3}^{5}$
    $=(2\log 3-\log 6-\frac{1}{2})-(0-\log 4-\frac{3}{4})$
    $=2\log 3-\log\frac{6}{4}+\frac{1}{4}=\log\frac{9\times 2}{3}+\frac{1}{4}=\log 6+\frac{1}{4}$

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct form of partial fractions $\frac{A}{x-2}+\frac{B}{x+1}+\frac{C}{(x+1)^{2}}$ (1 mark)
    • $A=2$, $B=-1$, $C=3$ (1½ marks)
    • Antiderivative $2\log|x-2|-\log|x+1|-\frac{3}{x+1}$ (1½ marks)
    • Correct value $\log 6+\frac{1}{4}$ (1 mark)

  3. Question 34 · 5 marks · Application of Integrals
    Using integration, find the area of the region bounded by the parabola $y=x^{2}$ and the line $y=2x+3$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Sketch: the upward parabola $y=x^{2}$ with vertex at the origin and the line $y=2x+3$ cutting it at two points; the region lies above the parabola and below the line.
    Points of intersection: $x^{2}=2x+3 \Rightarrow x^{2}-2x-3=0 \Rightarrow x=-1, 3$, i.e. $(-1,1)$ and $(3,9)$.
    Area $=\int_{-1}^{3}\left[(2x+3)-x^{2}\right]dx$
    $=\left[x^{2}+3x-\frac{x^{3}}{3}\right]_{-1}^{3}=(9+9-9)-\left(1-3+\frac{1}{3}\right)=9+\frac{5}{3}$
    $=\frac{32}{3}$ sq. units

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct sketch showing the region between the line and the parabola (1 mark)
    • Points of intersection $(-1,1)$ and $(3,9)$ (1 mark)
    • Area $=\int_{-1}^{3}\left[(2x+3)-x^{2}\right]dx$ (1 mark)
    • Correct integration $\left[x^{2}+3x-\frac{x^{3}}{3}\right]_{-1}^{3}$ (1 mark)
    • Area $=\frac{32}{3}$ sq. units (1 mark)

  4. Question 35 · 5 marks · Probability
    An online shop sends its parcels through three courier partners X, Y and Z, which handle 50%, 30% and 20% of the parcels respectively. From past records, 2%, 3% and 5% of the parcels handled by X, Y and Z respectively are delivered late.
    (i) Find the probability that a parcel chosen at random is delivered late.
    (ii) A parcel is found to have been delivered late. Find the probability that it was handled by courier partner Y.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Let $E_1, E_2, E_3$: parcel handled by X, Y, Z; $L$: parcel delivered late. $E_1, E_2, E_3$ form a partition of the sample space.
    $P(E_1)=0.5$, $P(E_2)=0.3$, $P(E_3)=0.2$; $P(L|E_1)=0.02$, $P(L|E_2)=0.03$, $P(L|E_3)=0.05$
    (i) By the theorem of total probability:
    $P(L)=0.5(0.02)+0.3(0.03)+0.2(0.05)=0.010+0.009+0.010=0.029$
    (ii) By Bayes' theorem:
    $P(E_2|L)=\frac{P(E_2)P(L|E_2)}{P(L)}=\frac{0.009}{0.029}=\frac{9}{29}$

    Marking scheme:

    • Events defined with $P(E_1)=0.5, P(E_2)=0.3, P(E_3)=0.2$ and the conditional probabilities $0.02, 0.03, 0.05$ (1 mark)
    • Total probability formula $P(L)=\sum P(E_i)P(L|E_i)$ (1 mark)
    • $P(L)=0.029$ (1 mark)
    • Bayes' theorem $P(E_2|L)=\frac{P(E_2)P(L|E_2)}{P(L)}$ (1 mark)
    • $P(E_2|L)=\frac{9}{29}$ (1 mark)

  1. Question 36 · 4 marks · Three Dimensional Geometry
    Two drones are flying in straight lines during a light show. With respect to a fixed origin on the ground, their paths are along the lines
    $L_1: \vec{r}=(\hat{i}+2\hat{j}+3\hat{k})+\lambda(2\hat{i}+3\hat{j}+6\hat{k})$
    $L_2: \vec{r}=(-3\hat{i}+3\hat{j}+12\hat{k})+\mu(6\hat{i}+2\hat{j}-3\hat{k})$
    Based on the given information, answer the following questions:

    (i) Find the direction cosines of the line $L_1$. [1]

    (ii) Show that the paths $L_1$ and $L_2$ are perpendicular to each other. [1]

    (iii) Show that the paths $L_1$ and $L_2$ intersect and find the point at which they meet. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Find the direction cosines of the line $L_1$.

    Model answer: Direction ratios of $L_1$: $2, 3, 6$; $\sqrt{4+9+36}=7$.
    Direction cosines: $\frac{2}{7}, \frac{3}{7}, \frac{6}{7}$

    Marking scheme:

    • Direction cosines $\frac{2}{7}, \frac{3}{7}, \frac{6}{7}$ (1 mark)

    (ii) Show that the paths $L_1$ and $L_2$ are perpendicular to each other.

    Model answer: $(2\hat{i}+3\hat{j}+6\hat{k})\cdot(6\hat{i}+2\hat{j}-3\hat{k})=12+6-18=0$, so the lines are perpendicular.

    Marking scheme:

    • Dot product of direction vectors $=12+6-18=0$, hence perpendicular (1 mark)

    (iii) Show that the paths $L_1$ and $L_2$ intersect and find the point at which they meet.

    Model answer: A general point on $L_1$: $(1+2\lambda, 2+3\lambda, 3+6\lambda)$; on $L_2$: $(-3+6\mu, 3+2\mu, 12-3\mu)$.
    Equating: $1+2\lambda=-3+6\mu$ and $2+3\lambda=3+2\mu$, i.e. $\lambda-3\mu=-2$ and $3\lambda-2\mu=1$.
    Solving: $\mu=1$, $\lambda=1$.
    Check in the third coordinate: $3+6(1)=9$ and $12-3(1)=9$. Equal, so the lines intersect.
    Point of intersection: $(3, 5, 9)$

    Marking scheme:

    • Solving two equations to get $\lambda=1$, $\mu=1$ (1 mark)
    • Third coordinate checked (both 9) and point $(3,5,9)$ (1 mark)

  2. Question 37 · 4 marks · Application of Derivatives
    A farmer has 200 m of fencing wire. He wants to enclose a rectangular field that lies along a straight river bank. No fencing is needed along the river. Let $x$ metres be the length of each of the two sides perpendicular to the river.
    Based on the given information, answer the following questions:

    (i) Express the area $A$ of the field as a function of $x$. [1]

    (ii) Find the value of $x$ for which $A'(x)=0$. [1]

    (iii) Using the second derivative test, show that the area is maximum at this value of $x$ and find the maximum area. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Express the area $A$ of the field as a function of $x$.

    Model answer: Side parallel to the river $=200-2x$ m. $A(x)=x(200-2x)=200x-2x^{2}$

    Marking scheme:

    • $A(x)=200x-2x^{2}$ (1 mark)

    (ii) Find the value of $x$ for which $A'(x)=0$.

    Model answer: $A'(x)=200-4x=0 \Rightarrow x=50$

    Marking scheme:

    • $A'(x)=200-4x$ and $x=50$ (1 mark)

    (iii) Using the second derivative test, show that the area is maximum at this value of $x$ and find the maximum area.

    Model answer: $A''(x)=-4\lt 0$, so $A$ is maximum at $x=50$.
    Maximum area $=50(200-100)=5000\ \mathrm{m^{2}}$

    Marking scheme:

    • $A''(x)=-4\lt 0$, hence maximum at $x=50$ (1 mark)
    • Maximum area $=5000\ \mathrm{m^{2}}$ (1 mark)

  3. Question 38 · 4 marks · Determinants
    A welfare group bought rice, wheat flour and pulses for three community kitchens. Let the prices be Rs $x$ per kg of rice, Rs $y$ per kg of wheat flour and Rs $z$ per kg of pulses.
    Kitchen 1: 1 kg rice, 1 kg flour and 1 kg pulses cost Rs 170.
    Kitchen 2: 2 kg rice, 1 kg flour and 3 kg pulses cost Rs 410.
    Kitchen 3: 1 kg rice, 2 kg flour and 1 kg pulses cost Rs 200.
    Based on the given information, answer the following questions:

    (i) Write the system of equations in the matrix form $AX=B$ and find $|A|$ and $\operatorname{adj}A$. [2]

    (ii) Using $A^{-1}$, find the price per kg of each item. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Write the system of equations in the matrix form $AX=B$ and find $|A|$ and $\operatorname{adj}A$.

    Model answer: $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 1\\ 2 & 1 & 3\\ 1 & 2 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x\\ y\\ z\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}170\\ 410\\ 200\end{bmatrix}$
    $|A|=1(1-6)-1(2-3)+1(4-1)=-5+1+3=-1$
    Cofactors: $C_{11}=-5, C_{12}=1, C_{13}=3, C_{21}=1, C_{22}=0, C_{23}=-1, C_{31}=2, C_{32}=-1, C_{33}=-1$
    $\operatorname{adj}A=\begin{bmatrix}-5 & 1 & 2\\ 1 & 0 & -1\\ 3 & -1 & -1\end{bmatrix}$

    Marking scheme:

    • Matrix form $AX=B$ and $|A|=-1$ (1 mark)
    • $\operatorname{adj}A=\begin{bmatrix}-5 & 1 & 2\\ 1 & 0 & -1\\ 3 & -1 & -1\end{bmatrix}$ (1 mark)

    (ii) Using $A^{-1}$, find the price per kg of each item.

    Model answer: $A^{-1}=\frac{1}{|A|}\operatorname{adj}A=\begin{bmatrix}5 & -1 & -2\\ -1 & 0 & 1\\ -3 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}$
    $X=A^{-1}B$: $x=850-410-400=40$, $y=-170+0+200=30$, $z=-510+410+200=100$
    Rice Rs 40 per kg, wheat flour Rs 30 per kg, pulses Rs 100 per kg.

    Marking scheme:

    • $A^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}5 & -1 & -2\\ -1 & 0 & 1\\ -3 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}$ and $X=A^{-1}B$ (1 mark)
    • $x=40$, $y=30$, $z=100$ (Rs per kg) (1 mark)
