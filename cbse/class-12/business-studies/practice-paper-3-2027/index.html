Section A: 1 mark each

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Nature and Significance of Management
    Management is often compared with a profession. Which of the following features of a profession is fully satisfied by management?

    1. Restricted entry through a prescribed degree or examination
    2. A well-defined, systematic body of knowledge
    3. A code of conduct that is legally binding on all managers
    4. Compulsory membership of a professional association
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) A well-defined, systematic body of knowledge

    Management has a systematic body of knowledge (principles and theories) that can be learnt. Entry into management is not restricted by any prescribed degree, membership of a body such as AIMA is not compulsory, and its code of conduct has no legal force. So only this feature is fully satisfied.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Principles of Management
    In the store room of Lakshmi Engineering, every tool has a fixed rack with a label, and every store-keeper has a fixed counter. As a result, no time is wasted in searching for tools or people. Which principle of management given by Henri Fayol is being followed?

    1. Discipline
    2. Unity of direction
    3. Order
    4. Stability of personnel
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Order

    Fayol's principle of order means 'a place for everything (everyone) and everything (everyone) in its place'. It avoids loss of time and materials in searching.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Organising
    Which of the following statements about delegation and decentralisation is correct?

    1. Delegation is a compulsory act, whereas decentralisation is an optional policy decision.
    2. Decentralisation is a narrow concept limited to a superior and his immediate subordinate.
    3. Under delegation, accountability is also passed on to the subordinate.
    4. Decentralisation means that all decisions are taken by top management.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Delegation is a compulsory act, whereas decentralisation is an optional policy decision.

    No manager can do all the work alone, so delegation is compulsory. Decentralisation, the systematic delegation of authority to all levels, is an optional policy decision of top management. Delegation is the narrower concept; accountability cannot be delegated; taking all decisions at the top is centralisation.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Business Environment
    Navya Textiles arranges loans from banks, buys cotton from farmers' co-operatives, hires skilled weavers trained at local institutes and gets electricity from the state grid. It then sells its fabric to customers in the market. Which point of importance of the business environment does this highlight?

    1. It helps in identifying threats and early warning signals.
    2. It helps in coping with rapid changes.
    3. It helps in tapping useful resources.
    4. It helps in improving performance.
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) It helps in tapping useful resources.

    The environment is a source of the resources a business needs, such as finance, raw materials, labour and power; the business obtains them from the environment and supplies its output back to it. This is 'tapping useful resources'.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Controlling
    After the managers of Pearl Jewellers began keeping a close check on the activities of the employees in the showroom and the store, cases of dishonest behaviour and pilferage fell sharply. Which point of importance of controlling is highlighted here?

    1. Accomplishing organisational goals
    2. Judging the accuracy of standards
    3. Improving employee motivation
    4. Ensuring order and discipline

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Financial Markets
    On a stock exchange, the share price of Asha Motors Ltd. rose after the company reported higher profits, and fell later when the demand for its shares declined. The exchange thus values securities on the basis of the forces of demand and supply. Which function of a stock exchange is highlighted here?

    1. Providing liquidity and marketability to existing securities
    2. Ensuring safety of transactions
    3. Spreading of equity cult
    4. Pricing of securities
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Pricing of securities

    Share prices on a stock exchange are determined by the forces of demand and supply, and the exchange is a mechanism for the constant valuation of securities. This is the function of pricing of securities.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Principles of Management
    At Mehra Tools, workers are selected scientifically, and each worker is given training that matches his or her abilities, so that each one can work at the highest efficiency and earn more. Which principle of Scientific Management is being followed?

    1. Development of each and every person to his or her greatest efficiency and prosperity
    2. Harmony, not discord
    3. Cooperation, not individualism
    4. Science, not rule of thumb
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Development of each and every person to his or her greatest efficiency and prosperity

    Taylor's principle of development of each and every person to his or her greatest efficiency and prosperity calls for scientific selection of workers and training suited to their abilities, which raises both efficiency and earnings.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Financial Management
    Trading on equity (using debt to increase earnings per share) will be favourable to the equity shareholders when:

    1. the rate of interest on debt is higher than the return on investment
    2. the return on investment is higher than the rate of interest on debt
    3. the company has no debt in its capital structure
    4. the tax rate is zero
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) the return on investment is higher than the rate of interest on debt

    Trading on equity raises EPS only when the return on investment is higher than the cost (rate of interest) of debt; the extra return then goes to the equity shareholders.

  13. Question 13 · 1 mark · Financial Markets
    In the trading procedure at a stock exchange, an investor must open an account with a depository participant in which the shares bought are held in electronic form. This account is called a:

    1. Savings bank account
    2. Current account
    3. Demat account
    4. Fixed deposit account
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Demat account

    A dematerialised (demat) account, opened with a depository participant, holds securities in electronic form. Shares bought are credited to it and shares sold are debited from it.

  14. Question 14 · 1 mark · Planning

  16. Question 16 · 1 mark · Marketing
    Lotus Ltd. has registered its brand name 'Glow' and its logo under the law, so that no other firm can use them. A brand, or a part of a brand, that is given legal protection in this way is called a:

    1. Trade mark
    2. Brand mark
    3. Label
    4. Grade
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Trade mark

    A brand or part of a brand that is given legal protection is called a trade mark; no other firm can use it. A brand mark is only the part of a brand that can be recognised but not spoken (a symbol or logo), a label carries information about the product, and a grade is a quality class.

  17. Question 17 · 1 mark · Financial Management

    1. Diversification
    2. Financing alternatives
    3. Scale of operations
    4. Level of collaboration
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Level of collaboration

    Sharing facilities with other organisations (level of collaboration) reduces the investment in fixed assets each one needs. NCERT gives the example of banks sharing ATMs.

  18. Question 18 · 1 mark · Marketing
    A tube of toothpaste is sold to customers in a printed cardboard box. The cardboard box is an example of:

    1. Primary packaging
    2. Transportation packaging
    3. Labelling
    4. Secondary packaging
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Secondary packaging

    Primary packaging is the product's immediate container (the tube). Secondary packaging is an additional layer of protection kept till the product is ready for use, such as the cardboard box. Transportation packaging is used for storage and transport, such as a carton holding many boxes.

  19. Question 19 · 1 mark · Financial Management
    Ravi Ltd. needs ₹20,00,000 for a new project. Plan I: issue equity shares of ₹10 each for the whole amount. Plan II: issue equity shares of ₹10 each for ₹10,00,000 and 12% debentures for ₹10,00,000. Its earnings before interest and tax (EBIT) are expected to be ₹2,00,000 and the tax rate is 30%. Which plan gives the higher earnings per share (EPS)?

    1. Plan II, with EPS of ₹0.80 against ₹0.70 under Plan I
    2. Plan I, with EPS of ₹0.70 against ₹0.56 under Plan II
    3. Plan II, with EPS of ₹1.40 against ₹0.70 under Plan I
    4. Both plans give the same EPS of ₹0.70
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Plan I, with EPS of ₹0.70 against ₹0.56 under Plan II

    Plan I: EBT = ₹2,00,000; tax 30% = ₹60,000; EAT = ₹1,40,000; shares = 2,00,000; EPS = ₹0.70. Plan II: interest = 12% of ₹10,00,000 = ₹1,20,000; EBT = ₹80,000; tax = ₹24,000; EAT = ₹56,000; shares = 1,00,000; EPS = ₹0.56. The return on investment (2,00,000/20,00,000 = 10%) is lower than the rate of interest (12%), so using debt lowers the EPS. (₹0.80 ignores tax; ₹1.40 ignores interest.)

  2. Question 22 · 3 marks · Directing
    Three managers of Vardhan Logistics lead their teams in different ways:
    (i) Arjun takes all the decisions himself, gives orders and expects his team to obey them without question.
    (ii) Bina discusses problems with her team, invites their suggestions and takes decisions after consulting them.

  3. Question 23 · 3 marks · Financial Management
    Explain the following factors affecting the working capital requirements of a company:
    (a) Nature of business
    (b) Operating efficiency

    OR Explain the following factors affecting the investment (capital budgeting) decision of a company:
    (a) Cash flows of the project
    (b) The rate of return
    (c) The investment criteria involved

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Nature of business: The basic nature of a business influences the amount of working capital needed. A trading organisation usually needs a smaller amount of working capital than a manufacturing organisation, because there is usually no processing; the cycle from cash to inventory to cash is short. Service businesses, which hold little or no inventory, need still less.
    (b) Operating efficiency: Firms manage their operations with different degrees of efficiency. Efficient management of inventory and debtors (better stock control, quicker collection from customers) reduces the amount of working capital needed; inefficiency increases it.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Nature of business: heading (½ marks)
    • (a) Explanation: trading and service firms need less working capital than manufacturing firms (1 mark)
    • (b) Operating efficiency: heading (½ marks)
    • (b) Explanation: efficient management of inventory and debtors reduces working capital needs (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (a) Cash flows of the project: When a company takes an investment decision involving a huge amount, it expects to generate cash flows over a period. These cash flows are a series of cash receipts and payments over the life of the investment, and they should be carefully analysed before the decision is taken.
    (b) The rate of return: The most important criterion is the return from the proposal. For example, if one project is expected to earn 10% and another 12% with the same risk, the second is preferred.
    (c) The investment criteria involved: A decision to invest in a project involves many calculations about the amount of investment, interest rate, cash flows and rate of return. There are different techniques (capital budgeting techniques) to evaluate projects, and these are applied before a proposal is accepted.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Cash flows of the project, explained (1 mark)
    • (b) The rate of return, explained (1 mark)
    • (c) The investment criteria involved, explained (1 mark)

  1. Question 25 · 4 marks · Principles of Management
    Explain the following principles of management given by Henri Fayol:
    (a) Unity of command
    (b) Discipline

    OR Explain the following techniques of Scientific Management given by F.W. Taylor:
    (a) Standardisation and simplification of work
    (b) Method study

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Unity of command: There should be one and only one boss for every individual employee; an employee should receive orders from only one superior. If an employee gets orders from two superiors at the same time, authority is undermined, discipline is weakened, there is confusion about which order to follow, and order and stability are disturbed. So the dual subordination of an employee should be avoided.
    (b) Discipline: Discipline is the obedience to organisational rules and the employment agreement that are necessary for the working of the organisation. According to Fayol, discipline requires good superiors at all levels, clear and fair agreements, and judicious application of penalties. For example, both workers and management should honour their commitments: workers should work to the agreed standards and management should pay the promised wages on time.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Unity of command: heading / meaning (one boss for each employee) (½ marks)
    • (a) Explanation, with the effect of violating it (1½ marks)
    • (b) Discipline: heading / meaning (obedience to rules and the employment agreement) (½ marks)
    • (b) Explanation (good superiors, clear and fair agreements, judicious penalties) (1½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (a) Standardisation and simplification of work: Standardisation means setting standards for every business activity, such as the process, raw materials, time, product, machinery, methods and working conditions. Its aims include making each process and product of a fixed type, quality and size, and establishing interchangeability of parts. Simplification means eliminating unnecessary diversity of products, such as varieties, sizes and dimensions. It saves the cost of labour, machines and tools, and leads to fuller use of equipment and higher turnover.
    (b) Method study: The objective of method study is to find out the one best way of doing a job. There are various methods of doing a job; to find the best one, the whole process, from buying raw materials to delivering the final product, is studied using techniques such as process charts and operations research. The aim is to minimise the cost of production and maximise the quality and the satisfaction of the customer.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Standardisation and simplification: heading / meaning (½ marks)
    • (a) Explanation (standards for activities; removing unnecessary diversity; benefits) (1½ marks)
    • (b) Method study: heading / meaning (one best way of doing a job) (½ marks)
    • (b) Explanation (study of the whole process; process charts, operations research; lower cost, better quality) (1½ marks)

  4. Question 28 · 4 marks · Directing
    Explain any four non-financial incentives that a manager can use to motivate employees.

    OR Explain any four points of importance of directing.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Non-financial incentives (any four):
    1. Status: the ranking of positions in the organisation. Higher status (authority, responsibility, perquisites) satisfies psychological, social and esteem needs.
    2. Organisational climate: the characteristics that describe an organisation, such as individual autonomy, reward orientation and consideration for employees. A positive climate motivates employees.
    3. Career advancement opportunity: opportunities for growth and promotion to higher levels, supported by suitable skill development programmes.
    4. Job enrichment: designing jobs to include a greater variety of work content, a higher level of knowledge and skill, more autonomy and responsibility, and opportunity for personal growth.
    5. Employee recognition programmes: acknowledging and appreciating good work, for example by displaying the names of the best performers or giving certificates.
    6. Job security: some certainty about future income and work, so that employees do not worry about losing their jobs.
    7. Employee participation: involving employees in decisions on issues related to them, for example through joint management committees.
    8. Employee empowerment: giving more autonomy and power to subordinates, which makes them feel that their jobs are important.

    Marking scheme:

    4. Directing facilitates the introduction of changes: people often resist change; through effective communication and motivation, directing helps employees accept the changes needed in the organisation.
    5. Directing brings stability and balance: effective leadership, communication, supervision and motivation help to maintain stability and balance among the different parts of the organisation.

    Swaad Foods Ltd. makes ready-to-eat snacks. Its Marketing Manager made the following observations about its business environment:
    (i) 'The incomes of middle-class families have risen, so they are spending more on packaged food.'
    (ii) 'People are becoming more conscious about health and fitness, and many prefer snacks made from millets and with less oil.'
    (iii) 'New packaging machines can now keep snacks fresh for longer without adding preservatives.'
    (iv) 'A stable government and peaceful conditions in the state have encouraged us to set up a new factory there.'
    Identify the dimension of the business environment in each observation, quoting the relevant words.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Economic environment: 'incomes of middle-class families have risen ... spending more' (income levels and spending power are economic factors).
    (ii) Social environment: 'more conscious about health and fitness' (changing attitudes, values and lifestyles of people).
    (iii) Technological environment: 'new packaging machines' (new methods and techniques of producing and packing goods).
    (iv) Political environment: 'a stable government and peaceful conditions in the state' (political conditions, such as stability and peace in the country and the attitude of the government towards business).

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Economic environment identified (½ marks)
    • (i) Relevant words quoted (½ marks)
    • (ii) Social environment identified (½ marks)
    • (ii) Relevant words quoted (½ marks)
    • (iii) Technological environment identified (½ marks)
    • (iii) Relevant words quoted (½ marks)
    • (iv) Political environment identified (½ marks)
    • (iv) Relevant words quoted (½ marks)

  6. Question 30 · 4 marks · Nature and Significance of Management
    When Mr Suresh Iyer became the Managing Director of Shree Cotton Mills, the company was making losses. Within three years:
    (i) all the departments began working towards the same company targets instead of their own separate aims;
    (ii) the cost of production fell, as resources were planned and used better and wastage was reduced;
    (iii) the employees, who had earlier resisted new machines, accepted the new technology and adapted to the changing market;
    (iv) the workers received better wages, training and opportunities to grow, and team spirit among them improved.
    Identify the points of importance of management highlighted in (i) to (iv).

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Management helps in achieving group goals: it gives a common direction to the individual efforts of all departments towards the overall goals of the organisation.
    (ii) Management increases efficiency: it aims to reduce costs and increase productivity through better planning, organising, directing, staffing and controlling of activities.
    (iii) Management creates a dynamic organisation: it helps people adapt to changes in the environment, such as new technology, so that the organisation keeps its competitive edge.
    (iv) Management helps in achieving personal objectives: through motivation and leadership, it helps individuals achieve personal goals (better rewards, growth, team spirit) while contributing to the organisation's objectives.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Achieving group goals (1 mark)
    • (ii) Increases efficiency (1 mark)
    • (iii) Creates a dynamic organisation (1 mark)
    • (iv) Helps in achieving personal objectives (1 mark)

  1. Question 31 · 6 marks · Marketing
    Arogya Foods Ltd. is launching a millet-based breakfast cereal. Its marketing team took the following decisions:
    (i) The cereal will contain no added sugar, will be sold in 250 g and 500 g packs under the brand name 'MilletMorn', and will be packed in a resealable pouch.
    (ii) The 500 g pack will be sold at ₹180, after studying the cost of production and the prices of competing cereals; retailers will get a trade discount of 10%.
    (iii) The cereal will be sold through supermarkets and online grocery platforms, and warehouses will be set up in four cities so that stock reaches the shops on time.
    (iv) Customers will be informed through television and social media advertisements, and free sample packs will be given in housing societies.
    Identify and explain the four elements of the marketing mix covered by these decisions.

    OR Describe the following factors that determine the price of a product:
    (a) Pricing objectives
    (b) Extent of competition
    (c) Government and legal regulations

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Product: Product means anything of value offered to the market for the purpose of exchange; it is a bundle of utilities that satisfies customers' needs. Product decisions cover features, quality, pack sizes, branding, packaging and labelling (no added sugar, 250 g and 500 g packs, brand name 'MilletMorn', resealable pouch).
    (ii) Price: Price is the amount of money customers have to pay to obtain the product. Pricing decisions depend on factors such as the cost of the product, the demand and the prices of competitors, and include discounts and credit terms (₹180 per pack, 10% trade discount).
    (iii) Place (physical distribution): Place covers the activities that make the product available to the target customers at the right place and time. It includes the choice of channels of distribution (supermarkets, online grocery platforms) and physical distribution such as warehousing, transportation, inventory control and order processing (warehouses in four cities).
    (iv) Promotion: Promotion means informing customers about the product and persuading them to buy it. Its elements are advertising, personal selling, sales promotion and public relations (television and social media advertisements; free samples, which are a sales promotion).

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Product identified (½ marks)
    • (i) Product explained, linked to the case (1 mark)
    • (ii) Price identified (½ marks)
    • (ii) Price explained, linked to the case (1 mark)
    • (iii) Place / physical distribution identified (½ marks)
    • (iii) Place explained, linked to the case (1 mark)
    • (iv) Promotion identified (½ marks)
    • (iv) Promotion explained, linked to the case (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (a) Pricing objectives: The price of a product is affected by what the firm wants to achieve through pricing. The objectives may be: (i) profit maximisation, in the short run (high price) or in the long run (lower price to build a large market); (ii) obtaining a larger market share, for which a low price is charged; (iii) surviving in a competitive market, for which the price may be reduced or a discount offered to clear stock; (iv) attaining product quality leadership, for which a higher price is charged to cover the cost of high quality and research.
    (b) Extent of competition: When there is little or no competition, the firm has more freedom to fix a higher price, up to the upper limit set by demand. When there is free competition, the price is set close to the prices charged by competitors for similar products, near the lower limit set by cost.
    (c) Government and legal regulations: To protect the public against unfair practices in pricing, the government can step in and regulate the prices of commodities, especially essential products such as medicines. It may declare a product an essential commodity and fix or cap its price.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Pricing objectives: heading (½ marks)
    • (a) Explanation with objectives such as profit maximisation, market share, survival, quality leadership (1½ marks)
    • (b) Extent of competition: heading (½ marks)
    • (b) Explanation: little competition allows a higher price; free competition keeps price near competitors' prices (1½ marks)
    • (c) Government and legal regulations: heading (½ marks)
    • (c) Explanation: government may regulate prices to protect the public, e.g. essential commodities (1½ marks)

  2. Question 32 · 6 marks · Financial Markets
    An organisation was set up by the Government of India in 1988 to regulate and develop the securities market, and was given statutory powers in 1992. Among its activities are the following:
    1. It registers and regulates stock brokers, sub-brokers, merchant bankers, underwriters and mutual funds.
    2. It conducts training programmes for intermediaries of the securities market and carries out research on the market.
    3. It promotes fair practices and a code of conduct for dealings in the securities market.
    Answer the following questions:

    (a) Name the organisation described above. [1]

    (b) State any two objectives of this organisation. [2]

    (c) Identify the category of functions to which each of the activities 1, 2 and 3 belongs, and state one more function of each category. [3]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (a) Name the organisation described above.

    Model answer: Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI).

    Marking scheme:

    • SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India) named (1 mark)

    (b) State any two objectives of this organisation.

    Model answer: Objectives of SEBI (any two):
    1. To regulate stock exchanges and the securities industry, to promote their orderly functioning.
    2. To protect the rights and interests of investors, particularly individual investors, and to guide and educate them.
    3. To prevent trading malpractices and achieve a balance between self-regulation by the securities industry and its statutory regulation.
    4. To regulate and develop a code of conduct and fair practices for intermediaries such as brokers and merchant bankers, with a view to making them competitive and professional.

    Marking scheme:

    • First objective correctly stated (1 mark)
    • Second objective correctly stated (1 mark)

    (c) Identify the category of functions to which each of the activities 1, 2 and 3 belongs, and state one more function of each category.

    Model answer: 1. Regulatory function. Another regulatory function: registration of collective investment schemes; regulation of takeover bids by companies; conducting inquiries and audits of stock exchanges and intermediaries; levying fees.
    2. Development function. Another development function: undertaking measures to develop the capital market by adopting a flexible approach (for example, permitting internet trading through registered stock brokers).
    3. Protective function. Another protective function: prohibiting fraudulent and unfair trade practices, such as misleading statements and price rigging; controlling insider trading and imposing penalties for it; undertaking steps for investor protection.

    Marking scheme:

    • 1: Regulatory function (½ marks)
    • 1: one more regulatory function (½ marks)
    • 2: Development function (½ marks)
    • 2: one more development function (½ marks)
    • 3: Protective function (½ marks)
    • 3: one more protective function (½ marks)

  3. Question 33 · 6 marks · Organising
    Cosmo Consumer Ltd. makes three product lines: personal care products, packaged foods and home cleaning products. Each product line is run as a separate unit with its own production, marketing, finance and human resource heads. The head of each unit is responsible for the profit or loss of that unit. Last year the company added a fourth unit for baby care products without disturbing the existing units.

    (a) Identify the organisation structure adopted by Cosmo Consumer Ltd. [1]

    (b) Explain any three advantages of this structure. [3]

    (c) State any two limitations of this structure. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (a) Identify the organisation structure adopted by Cosmo Consumer Ltd.

    Model answer: Divisional structure (the organisation is divided into separate units, or divisions, on the basis of products, each with its own functional departments).

    Marking scheme:

    • Divisional structure identified (1 mark)

    (b) Explain any three advantages of this structure.

    Model answer: Advantages of divisional structure (any three):
    1. Development of managerial skills: product specialisation helps in the development of varied skills in a divisional head, which prepares him or her for higher positions.
    2. Easy fixation of responsibility: each divisional head is accountable for the profit or loss of the division, so performance can be assessed easily and remedial action taken.
    3. Flexibility and initiative: each division works as an autonomous unit, which leads to faster decision making.
    4. Easy expansion and growth: new divisions can be added without disturbing the existing ones (as with the baby care unit).

    Marking scheme:

    • First advantage, explained (1 mark)
    • Second advantage, explained (1 mark)
    • Third advantage, explained (1 mark)

    (c) State any two limitations of this structure.

    Model answer: Limitations (any two):
    1. Conflict may arise among different divisions over the allocation of funds and other resources.
    2. It may be costly, because each division has its own set of similar functions, so activities and resources are duplicated.
    3. It gives managers the authority to supervise all activities of a division; in course of time a manager may gain too much power and ignore the interests of the organisation as a whole.

    Marking scheme:

    • First limitation (1 mark)
    • Second limitation (1 mark)

  4. Question 34 · 6 marks · Planning
    Swift Bikes Ltd. took the following steps while planning its growth:
    (i) It decided to increase its market share from 10% to 15% within two years.
    (ii) Its managers assumed that fuel prices would keep rising and that the government would continue its support for electric vehicles.
    (iii) They listed three ways of reaching the target: launching an electric scooter, entering rural markets with a low-priced motorcycle, or buying a smaller rival company.
    (iv) Each of the three ways was examined in terms of its cost, risk and likely returns.
    (v) The electric scooter was finally chosen as the best way.
    (vi) Production schedules were drawn up, resources were arranged and the sales team was given monthly targets.
    Identify and explain the six steps of the planning process described above.

    OR Explain the following types of plans, giving one example of each:
    (a) Method
    (b) Programme
    (c) Rule

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Setting objectives: the first step is to set the objectives or goals to be achieved, stated clearly for the whole organisation (raise market share from 10% to 15% in two years).
    (ii) Developing premises: planning is concerned with the future, so managers make assumptions about the future; these assumptions are called premises (rising fuel prices, continued government support).
    (iii) Identifying alternative courses of action: once objectives are set and premises developed, the various ways of achieving the objectives are identified (three ways listed).
    (iv) Evaluating alternative courses: the positive and negative aspects of each alternative are weighed in the light of their feasibility and consequences (cost, risk and returns examined).
    (v) Selecting an alternative: the best plan, the most feasible, profitable and with the least negative consequences, is adopted (electric scooter chosen).
    (vi) Implementing the plan: the plan is put into action; this involves the other managerial functions, such as organising resources and assigning work (production schedules, resources, sales targets).

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Setting objectives: identified and explained (1 mark)
    • (ii) Developing premises: identified and explained (1 mark)
    • (iii) Identifying alternative courses of action: identified and explained (1 mark)
    • (iv) Evaluating alternative courses: identified and explained (1 mark)
    • (v) Selecting an alternative: identified and explained (1 mark)
    • (vi) Implementing the plan: identified and explained (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (a) Method: Methods provide the prescribed ways or manner in which a task has to be performed, considering the objective. They deal with a single task, which comprises one step of a procedure, and specify how this step is to be performed. Using an appropriate method saves time, money and effort and increases efficiency. Example: the method of training to be used for newly recruited supervisors (for instance, lectures and seminars) is different from that for workers (on-the-job training).
    (b) Programme: Programmes are detailed statements about a project which outline the objectives, policies, procedures, rules, tasks, human and physical resources required and the budget to implement any course of action. Example: a programme for opening a new branch, covering everything from the target date and funds to staff, premises and equipment.
    (c) Rule: Rules are specific statements that tell what is to be done or not to be done. They do not allow for any flexibility or discretion, and they reflect a managerial decision that a certain action must or must not be taken. A penalty may be imposed for violating a rule. Example: 'Every worker must wear a safety helmet on the shop floor; a worker found without one will be fined ₹200'.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Method: correct explanation (1½ marks)
    • (a) Suitable example (½ marks)
    • (b) Programme: correct explanation (1½ marks)
    • (b) Suitable example (½ marks)
    • (c) Rule: correct explanation (1½ marks)
    • (c) Suitable example (½ marks)
