Section A: 1 mark each

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Nature and Significance of Management
    Prakash Industries completed its production target of 10,000 units on time. However, it used much more raw material and labour than was necessary, which pushed up its costs. Which of the following describes the management of Prakash Industries?

    1. Efficient but not effective
    2. Both efficient and effective
    3. Effective but not efficient
    4. Neither efficient nor effective
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Effective but not efficient

    Effectiveness means completing the job on time (achieving the target). Efficiency means doing it with the minimum cost and least wastage of resources. The target was met on time, but resources were wasted, so the management was effective but not efficient.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Principles of Management
    The management of Arya Motors invites suggestions from workers on how to cut costs and improve methods, and gives cash rewards for every suggestion that is put into use. Which principle of Scientific Management is being followed?

    1. Harmony, not discord
    2. Cooperation, not individualism
    3. Science, not rule of thumb
    4. Development of each and every person to his or her greatest efficiency and prosperity
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Cooperation, not individualism

    Under 'cooperation, not individualism', management should not close its ears to constructive suggestions made by employees, and employees should be rewarded for suggestions that result in substantial reduction in costs.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Organising
    Sona Paper Mills is a large company that makes only one product, writing paper. Its work needs a high degree of specialisation in production, finance, marketing and human resources. Which organisation structure is most suitable for it?

    1. Divisional structure
    2. Informal organisation
    3. Decentralised organisation
    4. Functional structure
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Functional structure

    A functional structure groups jobs by major functions such as production, finance and marketing. It is most suitable when the organisation is large, has a single or a few product lines and needs a high degree of specialisation. A divisional structure suits a company with several distinct product lines.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Staffing
    Read the following statements carefully:
    Statement I: Recruitment is a negative process, as it rejects unsuitable candidates.
    Statement II: Selection is a negative process, as it involves the rejection of unsuitable candidates.
    Choose the correct option from the following:

    1. Statement I is true and Statement II is false.
    2. Statement II is true and Statement I is false.
    3. Both the statements are true.
    4. Both the statements are false.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Statement II is true and Statement I is false.

    Recruitment is a positive process: it encourages as many people as possible to apply for the job. Selection is a negative process, because it involves the rejection of unsuitable candidates. So Statement I is false and Statement II is true.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Business Environment
    A new law made it compulsory for manufacturers to print a statutory health warning on every packet of tobacco products. Identify the dimension of business environment involved.

    1. Social environment
    2. Economic environment
    3. Legal environment
    4. Technological environment
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Legal environment

    The legal environment includes legislation passed by Parliament and other legal requirements that a business must follow. A statutory warning required by law is a part of the legal environment.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Directing
    Which of the following statements correctly describes a free-rein (laissez faire) leader?

    1. The leader gives followers a high degree of independence to set their own objectives and only supports them with the information they need.
    2. The leader gives orders and expects them to be obeyed, so communication is one-way.
    3. The leader develops plans and takes decisions in consultation with the followers.
    4. The leader depends mainly on rewards and punishments to get the work done.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) The leader gives followers a high degree of independence to set their own objectives and only supports them with the information they need.

    A laissez faire or free-rein leader does not use power unless it is absolutely essential. Followers are given a high degree of independence, and the leader is there only to support them and supply the required information. The other options describe autocratic and democratic leaders.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Financial Markets
    Which of the following statements about the primary market is correct?

    1. Securities that have already been issued are bought and sold among investors.
    2. It has a fixed geographical location where trading takes place.
    3. Prices of securities are determined by the demand and supply on the stock exchange.
    4. It deals with new securities and directly promotes capital formation.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) It deals with new securities and directly promotes capital formation.

    The primary (new issues) market deals with new securities issued for the first time, so funds flow directly from investors to the company and capital formation is promoted directly. The other statements describe the secondary market.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Principles of Management
    The manager of a call centre always says 'we' instead of 'I' when she talks to her team. She gives the team credit for its success and encourages its members to work together in harmony. Which principle of management does she follow?

    1. Unity of direction
    2. Discipline
    3. Order
    4. Esprit de corps
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Esprit de corps

    Esprit de corps means that management should promote team spirit, unity and harmony among employees. Fayol suggested that a manager should replace 'I' with 'we' in all conversations to foster team spirit.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Consumer Protection
    Assertion (A): Consumer protection is important for consumers as well as for businesses.
    Reason (R): Under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, a complaint can be filed only by the consumer who bought the goods.
    Choose the correct option:

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) A is true but R is false.

    A is true: consumer protection safeguards consumers and is also in the long-term interest of business, as satisfied customers bring repeat sales, and it is part of the social responsibility of business. R is false: besides the consumer, a registered consumers' association, the Central or a State Government, one or more consumers having the same interest, and the legal heir or representative of a deceased consumer can also file a complaint.

    Consumer organisations and NGOs educate the general public about consumer rights through training programmes, seminars and workshops. Passing refund orders is done by the consumer commissions; price fixing and quality certification are not their functions.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Financial Management
    Kavi Foods Ltd., a biscuit manufacturer, has decided to start making chocolates and soft drinks as well. This will need new plant and machinery. Identify the factor affecting its fixed capital requirement.

    1. Diversification
    2. Level of collaboration
    3. Financing alternatives
    4. Technology upgradation

    A firm may choose to diversify its operations for various reasons. With diversification into new product lines, the requirement of fixed capital rises, since new plant and machinery are needed.

  13. Question 13 · 1 mark · Financial Markets
    Name the two depositories in India that hold securities in electronic form.

    1. BSE and NSE
    2. NSDL and CDSL
    3. SEBI and RBI
    4. NSDL and SEBI
    Show answer

    The two depositories in India are the National Securities Depository Limited (NSDL) and the Central Depository Services (India) Limited (CDSL). BSE and NSE are stock exchanges, and SEBI and RBI are regulators.

  14. Question 14 · 1 mark · Planning
    After fixing the target of opening 20 new stores in a year, the managers of Supermart made assumptions about the future: that consumer spending would rise, shop rents would remain stable and no new competitor would enter the region. Identify the step of the planning process described.

    1. Setting objectives
    2. Identifying alternative courses of action
    3. Developing premises
    4. Evaluating alternative courses
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Developing premises

    Developing premises means making assumptions about the future, which form the base on which plans are drawn. Here the objective had already been set; the managers then made assumptions about spending, rents and competition.

  15. Question 15 · 1 mark · Financial Management
    The Finance Manager of Orbit Ltd. accepts only those proposals that are expected to increase the market price of the company's equity shares. Which objective of financial management does this reflect?

    1. Maximising the sales of the company
    2. Minimising the tax paid by the company
    3. Maximising the number of shareholders
    4. Maximising the wealth of the shareholders
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Maximising the wealth of the shareholders

    The primary objective of financial management is to maximise shareholders' wealth, which is reflected in the market price of the equity shares. Decisions that increase the market price of shares serve this objective.

  16. Question 16 · 1 mark · Marketing
    A car manufacturer sells its cars to consumers through authorised dealers (retailers) in each city. Identify the channel of distribution used.

    1. Zero level channel
    2. One level channel
    3. Two level channel
    4. Three level channel
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) One level channel

    In a one level channel there is one middleman, the retailer, between the manufacturer and the consumer: manufacturer → retailer → consumer.

  17. Question 17 · 1 mark · Financial Management
    Sagar Shipyard takes about two years to build one ship. During this period large amounts of money remain locked up in raw materials and partly built ships. Identify the factor that makes its working capital requirement high.

    1. Production cycle
    2. Business cycle
    3. Credit allowed
    4. Inflation
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Production cycle

    The production cycle is the time span between the receipt of raw material and its conversion into finished goods. The longer the production cycle, the larger the working capital required.

    1. Advertising
    2. Sales promotion

    4. Packaging
    Show answer

  19. Question 19 · 1 mark · Financial Management
    A company has earnings before interest and tax (EBIT) of ₹12,00,000. It has issued ₹30,00,000 of 10% debentures. What is its interest coverage ratio?

    1. 4 times
    2. 3 times
    3. 0.25 times
    4. 5 times
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) 4 times

    Interest = 10% of ₹30,00,000 = ₹3,00,000. Interest coverage ratio = EBIT ÷ interest = ₹12,00,000 ÷ ₹3,00,000 = 4 times.

  20. Question 20 · 1 mark · Controlling
    In Speedo Motors Ltd., the management keeps a close and constant watch on the cost of steel, the quality of engines and the safety of workers, because even a small deviation in any of these key areas can affect the success of the whole organisation. Identify the concept of controlling followed.

    1. Management by exception
    2. Critical point control
    3. Setting performance standards
    4. Taking corrective action
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Critical point control

    Critical point control means that control should focus on the key result areas (KRAs), which are critical to the success of the organisation, because a small change in them affects the whole organisation.

  2. Question 26 · 4 marks · Nature and Significance of Management
    Glorious Textiles has grown from 50 employees to over 2,000 employees in ten years. It now has separate departments for purchase, production, finance and marketing, and each department wants to pursue its own targets. The company has also hired many specialists, who often think that only they know what is best for the company. As a result, the efforts of different people and departments are no longer moving in the same direction.

    (a) Name the concept of management that the company needs to bring the efforts of all these people and departments together. [1]

    (b) Explain the three reasons, highlighted in the case, why this concept is needed. [3]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (a) Name the concept of management that the company needs to bring the efforts of all these people and departments together.

    Model answer: Coordination: the process by which a manager synchronises the activities of different departments and individuals towards the common goals of the organisation.

    Marking scheme:

    • Coordination (1 mark)

    (b) Explain the three reasons, highlighted in the case, why this concept is needed.

    Model answer: 1. Growth in size: As an organisation grows in size, the number of people employed also increases. Each individual has his or her own needs and goals, so coordination is needed to integrate individual goals with organisational goals ('grown from 50 employees to over 2,000 employees').
    2. Functional differentiation: An organisation is divided into departments such as purchase, production, finance and marketing, each of which may pursue its own objectives. Coordination is needed to link their activities ('each department wants to pursue its own targets').
    3. Specialisation: Specialists usually feel that they are qualified to judge matters according to their own views, which may cause conflict. Coordination is needed to reconcile their differences ('specialists, who often think that only they know what is best').

    Marking scheme:

    • Growth in size, identified (½ marks)
    • Growth in size, explained (½ marks)
    • Functional differentiation, identified (½ marks)
    • Functional differentiation, explained (½ marks)
    • Specialisation, identified (½ marks)
    • Specialisation, explained (½ marks)

  5. Question 29 · 4 marks · Controlling
    'Planning and controlling are inseparable twins of management.' Explain this statement with the help of any four points.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Relationship between planning and controlling (any four):
    1. Planning is the basis of controlling: planning provides the goals or standards against which actual performance is measured. Without planning there is nothing to control; controlling is blind without planning.
    2. Controlling makes planning effective: controlling checks whether the plans are being achieved and brings to light the deviations, so that corrective action can be taken. Planning without controlling is meaningless.
    3. Planning is prescriptive, while controlling is evaluative: planning lays down what should be done, while controlling evaluates whether decisions have been translated into the desired action.
    4. Planning is looking ahead and controlling is looking back, but this is only partly true: planning is also guided by past experience, and the corrective action taken under controlling aims to improve future performance. So both look ahead as well as back.
    5. Planning and controlling reinforce each other: planning based on facts makes controlling easier and more effective, and controlling improves future planning by providing information from past experience.

Section D: 6 marks each

  1. Question 31 · 6 marks · Planning
    Explain the following types of plans:
    (a) Objective
    (b) Policy
    (c) Budget

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Objective: Objectives are the end results which management seeks to achieve through its operations. They represent the desired future position of the organisation. Objectives are usually set by top management, should be expressed in specific terms and be measurable in quantitative terms, for example 'to increase sales by 10% this year'.
    (b) Policy: Policies are general statements that guide thinking or channelise energies towards a particular direction. They provide a basis for interpreting strategy and are guides to managerial action and decisions, for example the recruitment policy or the pricing policy of a firm.
    (c) Budget: A budget is a statement of expected results expressed in numerical terms for a definite future period, for example a sales budget giving the expected sales of each product in each area for the next year. It is also a control device, as actual performance can be compared with the budgeted figures.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Objective: end results that management seeks to achieve (1 mark)
    • (a) Objective: expressed in specific, measurable terms (with or without an example) (1 mark)
    • (b) Policy: general statements that guide thinking and decision making (1 mark)
    • (b) Policy: basis for interpreting strategy, with an example such as a recruitment or pricing policy (1 mark)
    • (c) Budget: statement of expected results in numerical terms for a definite period (1 mark)
    • (c) Budget: also a control device / suitable example such as a sales budget (1 mark)

  2. Question 32 · 6 marks · Organising
    Mehta Events Pvt. Ltd. won a contract to organise a three-day book fair. The manager first listed all the work to be done, such as booking the venue, inviting publishers, publicity, ticketing and security, and broke it into manageable tasks. Similar tasks were then grouped together into units such as Venue, Publicity and Ticketing. Each unit was handed over to staff members who had the right skills and experience for that work. Finally, the manager made it clear who would report to whom, so that everyone knew from whom to take orders and to whom to report.
    Identify and explain the four steps in the process of organising discussed above.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. Identification and division of work: The total work to be done is identified and divided into manageable activities, so that duplication of effort is avoided and the burden of work is shared ('listed all the work ... broke it into manageable tasks').
    2. Departmentalisation: Activities of a similar nature are grouped together into departments or units, which facilitates specialisation ('grouped together into units such as Venue, Publicity and Ticketing').
    3. Assignment of duties: Work is allocated to people according to their skills and competence, so that each job is done by the person best suited to it ('handed over to staff members who had the right skills').
    4. Establishing reporting relationships: Each individual is told from whom to take orders and to whom he or she is accountable. This creates a hierarchical structure and helps in coordination ('who would report to whom').

    Marking scheme:

    • Identification and division of work, identified (½ marks)
    • Identification and division of work, explained (1 mark)
    • Departmentalisation, identified (½ marks)
    • Departmentalisation, explained (1 mark)
    • Assignment of duties, identified (½ marks)
    • Assignment of duties, explained (1 mark)
    • Establishing reporting relationships, identified (½ marks)
    • Establishing reporting relationships, explained (1 mark)

  4. Question 34 · 6 marks · Marketing
    Explain the following marketing philosophies:
    (a) Product concept
    (b) Selling concept
    (c) Marketing concept

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Product concept: It holds that customers prefer products of good quality, performance and features, and are ready to pay a higher price for them. The focus of business is on product improvement, i.e., improving the quality and features of the product, and profit is earned through product quality.
    (b) Selling concept: It holds that customers will not buy enough of the firm's products unless they are persuaded to buy them. The focus is on selling the existing products through aggressive selling and promotion efforts, and profit is earned through a large volume of sales.
    (c) Marketing concept: It holds that the key to success lies in identifying the needs and wants of the target market and satisfying them better than competitors. The focus is on customer needs, the means used is integrated marketing, and profit is earned through customer satisfaction.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Product concept: customers prefer products of good quality, performance and features (1 mark)
    • (a) Product concept: focus on product improvement; profit through product quality (1 mark)
    • (b) Selling concept: customers buy only when persuaded (1 mark)
    • (b) Selling concept: focus on selling and promotion; profit through sales volume (1 mark)
    • (c) Marketing concept: identifying and satisfying customer needs better than competitors (1 mark)
    • (c) Marketing concept: integrated marketing as the means; profit through customer satisfaction (1 mark)
