Section A: 1 mark each

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Nature and Significance of Management
    Mr Anand is the Production Manager of a company. He reports to the Chief Executive Officer and has several supervisors working under him. Identify the function that he will perform at the level at which he is working.

    1. Formulating the overall organisational goals and strategies.
    2. Directly overseeing the efforts of the workers on the shop floor.
    3. Being responsible for the welfare and survival of the organisation.
    4. Interpreting the policies framed by top management and assigning duties to supervisors.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Interpreting the policies framed by top management and assigning duties to supervisors.

    As Production Manager between the CEO and the supervisors, Mr Anand is at the middle level. Middle-level managers interpret the policies framed by top management and assign necessary duties and responsibilities to supervisors. Goals, strategies and survival of the organisation are top-level tasks; overseeing workers is a supervisory task.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Principles of Management
    In a garment factory, the output of the workers fell sharply in the last two hours of every shift. An expert was asked to determine the amount and frequency of rest intervals the workers need to regain their energy. Name the technique of Scientific Management being used.

    1. Fatigue study
    2. Time study
    3. Motion study
    4. Method study
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Fatigue study

    Fatigue study seeks to determine the amount and frequency of rest intervals needed to complete a task, so that workers can regain lost stamina and keep up their efficiency.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Organising
    Which of the following statements about informal organisation is correct?

    1. It is deliberately designed by management to achieve organisational goals.
    2. It clearly defines the authority and responsibility of every member.
    3. It arises from the interaction of people at work and has no written rules.
    4. Communication in it flows only through the scalar chain.
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) It arises from the interaction of people at work and has no written rules.

    Informal organisation emerges on its own from the social interaction of people working together. It has no written rules, and communication does not follow a planned path. The other three statements describe formal organisation.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Controlling
    Choose the correct statement about the controlling function of management:

    1. Controlling is required only in large business organisations.
    2. Controlling is a pervasive function performed by managers at all levels and in all types of organisations.
    3. Controlling is only a backward-looking function.
    4. Controlling ensures that deviations from standards never occur.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Controlling is a pervasive function performed by managers at all levels and in all types of organisations.

    Controlling is a pervasive function: it is performed at all levels of management and in all types of organisations, large or small, business or non-business. It is both backward-looking and forward-looking, and it cannot prevent all deviations; it finds them and corrects them.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Consumer Protection
    Who among the following cannot file a complaint under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019?

    2. A wholesaler who bought the goods for resale
    3. A registered consumers' association
    4. The legal heir of a deceased consumer

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Principles of Management
    In Sudha Foods Ltd., Kavya, a supervisor in the Production Department, has to inform Rohit, a supervisor at the same level in the Sales Department, about an urgent change in the delivery schedule. Passing the message up and down through the departmental heads would cause delay, so with the prior permission of their superiors she contacts Rohit directly. This arrangement is provided for under which principle of management?

    1. Unity of command
    2. Order
    3. Unity of direction
    4. Scalar chain
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Scalar chain

    Under Fayol's principle of scalar chain, communication normally follows the formal line of authority. In an emergency, employees at the same level may contact each other directly through a 'gang plank' with the permission of their superiors, so that the chain is not violated and delay is avoided.

  11. Question 11 · 1 mark · Consumer Protection
    Meena bought a mixer-grinder from a local shop and did not take a bill, to save time. When the mixer stopped working within a week, the shopkeeper refused to accept that it had been bought from his shop. Which consumer responsibility did Meena ignore?

    1. Read labels carefully to know about price, net weight and the dates of manufacture and expiry.
    2. Buy only standardised goods, as they give an assurance of quality.
    3. Ask for a cash memo on the purchase of goods or services.
    4. Learn about the risks associated with products and services.
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Ask for a cash memo on the purchase of goods or services.

    A cash memo is the proof of purchase. Without it Meena cannot easily prove that she bought the mixer from that shop, which she would need to do to get it repaired or replaced or to file a complaint.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Financial Management
    Which of the following statements about the financing decision is correct?

    1. Issue of more debt dilutes the control of the existing equity shareholders.
    2. Debt is generally a cheaper source of finance than equity, but it is riskier for the business.
    3. Equity share capital involves a fixed obligation to pay dividend every year.
    4. Floatation costs have no effect on the choice of the source of finance.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Debt is generally a cheaper source of finance than equity, but it is riskier for the business.

    Interest on debt is usually lower than the return expected by equity shareholders and is tax-deductible, so debt is cheaper. But interest and repayment of principal are obligatory, so debt increases financial risk. Debt does not dilute control, equity has no fixed dividend obligation, and floatation costs do affect the choice.

  15. Question 15 · 1 mark · Financial Management
    Which of the following is an example of a capital budgeting (investment) decision?

    1. Deciding how much cash to keep for day-to-day expenses
    2. Deciding the credit period to be allowed to customers
    3. Purchasing a new machine with a working life of ten years
    4. Deciding the rate of dividend to be paid this year
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Purchasing a new machine with a working life of ten years

    Capital budgeting decisions are long-term investment decisions about fixed assets, such as buying a machine that will be used for ten years. Cash and credit decisions are working capital decisions; the rate of dividend is a dividend decision.

  18. Question 18 · 1 mark · Marketing

  19. Question 19 · 1 mark · Financial Management
    A company has 1,00,000 equity shares of ₹10 each and ₹20,00,000 of 10% debentures. Its earnings before interest and tax (EBIT) are ₹6,00,000 and the tax rate is 40%. What is its earnings per share (EPS)?

    1. ₹6.00
    2. ₹4.00
    3. ₹2.40
    4. ₹3.60
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) ₹2.40

    Interest = 10% of ₹20,00,000 = ₹2,00,000. Earnings before tax = ₹6,00,000 − ₹2,00,000 = ₹4,00,000. Tax at 40% = ₹1,60,000, so earnings after tax = ₹2,40,000. EPS = ₹2,40,000 ÷ 1,00,000 shares = ₹2.40.

  3. Question 23 · 3 marks · Financial Management
    Explain the following factors affecting the dividend decision of a company:
    (a) Stability of earnings
    (b) Taxation policy

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Stability of earnings: A company having stable earnings is in a better position to declare higher dividends. A company having unstable (fluctuating) earnings is likely to pay smaller dividends, so that it can keep up its payments in bad years.
    (b) Taxation policy: The choice between paying dividends and retaining earnings is affected by how dividends are taxed. If the tax on dividends is higher, it is better to pay less by way of dividends. If the tax rate is relatively lower, higher dividends may be declared.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) A company with stable earnings can declare higher dividends (1 mark)
    • (a) A company with unstable earnings is likely to pay smaller dividends (½ marks)
    • (b) Higher tax on dividends leads to lower dividends being paid (1 mark)
    • (b) Lower tax on dividends allows higher dividends to be declared (½ marks)

  3. Question 27 · 4 marks · Business Environment
    Explain any four features of business environment.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Features of business environment (any four):
    1. Totality of external forces: Business environment is the sum total of all things external to business firms and, as such, is aggregative in nature.
    2. Specific and general forces: Specific forces, such as investors, customers, competitors and suppliers, affect individual firms directly. General forces, such as social, political, legal and technological conditions, affect all firms and only indirectly.
    3. Inter-relatedness: The different elements or parts of business environment are closely inter-related. For example, a change in the life expectancy of people increases the demand for health care services.
    4. Dynamic nature: Business environment keeps on changing, whether in terms of technology, fashion and tastes of consumers, or government policies.
    5. Uncertainty: Business environment is largely uncertain, as it is very difficult to predict future happenings, especially when changes are taking place very fast.
    6. Complexity: Business environment consists of many inter-related and dynamic conditions arising from different sources, so it is difficult to understand it in its totality.
    7. Relativity: Business environment is a relative concept, since it differs from country to country and even from region to region.

    Marking scheme:

    • First feature: correct heading (any one from the model answer) (½ marks)
    • First feature: correct explanation of that heading (½ marks)
    • Second feature: correct heading (any one from the model answer) (½ marks)
    • Second feature: correct explanation of that heading (½ marks)
    • Third feature: correct heading (any one from the model answer) (½ marks)
    • Third feature: correct explanation of that heading (½ marks)

  5. Question 29 · 4 marks · Controlling
    Annapurna Bakers set a target that each worker would pack 400 boxes of biscuits in a shift. At the end of every shift, the supervisor recorded the actual number of boxes packed by each worker. On comparing these figures with the target, she found that one worker had packed only 320 boxes. She then found out that this was because the worker's packing machine was old and kept stopping.
    Identify and explain the four steps in the process of controlling discussed above.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. Setting performance standards: Standards are the criteria against which actual performance is measured. They may be set in quantitative or qualitative terms ('pack 400 boxes of biscuits in a shift').
    2. Measurement of actual performance: Performance is measured in an objective and reliable manner, for example by personal observation or reports ('recorded the actual number of boxes packed').
    3. Comparing actual performance with standards: This reveals the deviation between actual and desired results ('comparing these figures with the target ... only 320 boxes').
    4. Analysing deviations: The deviations are checked for their extent and their causes are found, so that suitable corrective action can be taken ('the worker's packing machine was old and kept stopping').

    Marking scheme:

    • Setting performance standards, identified (½ marks)
    • Setting performance standards, explained (½ marks)
    • Measurement of actual performance, identified (½ marks)
    • Measurement of actual performance, explained (½ marks)
    • Comparing actual performance with standards, identified (½ marks)
    • Comparing actual performance with standards, explained (½ marks)
    • Analysing deviations, identified (½ marks)

  6. Question 30 · 4 marks · Marketing
    Explain the following functions of marketing:
    (a) Standardisation and grading
    (b) Customer support services

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Standardisation and grading: Standardisation means producing goods of predetermined specifications, which helps in achieving uniformity and consistency in the output. It assures buyers that the goods conform to set standards of quality, price and packaging, and reduces the need for inspection and testing. Grading is the process of classifying products into different groups on the basis of important characteristics such as quality and size. It is especially needed for goods such as agricultural products, and it helps the producer get a higher price for better-quality goods.
    (b) Customer support services: Marketers provide services such as after-sales service, handling customer complaints and adjustments, procuring credit services, maintenance services, technical services and consumer information. These services aim at providing maximum satisfaction to customers, which helps in getting repeat sales and in developing brand loyalty.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Standardisation: producing goods of predetermined specifications to achieve uniformity and consistency (1 mark)
    • (a) Grading: classifying products into groups on the basis of characteristics such as quality and size (1 mark)
    • (b) Customer support services named, e.g., after-sales service, handling complaints, credit, maintenance and technical services (1 mark)
    • (b) Purpose: customer satisfaction, leading to repeat sales and brand loyalty (1 mark)

Section D: 6 marks each

  1. Question 31 · 6 marks · Planning
    Explain any four limitations of planning.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Limitations of planning (any four):
    1. Planning leads to rigidity: Once a well-defined plan is drawn up, managers may not be in a position to change it, even when circumstances change. Sticking rigidly to the plan may not be in the interest of the organisation.
    2. Planning may not work in a dynamic environment: The business environment is dynamic and nothing is constant. It is difficult to assess accurately future trends in the economic, political, legal and social dimensions, so plans may fail.
    3. Planning reduces creativity: Planning is done by top management and the rest of the organisation just implements the plans. Middle managers and others can neither deviate from plans nor act on their own, so initiative and creativity are discouraged.
    4. Planning involves huge costs: Considerable time and money are spent on gathering facts, consulting experts and evaluating alternatives. Sometimes the cost may not justify the benefits.
    5. Planning is a time-consuming process: Sometimes so much time is spent on planning that little time is left for implementation.
    6. Planning does not guarantee success: Managers may rely on previously tried and tested plans, which may not work again. A plan only gives a direction; success depends on its proper implementation.

    Marking scheme:

    • First limitation: correct heading (any one from the model answer) (½ marks)
    • First limitation: correct explanation of that heading (1 mark)
    • Second limitation: correct heading (any one from the model answer) (½ marks)
    • Second limitation: correct explanation of that heading (1 mark)
    • Third limitation: correct heading (any one from the model answer) (½ marks)
    • Third limitation: correct explanation of that heading (1 mark)
    • Fourth limitation: correct heading (any one from the model answer) (½ marks)
    • Fourth limitation: correct explanation of that heading (1 mark)

  2. Question 32 · 6 marks · Organising
    Rajat Sharma, Sales Manager of Swift Appliances Ltd., asked Neha, a sales executive, to take charge of the company's new northern zone. He gave her the right to offer discounts of up to 10% to dealers without asking him. Neha accepted the task of achieving a monthly sales target of ₹25 lakh in the zone. Rajat made it clear that she would have to report the zone's performance to him every week and would be answerable to him for the results.
    Identify and explain the three elements of delegation discussed above, quoting the relevant lines.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. Authority: 'the right to offer discounts of up to 10% to dealers without asking him'. Authority refers to the right of an individual to command subordinates and to take action within the scope of his or her position. It arises from the formal position, flows downward from superior to subordinate, and can be delegated.
    2. Responsibility: 'Neha accepted the task of achieving a monthly sales target of ₹25 lakh'. Responsibility is the obligation of a subordinate to properly perform the assigned duty. It arises from the superior-subordinate relationship and flows upward. When authority is delegated, the subordinate becomes responsible, but the superior's responsibility cannot be entirely passed on.
    3. Accountability: 'would have to report the zone's performance to him every week and would be answerable to him for the results'. Accountability means answerability for the final outcome. Once authority has been delegated and responsibility accepted, the subordinate is accountable to the superior. It flows upward and cannot be delegated.

    Marking scheme:

    • Authority identified (½ marks)
    • Authority: relevant line quoted (½ marks)
    • Authority explained: right to command and take decisions; flows downward (1 mark)
    • Responsibility identified (½ marks)
    • Responsibility: relevant line quoted (½ marks)
    • Responsibility explained: obligation to perform the assigned duty; flows upward (1 mark)
    • Accountability identified (½ marks)
    • Accountability: relevant line quoted (½ marks)
    • Accountability explained: answerability for the final outcome; cannot be delegated (1 mark)

  3. Question 33 · 6 marks · Financial Management
    Vardhan Steel Ltd. needs ₹50 crore for a new plant and is deciding how much of it to raise through debt. Its Finance Manager presents the following facts:
    (i) Its projected cash flows are large enough to meet interest and loan repayments on time and still leave a sufficient buffer for normal business operations.
    (ii) Its earnings before interest and tax are many times its interest charges.
    (iii) It earns a return of 18% on its investment, while it can borrow at 10%.
    (iv) Its promoters do not want to issue more equity shares, because they fear losing their hold over the management of the company.
    Identify and explain the four factors affecting the choice of capital structure discussed above.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. Cash flow position: The size of projected cash flows must be considered before borrowing. Cash flows must not only cover fixed cash payment obligations such as interest and repayment of principal, but there must also be a sufficient buffer for normal business operations. A company with a strong cash flow position, like Vardhan Steel, can use more debt.
    2. Interest coverage ratio (ICR): It refers to the number of times earnings before interest and tax (EBIT) of a company cover its interest obligation. The higher the ratio, the lower the risk of the company failing to meet its interest payments, and the more debt it can use.
    3. Return on investment (ROI): If the ROI of the company is higher than the rate of interest on debt, the company can use trading on equity and choose to use more debt to increase its earnings per share. Here ROI (18%) is higher than the cost of debt (10%).
    4. Control: Debt normally does not cause a dilution of control, while a public issue of equity may reduce the management's hold on the business. Companies afraid of losing control prefer debt.

    Marking scheme:

    • Cash flow position identified (½ marks)
    • Cash flow position explained: cash flows must cover interest and repayment; strong cash flows allow more debt (1 mark)
    • Interest coverage ratio identified (½ marks)
    • Interest coverage ratio explained: number of times EBIT covers interest; higher ratio, more debt can be used (1 mark)
    • Return on investment identified (½ marks)
    • Return on investment explained: ROI higher than rate of interest allows more debt (trading on equity) (1 mark)
    • Control identified (½ marks)
    • Control explained: debt does not dilute control, while equity may; such companies prefer debt (1 mark)

  4. Question 34 · 6 marks · Consumer Protection
    Explain the six rights of a consumer under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. Right to safety: The consumer has a right to be protected against goods and services that are hazardous to life, health and property, for example electrical appliances made of poor-quality material. Using ISI-marked products is a safeguard.
    2. Right to be informed: The consumer has a right to have complete information about the product, such as its ingredients, date of manufacture, price, quantity and directions for use.
    3. Right to be assured (right to choose): The consumer has the right to be assured, wherever possible, of access to a variety of products at competitive prices, so that he or she can choose freely.
    4. Right to be heard: The consumer has a right to file a complaint and to be heard in case of dissatisfaction with a good or a service. Many firms have set up consumer service cells for this purpose.
    5. Right to seek redressal: The consumer has a right to get relief if the product or service falls short of expectations, for example replacement of the product, removal of the defect or compensation for any loss or injury suffered.
    6. Right to consumer education (right to consumer awareness): The consumer has a right to acquire the knowledge needed to be a well-informed consumer throughout life, including knowledge of consumer rights and the remedies available.

    Marking scheme:

    • Right to safety, named (½ marks)
    • Right to safety, explained (½ marks)
    • Right to be informed, named (½ marks)
    • Right to be informed, explained (½ marks)
    • Right to be assured (right to choose), named (½ marks)
    • Right to be assured (right to choose), explained (½ marks)
    • Right to be heard, named (½ marks)
    • Right to be heard, explained (½ marks)
    • Right to seek redressal, named (½ marks)
    • Right to seek redressal, explained (½ marks)
    • Right to consumer education (consumer awareness), named (½ marks)
    • Right to consumer education (consumer awareness), explained (½ marks)
