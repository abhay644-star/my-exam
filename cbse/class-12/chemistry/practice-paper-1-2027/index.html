Section A: 1 mark each

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Solutions
    Four aqueous solutions are given below. Assuming complete dissociation of the electrolytes, which solution will have the highest freezing point?

    1. 0.05 m $\mathrm{CaCl_2}$
    2. 0.06 m $\mathrm{NaCl}$
    3. 0.04 m $\mathrm{Al_2(SO_4)_3}$
    4. 0.1 m urea
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) 0.1 m urea

    Depression in freezing point depends on $i \times m$: $\mathrm{CaCl_2}$ $3\times0.05=0.15$; urea $1\times0.1=0.10$; $\mathrm{NaCl}$ $2\times0.06=0.12$; $\mathrm{Al_2(SO_4)_3}$ $5\times0.04=0.20$. Urea has the smallest $i\times m$, so the smallest $\Delta T_f$ and the highest freezing point.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Solutions
    A mixture of ethanol and acetone shows positive deviation from Raoult's law. Which of the following is correct for this mixture?

    1. $\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H \lt 0$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V \lt 0$
    2. $\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H \gt 0$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V \gt 0$
    3. $\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H = 0$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V = 0$
    4. $\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H \gt 0$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V \lt 0$
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) $\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H \gt 0$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V \gt 0$

    Acetone breaks some of the hydrogen bonds between ethanol molecules, so A-B attractions are weaker than A-A and B-B attractions. Heat is absorbed on mixing ($\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H \gt 0$) and the volume increases ($\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V \gt 0$).

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Electrochemistry
    The quantity of electricity needed to reduce 1 mol of $\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-}}$ ions to $\mathrm{Cr^{3+}}$ ions is:

    1. 1 F
    2. 2 F
    3. 3 F
    4. 6 F
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) 6 F

    Each Cr changes from +6 to +3 (gain of 3 electrons). One $\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-}}$ has 2 Cr atoms, so $2\times3=6$ mol of electrons, that is 6 F, are needed.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Chemical Kinetics
    For the reaction $\mathrm{2A \rightarrow products}$, the unit of the rate constant is $\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$. If the initial concentration of A is doubled, the half-life of the reaction will:

    1. remain the same
    2. become half
    3. become four times
    4. become double
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) become double

    The unit $\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$ shows a zero order reaction, for which $t_{1/2} = \dfrac{[\mathrm{A}]_0}{2k}$. Half-life is directly proportional to the initial concentration, so it doubles.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · The d- and f-Block Elements
    Which of the following ions is colourless in aqueous solution? (Atomic numbers: Sc = 21, V = 23, Mn = 25, Cu = 29)

    1. $\mathrm{V^{3+}}$
    2. $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$
    3. $\mathrm{Sc^{3+}}$
    4. $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $\mathrm{Sc^{3+}}$

    $\mathrm{Sc^{3+}}$ has the configuration $3d^0$. With no d electron, no d-d transition is possible, so it is colourless. $\mathrm{V^{3+}}$ ($3d^2$), $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$ ($3d^5$) and $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ ($3d^9$) are coloured.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · The d- and f-Block Elements
    Zirconium (Z = 40) and hafnium (Z = 72) have almost the same atomic radii. This is due to:

    1. lanthanoid contraction
    2. actinoid contraction
    3. inert pair effect
    4. decrease in nuclear charge from Zr to Hf
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) lanthanoid contraction

    The 14 lanthanoids placed before Hf fill the 4f subshell. The poor shielding by 4f electrons makes the radius shrink (lanthanoid contraction), which cancels the expected increase in size from Zr to Hf.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Coordination Compounds
    In the complex $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_5(NO_2)]Cl_2}$, the $\mathrm{NO_2^-}$ ligand is bonded to cobalt through nitrogen. The IUPAC name of the complex is:

    1. pentaamminenitrito-N-cobalt(III) chloride
    2. pentaamminenitrito-O-cobalt(III) chloride
    3. pentaamminenitrito-N-cobalt(II) chloride
    4. pentaamminenitrito-N-cobaltate(III) chloride
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) pentaamminenitrito-N-cobalt(III) chloride

    Oxidation state of Co: $x + 5(0) + (-1) = +2$, so $x = +3$. The ligand bonded through N is nitrito-N. The complex ion is a cation, so the metal name does not end in -ate.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Haloalkanes and Haloarenes
    Which of the following compounds will undergo $\mathrm{S_N1}$ reaction fastest?

    1. $\mathrm{C_6H_5CH_2Br}$
    2. $\mathrm{C_6H_5CH(CH_3)Br}$
    3. $\mathrm{C_6H_5C(CH_3)_2Br}$
    4. $\mathrm{C_6H_5Br}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $\mathrm{C_6H_5C(CH_3)_2Br}$

    The $\mathrm{S_N1}$ rate depends on the stability of the carbocation. $\mathrm{C_6H_5C^+(CH_3)_2}$ is both tertiary and benzylic (stabilised by resonance and by two methyl groups), so it forms fastest. Bromobenzene does not form a carbocation easily.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers
    Which reagent will convert but-2-enal, $\mathrm{CH_3CH{=}CHCHO}$, into but-2-en-1-ol, $\mathrm{CH_3CH{=}CHCH_2OH}$?

    1. $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$
    2. $\mathrm{H_2/Ni}$
    3. $\mathrm{Zn\text{-}Hg}$ / conc. $\mathrm{HCl}$
    4. alkaline $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$

    $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ reduces the $\mathrm{-CHO}$ group to $\mathrm{-CH_2OH}$ but does not reduce the C=C bond. $\mathrm{H_2/Ni}$ would reduce both groups (giving butan-1-ol); Clemmensen reduction gives a hydrocarbon; $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ oxidises.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers
    Which of the following shows the correct order of acidic strength?

    1. phenol $\gt$ p-nitrophenol $\gt$ ethanol $\gt$ p-cresol
    2. p-nitrophenol $\gt$ phenol $\gt$ p-cresol $\gt$ ethanol
    3. p-cresol $\gt$ phenol $\gt$ p-nitrophenol $\gt$ ethanol
    4. ethanol $\gt$ p-cresol $\gt$ phenol $\gt$ p-nitrophenol
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) p-nitrophenol $\gt$ phenol $\gt$ p-cresol $\gt$ ethanol

    The electron-withdrawing $\mathrm{-NO_2}$ group stabilises the phenoxide ion and increases acidity; the electron-releasing $\mathrm{-CH_3}$ group decreases it. Ethanol is much weaker than any phenol because the ethoxide ion is not stabilised by resonance.

  11. Question 11 · 1 mark · Amines
    Which of the following amines cannot be prepared by Gabriel phthalimide synthesis?

    1. Ethanamine
    2. Benzylamine
    3. Aniline
    4. Propan-1-amine
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Aniline

    Aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution with the phthalimide anion, so primary aromatic amines such as aniline cannot be made by this method. Benzylamine can, because benzyl halides react readily.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Amines
    An amine with molecular formula $\mathrm{C_3H_9N}$ reacts with benzenesulphonyl chloride to give a solid that is insoluble in aqueous KOH. The amine is:

    1. propan-1-amine
    2. propan-2-amine
    3. N-methylethanamine
    4. N,N-dimethylmethanamine
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) N-methylethanamine

    A sulphonamide insoluble in alkali is given by a secondary amine (no acidic H on N in the product). The only secondary amine among the options is N-methylethanamine, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2NHCH_3}$. Primary amines give alkali-soluble products and tertiary amines do not react.

  13. Question 13 · 1 mark · Coordination Compounds
    Assertion (A): $\mathrm{[Ni(CO)_4]}$ is tetrahedral and diamagnetic.
    Reason (R): In $\mathrm{[Ni(CO)_4]}$, nickel is in the zero oxidation state and the strong field CO ligands cause the 4s electrons to pair up in the 3d orbitals, so nickel uses $sp^3$ hybrid orbitals.
    Select the most appropriate answer from the options given below:

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

    Ni(0) is $3d^8 4s^2$. CO pushes the 4s electrons into 3d, giving $3d^{10}$ with no unpaired electron (diamagnetic) and empty 4s and 4p orbitals for $sp^3$ hybridisation (tetrahedral). R explains A.

  14. Question 14 · 1 mark · Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers
    Assertion (A): Phenol is a weaker acid than ethanol.
    Reason (R): The phenoxide ion is stabilised by resonance.
    Select the most appropriate answer from the options given below:

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) A is false but R is true.

    Phenol ($\mathrm{p}K_a \approx 10$) is a stronger acid than ethanol ($\mathrm{p}K_a \approx 16$), so A is false. R is true: the negative charge of the phenoxide ion is delocalised over the ring, which is why phenol is the stronger acid.

  15. Question 15 · 1 mark · Amines
    Assertion (A): Ethanamine is soluble in water.
    Reason (R): Ethanamine is a stronger base than ammonia.
    Select the most appropriate answer from the options given below:

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.

    Both statements are true. Ethanamine dissolves in water because it forms hydrogen bonds with water molecules; its greater basicity (due to the +I effect of the ethyl group) does not explain solubility.

  16. Question 16 · 1 mark · Biomolecules
    Assertion (A): Vitamin C must be supplied regularly in our diet.
    Reason (R): Vitamin C is a fat-soluble vitamin that is stored in adipose tissue.
    Select the most appropriate answer from the options given below:

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) A is true but R is false.

    A is true. R is false: vitamin C is water-soluble, is excreted in urine and cannot be stored in the body, which is why it must be supplied regularly.

Section B: 2 marks each

  1. Question 17 · 2 marks · Solutions
    18 g of glucose ($\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}$, molar mass $180\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$) is dissolved in 178.2 g of water at 298 K. The vapour pressure of pure water at 298 K is 23.8 mm Hg. Calculate the vapour pressure of the solution.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Moles of glucose $= \dfrac{18}{180} = 0.1$ mol; moles of water $= \dfrac{178.2}{18} = 9.9$ mol.
    Mole fraction of glucose, $x_2 = \dfrac{0.1}{0.1 + 9.9} = 0.01$
    By Raoult's law, $\dfrac{p^\circ - p}{p^\circ} = x_2$, so $p = p^\circ(1 - x_2) = 23.8 \times 0.99$
    $p = 23.56$ mm Hg (23.562 mm Hg)

    Marking scheme:

    • Moles of glucose = 0.1 and moles of water = 9.9 (½ marks)
    • Mole fraction of solute $x_2 = 0.01$ (½ marks)
    • Relative lowering of vapour pressure $\dfrac{p^\circ - p}{p^\circ} = x_2$ (or $p = p^\circ x_1$) (½ marks)
    • Vapour pressure of solution = 23.56 mm Hg (½ marks)

  2. Question 18 · 2 marks · Chemical Kinetics
    The rate constant of a first order reaction is $0.0231\ \mathrm{min^{-1}}$. Calculate:
    (i) the half-life of the reaction;
    (ii) the time in which the concentration of the reactant falls to one-eighth of its initial value.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) $t_{1/2} = \dfrac{0.693}{k} = \dfrac{0.693}{0.0231\ \mathrm{min^{-1}}} = 30$ min
    (ii) $[\mathrm{R}]_0 \rightarrow \dfrac{1}{2} \rightarrow \dfrac{1}{4} \rightarrow \dfrac{1}{8}$ takes 3 half-lives. Time $= 3 \times 30 = 90$ min
    (Or: $t = \dfrac{2.303}{k}\log 8 = \dfrac{2.303 \times 0.903}{0.0231} = 90$ min)

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) $t_{1/2} = \dfrac{0.693}{k}$ (½ marks)
    • (i) $t_{1/2} = 30$ min (½ marks)
    • (ii) One-eighth needs 3 half-lives (or $t = \dfrac{2.303}{k}\log 8$) (½ marks)
    • (ii) Time = 90 min (½ marks)

  3. Question 19 · 2 marks · The d- and f-Block Elements
    Give reasons:
    (i) The $E^\circ(\mathrm{M^{2+}/M})$ value of copper is positive (+0.34 V), whereas it is negative for most other first-row transition metals.
    (ii) Zinc is not regarded as a transition element.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Copper has a high enthalpy of atomisation and a high sum of first and second ionisation enthalpies. The energy needed to convert Cu(s) into $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}(aq)}$ is not balanced by the hydration enthalpy of $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$, so $E^\circ$ is positive.
    (ii) Zinc has completely filled 3d orbitals ($3d^{10}$) both in its ground state and in its common oxidation state +2, so it does not have an incompletely filled d subshell.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) High enthalpy of atomisation and high ionisation enthalpies of Cu (½ marks)
    • (i) Not compensated by hydration enthalpy of $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) Zn has $3d^{10}$ configuration in ground state and in $\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}$ (no partly filled d orbitals) (1 mark)

  4. Question 20 · 2 marks · Haloalkanes and Haloarenes
    Write the main organic product and name the reaction in each case:
    (i) $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2Br + NaI \xrightarrow{dry\ acetone}}$ ?
    (ii) $\mathrm{2C_6H_5Cl + 2Na \xrightarrow{dry\ ether}}$ ?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2Br + NaI \xrightarrow{dry\ acetone} CH_3CH_2I + NaBr}$; product: iodoethane. Name: Finkelstein reaction.
    (ii) $\mathrm{2C_6H_5Cl + 2Na \xrightarrow{dry\ ether} C_6H_5\text{-}C_6H_5 + 2NaCl}$; product: biphenyl. Name: Fittig reaction.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Product iodoethane, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2I}$ (½ marks)
    • (i) Finkelstein reaction (½ marks)
    • (ii) Product biphenyl, $\mathrm{C_6H_5\text{-}C_6H_5}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) Fittig reaction (½ marks)

  5. Question 21 · 2 marks · Biomolecules
    (i) What is the difference between a nucleoside and a nucleotide?
    (ii) Name the linkage that joins two nucleotides in a nucleic acid chain.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) A nucleoside is made of a nitrogenous base joined to a pentose sugar (base + sugar). A nucleotide is a nucleoside in which a phosphate group is attached to the 5' position of the sugar (base + sugar + phosphate).
    (ii) Phosphodiester linkage (between the 5' and 3' carbon atoms of the sugar units of adjacent nucleotides).

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Nucleoside = base + pentose sugar (½ marks)
    • (i) Nucleotide = base + pentose sugar + phosphate group (½ marks)
    • (ii) Phosphodiester linkage (1 mark)

Section C: 3 marks each

  1. Question 22 · 3 marks · Solutions
    (i) 2.5 g of a non-volatile, non-electrolyte solute X is dissolved in 50 g of benzene. The freezing point of benzene is lowered by 1.024 K. Calculate the molar mass of X. ($K_f$ for benzene $= 5.12\ \mathrm{K\,kg\,mol^{-1}}$)
    (ii) The depression in freezing point of a 0.1 m aqueous solution of KCl is nearly twice that of a 0.1 m aqueous solution of glucose. Give reason.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) $\Delta T_f = K_f \times \dfrac{w_2 \times 1000}{M_2 \times w_1}$, so $M_2 = \dfrac{K_f \times w_2 \times 1000}{\Delta T_f \times w_1}$
    $M_2 = \dfrac{5.12 \times 2.5 \times 1000}{1.024 \times 50} = \dfrac{12800}{51.2} = 250\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$
    (ii) KCl dissociates completely into $\mathrm{K^+}$ and $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ ions, so the number of solute particles is doubled ($i \approx 2$). Depression in freezing point is a colligative property and depends on the number of solute particles; glucose does not dissociate ($i = 1$).

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Correct formula $M_2 = \dfrac{K_f w_2 \times 1000}{\Delta T_f\, w_1}$ (1 mark)
    • (i) Correct substitution and $M_2 = 250\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$ (1 mark)
    • (ii) KCl dissociates into 2 ions ($i \approx 2$) and colligative property depends on the number of particles (1 mark)

  2. Question 23 · 3 marks · Electrochemistry
    (i) A current of 1.93 A is passed through an aqueous solution of copper sulphate for 16 minutes 40 seconds using inert electrodes. Calculate the mass of copper deposited at the cathode. (Molar mass of Cu $= 63.5\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$, 1 F $= 96500\ \mathrm{C\,mol^{-1}}$)
    (ii) Name the products formed at the cathode and at the anode when a concentrated aqueous solution of NaCl is electrolysed using inert electrodes.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) $t = 16 \times 60 + 40 = 1000$ s
    $Q = I \times t = 1.93 \times 1000 = 1930$ C
    $\mathrm{Cu^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow Cu}$: 2 F (193000 C) deposit 63.5 g of Cu.
    Mass of Cu $= \dfrac{63.5 \times 1930}{2 \times 96500} = 0.635$ g
    (ii) Cathode: hydrogen gas ($\mathrm{2H_2O + 2e^- \rightarrow H_2 + 2OH^-}$). Anode: chlorine gas ($\mathrm{2Cl^- \rightarrow Cl_2 + 2e^-}$).

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) $Q = 1930$ C (½ marks)
    • (i) Use of $\mathrm{Cu^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow Cu}$ (2 F per mole of Cu) (½ marks)
    • (i) Mass of Cu deposited = 0.635 g (1 mark)
    • (ii) Cathode: $\mathrm{H_2}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) Anode: $\mathrm{Cl_2}$ (½ marks)

  3. Question 24 · 3 marks · The d- and f-Block Elements
    (i) Write the balanced ionic equation for the reaction of acidified potassium permanganate with oxalate ions.
    (ii) Why do actinoids show a wider range of oxidation states than lanthanoids?
    (iii) Calculate the spin-only magnetic moment of $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$ ion. (Atomic number of Mn = 25)

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) $\mathrm{2MnO_4^- + 5C_2O_4^{2-} + 16H^+ \rightarrow 2Mn^{2+} + 10CO_2 + 8H_2O}$
    (ii) In actinoids the 5f, 6d and 7s subshells have comparable energies, so electrons from all of them can take part in bonding. In lanthanoids the 4f electrons are more deeply buried and the energy gap is larger.
    (iii) $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$: $[\mathrm{Ar}]3d^5$, so $n = 5$ unpaired electrons. $\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)} = \sqrt{35} \approx 5.92$ BM

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Correct reactants and products (½ marks)
    • (i) Correctly balanced equation (½ marks)
    • (ii) Comparable energies of 5f, 6d and 7s subshells (1 mark)
    • (iii) 5 unpaired electrons ($3d^5$) (½ marks)
    • (iii) $\mu = \sqrt{35} \approx 5.92$ BM (½ marks)

  4. Question 25 · 3 marks · Haloalkanes and Haloarenes
    Account for the following:
    (i) Haloarenes are much less reactive than haloalkanes towards nucleophilic substitution.
    (ii) p-Dichlorobenzene has a higher melting point than its ortho and meta isomers.
    (iii) Ethane is formed when ethylmagnesium bromide is exposed to moist air.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) In haloarenes the lone pair on the halogen is in resonance with the ring, so the C-X bond gets partial double bond character and is harder to break. (The $sp^2$ carbon also holds the halogen more strongly, and the electron-rich ring repels the nucleophile.)
    (ii) The p-isomer is symmetrical and fits more closely in the crystal lattice, so stronger forces must be overcome to melt it.
    (iii) Grignard reagents react with any source of protons. Water converts it to the alkane: $\mathrm{C_2H_5MgBr + H_2O \rightarrow C_2H_6 + Mg(OH)Br}$

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Partial double bond character of C-X bond due to resonance (or $sp^2$ carbon / repulsion of nucleophile) (1 mark)
    • (ii) Symmetry of p-isomer gives better packing in the crystal lattice (1 mark)
    • (iii) Reaction with water giving ethane: $\mathrm{C_2H_5MgBr + H_2O \rightarrow C_2H_6 + Mg(OH)Br}$ (1 mark)

  5. Question 26 · 3 marks · Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers
    (i) Name the main product formed when phenol is treated with chloroform in the presence of aqueous sodium hydroxide, followed by acidification. Name the reaction.
    (ii) A student wants to prepare 2-methoxy-2-methylpropane, $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3COCH_3}$, by Williamson synthesis. Which pair of reactants should be used? Give a reason for your choice.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) 2-Hydroxybenzaldehyde (salicylaldehyde), $\mathrm{HOC_6H_4CHO}$. Reimer-Tiemann reaction.
    (ii) Sodium tert-butoxide and bromomethane: $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3CONa + CH_3Br \rightarrow (CH_3)_3COCH_3 + NaBr}$
    Reason: Williamson synthesis is an $\mathrm{S_N2}$ reaction and works best with a primary (methyl) halide. If tert-butyl bromide and sodium methoxide are used, the strongly basic alkoxide causes elimination and 2-methylpropene is formed instead of the ether.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde) (½ marks)
    • (i) Reimer-Tiemann reaction (½ marks)
    • (ii) Sodium tert-butoxide, $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3CONa}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) Methyl halide, $\mathrm{CH_3Br}$ (or $\mathrm{CH_3I}$) (½ marks)
    • (ii) Reason: tertiary halide with alkoxide gives elimination (alkene); $\mathrm{S_N2}$ needs a primary/methyl halide (1 mark)

  6. Question 27 · 3 marks · Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids
    An organic compound A, $\mathrm{C_3H_6O}$, gives a yellow precipitate when warmed with iodine and sodium hydroxide, but it does not reduce Tollens' reagent.
    (i) Identify A.
    (ii) Write the balanced equation for the reaction of A with iodine and sodium hydroxide.
    (iii) Write the structure of the product formed when A reacts with HCN.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) A is propanone (acetone), $\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}$. (It has a $\mathrm{CH_3CO-}$ group, so it gives the iodoform test; it is a ketone, so it does not reduce Tollens' reagent.)
    (ii) $\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3 + 3I_2 + 4NaOH \rightarrow CHI_3 + CH_3COONa + 3NaI + 3H_2O}$ (yellow precipitate of iodoform)
    (iii) $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2C(OH)CN}$, 2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanenitrile (acetone cyanohydrin)

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) A = propanone, $\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}$ (1 mark)
    • (ii) Products $\mathrm{CHI_3}$ and $\mathrm{CH_3COONa}$ shown (½ marks)
    • (ii) Equation correctly balanced (½ marks)
    • (iii) $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2C(OH)CN}$ (1 mark)

  7. Question 28 · 3 marks · Amines
    Write the chemical equations for the following:
    (i) Hoffmann bromamide degradation of ethanamide.
    (ii) Coupling reaction of benzenediazonium chloride with phenol in a basic medium.
    (iii) Reaction of aniline with bromine water at room temperature.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) $\mathrm{CH_3CONH_2 + Br_2 + 4NaOH \rightarrow CH_3NH_2 + Na_2CO_3 + 2NaBr + 2H_2O}$
    (ii) $\mathrm{C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^- + C_6H_5OH + NaOH \rightarrow \text{p-}HOC_6H_4N{=}NC_6H_5 + NaCl + H_2O}$ (p-hydroxyazobenzene, orange dye)
    (iii) $\mathrm{C_6H_5NH_2 + 3Br_2 \rightarrow 2,4,6\text{-}Br_3C_6H_2NH_2 + 3HBr}$ (white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline)

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Product methanamine, $\mathrm{CH_3NH_2}$ (½ marks)
    • (i) Reagents $\mathrm{Br_2/NaOH}$ and balanced equation (½ marks)
    • (ii) Product p-hydroxyazobenzene (½ marks)
    • (ii) Correct equation (basic medium) (½ marks)
    • (iii) Product 2,4,6-tribromoaniline (½ marks)
    • (iii) Balanced equation with 3 $\mathrm{Br_2}$ and 3 HBr (½ marks)

Section D: case-based, 4 marks each

  1. Question 29 · 4 marks · Biomolecules
    Effect of temperature on an enzyme
    Amylase is an enzyme present in saliva. It speeds up the hydrolysis of starch. A group of students mixed equal volumes of a starch solution and an amylase solution in three test tubes, kept them at different temperatures, and tested a drop from each tube with iodine solution every minute. Starch gives a blue-black colour with iodine; when the starch has been hydrolysed, the blue-black colour no longer appears. Their results are given below.

    TubeTemperatureTime after which blue-black colour no longer appeared
    P10 °C18 min
    Q37 °C4 min
    R80 °Cblue-black colour still seen after 30 min

    (i) Name the type of linkage in starch that is broken by amylase. [1]

    (ii) Why is starch hydrolysed more slowly in tube P than in tube Q? [1]

    (iii) Explain the observation in tube R. Which levels of the structure of the enzyme are lost and which level remains unchanged? [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Name the type of linkage in starch that is broken by amylase.

    Model answer: Glycosidic linkage (the C-O-C linkage between glucose units).

    Marking scheme:

    • Glycosidic linkage (1 mark)

    (ii) Why is starch hydrolysed more slowly in tube P than in tube Q?

    Model answer: At 10 °C the molecules have lower kinetic energy, so fewer effective collisions between enzyme and starch take place; the enzyme is not destroyed, it simply works more slowly. 37 °C (body temperature) is close to the optimum temperature for amylase.

    Marking scheme:

    • Lower temperature: fewer effective collisions / lower kinetic energy, or enzyme less active far below its optimum temperature of about 37 °C (enzyme not destroyed) (1 mark)

    (iii) Explain the observation in tube R. Which levels of the structure of the enzyme are lost and which level remains unchanged?

    Model answer: At 80 °C the enzyme (a globular protein) is denatured: the hydrogen bonds and other forces holding its folded shape are broken, so its active site is destroyed and it can no longer hydrolyse starch. Therefore starch remains and gives the blue-black colour.
    During denaturation the secondary and tertiary structures are lost, while the primary structure (sequence of amino acids joined by peptide bonds) remains intact.

    Marking scheme:

    • Enzyme is denatured at high temperature (loss of shape/active site), so starch is not hydrolysed (1 mark)
    • Secondary and tertiary structures lost (½ marks)
    • Primary structure remains intact (½ marks)

  2. Question 30 · 4 marks · Chemical Kinetics
    Following a reaction in the laboratory
    The decomposition of a compound A in solution, $\mathrm{A \rightarrow B + C}$, was studied at 300 K. The concentration of A was measured at regular intervals:

    Time (min)0102030
    [A] ($\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$)0.800.400.200.10
    The experiment was then repeated at 310 K, and the half-life was found to be 5 min.

    (i) What is the order of the reaction? Give a reason based on the data. [1]

    (ii) What will be the concentration of A after 50 min at 300 K? [1]

    (iii) Calculate the activation energy of the reaction. (Take $2.303 \times 8.314 \times \log 2 = 5.76\ \mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}$) [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) What is the order of the reaction? Give a reason based on the data.

    Model answer: First order. The concentration of A is halved every 10 min, whatever the starting concentration, so the half-life (10 min) is independent of concentration, which is true only for a first order reaction.

    Marking scheme:

    • First order, because half-life is constant (10 min) and independent of concentration (1 mark)

    (ii) What will be the concentration of A after 50 min at 300 K?

    Model answer: 50 min = 5 half-lives. $[\mathrm{A}] = 0.80 \times \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^5 = \dfrac{0.80}{32} = 0.025\ \mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$

    Marking scheme:

    • $[\mathrm{A}] = 0.025\ \mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ (5 half-lives) (1 mark)

    (iii) Calculate the activation energy of the reaction. (Take $2.303 \times 8.314 \times \log 2 = 5.76\ \mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}$)

    Model answer: For a first order reaction $k = \dfrac{0.693}{t_{1/2}}$, so $\dfrac{k_2}{k_1} = \dfrac{10}{5} = 2$.
    $\log\dfrac{k_2}{k_1} = \dfrac{E_a}{2.303R}\left(\dfrac{T_2 - T_1}{T_1T_2}\right)$
    $E_a = 2.303R\log 2 \times \dfrac{T_1T_2}{T_2 - T_1} = 5.76 \times \dfrac{300 \times 310}{10} = 5.76 \times 9300$
    $E_a = 53568\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}} \approx 53.6\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$

    Marking scheme:

    • $k_2/k_1 = 2$ and correct Arrhenius expression $\log\dfrac{k_2}{k_1} = \dfrac{E_a}{2.303R}\left(\dfrac{T_2-T_1}{T_1T_2}\right)$ (1 mark)
    • $E_a \approx 53.6\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ (53568 $\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}$) with unit (1 mark)

Section E: 5 marks each

  1. Question 31 · 5 marks · Electrochemistry
    (i) Consider the cell at 298 K:
    $\mathrm{Ni(s)\,|\,Ni^{2+}(0.01\,M)\,||\,Ag^+(0.001\,M)\,|\,Ag(s)}$
    Given: $E^\circ(\mathrm{Ni^{2+}/Ni}) = -0.25$ V, $E^\circ(\mathrm{Ag^+/Ag}) = +0.80$ V, 1 F $= 96500\ \mathrm{C\,mol^{-1}}$, and $\dfrac{2.303RT}{F} = 0.059$ V.
    (a) Write the cell reaction.
    (b) Calculate the emf of the cell.
    (c) Calculate $\Delta_rG^\circ$ for the cell reaction.
    (ii) The limiting molar ionic conductivities of $\mathrm{H^+}$ and $\mathrm{CH_3COO^-}$ are $349.6$ and $40.9\ \mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}$ respectively. The molar conductivity of a 0.001 M acetic acid solution is $39.05\ \mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}$. Calculate the limiting molar conductivity of acetic acid and its degree of dissociation at this concentration.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i)(a) $\mathrm{Ni(s) + 2Ag^+(aq) \rightarrow Ni^{2+}(aq) + 2Ag(s)}$ ($n = 2$)
    (b) $E^\circ_{cell} = E^\circ_{cathode} - E^\circ_{anode} = 0.80 - (-0.25) = 1.05$ V
    $E_{cell} = E^\circ_{cell} - \dfrac{0.059}{2}\log\dfrac{[\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}]}{[\mathrm{Ag^+}]^2} = 1.05 - \dfrac{0.059}{2}\log\dfrac{10^{-2}}{(10^{-3})^2}$
    $= 1.05 - \dfrac{0.059}{2}\log 10^4 = 1.05 - 0.0295 \times 4 = 1.05 - 0.118 = 0.932$ V
    (c) $\Delta_rG^\circ = -nFE^\circ_{cell} = -2 \times 96500 \times 1.05 = -202650\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}} = -202.65\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$
    (ii) $\Lambda^\circ_m(\mathrm{CH_3COOH}) = \lambda^\circ(\mathrm{H^+}) + \lambda^\circ(\mathrm{CH_3COO^-}) = 349.6 + 40.9 = 390.5\ \mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}$
    $\alpha = \dfrac{\Lambda_m}{\Lambda^\circ_m} = \dfrac{39.05}{390.5} = 0.1$ (10% dissociated)

    Marking scheme:

    • (i)(a) Cell reaction $\mathrm{Ni + 2Ag^+ \rightarrow Ni^{2+} + 2Ag}$ (½ marks)
    • (i)(b) $E^\circ_{cell} = 1.05$ V (½ marks)
    • (i)(b) Nernst equation with $n = 2$ and $Q = \dfrac{[\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}]}{[\mathrm{Ag^+}]^2} = 10^4$ (½ marks)
    • (i)(b) $E_{cell} = 0.932$ V (½ marks)
    • (i)(c) $\Delta_rG^\circ = -nFE^\circ_{cell}$ (½ marks)
    • (i)(c) $\Delta_rG^\circ = -202.65\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) $\Lambda^\circ_m = 390.5\ \mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}$ (Kohlrausch's law) (1 mark)
    • (ii) $\alpha = \Lambda_m/\Lambda^\circ_m = 0.1$ (1 mark)

  2. Question 32 · 5 marks · Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids
    (i) Account for the following:
    (a) Carboxylic acids do not give the characteristic reactions of the carbonyl group, such as formation of oximes.
    (b) Chloroacetic acid is a stronger acid than acetic acid.
    (c) Methanal undergoes Cannizzaro reaction, but ethanal does not.
    (ii) An organic compound X ($\mathrm{C_2H_4O}$) gives a silver mirror with Tollens' reagent. On oxidation, X gives Y. Y reacts with chlorine in the presence of a small amount of red phosphorus to give Z. Identify X, Y and Z, and name the reaction by which Y is converted into Z.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i)(a) In the carboxyl group the lone pair on the -OH oxygen is in resonance with the C=O group, so the carbonyl carbon is much less electrophilic. Hence carboxylic acids do not show typical carbonyl reactions.
    (b) Cl is an electron-withdrawing group (-I effect). It disperses the negative charge of the chloroacetate ion, $\mathrm{ClCH_2COO^-}$, and stabilises it, so chloroacetic acid loses $\mathrm{H^+}$ more easily.
    (c) Cannizzaro reaction is given by aldehydes with no $\alpha$-hydrogen. Methanal (HCHO) has no $\alpha$-hydrogen; ethanal ($\mathrm{CH_3CHO}$) has three $\alpha$-hydrogens and undergoes aldol condensation instead.
    (ii) X = ethanal, $\mathrm{CH_3CHO}$; Y = ethanoic acid, $\mathrm{CH_3COOH}$; Z = chloroethanoic acid, $\mathrm{ClCH_2COOH}$.
    $\mathrm{CH_3COOH \xrightarrow{Cl_2,\ red\ P} ClCH_2COOH + HCl}$: Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) reaction.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i)(a) Resonance in carboxyl group reduces electrophilic character of carbonyl carbon (1 mark)
    • (i)(b) -I effect of Cl stabilises the carboxylate ion (1 mark)
    • (i)(c) Methanal has no $\alpha$-hydrogen; ethanal has $\alpha$-hydrogens (1 mark)
    • (ii) X = ethanal, $\mathrm{CH_3CHO}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) Y = ethanoic acid, $\mathrm{CH_3COOH}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) Z = chloroethanoic acid, $\mathrm{ClCH_2COOH}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky reaction (½ marks)

  3. Question 33 · 5 marks · Coordination Compounds
    Answer the following:
    (i) Write the IUPAC name of $\mathrm{K_3[Fe(C_2O_4)_3]}$.
    (ii) Using valence bond theory, predict the type of hybridisation and the magnetic behaviour of $\mathrm{[CoF_6]^{3-}}$. (Atomic number of Co = 27)
    (iii) Name the type of isomerism shown by the square planar complex $\mathrm{[Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_2]}$ and state the number of such isomers.
    (iv) Arrange the following in increasing order of crystal field splitting energy ($\Delta_o$), and give the basis of your order: $\mathrm{[Co(CN)_6]^{3-}}$, $\mathrm{[Co(H_2O)_6]^{3+}}$, $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+}}$
    (v) When 1 mol of $\mathrm{CrCl_3 \cdot 6H_2O}$ is treated with excess $\mathrm{AgNO_3}$ solution, 2 mol of AgCl is precipitated. Write the structural formula of the complex.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Potassium trioxalatoferrate(III)
    (ii) $\mathrm{Co^{3+}}$: $3d^6$. $\mathrm{F^-}$ is a weak field ligand, so the 3d electrons do not pair up. The complex uses outer 4s, 4p and 4d orbitals: $sp^3d^2$ hybridisation (octahedral, outer orbital or high spin complex). It has 4 unpaired electrons, so it is paramagnetic.
    (iii) Geometrical (cis-trans) isomerism; two isomers (cis and trans).
    (iv) $\mathrm{[Co(H_2O)_6]^{3+} \lt [Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+} \lt [Co(CN)_6]^{3-}}$. Basis: spectrochemical series, field strength of ligands $\mathrm{H_2O \lt NH_3 \lt CN^-}$ (the metal ion is the same).
    (v) Two $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ ions are outside the coordination sphere (ionisable): $\mathrm{[Cr(H_2O)_5Cl]Cl_2 \cdot H_2O}$

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Potassium trioxalatoferrate(III) (1 mark)
    • (ii) $sp^3d^2$ hybridisation (½ marks)
    • (ii) Paramagnetic (4 unpaired electrons) (½ marks)
    • (iii) Geometrical (cis-trans) isomerism, 2 isomers (1 mark)
    • (iv) Order $\mathrm{[Co(H_2O)_6]^{3+} \lt [Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+} \lt [Co(CN)_6]^{3-}}$ (½ marks)
    • (iv) Basis: spectrochemical series / ligand field strength (½ marks)
    • (v) $\mathrm{[Cr(H_2O)_5Cl]Cl_2 \cdot H_2O}$ (1 mark)
