Section A: 1 mark each

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Solutions
    The Henry's law constants ($K_H$) of four gases P, Q, R and S in water at 298 K are 1.7 kbar, 35 kbar, 77 kbar and 145 kbar respectively. At the same partial pressure, which gas is the most soluble in water?

    1. Q
    2. R
    3. S
    4. P
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) P

    By Henry's law, $p = K_H x$, so $x = p/K_H$. At the same partial pressure, the gas with the lowest $K_H$ has the highest mole fraction in solution, that is, the highest solubility. P has the lowest $K_H$.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Solutions
    4 g of sodium hydroxide (molar mass $40\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$) is dissolved in 500 g of water. The molality of the solution is:

    1. $0.1\ \mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}$
    2. $0.2\ \mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}$
    3. $0.4\ \mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}$
    4. $8\ \mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) $0.2\ \mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}$

    Moles of NaOH $= 4/40 = 0.1$ mol. Mass of water $= 0.5$ kg. Molality $= 0.1/0.5 = 0.2\ \mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}$.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Electrochemistry
    Which of the following is a secondary cell, that is, a cell that can be recharged after use?

    1. Dry cell (Leclanche cell)
    2. Mercury cell
    3. Lead storage battery
    4. Hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Lead storage battery

    In a lead storage battery the cell reaction can be reversed by passing current in the opposite direction, so it can be recharged. Dry cells and mercury cells are primary cells; a fuel cell works only while reactants are supplied.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Electrochemistry
    For which of the following electrolytes can the limiting molar conductivity NOT be found by extending the graph of $\Lambda_m$ against $\sqrt{c}$ to zero concentration?

    1. $\mathrm{KCl}$
    2. $\mathrm{NaCl}$
    3. $\mathrm{CH_3COOH}$
    4. $\mathrm{HCl}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $\mathrm{CH_3COOH}$

    For a weak electrolyte such as acetic acid, $\Lambda_m$ rises very steeply at low concentration and the graph is not a straight line, so $\Lambda^\circ_m$ cannot be obtained by extrapolation. It is found using Kohlrausch's law.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Chemical Kinetics
    The unit of the rate constant of a reaction is $\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$. The order of the reaction is:

    1. zero
    2. one
    3. two
    4. three
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) two

    Unit of $k$ is $(\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}})^{1-n}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. For $\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$, $1 - n = -1$, so $n = 2$.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Chemical Kinetics
    The activation energy of a reaction is zero. Its rate constant at 300 K is $2 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$. The rate constant at 310 K will be:

    1. $2 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$
    2. $4 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$
    3. $1 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$
    4. $2 \times 10^{-2}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $2 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$

    By the Arrhenius equation $k = Ae^{-E_a/RT}$. If $E_a = 0$, $k = A$, which does not depend on temperature. So $k$ stays $2 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · The d- and f-Block Elements
    Which of the following ions has the maximum number of unpaired electrons? (Atomic numbers: Cr = 24, Fe = 26, Co = 27, Ni = 28)

    1. $\mathrm{Co^{2+}}$
    2. $\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}$
    3. $\mathrm{Cr^{3+}}$
    4. $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$

    $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ $3d^5$: 5 unpaired; $\mathrm{Co^{2+}}$ $3d^7$: 3; $\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}$ $3d^8$: 2; $\mathrm{Cr^{3+}}$ $3d^3$: 3.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · The d- and f-Block Elements
    Which of the following lanthanoid ions is a strong oxidising agent?

    1. $\mathrm{Ce^{4+}}$
    2. $\mathrm{Eu^{2+}}$
    3. $\mathrm{La^{3+}}$
    4. $\mathrm{Lu^{3+}}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\mathrm{Ce^{4+}}$

    The most stable oxidation state of lanthanoids is +3. $\mathrm{Ce^{4+}}$ readily gains an electron to become $\mathrm{Ce^{3+}}$, so it is a strong oxidising agent. $\mathrm{Eu^{2+}}$ is a reducing agent; $\mathrm{La^{3+}}$ and $\mathrm{Lu^{3+}}$ are stable.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Coordination Compounds
    The coordination number and the oxidation state of cobalt in $\mathrm{[Co(en)_2Cl_2]^+}$ are respectively: (en = ethane-1,2-diamine)

    1. 4 and +3
    2. 6 and +3
    3. 6 and +1
    4. 4 and +1
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) 6 and +3

    Each en is bidentate: $2 \times 2 + 2 = 6$ donor atoms. Oxidation state: $x + 2(0) + 2(-1) = +1$, so $x = +3$.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Haloalkanes and Haloarenes
    Which reagent converts ethanol into chloroethane such that both the by-products are gases that escape, leaving the product nearly pure?

    1. $\mathrm{PCl_5}$
    2. $\mathrm{SOCl_2}$
    3. $\mathrm{PCl_3}$
    4. $\mathrm{HCl}$ / anhydrous $\mathrm{ZnCl_2}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) $\mathrm{SOCl_2}$

    $\mathrm{C_2H_5OH + SOCl_2 \rightarrow C_2H_5Cl + SO_2\uparrow + HCl\uparrow}$. Both $\mathrm{SO_2}$ and HCl are gases.

  11. Question 11 · 1 mark · Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers
    Which of the following alcohols gives turbidity immediately with Lucas reagent (conc. HCl and anhydrous $\mathrm{ZnCl_2}$) at room temperature?

    1. Butan-1-ol
    2. Butan-2-ol
    3. 2-Methylpropan-1-ol
    4. 2-Methylpropan-2-ol
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) 2-Methylpropan-2-ol

    Tertiary alcohols react fastest with Lucas reagent because they form the most stable (tertiary) carbocation; the insoluble alkyl chloride appears at once as turbidity. 2-Methylpropan-2-ol is the only tertiary alcohol.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Biomolecules
    A doctor finds that a patient has pernicious anaemia caused by a vitamin deficiency. Which vitamin should be supplied?

    1. Vitamin $\mathrm{B_1}$
    2. Vitamin $\mathrm{B_6}$
    3. Vitamin $\mathrm{B_{12}}$
    4. Vitamin C
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Vitamin $\mathrm{B_{12}}$

    Deficiency of vitamin $\mathrm{B_{12}}$ causes pernicious anaemia (fewer red blood cells, with low haemoglobin). $\mathrm{B_1}$ deficiency causes beri-beri, $\mathrm{B_6}$ convulsions, C scurvy.

  13. Question 13 · 1 mark · Coordination Compounds
    Assertion (A): $\mathrm{[Fe(CN)_6]^{3-}}$ is weakly paramagnetic, whereas $\mathrm{[FeF_6]^{3-}}$ is strongly paramagnetic.
    Reason (R): $\mathrm{CN^-}$ is a strong field ligand that causes pairing of the 3d electrons, while $\mathrm{F^-}$ is a weak field ligand that does not.
    Select the most appropriate answer from the options given below:

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

    $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ is $3d^5$. With $\mathrm{CN^-}$ the electrons pair up, leaving 1 unpaired electron (weakly paramagnetic); with $\mathrm{F^-}$ all 5 remain unpaired (strongly paramagnetic). R explains A.

  14. Question 14 · 1 mark · Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers
    Assertion (A): Phenol undergoes electrophilic substitution more readily than benzene.
    Reason (R): The -OH group withdraws electrons from the benzene ring by its inductive effect.
    Select the most appropriate answer from the options given below:

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.

    Both are true. But the ring is activated because the lone pair on oxygen is delocalised into the ring (+R effect), which increases electron density at ortho and para positions. The -I effect actually opposes this, so R is not the explanation of A.

  15. Question 15 · 1 mark · Amines
    Assertion (A): Aniline readily undergoes Friedel-Crafts alkylation in the presence of anhydrous $\mathrm{AlCl_3}$.
    Reason (R): Anhydrous $\mathrm{AlCl_3}$ is a Lewis acid and forms a salt with aniline.
    Select the most appropriate answer from the options given below:

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) A is false but R is true.

    A is false: aniline does not undergo Friedel-Crafts reactions. R is true: the basic $\mathrm{-NH_2}$ group donates its lone pair to $\mathrm{AlCl_3}$; the positive charge on nitrogen then deactivates the ring.

  16. Question 16 · 1 mark · Biomolecules
    Assertion (A): Glucose reduces Tollens' reagent.
    Reason (R): Glucose contains a ketone group.
    Select the most appropriate answer from the options given below:

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) A is true but R is false.

    A is true: glucose is a reducing sugar. R is false: glucose is an aldohexose; it contains an aldehyde group (C-1), which is responsible for reducing Tollens' reagent.

Section B: 2 marks each

  1. Question 17 · 2 marks · Chemical Kinetics
    For the reaction $\mathrm{2NO(g) + O_2(g) \rightarrow 2NO_2(g)}$, the rate law is: rate $= k[\mathrm{NO}]^2[\mathrm{O_2}]$.
    (i) What is the overall order of the reaction?
    (ii) How will the rate change if the volume of the reaction vessel is reduced to half, at the same temperature?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Overall order $= 2 + 1 = 3$
    (ii) Halving the volume doubles the concentration of each gas.
    New rate $= k(2[\mathrm{NO}])^2(2[\mathrm{O_2}]) = 8k[\mathrm{NO}]^2[\mathrm{O_2}]$
    The rate becomes 8 times the original rate.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Overall order = 3 (1 mark)
    • (ii) Concentrations doubled: rate $= k(2[\mathrm{NO}])^2(2[\mathrm{O_2}])$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) Rate becomes 8 times (½ marks)

  2. Question 18 · 2 marks · Coordination Compounds
    (i) Write the formula of tetraamminediaquacobalt(III) chloride.
    (ii) What is a chelate ligand? Give one example.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_4(H_2O)_2]Cl_3}$
    (ii) A chelate ligand is a polydentate (di- or polydentate) ligand that binds the same central metal atom through two or more donor atoms at the same time, forming a ring. Example: ethane-1,2-diamine (en), $\mathrm{H_2NCH_2CH_2NH_2}$ (or oxalate, $\mathrm{C_2O_4^{2-}}$, or EDTA).

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_4(H_2O)_2]Cl_3}$ (1 mark)
    • (ii) Definition: di/polydentate ligand binding the same metal through two or more donor atoms (forming a ring) (½ marks)
    • (ii) Correct example such as en, oxalate or EDTA (½ marks)

  3. Question 19 · 2 marks · Haloalkanes and Haloarenes
    (i) Chloroform is stored in closed, dark coloured bottles that are completely filled. Why?
    (ii) Write the main product formed when 2-bromobutane is heated with alcoholic KOH, and name the rule that predicts it.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) In the presence of air and light, chloroform is slowly oxidised to carbonyl chloride (phosgene), a very poisonous gas: $\mathrm{2CHCl_3 + O_2 \xrightarrow{light} 2COCl_2 + 2HCl}$. Dark bottles keep out light and filling them completely keeps out air.
    (ii) But-2-ene, $\mathrm{CH_3CH{=}CHCH_3}$ (main product). Saytzeff (Zaitsev) rule: the more substituted alkene is the major product.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Oxidised by air in light to poisonous phosgene, $\mathrm{COCl_2}$ (1 mark)
    • (ii) But-2-ene (½ marks)
    • (ii) Saytzeff (Zaitsev) rule (½ marks)

  4. Question 20 · 2 marks · Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids
    Give one simple chemical test to distinguish between the following pairs of compounds:
    (i) Propanal and propanone
    (ii) Benzoic acid and phenol

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Tollens' test: on warming with Tollens' reagent, propanal gives a silver mirror; propanone does not. (Or Fehling's test: propanal gives a red-brown precipitate; propanone does not.)
    (ii) Sodium hydrogencarbonate test: benzoic acid gives brisk effervescence of $\mathrm{CO_2}$ with aqueous $\mathrm{NaHCO_3}$; phenol does not. (Or neutral $\mathrm{FeCl_3}$: phenol gives a violet colour; benzoic acid gives a buff precipitate.)

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Correct reagent (Tollens'/Fehling's) (½ marks)
    • (i) Correct observation: propanal positive, propanone negative (½ marks)
    • (ii) Correct reagent ($\mathrm{NaHCO_3}$ or neutral $\mathrm{FeCl_3}$) (½ marks)
    • (ii) Correct observation for each compound (½ marks)

  5. Question 21 · 2 marks · Biomolecules
    (i) Name the products obtained on hydrolysis of lactose.
    (ii) Amino acids show amphoteric behaviour in aqueous solution. Explain, using the zwitter ion structure of an $\alpha$-amino acid.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) $\beta$-D-galactose and $\beta$-D-glucose.
    (ii) In aqueous solution the $\mathrm{-COOH}$ group of an amino acid loses a proton and the $\mathrm{-NH_2}$ group gains it, forming a dipolar ion called a zwitter ion, $\mathrm{H_3\overset{+}{N}\text{-}CHR\text{-}COO^-}$. The $\mathrm{-COO^-}$ group can accept a proton from an acid and the $\mathrm{-NH_3^+}$ group can give a proton to a base, so amino acids react with both acids and bases (amphoteric).

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) $\beta$-D-galactose (½ marks)
    • (i) $\beta$-D-glucose (½ marks)
    • (ii) Zwitter ion structure $\mathrm{H_3\overset{+}{N}\text{-}CHR\text{-}COO^-}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) $\mathrm{-COO^-}$ accepts $\mathrm{H^+}$ from acids and $\mathrm{-NH_3^+}$ gives $\mathrm{H^+}$ to bases (½ marks)

Section C: 3 marks each

  1. Question 22 · 3 marks · Electrochemistry
    A conductivity cell filled with 0.1 M KCl solution has a resistance of 150 $\Omega$ at 298 K. The conductivity of 0.1 M KCl at 298 K is $0.0129\ \mathrm{S\,cm^{-1}}$. When the same cell is filled with a 0.02 M solution of an electrolyte, its resistance is 774 $\Omega$. Calculate:
    (i) the cell constant;
    (ii) the conductivity of the 0.02 M solution;
    (iii) the molar conductivity of the 0.02 M solution.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Cell constant $G^* = \kappa \times R = 0.0129 \times 150 = 1.935\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$
    (ii) $\kappa = \dfrac{G^*}{R} = \dfrac{1.935}{774} = 0.0025\ \mathrm{S\,cm^{-1}}$ ($2.5 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{S\,cm^{-1}}$)
    (iii) $\Lambda_m = \dfrac{\kappa \times 1000}{c} = \dfrac{0.0025 \times 1000}{0.02} = 125\ \mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}$

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) $G^* = \kappa R = 1.935\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ (1 mark)
    • (ii) $\kappa = 0.0025\ \mathrm{S\,cm^{-1}}$ (1 mark)
    • (iii) $\Lambda_m = \dfrac{\kappa \times 1000}{c} = 125\ \mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}$ (1 mark)

  2. Question 23 · 3 marks · Chemical Kinetics
    (i) The following initial rate data were obtained at 300 K for the reaction $\mathrm{A + B \rightarrow products}$:

    Experiment[A] ($\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$)[B] ($\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$)Initial rate ($\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}\,min^{-1}}$)
    10.10.1$2.0 \times 10^{-3}$
    20.20.1$4.0 \times 10^{-3}$
    30.10.2$8.0 \times 10^{-3}$
    Find the order of the reaction with respect to A and to B, write the rate law, and calculate the rate constant.
    (ii) How does a catalyst increase the rate of a reaction?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Let rate $= k[\mathrm{A}]^x[\mathrm{B}]^y$.
    Experiments 2 and 1: $\dfrac{4.0 \times 10^{-3}}{2.0 \times 10^{-3}} = \left(\dfrac{0.2}{0.1}\right)^x$, so $2 = 2^x$ and $x = 1$.
    Experiments 3 and 1: $\dfrac{8.0 \times 10^{-3}}{2.0 \times 10^{-3}} = \left(\dfrac{0.2}{0.1}\right)^y$, so $4 = 2^y$ and $y = 2$.
    Rate law: rate $= k[\mathrm{A}][\mathrm{B}]^2$
    From experiment 1: $k = \dfrac{2.0 \times 10^{-3}}{0.1 \times (0.1)^2} = 2.0\ \mathrm{L^2\,mol^{-2}\,min^{-1}}$
    (ii) A catalyst provides an alternative path (mechanism) for the reaction with a lower activation energy, so a larger fraction of molecules can react.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Order with respect to A = 1 (½ marks)
    • (i) Order with respect to B = 2 (½ marks)
    • (i) Rate law: rate $= k[\mathrm{A}][\mathrm{B}]^2$ (½ marks)
    • (i) $k = 2.0\ \mathrm{L^2\,mol^{-2}\,min^{-1}}$ (value with unit) (½ marks)
    • (ii) Alternative path with lower activation energy (1 mark)

  3. Question 24 · 3 marks · Coordination Compounds
    (i) Name the type of isomerism shown by $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_4Cl_2]^+}$ and name its isomers.
    (ii) $\mathrm{[Ti(H_2O)_6]^{3+}}$ is violet in colour. Explain on the basis of crystal field theory.
    (iii) Write the electronic configuration of a $d^6$ ion in an octahedral crystal field when (a) $\Delta_o \gt P$ and (b) $\Delta_o \lt P$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Geometrical isomerism; cis and trans isomers (the two Cl ligands adjacent in cis, opposite in trans).
    (ii) $\mathrm{Ti^{3+}}$ is $3d^1$. In the octahedral field the d orbitals split into $t_{2g}$ and $e_g$. The single electron in $t_{2g}$ absorbs light from the visible region and is promoted to $e_g$ (d-d transition). The complex shows the complementary colour of the light absorbed, which is violet.
    (iii) (a) $t_{2g}^6 e_g^0$ (low spin) (b) $t_{2g}^4 e_g^2$ (high spin)

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Geometrical isomerism: cis and trans (1 mark)
    • (ii) $3d^1$, d-d transition from $t_{2g}$ to $e_g$ by absorbing visible light; complementary colour seen (1 mark)
    • (iii)(a) $t_{2g}^6 e_g^0$ (½ marks)
    • (iii)(b) $t_{2g}^4 e_g^2$ (½ marks)

  4. Question 25 · 3 marks · Haloalkanes and Haloarenes
    Answer the following:
    (i) Which of the two, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2Br}$ or $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3CBr}$, reacts faster by the $\mathrm{S_N2}$ mechanism? Give reason.
    (ii) Optically active 2-chlorobutane gives a racemic mixture of butan-2-ol on hydrolysis under $\mathrm{S_N1}$ conditions. Explain.
    (iii) Haloalkanes are only very slightly soluble in water, although they are polar. Why?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2Br}$ (primary) reacts faster. In $\mathrm{S_N2}$ the nucleophile attacks the carbon from the back; the three methyl groups around the carbon in $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3CBr}$ cause steric hindrance.
    (ii) In $\mathrm{S_N1}$ a planar carbocation is formed. The nucleophile ($\mathrm{OH^-}$/water) can attack it equally from both faces, giving equal amounts of the two enantiomers, so the product is racemic (optically inactive).
    (iii) To dissolve, energy is needed to break the hydrogen bonds between water molecules. The new attractions between haloalkane and water molecules are weaker and release less energy, so solubility is very low.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2Br}$; less steric hindrance (primary) (1 mark)
    • (ii) Planar carbocation attacked equally from both sides, giving both enantiomers in equal amounts (1 mark)
    • (iii) Energy to break hydrogen bonds in water not compensated by new haloalkane-water attractions (1 mark)

  5. Question 26 · 3 marks · Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids
    Write the main organic product of each reaction and name the reaction:
    (i) $\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3 \xrightarrow{Zn\text{-}Hg,\ conc.\ HCl}}$ ?
    (ii) $\mathrm{C_6H_5COCl \xrightarrow{H_2,\ Pd\text{-}BaSO_4}}$ ?
    (iii) $\mathrm{C_6H_5CHO + CH_3CHO \xrightarrow{dil.\ NaOH,\ heat}}$ ?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Propane, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_3}$: Clemmensen reduction.
    (ii) Benzaldehyde, $\mathrm{C_6H_5CHO}$: Rosenmund reduction.
    (iii) 3-Phenylprop-2-enal (cinnamaldehyde), $\mathrm{C_6H_5CH{=}CHCHO}$ (with loss of $\mathrm{H_2O}$): cross aldol condensation (Claisen-Schmidt reaction).

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Propane (½ marks)
    • (i) Clemmensen reduction (½ marks)
    • (ii) Benzaldehyde (½ marks)
    • (ii) Rosenmund reduction (½ marks)
    • (iii) $\mathrm{C_6H_5CH{=}CHCHO}$ (½ marks)
    • (iii) Cross aldol condensation (or Claisen-Schmidt reaction) (½ marks)

  6. Question 27 · 3 marks · Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids
    (i) Arrange the following in increasing order of acidic strength: benzoic acid, 4-nitrobenzoic acid, 4-methoxybenzoic acid.
    (ii) Write the chemical equation for the reaction of sodium ethanoate with soda lime on heating.
    (iii) Carboxylic acids have higher boiling points than alcohols of comparable molecular mass. Why?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) 4-methoxybenzoic acid $\lt$ benzoic acid $\lt$ 4-nitrobenzoic acid. (The electron-releasing $\mathrm{-OCH_3}$ group destabilises the carboxylate ion; the electron-withdrawing $\mathrm{-NO_2}$ group stabilises it.)
    (ii) $\mathrm{CH_3COONa + NaOH \xrightarrow{CaO,\ heat} CH_4 + Na_2CO_3}$ (decarboxylation)
    (iii) Carboxylic acid molecules are held together by strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding, and they exist as dimers even in the vapour phase. More energy is needed to separate them.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) 4-methoxybenzoic acid $\lt$ benzoic acid $\lt$ 4-nitrobenzoic acid (1 mark)
    • (ii) Products $\mathrm{CH_4}$ and $\mathrm{Na_2CO_3}$ in a balanced equation (1 mark)
    • (iii) Extensive hydrogen bonding / dimer formation (1 mark)

  7. Question 28 · 3 marks · Biomolecules
    (i) What happens when D-glucose is heated for a long time with HI? What does this show about its structure?
    (ii) What happens when D-glucose is treated with bromine water? What does this show about its structure?
    (iii) Name the two types of secondary structure of proteins and the force that stabilises them.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) n-Hexane is formed. This shows that the six carbon atoms of glucose are linked in a straight chain.
    (ii) Gluconic acid, $\mathrm{HOCH_2(CHOH)_4COOH}$, is formed (bromine water is a mild oxidising agent). This shows that glucose contains an aldehyde ($\mathrm{-CHO}$) group.
    (iii) $\alpha$-helix and $\beta$-pleated sheet. They are stabilised by hydrogen bonds between the $\mathrm{C{=}O}$ and $\mathrm{-NH-}$ groups of the peptide bonds.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) n-Hexane formed; straight chain of six carbon atoms (1 mark)
    • (ii) Gluconic acid formed; presence of aldehyde group (1 mark)
    • (iii) $\alpha$-helix and $\beta$-pleated sheet (½ marks)
    • (iii) Hydrogen bonding (½ marks)

Section D: case-based, 4 marks each

  1. Question 29 · 4 marks · Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers
    From alcohol to ether
    Ethoxyethane (diethyl ether) was once widely used as an anaesthetic and is still an important solvent. In industry it can be made by heating ethanol with concentrated sulphuric acid. The product depends on the temperature: at about 413 K ethoxyethane is the main product, while at about 443 K the main product is a gas that decolourises bromine water. At 413 K the reaction takes place by nucleophilic attack of one ethanol molecule on a protonated ethanol molecule. This method gives good yields only for ethers made from primary alcohols; secondary and tertiary alcohols do not give good yields of ethers in this way.

    (i) Name the main product formed when ethanol is heated with concentrated sulphuric acid at 443 K. [1]

    (ii) Why does this method not give good yields of ethers from secondary and tertiary alcohols? [1]

    (iii) Write the steps of the mechanism of formation of ethoxyethane from ethanol at 413 K. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Name the main product formed when ethanol is heated with concentrated sulphuric acid at 443 K.

    Model answer: Ethene, $\mathrm{CH_2{=}CH_2}$ (formed by dehydration: $\mathrm{C_2H_5OH \rightarrow CH_2{=}CH_2 + H_2O}$).

    Marking scheme:

    • Ethene, $\mathrm{CH_2{=}CH_2}$ (1 mark)

    (ii) Why does this method not give good yields of ethers from secondary and tertiary alcohols?

    Model answer: Secondary and tertiary alcohols form stable carbocations and readily lose water to form alkenes. Elimination competes with (and dominates over) substitution, so alkenes are formed instead of ethers.

    Marking scheme:

    • Elimination to give alkenes dominates (stable carbocations, easy dehydration) (1 mark)

    (iii) Write the steps of the mechanism of formation of ethoxyethane from ethanol at 413 K.

    Model answer: Step 1 (protonation of ethanol): $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH + H^+ \rightarrow CH_3CH_2OH_2^+}$
    Step 2 (nucleophilic attack by a second ethanol molecule, $\mathrm{S_N2}$): $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH + CH_3CH_2OH_2^+ \rightarrow CH_3CH_2O^+(H)CH_2CH_3 + H_2O}$
    Step 3 (loss of a proton): $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2O^+(H)CH_2CH_3 \rightarrow CH_3CH_2OCH_2CH_3 + H^+}$

    Marking scheme:

    • Step 1: protonation of ethanol to $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH_2^+}$ (½ marks)
    • Step 2: attack of second ethanol molecule with loss of $\mathrm{H_2O}$ (1 mark)
    • Step 3: deprotonation to give ethoxyethane (½ marks)

  2. Question 30 · 4 marks · Electrochemistry
    Why iron rusts
    Rusting of iron is an electrochemical process. A film of water on the surface of iron, with dissolved oxygen and carbon dioxide, acts as the electrolyte. At some spots (anodic spots) iron is oxidised:
    $\mathrm{Fe(s) \rightarrow Fe^{2+}(aq) + 2e^-}$, $E^\circ(\mathrm{Fe^{2+}/Fe}) = -0.44$ V
    At other spots (cathodic spots) oxygen is reduced:
    $\mathrm{O_2(g) + 4H^+(aq) + 4e^- \rightarrow 2H_2O(l)}$, $E^\circ = +1.23$ V
    The $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$ ions are further oxidised by air to form rust, $\mathrm{Fe_2O_3 \cdot xH_2O}$. Iron objects can be protected by painting, by coating with zinc (galvanising) or with tin (tin plating), or by connecting them to a more reactive metal. Given: $E^\circ(\mathrm{Zn^{2+}/Zn}) = -0.76$ V, $E^\circ(\mathrm{Sn^{2+}/Sn}) = -0.14$ V.

    (i) Calculate $E^\circ$ for the overall corrosion reaction $\mathrm{2Fe(s) + O_2(g) + 4H^+(aq) \rightarrow 2Fe^{2+}(aq) + 2H_2O(l)}$. [1]

    (ii) Iron railings near the sea rust faster than those in dry inland areas. Give reason. [1]

    (iii) A galvanised (zinc-coated) iron bucket does not rust even when the coating is scratched, but a tin-plated iron can rusts quickly once its coating is scratched. Explain using the $E^\circ$ values. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Calculate $E^\circ$ for the overall corrosion reaction $\mathrm{2Fe(s) + O_2(g) + 4H^+(aq) \rightarrow 2Fe^{2+}(aq) + 2H_2O(l)}$.

    Model answer: $E^\circ_{cell} = E^\circ_{cathode} - E^\circ_{anode} = 1.23 - (-0.44) = 1.67$ V

    Marking scheme:

    • $E^\circ = 1.23 - (-0.44) = 1.67$ V (1 mark)

    (ii) Iron railings near the sea rust faster than those in dry inland areas. Give reason.

    Model answer: Sea air and spray contain dissolved salts such as NaCl. The salts increase the conductivity of the water film (better electrolyte), so the electrochemical process of rusting is faster.

    Marking scheme:

    • Dissolved salt (NaCl) makes the water film a better electrolyte (higher conductivity), speeding up the electrochemical process (1 mark)

    (iii) A galvanised (zinc-coated) iron bucket does not rust even when the coating is scratched, but a tin-plated iron can rusts quickly once its coating is scratched. Explain using the $E^\circ$ values.

    Model answer: $E^\circ(\mathrm{Zn^{2+}/Zn}) = -0.76$ V is more negative than $E^\circ(\mathrm{Fe^{2+}/Fe}) = -0.44$ V, so zinc is oxidised in preference to iron. Zinc acts as the anode (sacrificial protection) and iron stays protected even where it is exposed.
    $E^\circ(\mathrm{Sn^{2+}/Sn}) = -0.14$ V is less negative than that of iron, so when the tin coating is broken, iron becomes the anode and is oxidised; corrosion of the exposed iron is even speeded up.

    Marking scheme:

    • Zinc has more negative $E^\circ$ than iron, so Zn is oxidised preferentially (sacrificial anode), iron protected (1 mark)
    • Tin has less negative $E^\circ$ than iron, so exposed iron becomes the anode and rusts (1 mark)

  1. Question 31 · 5 marks · Solutions
    (i) 2 g of a synthetic polymer is dissolved in water to make 500 mL of solution at 300 K. The osmotic pressure of the solution is $2.49 \times 10^{-3}$ bar. ($R = 0.083\ \mathrm{L\,bar\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}$)
    (a) Calculate the molar mass of the polymer.
    (b) Why is osmotic pressure preferred over other colligative properties for finding the molar mass of polymers?
    (ii) A 0.01 m aqueous solution of a salt $\mathrm{AB_2}$, which dissociates as $\mathrm{AB_2 \rightarrow A^{2+} + 2B^-}$, freezes at $-0.0465\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$. ($K_f$ for water $= 1.86\ \mathrm{K\,kg\,mol^{-1}}$; freezing point of water $= 0\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$)
    Calculate the van't Hoff factor and the degree of dissociation of the salt.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i)(a) $\pi = \dfrac{w_2 RT}{M_2 V}$, so $M_2 = \dfrac{w_2 RT}{\pi V}$
    $M_2 = \dfrac{2 \times 0.083 \times 300}{2.49 \times 10^{-3} \times 0.5} = \dfrac{49.8}{1.245 \times 10^{-3}} = 40000\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$
    (b) Polymers have large molar masses, so the other colligative properties are too small to measure accurately; osmotic pressure is large enough to measure and is measured at room temperature (polymers may decompose on heating).
    (ii) $\Delta T_f = 0.0465$ K. $\Delta T_f = iK_f m$, so $i = \dfrac{0.0465}{1.86 \times 0.01} = 2.5$
    For $\mathrm{AB_2}$, $n = 3$ ions: $i = 1 + (n - 1)\alpha = 1 + 2\alpha$
    $2.5 = 1 + 2\alpha$, so $\alpha = 0.75$ (75% dissociated)

    Marking scheme:

    • (i)(a) Formula $M_2 = \dfrac{w_2 RT}{\pi V}$ with V = 0.5 L (1 mark)
    • (i)(a) $M_2 = 40000\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$ (1 mark)
    • (i)(b) Osmotic pressure is measurable even for very dilute solutions / measured at room temperature, suitable for large molar mass or heat-sensitive polymers (1 mark)
    • (ii) $i = 2.5$ (1 mark)
    • (ii) $i = 1 + 2\alpha$ gives $\alpha = 0.75$ (1 mark)

  2. Question 32 · 5 marks · The d- and f-Block Elements
    (i) Account for the following:
    (a) Transition metals and their compounds are good catalysts.
    (b) Manganese shows the largest number of oxidation states in the first transition series.
    (c) The $\mathrm{Cu^+}$ ion is not stable in aqueous solution.
    (ii) Write balanced ionic equations for the following:
    (a) A yellow solution of sodium chromate turns orange when acid is added.
    (b) Acidified potassium dichromate oxidises iodide ions.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i)(a) They can show variable oxidation states and so form unstable intermediates easily; they also provide a large surface area for adsorption of reactants (and can form complexes).
    (b) Mn ($3d^5 4s^2$) has the largest number of electrons in 3d and 4s that can take part in bonding, so it shows oxidation states from +2 to +7.
    (c) $\mathrm{Cu^+}$ disproportionates in water: $\mathrm{2Cu^+(aq) \rightarrow Cu^{2+}(aq) + Cu(s)}$. The much higher (more negative) hydration enthalpy of $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ more than compensates for the second ionisation enthalpy of copper.
    (ii)(a) $\mathrm{2CrO_4^{2-} + 2H^+ \rightarrow Cr_2O_7^{2-} + H_2O}$
    (b) $\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-} + 14H^+ + 6I^- \rightarrow 2Cr^{3+} + 3I_2 + 7H_2O}$

    Marking scheme:

    • (i)(a) Variable oxidation states / large surface area / complex formation (1 mark)
    • (i)(b) Maximum number of 3d and 4s electrons available for bonding (+2 to +7) (1 mark)
    • (i)(c) Disproportionation of $\mathrm{Cu^+}$; high hydration enthalpy of $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ (1 mark)
    • (ii)(a) $\mathrm{2CrO_4^{2-} + 2H^+ \rightarrow Cr_2O_7^{2-} + H_2O}$ (1 mark)
    • (ii)(b) $\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-} + 14H^+ + 6I^- \rightarrow 2Cr^{3+} + 3I_2 + 7H_2O}$ (1 mark)

  3. Question 33 · 5 marks · Amines
    (i) Identify the compounds A, B and C in the following sequence and write their IUPAC names:
    $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2Br \xrightarrow{KCN\ (alc.)} A \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} B \xrightarrow{NaNO_2\,+\,HCl,\ 273\text{-}278\ K} C}$
    (ii) Arrange the following in increasing order of their $\mathrm{p}K_b$ values in aqueous solution: $\mathrm{C_6H_5NH_2}$, $\mathrm{NH_3}$, $\mathrm{CH_3NH_2}$, $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2NH}$
    (iii) Aniline is insoluble in water but dissolves in dilute hydrochloric acid. Why?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) A = $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CN}$, propanenitrile
    B = $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2NH_2}$, propan-1-amine
    C = $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2OH}$, propan-1-ol (the aliphatic diazonium salt formed is unstable and loses $\mathrm{N_2}$ to give the alcohol)
    (ii) $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2NH \lt CH_3NH_2 \lt NH_3 \lt C_6H_5NH_2}$ (lower $\mathrm{p}K_b$ means stronger base)
    (iii) Aniline is basic and reacts with HCl to form anilinium chloride, $\mathrm{C_6H_5NH_3^+Cl^-}$, which is an ionic salt and dissolves in water.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) A = propanenitrile, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CN}$ (1 mark)
    • (i) B = propan-1-amine, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2NH_2}$ (1 mark)
    • (i) C = propan-1-ol, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2OH}$ (1 mark)
    • (ii) $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2NH \lt CH_3NH_2 \lt NH_3 \lt C_6H_5NH_2}$ (1 mark)
    • (iii) Forms water-soluble salt anilinium chloride, $\mathrm{C_6H_5NH_3^+Cl^-}$ (1 mark)
