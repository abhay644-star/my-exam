A few questions from this chapter. Open each one to see the answer.

  1. The half-life of a reaction of which order is independent of the initial concentration?

    1. first
    2. third
    3. second
    4. zero
    Show answer

    Answer: first

    For a first-order reaction t½ = 0.693/k, which does not depend on [A]0.

  2. A first-order reaction has a rate constant of 0.0693 min^-1. Find its half-life.

    1. 100 min
    2. 10 min
    3. 1 min
    4. 0.1 min
    Show answer

    Answer: 10 min

    t½ = 0.693/k = 0.693/0.0693 = 10 min.

  3. What fraction of a first-order reactant remains after three half-lives? (Give as a decimal.)

    1. 0.0625
    2. 0.125
    3. 0.333
    4. 0.25
    Show answer

    Answer: 0.125

    After each half-life half remains: (1/2)^3 = 1/8 = 0.125.

  4. The rate law of a reaction is Rate = k[A]^2[B]. If [A] is doubled and [B] is halved, the rate becomes how many times the original?

    1. 4
    2. 2
    3. 1
    4. 8
    Show answer

    Answer: 2

    New rate = k(2[A])^2([B]/2) = 4 × 0.5 = 2 times the original.

  5. The number of reacting species taking part in an elementary reaction is called the ______ of the reaction.

    1. order
    2. half-life
    3. rate constant
    4. molecularity
    Show answer

    Answer: molecularity

    Molecularity is always a whole number and applies only to elementary reactions; order is found by experiment.

  6. The extra energy that reactant molecules must absorb, over their average energy, to form products is called ______ energy.

    1. kinetic
    2. lattice
    3. activation
    4. ionisation
    Show answer

    Answer: activation

    Activation energy (Ea) appears in the Arrhenius equation k = A e^(−Ea/RT).

  7. A first-order reaction is 90% complete in 100 s. Find its rate constant. (k = (2.303 ÷ t) log([R]0 ÷ [R]))

    1. 0.02303 s^-1
    2. 0.0693 s^-1
    3. 0.2303 s^-1
    4. 0.002303 s^-1
    Show answer

    Answer: 0.02303 s^-1

    [R]0 ÷ [R] = 100 ÷ 10 = 10 and log 10 = 1, so k = 2.303 ÷ 100 = 0.02303 s^-1.

  8. What is the unit of the rate constant of a first-order reaction?

    1. mol L^-1
    2. mol L^-1 s^-1
    3. L mol^-1 s^-1
    4. s^-1
    Show answer

    Answer: s^-1

    For a first-order reaction, k = rate ÷ [A] = (mol L^-1 s^-1) ÷ (mol L^-1) = s^-1.
