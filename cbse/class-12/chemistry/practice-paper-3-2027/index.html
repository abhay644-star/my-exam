Questions

Section A: 1 mark each

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Solutions
    Which of the following ways of expressing the concentration of a solution does NOT change with temperature?

    1. Molarity
    2. Molality
    3. Mass by volume percentage
    4. Volume percentage
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Molality

    Molality is moles of solute per kilogram of solvent; it depends only on masses, which do not change with temperature. The other three involve the volume of the solution, which changes with temperature.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Electrochemistry
    The standard electrode potentials of some electrodes are: $E^\circ(\mathrm{Ag^+/Ag}) = +0.80$ V, $E^\circ(\mathrm{Cu^{2+}/Cu}) = +0.34$ V, $E^\circ(\mathrm{Zn^{2+}/Zn}) = -0.76$ V and $E^\circ(\mathrm{Mg^{2+}/Mg}) = -2.37$ V. Which of the following metals is the strongest reducing agent?

    1. Ag
    2. Cu
    3. Zn
    4. Mg
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Mg

    The lower (more negative) the reduction potential, the more easily the metal is oxidised, so the stronger it is as a reducing agent. Mg has the most negative $E^\circ$ ($-2.37$ V).

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Electrochemistry
    In a hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell, hydrogen and oxygen are bubbled through porous carbon electrodes into concentrated aqueous sodium hydroxide. The reaction at the cathode is:

    1. $\mathrm{2H_2(g) + 4OH^-(aq) \rightarrow 4H_2O(l) + 4e^-}$
    2. $\mathrm{4OH^-(aq) \rightarrow O_2(g) + 2H_2O(l) + 4e^-}$
    3. $\mathrm{O_2(g) + 2H_2O(l) + 4e^- \rightarrow 4OH^-(aq)}$
    4. $\mathrm{2H_2O(l) + 2e^- \rightarrow H_2(g) + 2OH^-(aq)}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $\mathrm{O_2(g) + 2H_2O(l) + 4e^- \rightarrow 4OH^-(aq)}$

    Reduction takes place at the cathode: oxygen is reduced to hydroxide ions. Hydrogen is oxidised at the anode (option A).

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Chemical Kinetics
    For a first order reaction, a graph of $\ln[\mathrm{R}]$ against time $t$ is a straight line. Its slope is:

    1. $k$
    2. $-k$
    3. $-\dfrac{k}{2.303}$
    4. $\dfrac{k}{2.303}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) $-k$

    $\ln[\mathrm{R}] = -kt + \ln[\mathrm{R}]_0$, so the slope is $-k$. (A graph of $\log[\mathrm{R}]$ against $t$ would have slope $-k/2.303$.)

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · The d- and f-Block Elements
    Which of the following elements has the highest third ionisation enthalpy? (Atomic numbers: V = 23, Cr = 24, Mn = 25, Fe = 26)

    1. V
    2. Cr
    3. Mn
    4. Fe
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Mn

    The third ionisation removes an electron from $\mathrm{M^{2+}}$. $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$ is $3d^5$, a stable half-filled configuration, so removing an electron from it needs the most energy.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · The d- and f-Block Elements
    Which of the following oxides of manganese is the most acidic?

    1. $\mathrm{MnO}$
    2. $\mathrm{Mn_2O_3}$
    3. $\mathrm{MnO_2}$
    4. $\mathrm{Mn_2O_7}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) $\mathrm{Mn_2O_7}$

    The acidic character of oxides of a transition metal increases with its oxidation state. In $\mathrm{Mn_2O_7}$ manganese is in the +7 state, so it is the most acidic (it gives permanganic acid with water).

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Coordination Compounds
    Which of the following is a homoleptic complex? (en = ethane-1,2-diamine)

    1. $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+}}$
    2. $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_4Cl_2]^+}$
    3. $\mathrm{[Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_2]}$
    4. $\mathrm{[Co(en)_2Cl_2]^+}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+}}$

    A homoleptic complex has only one kind of ligand bound to the metal. $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+}}$ has only $\mathrm{NH_3}$; the others have two kinds of ligands (heteroleptic).

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Coordination Compounds
    Through how many donor atoms does the ethylenediaminetetraacetate ion, $\mathrm{EDTA^{4-}}$, bind to a central metal ion?

    1. 2
    2. 4
    3. 5
    4. 6
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) 6

    $\mathrm{EDTA^{4-}}$ is a hexadentate ligand: it binds through its two nitrogen atoms and four carboxylate oxygen atoms.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Haloalkanes and Haloarenes
    Which of the following is a vinylic halide?

    1. $\mathrm{CH_2{=}CHCH_2Cl}$
    2. $\mathrm{C_6H_5CH_2Cl}$
    3. $\mathrm{CH_2{=}CHCl}$
    4. $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2Cl}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $\mathrm{CH_2{=}CHCl}$

    In a vinylic halide the halogen is bonded to an $sp^2$ carbon of a C=C double bond, as in $\mathrm{CH_2{=}CHCl}$. Option A is allylic, B is benzylic and D is a simple alkyl halide.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids
    Which of the following compounds gives a red precipitate when warmed with Fehling's solution?

    1. Benzaldehyde
    2. Propanone
    3. Propanal
    4. Acetophenone
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Propanal

    Aliphatic aldehydes reduce Fehling's solution to a red-brown precipitate of $\mathrm{Cu_2O}$. Aromatic aldehydes such as benzaldehyde do not respond to this test, and ketones (propanone, acetophenone) are not oxidised by it.

  11. Question 11 · 1 mark · Amines
    The IUPAC name of $\mathrm{CH_3NHCH_2CH_3}$ is:

    1. N-Methylethanamine
    2. N-Ethylmethanamine
    3. Propan-1-amine
    4. Ethylmethanamine
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) N-Methylethanamine

    It is a secondary amine. The longer alkyl group (ethyl) gives the parent amine, ethanamine, and the methyl group on nitrogen is shown by the prefix N-methyl.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Amines
    Which reagent replaces the diazonium group of benzenediazonium chloride by a hydrogen atom, giving benzene?

    1. $\mathrm{CuCl/HCl}$
    2. Hypophosphorous acid, $\mathrm{H_3PO_2}$
    3. $\mathrm{KI}$
    4. Warm water
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Hypophosphorous acid, $\mathrm{H_3PO_2}$

    Mild reducing agents such as $\mathrm{H_3PO_2}$ reduce the diazonium salt to benzene: $\mathrm{C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^- + H_3PO_2 + H_2O \rightarrow C_6H_6 + N_2 + H_3PO_3 + HCl}$. $\mathrm{CuCl/HCl}$ gives chlorobenzene, KI gives iodobenzene and warm water gives phenol.

  13. Question 13 · 1 mark · Chemical Kinetics
    Assertion (A): The rate of a reaction increases with temperature mainly because the total number of collisions per second increases.
    Reason (R): A rise in temperature increases the fraction of molecules having energy equal to or greater than the activation energy.
    Select the most appropriate answer from the options given below:

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) A is false but R is true.

    A is false: a 10 K rise increases the collision frequency only slightly, yet the rate often nearly doubles. The main reason is the one stated in R, which is true: the fraction of molecules with energy at least equal to the activation energy increases sharply.

  14. Question 14 · 1 mark · Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers
    Assertion (A): Ethers have much lower boiling points than the alcohols isomeric with them.
    Reason (R): Ether molecules cannot form hydrogen bonds with one another.
    Select the most appropriate answer from the options given below:

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

    Alcohol molecules are associated by intermolecular hydrogen bonds (O-H...O), which need extra energy to break. Ethers have no H atom on oxygen, so they cannot form such bonds among themselves, and their boiling points are much lower. R explains A.

  15. Question 15 · 1 mark · Amines
    Assertion (A): Arenediazonium salts are more stable than alkyldiazonium salts.
    Reason (R): In an arenediazonium ion, the positive charge is dispersed over the benzene ring by resonance.
    Select the most appropriate answer from the options given below:

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

    Alkyldiazonium salts decompose even at low temperature, while arenediazonium salts can be kept in cold solution for a short time. Their extra stability comes from resonance of the diazonium group with the benzene ring, as stated in R.

  16. Question 16 · 1 mark · Biomolecules
    Assertion (A): Sucrose is a non-reducing sugar.
    Reason (R): On hydrolysis, sucrose gives an equimolar mixture of D-glucose and D-fructose.
    Select the most appropriate answer from the options given below:

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.

    Both are true. Sucrose is non-reducing because the glucose and fructose units are joined through their reducing groups (C1 of glucose and C2 of fructose), so no free aldehyde or ketone group is available. The products of hydrolysis do not explain this, so R is not the explanation of A.

Section B: 2 marks each

  1. Question 17 · 2 marks · The d- and f-Block Elements
    (i) Complete and balance the following ionic equation: $\mathrm{MnO_4^- + NO_2^- + H^+ \rightarrow}$
    (ii) Transition metals have high enthalpies of atomisation. Why?

    OR (i) $\mathrm{Eu^{2+}}$ is a strong reducing agent. Why?
    (ii) Complete and balance the following ionic equation: $\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-} + H_2S + H^+ \rightarrow}$

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) $\mathrm{2MnO_4^- + 5NO_2^- + 6H^+ \rightarrow 2Mn^{2+} + 5NO_3^- + 3H_2O}$
    (ii) Transition metals have a large number of unpaired d electrons, which take part in metallic bonding along with the s electrons. This gives strong interatomic (metallic) bonding, so a lot of energy is needed to separate the atoms.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Products $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$, $\mathrm{NO_3^-}$ and $\mathrm{H_2O}$ (½ marks)
    • (i) Correctly balanced: $\mathrm{2MnO_4^- + 5NO_2^- + 6H^+ \rightarrow 2Mn^{2+} + 5NO_3^- + 3H_2O}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) Many unpaired d electrons, so strong metallic bonding / strong interatomic interaction (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (i) The common and most stable oxidation state of lanthanoids is +3. $\mathrm{Eu^{2+}}$ readily loses an electron to change to $\mathrm{Eu^{3+}}$, so it acts as a strong reducing agent.
    (ii) $\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-} + 3H_2S + 8H^+ \rightarrow 2Cr^{3+} + 3S + 7H_2O}$

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) +3 is the most stable state of lanthanoids, so $\mathrm{Eu^{2+}}$ readily changes to $\mathrm{Eu^{3+}}$ (1 mark)
    • (ii) Products $\mathrm{Cr^{3+}}$, S and $\mathrm{H_2O}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) Correctly balanced: $\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-} + 3H_2S + 8H^+ \rightarrow 2Cr^{3+} + 3S + 7H_2O}$ (½ marks)

  2. Question 18 · 2 marks · Solutions
    Two liquids A and B form an ideal solution. At a certain temperature, the vapour pressure of pure A is 450 mm Hg and that of pure B is 700 mm Hg. A solution is prepared in which the mole fraction of A is 0.4. Calculate
    (i) the total vapour pressure of the solution, and
    (ii) the mole fraction of B in the vapour in equilibrium with the solution.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) By Raoult's law: $p_A = x_Ap_A^\circ = 0.4 \times 450 = 180$ mm Hg; $p_B = x_Bp_B^\circ = 0.6 \times 700 = 420$ mm Hg.
    $p_{total} = 180 + 420 = 600$ mm Hg.
    (ii) $y_B = \dfrac{p_B}{p_{total}} = \dfrac{420}{600} = 0.7$

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Partial pressures: $p_A = 180$ mm Hg and $p_B = 420$ mm Hg (½ marks)
    • (i) $p_{total} = 600$ mm Hg (½ marks)
    • (ii) $y_B = p_B/p_{total}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) $y_B = 0.7$ (½ marks)

  3. Question 19 · 2 marks · Coordination Compounds

    (ii) $\mathrm{K_2[Zn(OH)_4]}$

    Marking scheme:

  4. Question 20 · 2 marks · Haloalkanes and Haloarenes
    Name the main products formed when chlorobenzene is treated with:
    (i) chloromethane in the presence of anhydrous $\mathrm{AlCl_3}$;
    (ii) a mixture of concentrated nitric acid and concentrated sulphuric acid.
    In each case, state which product is the major one.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Chlorine is ortho and para directing, so a mixture of ortho and para products is formed in each case. The para product is the major one, because the ortho position is crowded (steric hindrance).
    (i) 1-Chloro-2-methylbenzene and 1-chloro-4-methylbenzene (Friedel-Crafts alkylation); 1-chloro-4-methylbenzene is major.
    (ii) 1-Chloro-2-nitrobenzene and 1-chloro-4-nitrobenzene (nitration); 1-chloro-4-nitrobenzene is major.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) 1-Chloro-2-methylbenzene and 1-chloro-4-methylbenzene (½ marks)
    • (i) Para isomer (1-chloro-4-methylbenzene) is major (½ marks)
    • (ii) 1-Chloro-2-nitrobenzene and 1-chloro-4-nitrobenzene (½ marks)
    • (ii) Para isomer (1-chloro-4-nitrobenzene) is major (½ marks)

  5. Question 21 · 2 marks · Electrochemistry
    The mercury cell is used in hearing aids and watches.
    (i) Write the reactions taking place at the anode and at the cathode of this cell.
    (ii) Why does the potential of a mercury cell remain nearly constant during its life?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Anode: $\mathrm{Zn(Hg) + 2OH^- \rightarrow ZnO(s) + H_2O + 2e^-}$
    Cathode: $\mathrm{HgO(s) + H_2O + 2e^- \rightarrow Hg(l) + 2OH^-}$
    (ii) The overall reaction is $\mathrm{Zn(Hg) + HgO(s) \rightarrow ZnO(s) + Hg(l)}$. It does not involve any ion in solution whose concentration changes during the life of the cell, so the cell potential stays nearly constant (about 1.35 V).

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Anode: $\mathrm{Zn(Hg) + 2OH^- \rightarrow ZnO(s) + H_2O + 2e^-}$ (½ marks)
    • (i) Cathode: $\mathrm{HgO(s) + H_2O + 2e^- \rightarrow Hg(l) + 2OH^-}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) Overall reaction involves no ion whose concentration changes, so potential stays constant (1 mark)

Section C: 3 marks each

  1. Question 22 · 3 marks · The d- and f-Block Elements
    Account for the following:
    (i) The $E^\circ(\mathrm{M^{3+}/M^{2+}})$ value for manganese (+1.57 V) is much more positive than that for chromium ($-0.41$ V) or iron (+0.77 V).
    (ii) The actinoid contraction is greater from element to element than the lanthanoid contraction.
    (iii) $\mathrm{Cr^{2+}}$ is a strong reducing agent. (Atomic numbers: Cr = 24, Mn = 25, Fe = 26)

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$ has the configuration $3d^5$, which is half-filled and extra stable. So $\mathrm{Mn^{3+}}$ ($3d^4$) readily accepts an electron to become $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$, giving a large positive $E^\circ$.
    (ii) In actinoids the 5f electrons shield the outer electrons from the nuclear charge even more poorly than the 4f electrons do in lanthanoids. So the effective nuclear charge increases more from one element to the next, and the size decreases more.
    (iii) $\mathrm{Cr^{2+}}$ ($3d^4$) loses an electron to become $\mathrm{Cr^{3+}}$ ($3d^3$). In an octahedral field $3d^3$ is $t_{2g}^3$, a half-filled $t_{2g}$ level, which is stable. So $\mathrm{Cr^{2+}}$ is readily oxidised and acts as a strong reducing agent.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$ is $3d^5$ (half-filled, stable) (½ marks)
    • (i) so $\mathrm{Mn^{3+}}$ is readily reduced to $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) 5f electrons shield more poorly than 4f electrons (1 mark)
    • (iii) $\mathrm{Cr^{2+}}$ ($d^4$) changes to $\mathrm{Cr^{3+}}$ ($d^3$) (½ marks)
    • (iii) $d^3$ has a half-filled $t_{2g}$ level, which is stable (½ marks)

  2. Question 23 · 3 marks · Coordination Compounds
    (i) Using valence bond theory, explain the type of hybridisation, the geometry and the magnetic behaviour of $\mathrm{[Ni(CN)_4]^{2-}}$. (Atomic number of Ni = 28)
    (ii) Low spin tetrahedral complexes are rarely observed. Why?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Ni is in the +2 state: $\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}$ is $3d^8$, with two unpaired electrons. $\mathrm{CN^-}$ is a strong field ligand; it makes the two unpaired 3d electrons pair up, which leaves one 3d orbital empty. This 3d orbital, the 4s orbital and two 4p orbitals form four $dsp^2$ hybrid orbitals, which accept four electron pairs from the four $\mathrm{CN^-}$ ions.
    Hybridisation $dsp^2$; geometry square planar; no unpaired electrons, so the complex is diamagnetic.
    (ii) The crystal field splitting in a tetrahedral field is small: $\Delta_t \approx \frac{4}{9}\Delta_o$. It is almost always smaller than the pairing energy, so electrons do not pair up and tetrahedral complexes are high spin.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) $\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}$ $3d^8$; $\mathrm{CN^-}$ (strong field) pairs the 3d electrons (½ marks)
    • (i) $dsp^2$ hybridisation (½ marks)
    • (i) Square planar (½ marks)
    • (i) Diamagnetic (no unpaired electrons) (½ marks)
    • (ii) $\Delta_t \approx \frac{4}{9}\Delta_o$ (small splitting) (½ marks)
    • (ii) $\Delta_t$ is less than the pairing energy, so electrons stay unpaired (½ marks)

  3. Question 24 · 3 marks · Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids
    Write the chemical equations for the following:
    (i) Stephen reaction, taking ethanenitrile as the starting compound.
    (ii) Gattermann-Koch reaction.
    (iii) Reaction of ethanal with methylmagnesium bromide, followed by hydrolysis.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) $\mathrm{CH_3C{\equiv}N + SnCl_2 + HCl \rightarrow CH_3CH{=}NH \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} CH_3CHO}$
    (ii) $\mathrm{C_6H_6 + CO + HCl \xrightarrow{anhyd.\ AlCl_3/CuCl} C_6H_5CHO}$
    (iii) $\mathrm{CH_3CHO + CH_3MgBr \rightarrow (CH_3)_2CH{-}OMgBr \xrightarrow{H_2O} (CH_3)_2CHOH + Mg(OH)Br}$ (product: propan-2-ol)

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Reduction by $\mathrm{SnCl_2/HCl}$ to the imine $\mathrm{CH_3CH{=}NH}$ (½ marks)
    • (i) Hydrolysis to ethanal, $\mathrm{CH_3CHO}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) Benzene with CO and HCl (½ marks)
    • (ii) Catalyst anhydrous $\mathrm{AlCl_3}$/CuCl; product benzaldehyde (½ marks)
    • (iii) Addition product $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2CH{-}OMgBr}$ (½ marks)
    • (iii) Propan-2-ol, $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2CHOH}$, on hydrolysis (½ marks)

  4. Question 25 · 3 marks · Amines
    (i) Give one chemical test to distinguish between propan-1-amine and N-methylethanamine.
    (ii) Give one chemical test to distinguish between aniline and benzylamine.
    (iii) Primary amines have higher boiling points than tertiary amines of comparable molecular mass. Why?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Carbylamine test: warm each amine with chloroform and alcoholic KOH. Propan-1-amine (a primary amine) gives an offensive-smelling isocyanide; N-methylethanamine (a secondary amine) does not. (Or Hinsberg test: with benzenesulphonyl chloride, propan-1-amine gives a sulphonamide that dissolves in aqueous KOH; the sulphonamide from N-methylethanamine does not dissolve.)
    (ii) Treat each with $\mathrm{NaNO_2}$ and dilute HCl at 273-278 K, then add an alkaline solution of naphthalen-2-ol ($\beta$-naphthol). Aniline forms benzenediazonium chloride, which gives an orange-red dye. Benzylamine (an aliphatic primary amine) gives nitrogen gas (effervescence) and no dye. (Or bromine water: aniline gives a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline; benzylamine does not.)
    (iii) Primary amines have N-H bonds, so their molecules are associated by intermolecular hydrogen bonding. Tertiary amines have no hydrogen on nitrogen and cannot form such hydrogen bonds.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) A correct test: carbylamine test (chloroform + alcoholic KOH) or Hinsberg test (½ marks)
    • (i) Correct result: propan-1-amine gives a foul-smelling isocyanide, N-methylethanamine does not (Hinsberg: only the sulphonamide of propan-1-amine dissolves in alkali) (½ marks)
    • (ii) A correct test: nitrous acid ($\mathrm{NaNO_2}$ + HCl) at 273-278 K, then alkaline naphthalen-2-ol (or bromine water) (½ marks)
    • (ii) Correct result: aniline gives an orange-red dye (or a white precipitate with bromine water); benzylamine does not (½ marks)
    • (iii) Intermolecular hydrogen bonding in primary amines (N-H present) (½ marks)
    • (iii) No H on N in tertiary amines, so no hydrogen bonding (½ marks)

  5. Question 26 · 3 marks · Biomolecules
    (i) Glucose pentaacetate does not react with hydroxylamine. What does this show about the structure of glucose?
    (ii) What are anomers? Name the two anomers of D-glucose.
    (iii) Name the two monosaccharide units present in maltose and the linkage between them.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) It shows that glucose pentaacetate has no free -CHO group. So glucose exists mainly in a cyclic (hemiacetal) form, in which the -CHO group has reacted with the -OH group at C5.
    (ii) Anomers are cyclic forms of a sugar that differ only in the configuration of the -OH group at C1 (the anomeric carbon). For D-glucose they are $\alpha$-D-glucose and $\beta$-D-glucose.
    (iii) Maltose is made of two $\alpha$-D-glucose units, joined by an $\alpha$-glycosidic linkage between C1 of one unit and C4 of the other.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) No free -CHO group (½ marks)
    • (i) Glucose exists in a cyclic (ring) form (½ marks)
    • (ii) Differ only in configuration at C1 (anomeric carbon) (½ marks)
    • (ii) $\alpha$-D-glucose and $\beta$-D-glucose (½ marks)
    • (iii) Two $\alpha$-D-glucose units (½ marks)
    • (iii) $\alpha$-glycosidic linkage, C1 to C4 (½ marks)

  6. Question 27 · 3 marks · Haloalkanes and Haloarenes
    (i) Write the IUPAC name of $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2CHCH(Cl)CH_3}$.
    (ii) Bromoethane is heated separately with (a) aqueous KOH and (b) alcoholic KOH. Name the main organic product in each case and explain why the products are different.

    OR (i) Write the IUPAC name of $\mathrm{CH_2{=}CHCH_2Br}$.
    (ii) Bromoethane reacts with KCN to give propanenitrile as the main product, but with AgCN it gives ethyl isocyanide as the main product. Explain.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) 2-Chloro-3-methylbutane.
    (ii) (a) Ethanol, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH}$ (substitution). (b) Ethene, $\mathrm{CH_2{=}CH_2}$ (elimination of HBr).
    In aqueous solution, $\mathrm{OH^-}$ is strongly hydrated and acts mainly as a nucleophile, replacing the bromine. In alcoholic KOH, the alkoxide ion $\mathrm{RO^-}$ (and $\mathrm{OH^-}$) is a strong base, which removes a $\beta$-hydrogen, so elimination takes place.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) 2-Chloro-3-methylbutane (1 mark)
    • (ii)(a) Ethanol (½ marks)
    • (ii)(b) Ethene (½ marks)
    • (ii) Aqueous: $\mathrm{OH^-}$ acts as a nucleophile (substitution) (½ marks)
    • (ii) Alcoholic: alkoxide/$\mathrm{OH^-}$ acts as a base removing $\beta$-H (elimination) (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (i) 3-Bromoprop-1-ene.
    (ii) The cyanide ion is an ambident nucleophile: it can attack through carbon or through nitrogen. KCN is ionic and gives free $\mathrm{CN^-}$ ions, which attack mainly through carbon, because a C-C bond is more stable than a C-N bond; so propanenitrile, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CN}$, is formed. AgCN is mainly covalent (Ag-C bond), so only the lone pair on nitrogen is free to attack, and ethyl isocyanide, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2NC}$, is formed.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) 3-Bromoprop-1-ene (1 mark)
    • (ii) Cyanide ion is ambident (can attack through C or N) (½ marks)
    • (ii) KCN is ionic: free $\mathrm{CN^-}$ attacks through C (C-C bond more stable), giving the nitrile (½ marks)
    • (ii) AgCN is covalent: only the lone pair on N is free to attack (½ marks)
    • (ii) so the isocyanide is formed (½ marks)

    (i) Distinguish between fibrous and globular proteins. Give one example of each.
    (ii) Give two differences between DNA and RNA in their chemical make-up.
    (iii) What are essential amino acids? Give one example.

    Model answer: (i) Fibrous proteins: the polypeptide chains run parallel and are held together by hydrogen and disulphide bonds, giving a fibre-like structure; they are generally insoluble in water. Example: keratin (or myosin). Globular proteins: the chains coil around to give a spherical shape; they are usually soluble in water. Example: insulin (or albumin).

    (iii) Amino acids that cannot be made in the body and must be obtained through the diet. Example: valine (or leucine, lysine, isoleucine, phenylalanine).

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Correct difference in shape/solubility (½ marks)
    • (i) One correct example of each (½ marks)
    • (ii) Sugar: 2-deoxyribose in DNA, ribose in RNA (½ marks)
    • (ii) Base: thymine in DNA, uracil in RNA (or double vs single strand) (½ marks)
    • (iii) Not made in the body; must be taken in the diet (½ marks)
    • (iii) Correct example (½ marks)

  1. Question 29 · 4 marks · Solutions
    Saline drips and red blood cells
    The membrane of a red blood cell behaves as a semipermeable membrane. A 0.9% (mass by volume) solution of sodium chloride, called normal saline, has the same osmotic pressure as the fluid inside red blood cells, so it can be injected safely into a vein. If red blood cells are placed in a solution of lower osmotic pressure, water flows into them; in a solution of higher osmotic pressure, water flows out of them. The osmotic pressure of a dilute solution is given by $\pi = iCRT$, where $i$ is the van't Hoff factor, $C$ the molar concentration, $R$ the gas constant and $T$ the temperature.

    (i) What are isotonic solutions? [1]

    (ii) What will happen to red blood cells placed in a 0.5% (mass by volume) sodium chloride solution? Give a reason. [1]

    (iii) A doctor needs a glucose solution that is isotonic with a 0.15 M sodium chloride solution at the same temperature. Assuming that sodium chloride dissociates completely, calculate the concentration of glucose needed in $\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ and in $\mathrm{g\,L^{-1}}$. (Molar mass of glucose = $180\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$) [2]

    OR (iii) Calculate the osmotic pressure, at body temperature (310 K), of a solution that contains 3 g of urea, $\mathrm{NH_2CONH_2}$ (molar mass $60\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$), in 500 mL of solution. Urea is a non-electrolyte. ($R = 0.083\ \mathrm{L\,bar\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}$)

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) What are isotonic solutions?

    Model answer: Two solutions that have the same osmotic pressure at a given temperature are called isotonic solutions. No net flow of solvent takes place between them through a semipermeable membrane.

    Marking scheme:

    • Same osmotic pressure at the same temperature (1 mark)

    (ii) What will happen to red blood cells placed in a 0.5% (mass by volume) sodium chloride solution? Give a reason.

    Model answer: The 0.5% solution has a lower osmotic pressure than the fluid in the cells (it is hypotonic). Water flows into the cells by osmosis, so the cells swell and may burst.

    Marking scheme:

    • 0.5% solution is hypotonic, so water flows into the cells (½ marks)
    • Cells swell and may burst (½ marks)

    (iii) A doctor needs a glucose solution that is isotonic with a 0.15 M sodium chloride solution at the same temperature. Assuming that sodium chloride dissociates completely, calculate the concentration of glucose needed in $\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ and in $\mathrm{g\,L^{-1}}$. (Molar mass of glucose = $180\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$)

    Model answer: For isotonic solutions at the same temperature, $i_1C_1 = i_2C_2$.
    For NaCl, $i = 2$; for glucose (non-electrolyte), $i = 1$.
    $C_{glucose} = 2 \times 0.15 = 0.30\ \mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$
    Mass concentration $= 0.30 \times 180 = 54\ \mathrm{g\,L^{-1}}$

    Marking scheme:

    • Condition for isotonic solutions: $i_1C_1 = i_2C_2$ (½ marks)
    • $i = 2$ for NaCl, $i = 1$ for glucose (½ marks)
    • $C = 0.30\ \mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ (½ marks)
    • $54\ \mathrm{g\,L^{-1}}$ (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: Moles of urea $= \dfrac{3}{60} = 0.05$ mol
    $C = \dfrac{0.05}{0.5\ \mathrm{L}} = 0.1\ \mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$; urea is a non-electrolyte, so $i = 1$.
    $\pi = CRT = 0.1 \times 0.083 \times 310 = 2.573$ bar (about 2.57 bar)

    Marking scheme:

    • $\pi = CRT$ (with $i = 1$) (½ marks)
    • $C = 0.1\ \mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ (½ marks)
    • Substitution: $0.1 \times 0.083 \times 310$ (½ marks)
    • $\pi \approx 2.57$ bar (½ marks)

  2. Question 30 · 4 marks · Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids
    Carbonyl compounds: boiling points and reactions
    The boiling points of some compounds of comparable molar mass are given below.

    CompoundFormulaMolar mass ($\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$)Boiling point (K)
    Butane$\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_3}$58273
    Methoxyethane$\mathrm{CH_3OCH_2CH_3}$60281
    Propanal$\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CHO}$58322
    Propanone$\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}$58329
    Propan-1-ol$\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2OH}$60370
    The carbonyl group is polar, because oxygen is more electronegative than carbon. The carbonyl carbon is therefore electrophilic, and nucleophiles add to it. Both electronic and steric factors decide how readily an aldehyde or ketone undergoes nucleophilic addition.

    (i) Why is the boiling point of propanal much higher than that of butane? [1]

    (ii) Why is the boiling point of propanal lower than that of propan-1-ol? [1]

    (iii) Arrange methanal, ethanal and propanone in decreasing order of reactivity towards nucleophilic addition. Give two reasons for your order. [2]

    OR (iii) Write the structure and name of the product formed when propanal reacts with (a) hydroxylamine and (b) sodium hydrogensulphite.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Why is the boiling point of propanal much higher than that of butane?

    Model answer: Propanal is a polar molecule, so its molecules are held together by dipole-dipole attractions (due to the polar C=O group). Butane is non-polar and has only weak van der Waals forces.

    Marking scheme:

    • Propanal: dipole-dipole attractions due to polar C=O (½ marks)
    • Butane: only weak van der Waals forces (½ marks)

    (ii) Why is the boiling point of propanal lower than that of propan-1-ol?

    Model answer: Propan-1-ol molecules are associated by intermolecular hydrogen bonding (O-H group). Propanal has no H attached to oxygen, so its molecules cannot form hydrogen bonds with one another.

    Marking scheme:

    • Propan-1-ol: intermolecular hydrogen bonding (½ marks)
    • Propanal: no hydrogen bonding between its molecules (½ marks)

    (iii) Arrange methanal, ethanal and propanone in decreasing order of reactivity towards nucleophilic addition. Give two reasons for your order.

    Model answer: Methanal > ethanal > propanone.
    Steric reason: alkyl groups crowd the carbonyl carbon and hinder the approach of the nucleophile; methanal has none, ethanal one and propanone two.
    Electronic reason: alkyl groups release electrons (+I effect) and reduce the positive charge (electrophilicity) of the carbonyl carbon.

    Marking scheme:

    • Order: methanal > ethanal > propanone (1 mark)
    • Steric hindrance by alkyl groups (½ marks)
    • +I effect of alkyl groups lowers electrophilicity of carbonyl carbon (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (a) $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CHO + NH_2OH \rightarrow CH_3CH_2CH{=}NOH + H_2O}$; product: propanal oxime.
    (b) $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CHO + NaHSO_3 \rightarrow CH_3CH_2CH(OH)SO_3Na}$; product: the sodium hydrogensulphite addition compound of propanal (a crystalline solid).

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH{=}NOH}$ (½ marks)
    • (a) Propanal oxime (½ marks)
    • (b) $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH(OH)SO_3Na}$ (½ marks)
    • (b) Hydrogensulphite (bisulphite) addition compound (½ marks)

  1. Question 31 · 5 marks · Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers
    (i) Account for the following:
    (a) o-Nitrophenol is steam volatile, but p-nitrophenol is not.
    (b) The C-O bond in phenol is shorter than the C-O bond in methanol.
    (c) Tertiary alcohols undergo acid-catalysed dehydration more readily than primary alcohols.
    (ii) Write the chemical equations for the following:
    (a) Kolbe's reaction.
    (b) Reaction of anisole with hydrogen iodide.

    OR (i) Write chemical equations to show how the following are prepared:
    (a) propan-2-ol from propene;
    (b) phenol from cumene;
    (c) phenol from benzenesulphonic acid.
    (ii) Give one chemical test to distinguish between the following pairs of compounds:
    (a) ethanol and phenol;
    (b) methanol and ethanol.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) (a) In o-nitrophenol, the -OH and -NO2 groups are close together and form an intramolecular hydrogen bond, so the molecules do not associate and the compound is volatile. In p-nitrophenol, the groups are far apart, so the molecules are associated by intermolecular hydrogen bonds; it is less volatile.
    (b) In phenol, the lone pair on oxygen is delocalised into the benzene ring (resonance), giving the C-O bond partial double bond character; also the carbon is $sp^2$ hybridised. In methanol the carbon is $sp^3$ and there is no resonance.
    (c) Dehydration takes place through a carbocation. A tertiary carbocation is much more stable than a primary carbocation, so tertiary alcohols lose water most easily.
    (ii) (a) $\mathrm{C_6H_5OH \xrightarrow{NaOH} C_6H_5ONa \xrightarrow{(i)\ CO_2,\ 4\text{-}7\ atm,\ 400\ K\ \ (ii)\ H^+} o\text{-}HOC_6H_4COOH}$ (2-hydroxybenzoic acid, salicylic acid)
    (b) $\mathrm{C_6H_5OCH_3 + HI \rightarrow C_6H_5OH + CH_3I}$

    Marking scheme:

    • (i)(a) Intramolecular H-bond in o-nitrophenol; intermolecular H-bonding (association) in p-nitrophenol (1 mark)
    • (i)(b) Resonance gives partial double bond character / $sp^2$ carbon in phenol (1 mark)
    • (i)(c) Tertiary carbocation is more stable (1 mark)
    • (ii)(a) Sodium phenoxide with CO2, then acid (½ marks)
    • (ii)(a) Product 2-hydroxybenzoic (salicylic) acid (½ marks)
    • (ii)(b) Products phenol and $\mathrm{CH_3I}$ (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (i) (a) $\mathrm{CH_3CH{=}CH_2 + H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+} CH_3CH(OH)CH_3}$ (acid-catalysed hydration, Markovnikov addition)
    (b) $\mathrm{C_6H_5CH(CH_3)_2 \xrightarrow{O_2\ (air)} C_6H_5C(CH_3)_2OOH \xrightarrow{H^+,\ H_2O} C_6H_5OH + CH_3COCH_3}$ (cumene hydroperoxide is formed and then split by dilute acid)
    (c) $\mathrm{C_6H_5SO_3H \xrightarrow{(i)\ NaOH\ (fused)} C_6H_5ONa \xrightarrow{(ii)\ H^+} C_6H_5OH}$
    (ii) (a) Add neutral iron(III) chloride solution: phenol gives a violet colour; ethanol does not.
    (b) Iodoform test: warm with iodine and sodium hydroxide solution. Ethanol gives a yellow precipitate of iodoform; methanol does not.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i)(a) Propene + water with acid catalyst gives propan-2-ol (1 mark)
    • (i)(b) Cumene to cumene hydroperoxide (air), then dilute acid gives phenol + propanone (1 mark)
    • (i)(c) Fusion with NaOH gives sodium phenoxide; acidification gives phenol (1 mark)
    • (ii)(a) Neutral $\mathrm{FeCl_3}$: violet colour with phenol only (1 mark)
    • (ii)(b) Iodoform test: yellow precipitate with ethanol only (1 mark)

  2. Question 32 · 5 marks · Chemical Kinetics
    (i) Derive the integrated rate equation for a zero order reaction. Hence write the expression for its half-life.
    (ii) Show that the time needed for 90% completion of a first order reaction is about 3.3 times its half-life. If the half-life of such a reaction is 30 min, how long will it take for 90% completion? (Given: log 2 = 0.301)
    (iii) What is a pseudo first order reaction? Give one example.

    OR (i) The rate constant of a reaction increases ten times when the temperature is raised from 300 K to 350 K. Calculate the activation energy of the reaction. (Take $2.303 \times 8.314\ \mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}} = 19.15\ \mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}$)
    (ii) According to the collision theory, which two conditions must a collision satisfy to be effective?
    (iii) What is threshold energy? How is it related to the activation energy?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) For R $\rightarrow$ P of zero order: rate $= -\dfrac{d[\mathrm{R}]}{dt} = k[\mathrm{R}]^0 = k$, so $d[\mathrm{R}] = -k\,dt$.
    Integrating: $[\mathrm{R}] = -kt + I$. At $t = 0$, $[\mathrm{R}] = [\mathrm{R}]_0$, so $I = [\mathrm{R}]_0$.
    $[\mathrm{R}] = [\mathrm{R}]_0 - kt$, or $k = \dfrac{[\mathrm{R}]_0 - [\mathrm{R}]}{t}$.
    At $t_{1/2}$, $[\mathrm{R}] = \frac{1}{2}[\mathrm{R}]_0$, so $t_{1/2} = \dfrac{[\mathrm{R}]_0}{2k}$.
    (ii) $t = \dfrac{2.303}{k}\log\dfrac{[\mathrm{R}]_0}{[\mathrm{R}]}$. For 90% completion, $[\mathrm{R}] = 0.1[\mathrm{R}]_0$: $t_{90} = \dfrac{2.303}{k}\log10 = \dfrac{2.303}{k}$.
    $t_{1/2} = \dfrac{2.303}{k}\log2 = \dfrac{2.303 \times 0.301}{k}$.
    $\dfrac{t_{90}}{t_{1/2}} = \dfrac{1}{0.301} = 3.32 \approx 3.3$.
    $t_{90} = 3.32 \times 30 \approx 99.7$ min (about 100 min).
    (iii) A reaction that is really of higher order but behaves as a first order reaction because one reactant is present in large excess, so its concentration hardly changes. Example: acid hydrolysis of ethyl ethanoate in a large excess of water, $\mathrm{CH_3COOC_2H_5 + H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+} CH_3COOH + C_2H_5OH}$ (or inversion of cane sugar).

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Rate $= -\frac{d[\mathrm{R}]}{dt} = k$ (½ marks)
    • (i) Integration with the boundary condition (½ marks)
    • (i) $[\mathrm{R}] = [\mathrm{R}]_0 - kt$ (½ marks)
    • (i) $t_{1/2} = \frac{[\mathrm{R}]_0}{2k}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) $t_{90} = \frac{2.303}{k}\log10$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) $t_{1/2} = \frac{2.303}{k}\log2$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) Ratio $= 1/0.301 = 3.32$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) $t_{90} \approx 99.7$ min (about 100 min) (½ marks)
    • (iii) Definition (one reactant in large excess) (½ marks)
    • (iii) Correct example (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (i) $\log\dfrac{k_2}{k_1} = \dfrac{E_a}{2.303R}\left[\dfrac{T_2 - T_1}{T_1T_2}\right]$
    $\log10 = 1 = \dfrac{E_a}{19.15}\left[\dfrac{350 - 300}{300 \times 350}\right] = \dfrac{E_a}{19.15} \times \dfrac{1}{2100}$
    $E_a = 19.15 \times 2100 = 40215\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}} \approx 40.2\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$
    (ii) The colliding molecules must have energy equal to or greater than the threshold energy, and they must collide with the proper orientation.
    (iii) Threshold energy is the minimum energy that colliding molecules must have for the collision to be effective. Activation energy = threshold energy - average energy of the reacting molecules.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Arrhenius equation: $\log\frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{E_a}{2.303R}\left[\frac{T_2-T_1}{T_1T_2}\right]$ (1 mark)
    • (i) Substitution: $\log10 = 1$ and $\frac{50}{300\times350} = \frac{1}{2100}$ (1 mark)
    • (i) $E_a \approx 40.2\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ (40215 J/mol) (1 mark)
    • (ii) Energy at least equal to the threshold energy (½ marks)
    • (ii) Proper orientation (½ marks)
    • (iii) Definition of threshold energy (½ marks)
    • (iii) Activation energy = threshold energy - average energy of reactants (½ marks)

  3. Question 33 · 5 marks · Electrochemistry
    (i) Consider the following cell at 298 K:
    $\mathrm{Zn(s)\,|\,Zn^{2+}(aq)\,||\,Cu^{2+}(0.1\,M)\,|\,Cu(s)}$
    Given: $E^\circ(\mathrm{Zn^{2+}/Zn}) = -0.76$ V, $E^\circ(\mathrm{Cu^{2+}/Cu}) = +0.34$ V and $\dfrac{2.303RT}{F} = 0.059$ V.
    (a) Write the cell reaction and calculate the standard emf of the cell.
    (b) The emf of the cell is found to be 1.159 V. Calculate the concentration of $\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}$ ions in the cell.
    (ii) What is the function of a salt bridge in a galvanic cell?
    (iii) Why does the conductivity of a solution of an electrolyte decrease on dilution?

    OR (i) How does the molar conductivity of (a) a strong electrolyte such as KCl and (b) a weak electrolyte such as ethanoic acid change with dilution? Explain each.
    (ii) State Kohlrausch's law of independent migration of ions. The limiting molar conductivities ($\Lambda_m^\circ$) of HCl, HCOONa and NaCl are 426, 105 and 126 $\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}$ respectively. Calculate $\Lambda_m^\circ$ for methanoic acid, HCOOH.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) (a) $\mathrm{Zn(s) + Cu^{2+}(aq) \rightarrow Zn^{2+}(aq) + Cu(s)}$ ($n = 2$)
    $E^\circ_{cell} = E^\circ_{cathode} - E^\circ_{anode} = 0.34 - (-0.76) = 1.10$ V
    (b) $E_{cell} = E^\circ_{cell} - \dfrac{0.059}{2}\log\dfrac{[\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}]}{[\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}]}$
    $1.159 = 1.10 - 0.0295\log\dfrac{[\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}]}{0.1}$
    $\log\dfrac{[\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}]}{0.1} = -\dfrac{0.059}{0.0295} = -2$, so $\dfrac{[\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}]}{0.1} = 10^{-2}$
    $[\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}] = 1 \times 10^{-3}$ M (0.001 M)
    (ii) The salt bridge completes the circuit by allowing ions to move between the two solutions, and it keeps each solution electrically neutral.
    (iii) Conductivity is the conductance of unit volume of the solution. On dilution, the number of ions per unit volume decreases, so the conductivity decreases.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i)(a) $\mathrm{Zn + Cu^{2+} \rightarrow Zn^{2+} + Cu}$ (½ marks)
    • (i)(a) $E^\circ_{cell} = 1.10$ V (½ marks)
    • (i)(b) Nernst equation with $n = 2$: $E = E^\circ - \frac{0.059}{2}\log\frac{[\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}]}{[\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}]}$ (½ marks)
    • (i)(b) Substitution: $1.159 = 1.10 - 0.0295\log\frac{[\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}]}{0.1}$ (½ marks)
    • (i)(b) $\log\frac{[\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}]}{0.1} = -2$ (½ marks)
    • (i)(b) $[\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}] = 1\times10^{-3}$ M (½ marks)
    • (ii) Completes the circuit (allows ion flow) and keeps the solutions electrically neutral (1 mark)
    • (iii) Number of ions per unit volume decreases on dilution (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (i) (a) Strong electrolyte: $\Lambda_m$ increases slowly with dilution, and varies linearly with $\sqrt{c}$ ($\Lambda_m = \Lambda_m^\circ - A\sqrt{c}$). It is already fully dissociated; dilution only reduces the attraction between ions, so they move a little faster.
    (b) Weak electrolyte: $\Lambda_m$ increases steeply on dilution, especially at low concentration, because the degree of dissociation increases greatly, so the number of ions in the solution increases.
    (ii) The limiting molar conductivity of an electrolyte is the sum of the limiting molar conductivities of its cation and anion, each multiplied by the number of such ions in its formula.
    $\Lambda_m^\circ(\mathrm{HCOOH}) = \Lambda_m^\circ(\mathrm{HCl}) + \Lambda_m^\circ(\mathrm{HCOONa}) - \Lambda_m^\circ(\mathrm{NaCl}) = 426 + 105 - 126 = 405\ \mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}$

    Marking scheme:

    • (i)(a) Strong: $\Lambda_m$ increases slowly (linear with $\sqrt{c}$) (½ marks)
    • (i)(a) Reason: interionic attraction decreases (½ marks)
    • (i)(b) Weak: $\Lambda_m$ increases steeply at low concentration (½ marks)
    • (i)(b) Reason: degree of dissociation increases (½ marks)
    • (ii) Statement of Kohlrausch's law (1 mark)
    • (ii) $\Lambda_m^\circ(\mathrm{HCOOH}) = \Lambda_m^\circ(\mathrm{HCl}) + \Lambda_m^\circ(\mathrm{HCOONa}) - \Lambda_m^\circ(\mathrm{NaCl})$ (1 mark)
    • (ii) $405\ \mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}$ (1 mark)
