Section A: Macroeconomics

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · National Income Accounting
    Identify which of the following are stock variables.
    (i) Depreciation of machines during 2025-26
    (ii) Capital stock of a firm on 1 April 2025
    (iii) Change in stock (inventory) during 2025-26
    (iv) Bank deposits held by a household on 31 March 2026
    (Choose the correct option)

    1. (i) and (iii)
    2. (ii) and (iv)
    3. (ii) and (iii)
    4. (i) and (iv)
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) (ii) and (iv)

    A stock is measured at a point of time, while a flow is measured over a period of time. Capital stock on a date and bank deposits on a date are stocks. Depreciation during a year and change in stock during a year are flows.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Money and Banking
    Read the following statements carefully:
    Statement 1: Under a barter system, a double coincidence of wants is not needed for an exchange to take place.
    Statement 2: Money serves as a standard of deferred payments, as future payments such as loan repayments can be stated in terms of money.
    In light of the given statements, choose the correct option from the following:

    1. Statement 1 is true and Statement 2 is false.
    2. Statement 1 is false and Statement 2 is true.
    3. Both Statements 1 and 2 are true.
    4. Both Statements 1 and 2 are false.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Statement 1 is false and Statement 2 is true.

    Statement 1 is false: barter needs a double coincidence of wants, as each party must want what the other offers. Statement 2 is true: debts payable in the future are stated in money, so money acts as a standard of deferred payments.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Open Economy Macroeconomics
    Ceteris paribus, a large inflow of foreign investment into India sets off a chain of effects in the foreign exchange market.
    Identify which of the following sequences explains this process.
    (i) Supply of US dollars in the foreign exchange market rises
    (ii) Indian exports become costlier for foreign buyers
    (iii) Rupee price of the US dollar falls
    (Choose the correct option)

    1. (i), (ii), (iii)
    2. (i), (iii), (ii)
    3. (iii), (i), (ii)
    4. (ii), (iii), (i)
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) (i), (iii), (ii)

    Foreign investors bring in dollars, so the supply of dollars rises; with demand unchanged, the rupee price of the dollar falls (the rupee appreciates); foreign buyers then need more dollars to buy the same rupee value of goods, so Indian exports become costlier for them.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · National Income Accounting
    For a hypothetical economy: Gross Domestic Product at market price = ₹5,000 crore, consumption of fixed capital = ₹400 crore, net factor income from abroad = ($-$) ₹100 crore and net indirect taxes = ₹300 crore.
    The value of National Income (Net National Product at factor cost) is ₹ ________ crore.
    (Choose the correct option to fill up the blank)

    1. 4,200
    2. 4,300
    3. 4,400
    4. 4,800
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) 4,200

    NNP at FC = GDP at MP $-$ Consumption of fixed capital + NFIA $-$ Net indirect taxes = 5,000 $-$ 400 + ($-$100) $-$ 300 = ₹4,200 crore.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Determination of Income and Employment
    Which of the following is always true?
    (Choose the correct option)

    1. APC + MPC = 1
    2. APC can never be greater than 1
    3. MPC + MPS = 1
    4. APS can never be negative
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) MPC + MPS = 1

    Every addition to income is either consumed or saved, so $\Delta C + \Delta S = \Delta Y$, which gives MPC + MPS = 1. Below the break-even level of income, consumption exceeds income, so APC is greater than 1 and APS is negative.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Open Economy Macroeconomics
    Read the following statements: Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Choose the correct option from those given below:
    Assertion (A): A rise in the price of foreign currency makes imports cheaper for the residents of a country.
    Reason (R): The demand for foreign exchange falls when its price rises.

    1. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    2. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    3. Assertion (A) is true, but Reason (R) is false.
    4. Assertion (A) is false, but Reason (R) is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Assertion (A) is false, but Reason (R) is true.

    A is false: when foreign currency becomes costlier, residents must pay more domestic currency for the same imports, so imports become costlier. R is true: as foreign exchange becomes costlier, imports become costlier and fewer are bought, so the demand for foreign exchange falls.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Determination of Income and Employment
    In an economy, the marginal propensity to consume is 0.9. To raise income by ₹5,000 crore, investment must increase by ₹ ________ crore.
    (Choose the correct option to fill up the blank)

    1. 500
    2. 1,000
    3. 4,500
    4. 50,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) 500

    Multiplier $k = \frac{1}{1 - MPC} = \frac{1}{0.1} = 10$. Required increase in investment $= \frac{\Delta Y}{k} = \frac{5{,}000}{10}$ = ₹500 crore.

  11. Question 11 · 3 marks · National Income Accounting
    Giving a valid reason, state whether the following will be included in the national income of India:
    (a) Profit earned by a branch of an Indian bank located in Singapore
    (b) Old-age pension paid by the government to retired citizens
    (c) Purchase of newly issued shares of an Indian company by a household

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Included. National income (NNP at factor cost) includes net factor income from abroad. The profit is a factor income earned abroad by a normal resident of India (the Indian bank).
    (b) Not included. Old-age pension is a transfer payment; no productive service is rendered in return for it in the current year.
    (c) Not included. Purchase of shares is only a financial transaction (a transfer of ownership claims); it does not add to the flow of goods and services.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Included (½ marks)

    • (b) Not included (½ marks)
    • Reason: transfer payment, no productive service in return (½ marks)
    • (c) Not included (½ marks)
    • Reason: financial transaction, no production of goods or services (½ marks)

  12. Question 12 · 3 marks · Money and Banking
    Assume that the entire banking system works as a single bank, all transactions are routed through banks and the legal reserve ratio is 25%. Explain the process of money creation by commercial banks when a fresh deposit of ₹1,000 is made.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: When ₹1,000 is deposited, the bank keeps 25% of it, that is ₹250, as reserves and lends the remaining ₹750.
    The borrowers spend the ₹750, and since all transactions are routed through banks, it comes back to the banking system as fresh deposits. The bank again keeps 25% (₹187.50) as reserves and lends ₹562.50. This chain of lending and re-depositing goes on, each round smaller than the last, until the total reserves equal the initial deposit of ₹1,000.
    Total deposits created = Initial deposit $\times \frac{1}{LRR} = 1{,}000 \times \frac{1}{0.25}$ = ₹4,000. So an initial deposit of ₹1,000 leads to total deposits of ₹4,000.

    Marking scheme:

    • Bank keeps 25% (₹250) as reserves and lends the remaining ₹750 (1 mark)
    • Loans come back as fresh deposits and the lending process repeats in smaller rounds (1 mark)
    • Total deposits = 1,000 $\times$ 1/0.25 = ₹4,000 (1 mark)

  14. Question 14 · 4 marks · Open Economy Macroeconomics
    Read the following text carefully:
    The exchange rate of a country's currency is normally determined by the demand for and supply of foreign exchange in the market. Last month, however, the currency fell sharply against the US dollar within a few days. The country's central bank then sold US dollars from its foreign exchange reserves to limit the fall.

    (I) Identify the exchange rate system indicated in the text and explain it. [2]

    (II) Explain how the sale of US dollars by the central bank helps to limit the fall of the domestic currency. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Identify the exchange rate system indicated in the text and explain it.

    Model answer: The system is the managed floating exchange rate system. Under it, the exchange rate is mainly determined by market forces of demand and supply, but the central bank intervenes by buying or selling foreign currency to limit large fluctuations in the exchange rate.

    Marking scheme:

    • Managed floating exchange rate system (1 mark)
    • Rate set by market forces, with central bank intervention to limit fluctuations (1 mark)

    (II) Explain how the sale of US dollars by the central bank helps to limit the fall of the domestic currency.

    Model answer: When the central bank sells dollars from its reserves, the supply of dollars in the foreign exchange market increases. With demand unchanged, the price of the dollar in terms of domestic currency falls (or rises less than it otherwise would). So the domestic currency regains value or stops falling, and the exchange rate is stabilised.

    Marking scheme:

    • Sale of dollars increases the supply of dollars in the foreign exchange market (1 mark)
    • Price of the dollar falls/rises less, so the fall of the domestic currency is checked (1 mark)

  15. Question 15 · 4 marks · Determination of Income and Employment
    In a hypothetical economy, the equilibrium level of income is ₹5,000 crore, while the full employment level of income is ₹6,000 crore.

    (I) Identify the problem faced by the economy and state its effect on output and employment. [2]

    (II) Explain how a reduction in the cash reserve ratio by the central bank can help to correct this problem. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Identify the problem faced by the economy and state its effect on output and employment.

    Model answer: The economy faces deficient demand: aggregate demand is less than aggregate supply at the full employment level, so equilibrium is reached below full employment.
    Output (₹5,000 crore) is below the full employment level, so there is involuntary unemployment: some people willing to work at the existing wage do not get work.

    Marking scheme:

    • Deficient demand (AD less than AS at full employment) (1 mark)
    • Output below full employment level, causing involuntary unemployment (1 mark)

    (II) Explain how a reduction in the cash reserve ratio by the central bank can help to correct this problem.

    Model answer: Cash reserve ratio is the minimum percentage of deposits that commercial banks must keep as cash reserves with the central bank. When it is reduced, banks have more funds available to lend, and their capacity to create credit rises. More credit is available at lower cost, so investment and consumption spending rise. Aggregate demand increases, which raises output and employment towards the full employment level.

    Marking scheme:

    • Lower CRR frees funds with banks and raises their credit creation capacity (1 mark)
    • More credit raises investment/consumption, so AD, output and employment rise (1 mark)

  16. Question 16 · 6 marks · Government Budget and the Economy
    Read the following text carefully and answer the questions that follow.
    The Finance Minister of the hypothetical country of Sundaram presented the budget for 2026-27 with the following estimates (in ₹ crore):

    ItemAmount
    Tax revenue16,000
    Non-tax revenue4,000
    Recovery of loans600
    Disinvestment proceeds400
    Borrowings14,000
    Revenue expenditure26,000
    of which: interest payments7,000
    Capital expenditure9,000
    Opposition members criticised the budget. They said that a large part of the government's borrowing was being used to pay interest on past loans and to meet day-to-day expenses rather than to build assets.

    (I) Classify disinvestment proceeds as a revenue receipt or a capital receipt, giving a reason. [1]

    (II) Calculate the revenue deficit and the fiscal deficit of Sundaram. [2]

    (III) Calculate the primary deficit, state what it shows, and comment on the opposition's criticism. [3]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Classify disinvestment proceeds as a revenue receipt or a capital receipt, giving a reason.

    Model answer: Capital receipt, because disinvestment (sale of the government's shares in public sector enterprises) reduces the assets of the government.

    Marking scheme:

    • Capital receipt (½ marks)
    • Reason: it reduces the assets of the government (½ marks)

    (II) Calculate the revenue deficit and the fiscal deficit of Sundaram.

    Model answer: Revenue receipts = 16,000 + 4,000 = ₹20,000 crore
    Revenue deficit = Revenue expenditure $-$ Revenue receipts = 26,000 $-$ 20,000 = ₹6,000 crore
    Fiscal deficit = Total expenditure $-$ (Revenue receipts + Non-debt capital receipts)
    = (26,000 + 9,000) $-$ (20,000 + 600 + 400) = 35,000 $-$ 21,000 = ₹14,000 crore
    (This equals the borrowings of ₹14,000 crore.)

    Marking scheme:

    • Revenue deficit = 26,000 $-$ 20,000 = ₹6,000 crore (1 mark)
    • Fiscal deficit = 35,000 $-$ 21,000 = ₹14,000 crore (1 mark)

    (III) Calculate the primary deficit, state what it shows, and comment on the opposition's criticism.

    Model answer: Primary deficit = Fiscal deficit $-$ Interest payments = 14,000 $-$ 7,000 = ₹7,000 crore
    The primary deficit shows the borrowing requirement of the government excluding interest payments, that is, the borrowing needed to meet expenditure other than interest on past loans.
    The criticism is valid. Half of the borrowing (₹7,000 crore out of ₹14,000 crore) is needed only to pay interest on past loans, and a revenue deficit of ₹6,000 crore means borrowing is also used for current expenditure that creates no assets. Such borrowing adds to future interest burden and may push the government into a debt trap.

    Marking scheme:

    • Primary deficit = 14,000 $-$ 7,000 = ₹7,000 crore (1 mark)
    • Meaning: borrowing requirement excluding interest payments (1 mark)
    • Comment: half the borrowing pays interest and revenue deficit is financed by borrowing, raising future burden / debt trap (1 mark)

  17. Question 17 · 6 marks · Determination of Income and Employment
    Answer the following questions for a hypothetical economy (all figures in ₹ crore).

    (I) The saving function of the economy is $S = -50 + 0.25Y$ and autonomous investment is 100. Calculate (a) the equilibrium level of income, and (b) consumption expenditure at the equilibrium level of income. [3]

    (II) With the help of a numerical example, explain the working of the investment multiplier, assuming that the marginal propensity to consume is 0.75 and investment rises by ₹100 crore. [3]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) The saving function of the economy is $S = -50 + 0.25Y$ and autonomous investment is 100. Calculate (a) the equilibrium level of income, and (b) consumption expenditure at the equilibrium level of income.

    Model answer: (a) At equilibrium, planned saving = planned investment:
    $-50 + 0.25Y = 100$
    $0.25Y = 150$
    $Y$ = ₹600 crore
    (b) Saving at equilibrium $= -50 + 0.25 \times 600 = 100$
    Consumption $= Y - S = 600 - 100$ = ₹500 crore

    Marking scheme:

    • Equilibrium condition: $-50 + 0.25Y = 100$ (1 mark)
    • Equilibrium income = ₹600 crore (1 mark)
    • Consumption = 600 $-$ 100 = ₹500 crore (1 mark)

    (II) With the help of a numerical example, explain the working of the investment multiplier, assuming that the marginal propensity to consume is 0.75 and investment rises by ₹100 crore.

    Model answer: Multiplier $k = \frac{1}{1 - MPC} = \frac{1}{1 - 0.75} = 4$, so the final increase in income is $4 \times 100$ = ₹400 crore.
    Working: the extra investment of ₹100 crore becomes income of those who produce the capital goods (round 1: income rises by 100). They spend 75% of it, so consumption and the income of others rise by 75 (round 2). Those receiving it spend 75% again, raising income by 56.25 (round 3), and so on. Each round is smaller than the last.

    RoundIncrease in incomeIncrease in consumption
    110075
    27556.25
    356.2542.19
    .........
    Total400300
    Total increase in income $= 100 + 75 + 56.25 + \dots = \frac{100}{1 - 0.75}$ = ₹400 crore.

    Marking scheme:

    • Multiplier $k = \frac{1}{1-0.75} = 4$ (1 mark)
    • Round-wise working: spending out of each round's income becomes income for others (100, 75, 56.25 ...) (1 mark)
    • Total increase in income = ₹400 crore (1 mark)

  11. Question 28 · 3 marks · Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation
    Read the following text carefully:
    The government of a country sold 20% of its shares in a profit-making public sector power company to the public and to financial institutions. The government still owns 80% of the shares and continues to manage the company.

    (I) Identify the policy indicated in the text. [1]

    (II) Explain any two objectives of this policy. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Identify the policy indicated in the text.

    Model answer: Disinvestment: the sale of a part of the government's equity in a public sector enterprise (a form of privatisation).

    Marking scheme:

    • Disinvestment (a form of privatisation) (1 mark)

    (II) Explain any two objectives of this policy.

    Model answer: 1. To improve financial discipline: sharing ownership with private shareholders puts pressure on the enterprise to use resources efficiently and earn profits.
    2. To facilitate modernisation: private capital and managerial capabilities can be used to improve the performance of public sector enterprises. (The funds raised also help the government meet its expenditure.)

    Marking scheme:

    • Improve financial discipline of public sector enterprises (1 mark)
    • Facilitate modernisation using private capital and managerial skills (or: raise funds for the government) (1 mark)

  12. Question 29 · 3 marks · Rural Development
    Farmers in many parts of rural India sell their produce to traders at low prices soon after the harvest. Explain any three measures taken by the government to improve the marketing of agricultural produce.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. Regulation of markets: markets were regulated to create orderly and transparent marketing conditions. This protects farmers from malpractices such as faulty weighing and manipulation of accounts, so they get fair prices.
    2. Physical infrastructure: roads, railways, warehouses, godowns, cold storages and processing units were provided, so farmers can store produce and sell it later at better prices instead of selling in distress.
    3. Policy instruments: assurance of minimum support prices (MSP), maintenance of buffer stocks of wheat and rice by the Food Corporation of India and distribution through the Public Distribution System. MSP protects farmers from a fall in prices. (Cooperative marketing, which gives farmers a better price, is also a valid measure.)

    Marking scheme:

    • Regulation of markets for orderly and transparent marketing (1 mark)
    • Physical infrastructure: roads, warehouses, cold storages, processing units (1 mark)
    • Policy instruments: MSP / buffer stocks / PDS (or cooperative marketing) (1 mark)

  13. Question 30 · 4 marks · Environment and Sustainable Development
    Answer the following questions on the environment.

    (I) Explain how the use of compressed natural gas (CNG) in public transport is a strategy for sustainable development. Give an example from India. [2]

    (II) State the meaning of global warming and explain any one of its effects. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Explain how the use of compressed natural gas (CNG) in public transport is a strategy for sustainable development. Give an example from India.

    Model answer: CNG is a cleaner fuel than petrol or diesel, so buses and auto-rickshaws running on CNG release much less air pollution. Cleaner air protects health and the environment while transport services continue, so present needs are met without harming future generations.
    Example: in Delhi, the use of CNG in the public transport system has significantly lowered air pollution.

    Marking scheme:

    • CNG is a cleaner fuel, so vehicles cause less air pollution (1 mark)
    • Example: Delhi's public transport on CNG lowered air pollution (1 mark)

    (II) State the meaning of global warming and explain any one of its effects.

    Model answer: Global warming is a gradual increase in the average temperature of the earth's lower atmosphere, caused by the rise in greenhouse gases (such as carbon dioxide from the burning of fossil fuels, and methane) since the Industrial Revolution.
    Effect: the polar ice caps and glaciers melt, so the sea level rises. This causes flooding of low-lying coastal areas and can displace the people living there. (Other valid effects: more frequent tropical storms, disruption of drinking water supplies, changes in habitats of plants and animals, spread of tropical diseases.)

    Marking scheme:

    • Meaning: gradual rise in the average temperature of the earth's lower atmosphere due to more greenhouse gases (1 mark)
    • One effect explained, e.g. melting of polar ice raises the sea level and floods coastal areas (1 mark)

  14. Question 31 · 4 marks · Indian Economy 1950–1990
    When the Green Revolution began, there were fears that it would widen the gap between small and big farmers, and that small farmers who grew high yielding variety (HYV) crops could lose everything in a pest attack.
    Explain how the steps taken by the government ensured that these fears did not come true.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. Access to inputs for small farmers: only big farmers could easily afford the inputs (fertilisers, pesticides, irrigation) needed with HYV seeds. The government provided loans to small farmers at low rates of interest and subsidised fertilisers, so that small farmers could also obtain the inputs and benefit from the new technology. This prevented a widening of the gap between small and big farmers.
    2. Protection against pests: HYV crops were more prone to pest attacks. The services rendered by research institutes established by the government (such as advice and support to farmers on dealing with pests) considerably reduced the risk of small farmers being ruined by a pest attack.

    Marking scheme:

    • Loans at low interest rates to small farmers (1 mark)
    • Subsidised fertilisers, so small farmers could also afford inputs and the gap did not widen (1 mark)
    • Research institutes set up by the government (1 mark)
    • Their services reduced the risk of loss from pest attacks for small farmers (1 mark)

  15. Question 32 · 4 marks · Employment
    State the meaning of worker-population ratio. Explain any two reasons why it is higher in rural areas than in urban areas of India.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Meaning: worker-population ratio is the percentage of the total population that is engaged in work. It is an indicator used to analyse the employment situation in a country.
    Reason 1: People in rural areas have limited resources to earn a higher income, and their economic condition does not allow them to stay at home, so more of them take part in the employment market.
    Reason 2: Many people in rural areas do not go to schools, colleges and training institutions, or discontinue studies midway to join the workforce. In urban areas, a considerable section is studying in educational institutions, and urban people can wait for jobs that suit their qualifications and skills.

    Marking scheme:

    • Meaning: percentage of total population engaged in work (1 mark)
    • Reason 1: limited resources/poverty means rural people cannot stay out of work (1½ marks)
    • Reason 2: fewer rural people are in education, while urban people study longer and wait for suitable jobs (1½ marks)

  16. Question 33 · 6 marks · Comparative Development Experiences of India and its Neighbours
    Answer the following questions on China's development experience.

    (I) Explain the main features of the Great Leap Forward campaign launched in China in 1958. [3]

    (II) China introduced the one-child norm in the late 1970s. Explain any three consequences of this measure. [3]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Explain the main features of the Great Leap Forward campaign launched in China in 1958.

    Model answer: 1. Massive industrialisation: the campaign aimed at industrialising the country on a massive scale.
    2. Backyard industries: people were encouraged to set up industries in their backyards.
    3. Communes: in rural areas, communes were started, under which people collectively cultivated land.

    Marking scheme:

    • Aimed at industrialising the country on a massive scale (1 mark)
    • People encouraged to set up industries in their backyards (1 mark)
    • Communes started in rural areas with collective cultivation of land (1 mark)

    (II) China introduced the one-child norm in the late 1970s. Explain any three consequences of this measure.

    Model answer: 1. Low population growth: the norm is the major reason for the sharp fall in China's population growth rate.
    2. Fall in sex ratio: it led to a decline in the sex ratio, that is, the number of females per 1,000 males.
    3. Ageing population: in the coming decades there will be more elderly people in proportion to young people in China, which may create a shortage of workers and a heavier burden of supporting the old.

    Marking scheme:

    • Sharp decline in the population growth rate (1 mark)
    • Decline in the sex ratio (1 mark)
    • Rising proportion of elderly people relative to the young (1 mark)

  17. Question 34 · 6 marks · Human Capital Formation
    Read the following text carefully and answer the questions that follow.
    Meghpur is a hill village. Five years ago its only school taught up to Class V, and the nearest health centre was 20 km away. Since then, the state government has upgraded the school to Class XII, opened a primary health centre in the village and started a skill centre where young people learn plumbing, electrical repair and computer work.
    Lata, who completed a computer course at the centre, moved to Pune, where she works for a software firm and earns three times what she could earn in the village. Her brother Arjun learnt electrical repair and now runs his own shop in the nearby town. The village panchayat has noticed that fewer working days are lost to illness, and that farmers now read crop advice on their phones before sowing.

    (I) State the meaning of human capital. [1]

    (II) Identify the source of human capital formation shown by Lata's move to Pune. [1]

    (III) With reference to the text, explain how investment in education and in health contributes to economic growth. [4]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) State the meaning of human capital.

    Model answer: Human capital is the stock of skill and expertise of a nation (embodied in its people) at a point of time.

    Marking scheme:

    • Stock of skill and expertise of the people of a nation at a point of time (1 mark)

    (II) Identify the source of human capital formation shown by Lata's move to Pune.

    Model answer: Migration: people move from one place to another in search of jobs with higher earnings. The higher earnings in the new place outweigh the costs of migration.

    Marking scheme:

    • Migration (in search of better-paid jobs) (1 mark)

    (III) With reference to the text, explain how investment in education and in health contributes to economic growth.

    Model answer: Education: education and skill training raise the productivity and earning capacity of people. Arjun's training lets him run his own repair shop, and Lata earns much more than before. Educated people can also understand and use new technology and information, as the farmers do when they read crop advice before sowing, which raises output. Higher productivity of workers adds to national output and growth.
    Health: a healthy worker can work more days and more efficiently. With the health centre, fewer working days are lost to illness in Meghpur, so the village produces more. Healthier people also have a longer working life, which raises the total supply of productive labour in the economy.

    Marking scheme:

    • Education raises productivity and earning capacity (e.g. Arjun's shop, Lata's higher earnings) (1 mark)
    • Education helps people absorb new technology and information, raising output (e.g. farmers using crop advice) (1 mark)
    • Health: healthy workers lose fewer working days (e.g. fewer days lost to illness in Meghpur) (1 mark)
    • Health: healthy workers work more efficiently and longer, raising the supply of productive labour and output (1 mark)
