Section A: Macroeconomics

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Government Budget and the Economy
    Identify which of the following are capital receipts of the government.
    (i) Borrowings from the public
    (ii) Proceeds from disinvestment
    (iii) Interest received on loans given to state governments
    (iv) Grants received from foreign governments
    (Choose the correct option)

    1. (i) and (iii)
    2. (ii) and (iv)
    3. (i) and (ii)
    4. (iii) and (iv)
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) (i) and (ii)

    Capital receipts either create a liability or reduce the assets of the government. Borrowings create a liability and disinvestment reduces assets. Interest received and grants from foreign governments neither create a liability nor reduce assets, so they are revenue receipts.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Money and Banking
    Read the following statements carefully:
    Statement 1: Currency notes and coins are called fiat money; unlike a gold or silver coin, they have no intrinsic value.
    Statement 2: Demand deposits are legal tender, so nobody can refuse a cheque as a mode of payment.
    In light of the given statements, choose the correct option from the following:

    1. Statement 1 is true and Statement 2 is false.
    2. Statement 1 is false and Statement 2 is true.
    3. Both Statements 1 and 2 are true.
    4. Both Statements 1 and 2 are false.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Statement 1 is true and Statement 2 is false.

    Statement 1 is true: currency notes and coins are fiat money; they do not have intrinsic value like a gold or silver coin. Statement 2 is false: currency notes and coins are legal tender, but a cheque drawn on a demand deposit can be refused as a mode of payment, so demand deposits are not legal tender.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Open Economy Macroeconomics
    Under a fixed exchange rate system, the government decides to raise the price of foreign currency in terms of the domestic currency. This is called ________ of the domestic currency.
    (Choose the correct option to fill up the blank)

    1. depreciation
    2. appreciation
    3. devaluation
    4. revaluation
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) devaluation

    Under a fixed exchange rate, a fall in the value of the domestic currency brought about by the government (a rise in the price of foreign currency) is devaluation. Depreciation is the fall in value that happens through market forces under a flexible exchange rate.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Government Budget and the Economy
    For a given year, the total expenditure of a government is ₹50,000 crore. Its revenue receipts are ₹35,000 crore, recovery of loans is ₹2,000 crore and disinvestment proceeds are ₹3,000 crore. The fiscal deficit of the government is ₹ ________ crore.
    (Choose the correct option to fill up the blank)

    1. 5,000
    2. 10,000
    3. 13,000
    4. 15,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) 10,000

    Fiscal deficit = Total expenditure $-$ (Revenue receipts + Non-debt creating capital receipts) = 50,000 $-$ (35,000 + 2,000 + 3,000) = ₹10,000 crore.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Government Budget and the Economy
    The government provides national defence and street lighting, which the market mechanism fails to provide because people who do not pay cannot be excluded from their benefits.
    Identify the function of the government budget reflected here.
    (Choose the correct option)

    1. Redistribution function
    2. Stabilisation function
    3. Regulation function
    4. Allocation function
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Allocation function

    National defence and street lights are public goods: they are non-rival and non-excludable, so the market does not supply them. Their provision by the government through the budget is the allocation function.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Determination of Income and Employment
    At the break-even level of income in an economy:
    (Choose the correct option)

    1. APC = 1 and saving is zero
    2. APS = 1 and consumption is zero
    3. MPC = 1 and saving is positive
    4. APC = 0 and saving is zero
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) APC = 1 and saving is zero

    At the break-even point, consumption equals income, so saving is zero and APC = C/Y = 1.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · National Income Accounting
    Read the following statements: Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Choose the correct option from those given below:
    Assertion (A): Domestic services performed by women in their own homes are not included in the Gross Domestic Product.
    Reason (R): Such services do not add to the welfare of the people.

    1. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    2. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    3. Assertion (A) is true, but Reason (R) is false.
    4. Assertion (A) is false, but Reason (R) is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Assertion (A) is true, but Reason (R) is false.

    A is true: many activities, such as the domestic services women perform at home, are not paid for and are not evaluated in monetary terms, so they are left out of GDP. R is false: these services do add to welfare; leaving them out is one reason why GDP underestimates the welfare of the people.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Open Economy Macroeconomics
    An Indian company pays interest on a loan it had taken from a bank in Germany. This transaction will be recorded on the ______ side of the ______ account of India's Balance of Payments.
    (Choose the correct option to fill up the blanks)

    1. credit, current
    2. debit, current
    3. credit, capital
    4. debit, capital
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) debit, current

    Interest paid abroad is a payment of factor (primary) income to non-residents. It is recorded in the current account and, being a payment, on the debit side. (Repayment of the loan itself would be a capital account item.)

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Money and Banking
    The central bank fixes the difference between the market value of a security offered by a borrower and the amount of loan that commercial banks may give against it. Identify this instrument of credit control.
    (Choose the correct option)

    1. Cash reserve ratio
    2. Open market operations
    3. Margin requirement
    4. Repo rate
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Margin requirement

    Margin requirement is the difference between the market value of the security offered and the loan given against it. Raising it lowers the amount that can be borrowed against a given security, so credit is reduced. The cash reserve ratio is the minimum share of deposits that banks keep with the central bank in cash, open market operations are the purchase and sale of government securities, and the repo rate is the rate at which the central bank lends to commercial banks.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Determination of Income and Employment
    When aggregate demand exceeds aggregate supply at the full employment level of output, the excess is called:
    (Choose the correct option)

    1. deflationary gap
    2. inflationary gap
    3. excess of saving over investment
    4. full employment equilibrium
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) inflationary gap

    At full employment, output cannot rise further, so excess demand pushes up the general price level. The amount by which aggregate demand exceeds aggregate supply at full employment is the inflationary gap.

  11. Question 11 · 3 marks · National Income Accounting
    From the following data for a hypothetical economy, calculate (a) Gross Domestic Product at market price and (b) National Income, by the expenditure method.

    S. No.ItemsAmount (in ₹ crore)
    (i)Private final consumption expenditure1,200
    (ii)Government final consumption expenditure400
    (iii)Gross domestic fixed capital formation350
    (iv)Change in stock50
    (v)Exports150
    (vi)Imports200
    (vii)Consumption of fixed capital60
    (viii)Net indirect taxes90
    (ix)Net factor income from abroad($-$) 20

    OR Explain the circular flow of income in a simple economy with two sectors, households and firms.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) GDP$_{MP}$ = Private final consumption expenditure + Government final consumption expenditure + Gross domestic capital formation (Gross domestic fixed capital formation + Change in stock) + Net exports (Exports $-$ Imports)
    = 1,200 + 400 + (350 + 50) + (150 $-$ 200) = 2,000 $-$ 50 = ₹1,950 crore.
    (b) National Income (NNP$_{FC}$) = GDP$_{MP}$ $-$ Consumption of fixed capital + Net factor income from abroad $-$ Net indirect taxes
    = 1,950 $-$ 60 + ($-$20) $-$ 90 = ₹1,780 crore.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Correct substitution: 1,200 + 400 + (350 + 50) + (150 $-$ 200) (1 mark)
    • (a) GDP$_{MP}$ = ₹1,950 crore (½ marks)
    • (b) Correct substitution: 1,950 $-$ 60 + ($-$20) $-$ 90 (1 mark)
    • (b) National Income = ₹1,780 crore (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: 1. Households own the factors of production (land, labour, capital and entrepreneurship) and supply factor services to firms. Firms use them to produce goods and services.
    2. In return, firms make factor payments to households as rent, wages, interest and profits. Households spend this income on the goods and services produced by the firms, so the money comes back to the firms as their sales receipts.
    3. Thus there is a real flow (factor services from households to firms and goods and services from firms to households) and a money flow in the opposite direction (factor incomes to households and consumption expenditure to firms). In this circular flow, aggregate income, aggregate expenditure and the value of aggregate output are equal.

    Marking scheme:

    • Households supply factor services to firms, which produce goods and services (1 mark)
    • Firms pay factor incomes (rent, wages, interest, profit); households spend them on firms' goods and services (1 mark)
    • Real flow and money flow move in opposite directions; income = expenditure = output (1 mark)

  12. Question 12 · 3 marks · Government Budget and the Economy
    State whether the following statements are true or false. Give a valid reason in support of each answer.
    (a) Fiscal deficit shows the borrowing requirement of the government.
    (b) A revenue deficit implies that the government is dissaving.
    (c) If the primary deficit of a government is zero, it does not need to borrow at all.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) True. Fiscal deficit is the excess of total expenditure over revenue receipts and non-debt creating capital receipts; this gap has to be met by borrowing, so it equals the total borrowing requirement of the government.
    (b) True. A revenue deficit means revenue receipts are not enough to meet revenue (consumption) expenditure, so the government uses capital receipts, that is, borrowing or the sale of assets, to finance its current expenditure. This is dissaving.
    (c) False. Primary deficit = Fiscal deficit $-$ Interest payments. If it is zero, the fiscal deficit equals the interest payments, so the government still has to borrow to pay interest on its past borrowings.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) True: fiscal deficit = borrowing requirement (1 mark)
    • (b) True: revenue (current) expenditure is financed by borrowing or sale of assets, which is dissaving (1 mark)
    • (c) False: zero primary deficit means fiscal deficit = interest payments, which must still be borrowed (1 mark)

  13. Question 13 · 4 marks · Money and Banking
    Explain the following functions of the central bank:
    (I) Bank of issue
    (II) Lender of last resort

    OR Inflation in an economy is rising because of excess money supply. Explain how the central bank can use (I) the reverse repo rate and (II) the statutory liquidity ratio to correct the situation.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (I) Bank of issue: the central bank (the Reserve Bank of India in India) has the sole authority to issue currency notes. This gives uniformity to the currency and lets the central bank control the supply of currency in the economy.
    (II) Lender of last resort: when commercial banks face a shortage of funds, for example when many depositors withdraw money at the same time, they can borrow from the central bank. Because the central bank stands behind them in a crisis, people's confidence in the banking system is maintained and banks do not fail.

    Marking scheme:

    • (I) Sole authority to issue currency notes (RBI in India) (1 mark)
    • (I) Gives uniformity / control over the supply of currency (1 mark)
    • (II) Banks short of funds can borrow from the central bank in a crisis (1 mark)
    • (II) Prevents bank failure and maintains public confidence in banks (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (I) Reverse repo rate is the rate at which the central bank borrows from commercial banks. The central bank should raise it. Banks then find it more attractive to park their funds with the central bank, so they have less to lend; credit and money supply fall, and aggregate demand and inflation come down.
    (II) Statutory liquidity ratio (SLR) is the fraction of their total demand and time deposits that banks must keep in specified liquid assets such as cash, gold and approved government securities. The central bank should raise it. Banks then have fewer funds available for lending, so their capacity to create credit falls, which reduces aggregate demand.

    Marking scheme:

    • (I) Meaning: rate at which the central bank borrows from commercial banks (1 mark)
    • (I) Raise it: banks park more funds with the central bank, lend less; money supply falls (1 mark)
    • (II) Meaning: share of deposits banks must keep in liquid assets (cash, gold, approved securities) (1 mark)
    • (II) Raise it: less funds to lend, credit creation and aggregate demand fall (1 mark)

  14. Question 14 · 4 marks · Open Economy Macroeconomics
    Answer the following questions on the Balance of Payments.

    (I) Distinguish between autonomous and accommodating transactions in the Balance of Payments. [2]

    (II) For a hypothetical country, the following transactions took place during a year (in USD million): exports of goods 4,500; imports of goods 5,200; net exports of services (+) 900; net primary income from abroad ($-$) 150; net transfers received (+) 250. Calculate the balance of trade and the current account balance, and state whether each is in deficit or surplus. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Distinguish between autonomous and accommodating transactions in the Balance of Payments.

    Model answer: Autonomous transactions are international economic transactions made for their own sake, such as for profit or other economic motives, independently of the state of the Balance of Payments. They are called 'above the line' items.
    Accommodating transactions are undertaken to cover a deficit or surplus in the autonomous transactions, for example drawing down or adding to the foreign exchange reserves of the central bank. They are called 'below the line' items.

    Marking scheme:

    • Autonomous: made for their own sake (profit motive), independent of the BoP position (1 mark)
    • Accommodating: made to cover a deficit or surplus in autonomous transactions (e.g. change in reserves) (1 mark)

    (II) For a hypothetical country, the following transactions took place during a year (in USD million): exports of goods 4,500; imports of goods 5,200; net exports of services (+) 900; net primary income from abroad ($-$) 150; net transfers received (+) 250. Calculate the balance of trade and the current account balance, and state whether each is in deficit or surplus.

    Model answer: Balance of trade = Exports of goods $-$ Imports of goods = 4,500 $-$ 5,200 = ($-$) USD 700 million, a deficit.
    Current account balance = Balance of trade + Net services + Net primary income + Net transfers = $-$700 + 900 $-$ 150 + 250 = (+) USD 300 million, a surplus.

    Marking scheme:

    • Balance of trade = ($-$) 700 million: deficit (1 mark)
    • Current account balance = (+) 300 million: surplus (1 mark)

  15. Question 15 · 4 marks · Determination of Income and Employment
    In a hypothetical economy, the consumption function is $C = 60 + 0.6Y$ and autonomous investment is ₹140 crore (all figures in ₹ crore).

    (I) Determine the equilibrium level of income. [2]

    (II) Suppose producers plan an output of ₹600 crore. Calculate aggregate demand at this level, and explain how the economy will move towards equilibrium. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Determine the equilibrium level of income.

    Model answer: At equilibrium, $Y = C + I$
    $Y = 60 + 0.6Y + 140$
    $0.4Y = 200$
    $Y = 500$
    Equilibrium level of income = ₹500 crore.

    Marking scheme:

    • Equilibrium condition $Y = C + I$ with correct substitution (1 mark)
    • Equilibrium income = ₹500 crore (1 mark)

    (II) Suppose producers plan an output of ₹600 crore. Calculate aggregate demand at this level, and explain how the economy will move towards equilibrium.

    Model answer: At $Y = 600$: AD $= 60 + 0.6 \times 600 + 140 = 60 + 360 + 140 = 560$, i.e. ₹560 crore.
    Planned output (₹600 crore) exceeds aggregate demand (₹560 crore), so goods worth ₹40 crore remain unsold and there is an unplanned accumulation of inventories. Producers will cut output (and employment) until output falls to ₹500 crore, where AD equals output.

    Marking scheme:

    • AD at Y = 600 is ₹560 crore (1 mark)
    • Unplanned inventory accumulation of ₹40 crore; output falls to equilibrium ₹500 crore (1 mark)

  16. Question 16 · 6 marks · National Income Accounting
    Read the following text carefully and answer the questions that follow.
    In the hypothetical village of Sonapur, a farmer grows cotton worth ₹50,000 without buying any inputs and sells it to a spinning unit. The spinning unit turns the cotton into yarn and sells the yarn to a weaver for ₹80,000. The weaver makes cloth and sells it to a garment maker for ₹1,10,000. The garment maker stitches shirts and sells them to households for ₹1,60,000. A local newspaper added up all the sales in this chain and reported that the producers of Sonapur had created output worth ₹4,00,000.
    (Assume that there are no other inputs and no depreciation.)

    (I) Define intermediate goods. [1]

    (II) Calculate the value added by each of the four producers and the total value added. [2]

    (III) Explain why the newspaper's figure is wrong, and how the value added method avoids this mistake. [3]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Define intermediate goods.

    Model answer: Intermediate goods are goods that are used up as raw material or inputs in the production of other goods (or are bought for resale) within the same year; they are not meant for final use.

    Marking scheme:

    • Goods used up as inputs in producing other goods / for resale during the year, not for final use (1 mark)

    (II) Calculate the value added by each of the four producers and the total value added.

    Model answer: Value added = Value of output $-$ Intermediate consumption
    Farmer: 50,000 $-$ 0 = ₹50,000
    Spinning unit: 80,000 $-$ 50,000 = ₹30,000
    Weaver: 1,10,000 $-$ 80,000 = ₹30,000
    Garment maker: 1,60,000 $-$ 1,10,000 = ₹50,000
    Total value added = 50,000 + 30,000 + 30,000 + 50,000 = ₹1,60,000

    Marking scheme:

    • Value added by each producer: 50,000; 30,000; 30,000; 50,000 (1 mark)
    • Total value added = ₹1,60,000 (1 mark)

    (III) Explain why the newspaper's figure is wrong, and how the value added method avoids this mistake.

    Model answer: 1. The newspaper's figure suffers from double counting. It adds the value of intermediate goods (cotton, yarn, cloth) again and again.
    2. For example, the value of cotton (₹50,000) is counted when the cotton is sold, again inside the price of the yarn, again inside the cloth and again inside the shirts; so the total of all sales (₹4,00,000) overstates the output actually produced.
    3. The value added method counts only the value added by each producer (value of output minus intermediate consumption). The total value added, ₹1,60,000, equals the value of the final good (the shirts sold to households), so each good is counted only once.

    Marking scheme:

    • Problem of double counting (intermediate goods counted again and again) (1 mark)
    • Example: value of cotton counted in the yarn, cloth and shirts; ₹4,00,000 overstates output (1 mark)
    • Value added method counts only value added; total ₹1,60,000 = value of final goods (1 mark)

  17. Question 17 · 6 marks · Determination of Income and Employment
    (I) Distinguish between ex-ante and ex-post investment. Why is ex-post saving always equal to ex-post investment? (3)
    (II) 'An economy can be in equilibrium even when it is not at full employment.' State the meaning of the full employment level of income, and defend or refute the given statement with a valid reason. (3)

    OR (I) In a hypothetical economy, the consumption function is $C = 40 + 0.75Y$ and autonomous investment is ₹60 crore (figures in ₹ crore). Calculate the equilibrium level of income, and show that saving equals investment at this level. (3)
    (II) 'The average propensity to consume can be greater than one, but the marginal propensity to consume cannot.' Defend or refute the statement, giving valid reasons. (3)

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (I) Ex-ante investment is the planned (desired or intended) investment that firms plan to undertake during a period. Ex-post investment is the actual (realised) investment at the end of the period; it includes unplanned changes in inventories.
    Ex-post saving always equals ex-post investment because any gap between planned saving and planned investment shows up as an unplanned change in inventories, which is counted as part of actual investment.
    (II) Full employment level of income is that level of income where all the factors of production are fully employed in the production process.
    The statement is defended. Equilibrium, where ex-ante aggregate demand equals ex-ante aggregate supply, only means that, if left to itself, the level of income in the economy will not change, even when there is unemployment in the economy.
    The equilibrium level of output may be less than the full employment level of output. This happens when aggregate demand is not enough to employ all the factors of production (deficient demand), so some people who are willing to work at the prevailing wage do not find work.

    Marking scheme:

    • (I) Ex-ante = planned investment; ex-post = actual (realised) investment (1 mark)
    • (I) Ex-post investment includes unplanned inventory change (1 mark)
    • (I) So actual saving and actual investment are always equal (1 mark)
    • (II) Full employment income: all factors of production are fully employed (1 mark)
    • (II) Statement defended: equilibrium only means income has no tendency to change, even with unemployment (1 mark)
    • (II) Equilibrium output can be below full employment output when aggregate demand is deficient (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (I) At equilibrium, $Y = C + I$: $Y = 40 + 0.75Y + 60$, so $0.25Y = 100$ and $Y = 400$.
    Equilibrium income = ₹400 crore.
    Saving function: $S = Y - C = -40 + 0.25Y$. At $Y = 400$: $S = -40 + 0.25 \times 400 = -40 + 100 = 60$, which equals investment of ₹60 crore.
    (II) The statement is defended.
    APC = C/Y. Because of autonomous consumption, people consume something even at very low or zero income. At incomes below the break-even point, consumption is greater than income (they draw on past savings or borrow), so APC is greater than one.
    MPC = $\Delta C / \Delta Y$. When income rises, consumption also rises, but by less than the rise in income, because a part of the additional income is saved. So MPC lies between 0 and 1 and cannot be greater than one.

    Marking scheme:

    • (I) $Y = 40 + 0.75Y + 60$ with $Y = C + I$ (1 mark)
    • (I) Equilibrium income = ₹400 crore (1 mark)
    • (I) $S = -40 + 0.25 \times 400 = 60 = I$ (1 mark)
    • (II) Statement defended (1 mark)
    • (II) APC is more than 1 below the break-even income, because of autonomous consumption (C exceeds Y) (1 mark)
    • (II) MPC is less than 1 because a part of additional income is saved (1 mark)

  2. Question 19 · 1 mark · Employment
    Which of the following persons is counted as a worker?
    (Choose the correct option)

  3. Question 20 · 1 mark · Indian Economy 1950–1990

    Assertion (A): Under the policy of import substitution, the government protected domestic industries from foreign competition.

    1. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

  4. Question 21 · 1 mark · Human Capital Formation
    Which of the following best brings out the difference between 'human capital' and 'human development'?
    (Choose the correct option)

    1. Human capital treats education and health as ends in themselves, while human development treats them as a means to raise productivity.
    2. Human capital treats education and health as a means to raise the productivity of labour, while human development treats them as integral to human well-being.

Human development is based on the idea that education and health are integral to human well-being, because only when people can read and write and lead long and healthy lives can they make other choices they value.

  5. Question 22 · 1 mark · Indian Economy 1950–1990
    During 1950-1990, it was easier to obtain an industrial licence if the unit was set up in an economically backward area, and such units also received concessions such as tax benefits and electricity at a lower tariff. The purpose of this policy was to:
    (Choose the correct option)

    1. promote exports of manufactured goods
    2. replace public sector units with private firms
    3. attract foreign direct investment
    4. promote regional equality
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) promote regional equality

    The licensing policy was used to promote industry in backward regions; units set up there got licences more easily and received concessions. The purpose was to promote regional equality.

  6. Question 23 · 1 mark · Comparative Development Experiences of India and its Neighbours
    Among India, China and Pakistan, China occupies the largest geographical area. Which of the following is true about China in comparison with the other two countries?
    (Choose the correct option)

    1. Its population growth is the highest.
    2. Its fertility rate is the highest.
    3. Its share of urban population is the lowest.
    4. Its density of population is the lowest.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Its density of population is the lowest.

    Though China is the largest nation and occupies the largest area among the three, its density of population is the lowest. Its population growth and fertility rate are low (the one-child norm), and its urbanisation is high.

    In light of the given statements, choose the correct option from the following:

  11. Question 28 · 3 marks · Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation
    Read the following text carefully:
    Since 1991, the Government of India has continuously reduced the rates of tax on individual incomes. The rate of corporation tax, which was very high earlier, has also been gradually reduced, and the procedures for paying taxes have been simplified.
    Answer the following questions in the light of the given text and common understanding:

    (I) Identify the area of economic reforms indicated in the text. [1]

    (II) Explain two reasons for reducing the rates of income tax. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Identify the area of economic reforms indicated in the text.

    Model answer: Tax reforms (reforms in the government's taxation policy, a part of fiscal policy).

    Marking scheme:

    • Tax reforms (1 mark)

    (II) Explain two reasons for reducing the rates of income tax.

    Model answer: 1. High rates of income tax were felt to be an important reason for tax evasion; lower rates reduce the incentive to evade taxes.
    2. Moderate rates of income tax encourage savings and voluntary disclosure of income, which can raise the government's tax revenue.

    Marking scheme:

    • High rates encouraged tax evasion; lower rates reduce it (1 mark)
    • Moderate rates encourage savings and voluntary disclosure of income (1 mark)

  12. Question 29 · 3 marks · Rural Development
    Explain how horticulture has helped in the diversification of agriculture and in improving the livelihood of rural people.

    OR State the meaning of organic farming. Explain any two of its benefits. (1 + 2 = 3)

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. India's varied climate and soils allow a wide range of horticultural crops, such as fruits, vegetables, tuber crops, flowers, medicinal and aromatic plants, spices and plantation crops. These provide food and nutrition and reduce the dependence of farmers on a few cereal crops.
    2. The economic condition of many farmers engaged in horticulture has improved, and it has become a means of improving the livelihood of many underprivileged classes.
    3. Activities such as flower harvesting, nursery maintenance, hybrid seed production, tissue culture, propagation of fruits and flowers and food processing are highly paying employment options, especially for women in rural areas.

    Marking scheme:

    • Wide range of horticultural crops: food and nutrition; less dependence on a few crops (1 mark)
    • Improved economic condition / livelihood of farmers and underprivileged classes (1 mark)
    • Remunerative employment, especially for rural women (flowers, nurseries, seeds, tissue culture, food processing) (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: Organic farming is a whole system of farming that restores, maintains and enhances the ecological balance. It uses organic inputs instead of chemical fertilisers and pesticides.
    Benefits:
    1. It replaces costlier inputs such as HYV seeds, chemical fertilisers and pesticides with locally produced organic inputs that are cheaper, so it gives good returns on investment.
    2. Organically grown food has more nutritional value than food from chemical farming and is free of pesticides, so it gives healthy food; it also earns income from exports, as the world demand for organically grown crops is rising. (Also accepted: it needs more labour, so it creates more employment; it is environmentally sustainable.)

    Marking scheme:

    • Meaning: farming system that restores and enhances ecological balance, using organic inputs (1 mark)
    • Benefit 1, e.g. cheaper local inputs and good returns on investment (1 mark)
    • Benefit 2, e.g. healthy, more nutritious food / export income / more employment / environmentally sustainable (1 mark)

  13. Question 30 · 4 marks · Indian Economy 1950–1990
    Answer the following questions on India's development policies during 1950-1990.

    (I) Give one argument in favour of, and one argument against, continuing fertiliser subsidies to farmers. [2]

    (II) Explain two reasons why the government had to play an extensive role in promoting the industrial sector after independence. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Give one argument in favour of, and one argument against, continuing fertiliser subsidies to farmers.

    Model answer: For: farming in India continues to be a risky business and most farmers are very poor; without subsidies they cannot afford the required inputs, and removing subsidies would increase the inequality between rich and poor farmers.
    Against: a large part of the fertiliser subsidy benefits the fertiliser industry and farmers in the more prosperous regions, not the target group, and it is a huge burden on the government's finances.

    Marking scheme:

    • For: farming is risky and most farmers are poor; removal would raise inequality (1 mark)
    • Against: benefits the fertiliser industry and prosperous farmers; heavy burden on government finances (1 mark)

    (II) Explain two reasons why the government had to play an extensive role in promoting the industrial sector after independence.

    Model answer: 1. Indian industrialists did not have the capital needed for the large investments in industrial ventures required for the development of the economy.
    2. The market was not big enough to encourage industrialists to undertake major projects, even if they had the capital. (Also: the decision to develop the economy on socialist lines meant that the state would control the commanding heights of the economy.)

    Marking scheme:

    • Private industrialists lacked the capital for large industrial investment (1 mark)
    • Market not big enough for major projects / socialist goal of controlling the commanding heights (1 mark)

  14. Question 31 · 4 marks · Human Capital Formation
    Explain the need for government intervention in the education and health sectors of India.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. Expenditure on education and health has a substantial long-term impact that cannot easily be reversed. For example, if a child is admitted to a school or health centre where the required service is not provided, much damage is done before the decision is taken to shift the child elsewhere.
    2. Individual consumers of these services do not have complete information about the quality of the services and their costs.
    3. In this situation, the private providers of education and health services can acquire monopoly power and exploit the people.
    4. So the government must ensure that private providers keep to the standards it lays down and charge the correct price.

    Marking scheme:

    • Long-term impact of spending on education and health that cannot easily be reversed (1 mark)
    • Consumers lack complete information about quality and costs (1 mark)
    • Private providers can gain monopoly power and exploit people (1 mark)
    • Government ensures private providers keep to its standards and charge the correct price (1 mark)

  15. Question 32 · 4 marks · Employment
    Distinguish between formal sector and informal sector workers in India: state who belongs to each sector, and compare them on any two bases.

    OR (I) What is meant by 'jobless growth'? (2)
    (II) Describe the change in the distribution of India's workforce by industrial sectors since the early 1970s. (2)

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. Formal sector: all public sector establishments and those private sector establishments that employ 10 hired workers or more are formal sector establishments; those who work in them are formal sector workers (for example, a clerk in a government office or a worker in a large factory).
    2. Informal sector: all other enterprises and the workers in them, such as farmers, agricultural labourers, owners of small enterprises and the people working in them, the self-employed who have no hired workers, and non-farm casual wage labourers who work for more than one employer, such as construction and headload workers.
    3. Social security and protection: formal sector workers enjoy social security benefits; through labour laws the government protects their rights, and they can form trade unions and bargain for better wages. Informal sector workers have no such protection or regulation from the government and can be dismissed without any compensation.
    4. Income: formal sector workers get a regular income and earn more than informal sector workers, who do not get a regular income.

    Marking scheme:

    • Formal: public establishments and private ones with 10 or more hired workers (with example) (1 mark)
    • Informal: all other enterprises and workers, e.g. farmers, self-employed without hired workers, construction workers (1 mark)
    • Basis 1: social security, labour-law protection and trade unions vs no protection, dismissal without compensation (1 mark)
    • Basis 2: regular and higher income vs irregular and lower income (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (I) In the late 1990s, the growth of employment started declining, and a widening gap appeared between the growth of GDP and the growth of employment. This means that the Indian economy has been able to produce more goods and services without generating a matching rise in employment. Scholars call this jobless growth.
    (II) There has been a substantial shift from farm work to non-farm work. The share of the workforce in the primary sector fell from about 74 per cent in 1972-73 to about 46 per cent in 2023-24, while the shares of the secondary sector (from about 11 to 24 per cent) and the service sector (from about 15 to 30 per cent) rose, showing a promising future for the workforce in these sectors.

    Marking scheme:

    • (I) Widening gap between the growth of GDP and the growth of employment (1 mark)
    • (I) More goods and services produced without a matching rise in employment (1 mark)
    • (II) Substantial shift from farm to non-farm work; primary share fell (about 74 to 46 per cent) (1 mark)
    • (II) Shares of the secondary and service sectors rose (1 mark)

  16. Question 33 · 6 marks · Comparative Development Experiences of India and its Neighbours
    (I) Explain why China introduced structural reforms in 1978. (3)
    (II) Describe the development strategy followed by Pakistan from the late 1950s until it introduced reforms in 1988. (3)

    OR (I) 'In the normal course of development, countries first shift their employment and output from agriculture to industry and then to services.' Examine how far China, India and Pakistan have followed this path. (3)
    (II) 'Human development indicators are extremely important, but they are not sufficient.' Explain. (3)

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (I) 1. China was under no compulsion from the World Bank or the IMF, unlike India and Pakistan; the new leadership was not happy with the slow pace of growth and the lack of modernisation of the Chinese economy under Maoist rule.
    2. They felt that the Maoist vision of development, based on decentralisation, self-sufficiency and shunning of foreign technology, goods and capital, had failed.
    3. Despite extensive land reforms, collectivisation, the Great Leap Forward and other initiatives, the per capita grain output in 1978 was the same as it had been in the mid-1950s.
    (II) 1. Pakistan followed a mixed economy model. In the late 1950s and 1960s it adopted a regulated policy framework for import substitution-based industrialisation, combining tariff protection for consumer goods manufacturing with direct controls on competing imports.
    2. The Green Revolution led to mechanisation and greater public investment in infrastructure in select areas, which raised foodgrain production; in the 1970s, capital goods industries were nationalised.
    3. In the late 1970s and 1980s the policy shifted to denationalisation and encouragement of the private sector; Pakistan received financial support from western nations and remittances from emigrants to the Middle East, which stimulated growth before the reforms of 1988.

    Marking scheme:

    • (I) New leadership unhappy with slow growth and lack of modernisation (no external compulsion) (1 mark)
    • (I) Failure of the Maoist vision: decentralisation, self-sufficiency, shunning foreign technology and capital (1 mark)
    • (I) Per capita grain output in 1978 was the same as in the mid-1950s (1 mark)
    • (II) Import substitution with tariff protection and import controls (late 1950s-1960s) (1 mark)
    • (II) Green Revolution with mechanisation; nationalisation of capital goods industries in the 1970s (1 mark)
    • (II) Denationalisation and private sector encouragement; western aid and Middle East remittances (late 1970s-1980s) (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (I) 1. China has followed this normal path: the share of agriculture in its output and workforce has fallen sharply, and industry (manufacturing) contributes a large share of its output and employs a large share of its workforce.
    2. In India and Pakistan, the share of the workforce in industry and the contribution of industry to output have remained low; the shift is taking place directly from agriculture to the service sector.
    3. In all three countries the service sector is emerging as a major player of development: it contributes the largest share of output and is emerging as a prospective employer. (China's growth has come from manufacturing and services; India's mainly from services.)
    (II) 1. Indicators such as life expectancy, schooling, mortality rates, sanitation and poverty are extremely important for judging the well-being of people, but they are not sufficient on their own.
    2. Along with them we need 'liberty indicators'. One such indicator, the extent of democratic participation in social and political decision-making, has been added but is not given any extra weight.
    3. Obvious liberty indicators, such as the extent of constitutional protection of the rights of citizens and of the independence of the judiciary and the rule of law, have not been included. Without them, a human development index is incomplete and its usefulness is limited.

    Marking scheme:

    • (I) China follows the normal path: big shift to industry (manufacturing) (1 mark)
    • (I) India and Pakistan: low industry share; shift directly from agriculture to services (1 mark)
    • (I) Services the largest contributor to output and an emerging employer in all three (1 mark)
    • (II) HD indicators important but not sufficient (1 mark)
    • (II) Need for liberty indicators; democratic participation added but given no extra weight (1 mark)
    • (II) Constitutional rights, independent judiciary and rule of law not included: index incomplete (1 mark)

  17. Question 34 · 6 marks · Environment and Sustainable Development
    Read the following text carefully and answer the questions that follow.
    Pahargaon is a hypothetical hill district. Its population and the number of tourists visiting it have grown fast, and the demand for water and fuelwood now exceeds what its springs and forests can renew. Many villages were far from the power grid, so the district helped them set up small power plants on the perennial streams nearby; the electricity is used in the villages themselves. In the valleys, farmers who had been spraying large amounts of chemical pesticides found that their wells and fish ponds were contaminated. An agricultural officer then persuaded them to use pesticides made from neem, to grow different crops in consecutive years on the same land, and to protect owls and snakes near their fields.

    (I) State the meaning of 'carrying capacity' of the environment. [1]

    (II) The demand for water and fuelwood in Pahargaon now exceeds what its springs and forests can renew. What is this situation called? [1]

    (III) Explain how the small power plants and the farmers' new methods of pest control promote sustainable development. [4]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) State the meaning of 'carrying capacity' of the environment.

    Model answer: The environment can perform its functions without interruption only when resource extraction is not above the rate of regeneration of the resource and the wastes generated are within the assimilating capacity of the environment. This limit is its carrying capacity.

    Marking scheme:

    • Resource extraction within the rate of regeneration and wastes within the assimilating capacity (1 mark)

    (II) The demand for water and fuelwood in Pahargaon now exceeds what its springs and forests can renew. What is this situation called?

    Model answer: Supply-demand reversal of environmental resources: the demand for environmental resources and services has become greater than their supply, which is limited because of overuse and misuse.

    Marking scheme:

    • Supply-demand reversal (demand for environmental resources exceeds their limited supply); also accept environmental crisis (carrying capacity exceeded) (1 mark)

    (III) Explain how the small power plants and the farmers' new methods of pest control promote sustainable development.

    Model answer: Small power plants (mini-hydel plants):
    1. They use the energy of perennial streams, a renewable source, to move small turbines, and are more or less environment-friendly: they do not change the land use pattern of the area.
    2. They generate enough power to meet local demand, so they do away with the need for large-scale transmission towers and cables and avoid transmission losses.
    Biopest control:
    3. Pesticides based on plant products such as neem replace chemical pesticides, which contaminate food, soil, water bodies and groundwater.
    4. Growing different crops in consecutive years (and mixed cropping) and protecting animals and birds such as snakes and owls, which prey on rats, mice and other pests, control pests naturally. Resources are thus used without harming the ability of future generations to meet their needs.

    Marking scheme:

    • Mini-hydel: renewable stream energy; environment-friendly, no change in land use (1 mark)
    • Mini-hydel: meets local demand; no long transmission lines; avoids transmission loss (1 mark)
    • Neem-based pesticides replace chemicals that pollute food, soil and water (1 mark)
    • Crop rotation and protecting owls and snakes control pests naturally (1 mark)
