Section A: Macroeconomics

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Government Budget and the Economy
    Identify which of the following are revenue receipts of the government.
    (i) Dividend received from a public sector company
    (ii) Borrowings from the Reserve Bank of India
    (iii) Recovery of loans given to state governments
    (iv) Fees collected for issuing passports
    (Choose the correct option)

    1. (ii) and (iii)
    2. (i) and (iii)
    3. (i) and (iv)
    4. (ii) and (iv)
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) (i) and (iv)

    Revenue receipts neither create a liability nor reduce an asset of the government. Dividends and fees are non-tax revenue receipts. Borrowings create a liability and recovery of loans reduces an asset, so both are capital receipts.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Money and Banking
    Read the following statements carefully:
    Statement 1: The Reserve Bank of India acts as the lender of last resort for commercial banks.
    Statement 2: An increase in the cash reserve ratio raises the credit creation capacity of commercial banks.
    In light of the given statements, choose the correct option from the following:

    1. Statement 1 is true and Statement 2 is false.
    2. Statement 1 is false and Statement 2 is true.
    3. Both Statements 1 and 2 are true.
    4. Both Statements 1 and 2 are false.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Statement 1 is true and Statement 2 is false.

    Statement 1 is true: as the banker's bank, the RBI lends to commercial banks in times of financial difficulty. Statement 2 is false: a higher cash reserve ratio means banks must keep a larger part of their deposits as reserves with the RBI, so they have less to lend and their credit creation capacity falls.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Open Economy Macroeconomics
    Under a flexible exchange rate system, the price of one US dollar in the foreign exchange market rises from ₹84 to ₹86. Ceteris paribus, which of the following is correct?

    1. The rupee has appreciated, and Indian exports become costlier for foreign buyers.
    2. The rupee has depreciated, and Indian exports become cheaper for foreign buyers.
    3. The rupee has been devalued by the government, and imports into India become cheaper.
    4. The rupee has depreciated, and imports into India become cheaper.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) The rupee has depreciated, and Indian exports become cheaper for foreign buyers.

    More rupees are now needed to buy one dollar, so the rupee has lost value. Under a flexible system this is called depreciation (devaluation is a decision of the government under a fixed system). Foreign buyers now get more rupees for each dollar, so Indian goods become cheaper for them, while imports become costlier for Indians.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Government Budget and the Economy
    For a given year, the fiscal deficit of a government is ₹12,000 crore, its revenue deficit is ₹7,000 crore and its interest payments are ₹4,500 crore.
    The primary deficit of the government is ₹ ________ crore.
    (Choose the correct option to fill up the blank)

    1. 2,500
    2. 5,000
    3. 7,500
    4. 16,500
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) 7,500

    Primary deficit = Fiscal deficit $-$ Interest payments = 12,000 $-$ 4,500 = ₹7,500 crore. The revenue deficit is not needed.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Government Budget and the Economy
    During a period of rapidly rising prices, the government of a country raises income tax rates and cuts its own spending on non-essential items.
    Identify the objective of the government budget reflected in this step.
    (Choose the correct option)

    1. Allocation function
    2. Redistribution function
    3. Regulation function
    4. Stabilisation function
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Stabilisation function

    Higher taxes and lower government spending reduce aggregate demand, which helps to control inflation. Reducing fluctuations in output and prices is the stabilisation function of the budget.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Determination of Income and Employment
    In an economy, the marginal propensity to save is 0.2. If investment increases by ₹500 crore, the total increase in income will be ₹ ________ crore.
    (Choose the correct option to fill up the blank)

    1. 100
    2. 625
    3. 2,500
    4. 5,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) 2,500

    Investment multiplier $k = \frac{1}{MPS} = \frac{1}{0.2} = 5$. Increase in income $= k \times \Delta I = 5 \times 500$ = ₹2,500 crore.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · National Income Accounting
    Read the following statements: Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Choose the correct option from those given below:
    Assertion (A): The purchase of a new machine by a firm for its factory is included in gross domestic capital formation.
    Reason (R): Capital goods are completely used up within a single year of production.

    1. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    2. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    3. Assertion (A) is true, but Reason (R) is false.
    4. Assertion (A) is false, but Reason (R) is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Assertion (A) is true, but Reason (R) is false.

    A is true: a new machine bought by a producer is a capital good and is part of gross investment. R is false: capital goods are durable and are used in production over many years; only a part of their value (depreciation) is used up each year.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Open Economy Macroeconomics
    Which of the following transactions will be recorded in the capital account of India's Balance of Payments?

    1. Export of software services by an Indian firm to a company in the USA
    2. Purchase of shares of an Indian company by an investor in Japan
    3. Money sent home by Indians working in the UAE to their families
    4. Import of crude oil by an Indian refinery
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Purchase of shares of an Indian company by an investor in Japan

    Purchase of shares by a foreign investor is a portfolio investment: it changes the assets and liabilities of the country, so it is a capital account item. Export of services and import of oil are trade items, and remittances are transfers; all three are current account items.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Money and Banking
    Assume that the entire banking system works as a single bank and all transactions are routed through banks. If fresh deposits of ₹10,000 crore are made and the legal reserve ratio is 20%, the total deposits in the banking system will be ₹ ________ crore.
    (Choose the correct option to fill up the blank)

    1. 2,000
    2. 12,000
    3. 40,000
    4. 50,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) 50,000

    Money multiplier $= \frac{1}{LRR} = \frac{1}{0.2} = 5$. Total deposits $= 5 \times 10{,}000$ = ₹50,000 crore. (₹40,000 crore is only the additional deposits created over and above the initial deposit.)

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Determination of Income and Employment
    Identify which of the following measures can be used to correct a situation of deficient demand in an economy.
    (i) Reduction in the bank rate
    (ii) Sale of government securities in the open market
    (iii) Increase in government expenditure
    (iv) Increase in the margin requirement on secured loans
    (Choose the correct option)

    1. (ii) and (iv)
    2. (i) and (iii)
    3. (i) and (ii)
    4. (iii) and (iv)
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) (i) and (iii)

    Deficient demand needs a rise in aggregate demand. A lower bank rate makes credit cheaper and higher government spending adds directly to demand. Selling securities and raising margin requirements reduce the money supply and credit, so they are used against excess demand.

  12. Question 12 · 3 marks · Government Budget and the Economy

  13. Question 13 · 4 marks · Money and Banking
    Inflation in an economy has stayed above the target for several months. To bring it down, the central bank decides to (I) raise the repo rate, and (II) sell government securities in the open market.
    State the meaning of each of these two steps and explain how each helps to control inflation.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (I) Repo rate is the rate at which the central bank lends short-term funds to commercial banks. When it is raised, borrowing from the central bank becomes costlier for banks. Banks in turn raise their own lending rates, so people and firms borrow less. Lower credit reduces spending, aggregate demand falls and the pressure on prices eases.
    (II) Open market operations are the purchase and sale of government securities by the central bank in the open market. When the central bank sells securities, buyers pay for them from their bank deposits, so the cash reserves of commercial banks fall. Their capacity to create credit falls, the money supply contracts, aggregate demand falls and inflation is controlled.

    Marking scheme:

    • (I) Meaning: repo rate is the rate at which the central bank lends to commercial banks (1 mark)
    • (I) Effect: costlier borrowing, banks raise lending rates, credit and aggregate demand fall (1 mark)
    • (II) Meaning: open market operations are purchase/sale of government securities by the central bank (1 mark)
    • (II) Effect: sale of securities reduces bank reserves and credit creation, so money supply and aggregate demand fall (1 mark)

  14. Question 14 · 4 marks · Open Economy Macroeconomics
    In 2025-26, Country Teralia had the following autonomous transactions in its Balance of Payments: its current account showed a deficit of USD 80 billion, while its capital account showed autonomous inflows (net) of USD 60 billion. All other autonomous items were zero.

    (I) Identify whether Teralia's Balance of Payments is in surplus or deficit, and find its size. [2]

    (II) Explain how this deficit would be settled. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Identify whether Teralia's Balance of Payments is in surplus or deficit, and find its size.

    Model answer: Total autonomous payments exceed total autonomous receipts: the current account deficit of USD 80 billion is only partly covered by net autonomous capital inflows of USD 60 billion.
    BoP deficit = 80 $-$ 60 = USD 20 billion. Teralia's Balance of Payments is in deficit by USD 20 billion.

    Marking scheme:

    • BoP is in deficit, as autonomous payments exceed autonomous receipts (1 mark)
    • Size of deficit: 80 $-$ 60 = USD 20 billion (1 mark)

    (II) Explain how this deficit would be settled.

    Model answer: The deficit is settled through accommodating (below the line) transactions, which take place only to cover the gap in autonomous transactions. Teralia's central bank would draw down its foreign exchange reserves (official reserve sale) by USD 20 billion, and/or the country would borrow from abroad, for example from the International Monetary Fund.

    Marking scheme:

    • Settled through accommodating transactions (undertaken to cover the BoP gap) (1 mark)
    • Example: drawing down foreign exchange reserves of the central bank / borrowing from the IMF (1 mark)

  15. Question 15 · 4 marks · Determination of Income and Employment
    For a hypothetical economy:
    Consumption function, $C = 100 + 0.75Y$
    Autonomous investment, $\bar{I} = 200$ (all figures in ₹ crore)

    (I) Determine the equilibrium level of income. [2]

    (II) If investment rises by ₹50 crore, find the value of the investment multiplier and the new equilibrium level of income. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Determine the equilibrium level of income.

    Model answer: At equilibrium, $Y = C + I$
    $Y = 100 + 0.75Y + 200$
    $0.25Y = 300$
    $Y$ = ₹1,200 crore

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct equilibrium condition: $Y = 100 + 0.75Y + 200$ (1 mark)
    • Correct answer: equilibrium income = ₹1,200 crore (1 mark)

    (II) If investment rises by ₹50 crore, find the value of the investment multiplier and the new equilibrium level of income.

    Model answer: Investment multiplier $k = \frac{1}{1 - MPC} = \frac{1}{1 - 0.75} = 4$
    Increase in income $= k \times \Delta I = 4 \times 50$ = ₹200 crore
    New equilibrium income = 1,200 + 200 = ₹1,400 crore
    (Check: $Y = 100 + 0.75Y + 250$ gives $0.25Y = 350$, $Y = 1{,}400$.)

    Marking scheme:

    • Multiplier $k = \frac{1}{1-0.75} = 4$ (1 mark)
    • New equilibrium income = 1,200 + 4 $\times$ 50 = ₹1,400 crore (1 mark)

  16. Question 16 · 6 marks · National Income Accounting
    Read the following text carefully and answer the questions that follow.
    A news channel in the hypothetical country of Rivania ran the headline: "Rivania's economy grows by 15% in one year!" The statistics office had reported that Rivania's Gross Domestic Product at current prices rose from ₹40,000 crore in 2024-25 to ₹46,000 crore in 2025-26. The year 2024-25 is the base year, and the GDP deflator for 2025-26 is 115.
    An economist on the same channel was not impressed. She pointed out that even a genuine rise in GDP need not make every citizen better off. Much of Rivania's new income had gone to a few large companies, smoke from new factories had worsened the air in its cities, and the large amount of unpaid work done at home by family members was not counted in GDP at all.

    (I) Define GDP deflator. [1]

    (II) Calculate the real GDP of Rivania for 2025-26. Is the headline justified? Give a reason. [2]

    (III) In the light of the economist's views, explain three reasons why GDP may not be a good index of the welfare of the people. [3]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Define GDP deflator.

    Model answer: GDP deflator is the ratio of nominal GDP to real GDP, multiplied by 100. It shows the change in the general price level of all final goods and services relative to the base year.

    Marking scheme:

    • GDP deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) $\times$ 100, a measure of the change in prices (1 mark)

    (II) Calculate the real GDP of Rivania for 2025-26. Is the headline justified? Give a reason.

    Model answer: Real GDP = $\frac{\text{Nominal GDP}}{\text{GDP deflator}} \times 100 = \frac{46{,}000}{115} \times 100$ = ₹40,000 crore
    Real GDP in 2025-26 (₹40,000 crore) is the same as GDP in the base year 2024-25 (₹40,000 crore). The 15% rise is only in nominal GDP and is entirely due to a rise in prices. The physical output of goods and services has not grown, so the headline is not justified.

    Marking scheme:

    • Real GDP = 46,000 / 115 $\times$ 100 = ₹40,000 crore (1 mark)
    • Not justified: real GDP is unchanged, the rise is only due to higher prices (1 mark)

    (III) In the light of the economist's views, explain three reasons why GDP may not be a good index of the welfare of the people.

    Model answer: 1. Distribution of GDP: if a rise in GDP is concentrated in the hands of a few (as with the large companies in Rivania), most people may not become better off, and inequality may rise.
    2. Externalities: GDP does not deduct the harm caused by economic activity, for which nobody is paid, such as air pollution from factories. GDP may rise while welfare falls.
    3. Non-monetary exchanges: many activities, such as domestic work done by family members and barter, are not valued in money and are left out of GDP. So GDP underestimates the true level of well-being.

    Marking scheme:

    • Distribution of GDP: gains going to a few may not raise the welfare of most people (1 mark)
    • Externalities: harm such as pollution is not deducted from GDP (1 mark)
    • Non-monetary exchanges: unpaid domestic work and barter are not counted in GDP (1 mark)

  17. Question 17 · 6 marks · Determination of Income and Employment
    The following table shows the consumption schedule of a hypothetical economy (figures in ₹ crore).

    Income (Y)0100200300400
    Consumption (C)40120200280360

    (I) From the given schedule, find: (a) the marginal propensity to consume, (b) the average propensity to save at an income of ₹400 crore, and (c) the break-even level of income. [3]

    (II) At the current level of output of an economy, ex-ante saving is greater than ex-ante investment. Explain how the economy will move towards equilibrium. [3]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) From the given schedule, find: (a) the marginal propensity to consume, (b) the average propensity to save at an income of ₹400 crore, and (c) the break-even level of income.

    Model answer: (a) $MPC = \frac{\Delta C}{\Delta Y} = \frac{120 - 40}{100 - 0} = \frac{80}{100} = 0.8$ (the same for every step of ₹100 crore).
    (b) At $Y = 400$: $S = Y - C = 400 - 360 = 40$, so $APS = \frac{S}{Y} = \frac{40}{400} = 0.1$
    (c) Break-even income is where $C = Y$ (saving is zero): ₹200 crore.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) MPC = 80/100 = 0.8 (1 mark)
    • (b) APS = 40/400 = 0.1 (1 mark)
    • (c) Break-even income = ₹200 crore (where C = Y) (1 mark)

    (II) At the current level of output of an economy, ex-ante saving is greater than ex-ante investment. Explain how the economy will move towards equilibrium.

    Model answer: When ex-ante (planned) saving exceeds ex-ante investment, households plan to consume less than producers expected. So aggregate demand is less than aggregate supply (the value of output).
    Part of the output remains unsold and there is an unplanned accumulation of inventories with the firms.
    Firms respond by cutting production. Output, income and employment fall. As income falls, saving also falls, until planned saving becomes equal to planned investment and equilibrium is restored at a lower level of income.

    Marking scheme:

    • Planned saving greater than planned investment means AD is less than AS (1 mark)
    • Unplanned accumulation of inventories with firms (1 mark)
    • Firms cut output; income and saving fall until S = I at a lower income (1 mark)

  11. Question 28 · 3 marks · Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation
    Read the following text carefully:
    In 1991, India faced an economic crisis. The government was unable to repay its borrowings from abroad, and its foreign exchange reserves had fallen so low that they could pay for imports for only about two weeks. India approached two international institutions for a loan, and in return agreed to a set of economic reforms. These reforms were of two kinds: stabilisation measures and structural reform measures.

    (I) Name the two international institutions that India approached in 1991. [1]

    (II) Distinguish between stabilisation measures and structural reform measures. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Name the two international institutions that India approached in 1991.

    Model answer: The International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD, popularly called the World Bank) and the International Monetary Fund (IMF).

    Marking scheme:

    • World Bank (IBRD) (½ marks)
    • International Monetary Fund (IMF) (½ marks)

    (II) Distinguish between stabilisation measures and structural reform measures.

    Model answer: Stabilisation measures are short-term measures meant to correct the weaknesses that had developed in the balance of payments and to bring inflation under control, for example by keeping enough foreign exchange reserves and controlling rising prices.
    Structural reform measures are long-term measures meant to improve the efficiency of the economy and its international competitiveness by removing the rigidities in various sectors of the economy.

    Marking scheme:

    • Stabilisation measures: short-term, to correct the BoP and control inflation (1 mark)
    • Structural reforms: long-term, to improve efficiency and international competitiveness by removing rigidities (1 mark)

  12. Question 29 · 3 marks · Rural Development
    "Self-help groups have helped to fill the gap in the formal credit system for the rural poor."
    Explain any three ways in which self-help groups (SHGs) have helped to meet the credit needs of the rural poor.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. Pooling of savings: SHGs promote thrift in small amounts. Each member contributes a small sum regularly, and the pooled fund is used to give loans to members.
    2. Credit at reasonable terms: members get small loans at reasonable rates of interest, repayable in small instalments, without the collateral demanded by formal banks. This frees them from moneylenders who charge very high interest.
    3. Link with banks (microcredit): a group with a good record can get loans from banks, so the poor gain access to formal credit through the group. SHGs have also helped in the empowerment of women, who form most of their members.

    Marking scheme:

    • Pooling of small regular savings of members into a common fund (1 mark)
    • Small loans at reasonable interest and in small instalments without collateral, reducing dependence on moneylenders (1 mark)
    • Access to bank credit (microcredit) through the group / empowerment of women (1 mark)

  13. Question 30 · 4 marks · Indian Economy 1950–1990
    Answer the following questions about Indian agriculture and industry during 1950-1990.

    (I) Explain any two reasons why small-scale industries were promoted in India during this period. [2]

    (II) State the meaning of marketable surplus. Why was it important during the Green Revolution? [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Explain any two reasons why small-scale industries were promoted in India during this period.

    Model answer: 1. Employment: small-scale industries are more labour-intensive than large-scale industries, that is, they use more labour, so they generate more employment.
    2. Rural development: the Village and Small-Scale Industries Committee (Karve Committee, 1955) noted that small-scale industries could be used for promoting rural development, as they can be set up in villages and small towns and provide work and income there.

    Marking scheme:

    • Labour-intensive, so they generate more employment (1 mark)
    • Promote rural development (Karve Committee, 1955): can be set up in villages, giving work and income there (1 mark)

    (II) State the meaning of marketable surplus. Why was it important during the Green Revolution?

    Model answer: Marketable surplus is the portion of agricultural produce that farmers sell in the market (after keeping what they need for their own use).
    During the Green Revolution, a good proportion of the extra rice and wheat produced was sold in the market as marketable surplus. As a result, the price of foodgrains fell relative to other items of consumption, which benefited low-income groups, and the government could procure enough foodgrains to build buffer stocks.

    Marking scheme:

    • Meaning: the part of farm produce sold in the market by farmers (1 mark)
    • Importance: relative price of foodgrains fell, helping the poor / government could build buffer stocks (1 mark)

  14. Question 31 · 4 marks · Employment
    "Over the years, the share of casual wage labourers in India's workforce has increased."
    Explain the meaning of casualisation of the workforce, and discuss any two of its effects on the workers.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Meaning: casualisation of the workforce is a situation in which the share of workers employed as casual wage labourers rises, compared with self-employed and regular salaried workers.
    Effect 1, no job security and irregular income: casual workers are hired on a daily or short-term basis. They can lose work at any time and do not get work every day, so their income is low and uncertain.
    Effect 2, no social security: casual workers are not entitled to benefits such as provident fund, pension, paid leave or medical benefits, and they cannot form strong trade unions. This makes them vulnerable and keeps their living standards low.

    Marking scheme:

    • Meaning: rising share of casual wage labourers in the workforce (1 mark)
    • Effect 1: no job security; work and income are irregular and low (1½ marks)
    • Effect 2: no social security benefits (provident fund, pension, paid leave), so workers are vulnerable (1½ marks)

  15. Question 32 · 4 marks · Human Capital Formation
    Distinguish between physical capital and human capital on any two bases.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. Tangibility and sale: physical capital (such as machines and buildings) is tangible and can easily be sold in the market like any other commodity. Human capital is intangible; it is built into the body and mind of its owner and cannot be sold separately.
    2. Mobility: physical capital is completely mobile between countries, except for some artificial trade restrictions. Human capital is not perfectly mobile between countries, as its movement is restricted by nationality and culture.
    (Other valid bases: physical capital can be separated from its owner, human capital cannot; physical capital depreciates with use, while human capital depreciates with ageing but this can be reduced through continuous investment in education and health.)

    Marking scheme:

    • Basis 1 named (e.g. tangibility) (½ marks)
    • Correct contrast on basis 1 (e.g. physical capital tangible and saleable; human capital intangible, part of its owner) (1½ marks)
    • Basis 2 named (e.g. mobility) (½ marks)
    • Correct contrast on basis 2 (e.g. physical capital fully mobile between countries; human capital restricted by nationality and culture) (1½ marks)

  16. Question 33 · 6 marks · Comparative Development Experiences of India and its Neighbours
    Answer the following questions on the development experiences of China and Pakistan.

    (I) Explain any three reasons for China's rapid economic growth after the reforms of 1978. [3]

    (II) Explain any three reasons for the slowdown in Pakistan's economic growth. [3]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) Explain any three reasons for China's rapid economic growth after the reforms of 1978.

    Model answer: 1. Reforms in agriculture: commune lands were divided into small plots and allotted to individual households for cultivation. Households could keep the income from the land after paying taxes, which raised agricultural output and rural incomes.
    2. Dual pricing and private enterprise: farmers and firms had to sell fixed quantities at prices set by the government and could sell the rest at market prices. Private firms, township and village enterprises were allowed to compete with state enterprises.
    3. Special Economic Zones: SEZs were set up to attract foreign investors, which brought in foreign capital, technology and exports. (China had also built a good base in education, health and land reforms before 1978, which helped the reforms succeed.)

    Marking scheme:

    • Agricultural reform: commune land given to households for cultivation (1 mark)
    • Dual pricing / freedom for private and township enterprises (1 mark)
    • Special Economic Zones to attract foreign investment (or: strong pre-reform base in education and health) (1 mark)

    (II) Explain any three reasons for the slowdown in Pakistan's economic growth.

    Model answer: 1. Volatile agriculture: growth in agriculture was not based on an institutional process of technical change but on good harvests, so output rose in good years and fell in bad years.
    2. Over-dependence on remittances and foreign aid: much of Pakistan's foreign exchange came from remittances of workers in the Middle East and from volatile agricultural exports, and the country depended more and more on foreign loans, which also became harder to repay.
    3. Political instability: frequent changes and instability in government made it difficult to follow consistent long-term policies for growth.

    Marking scheme:

    • Agricultural growth depended on good harvests, not technical change (1 mark)
    • Over-dependence on remittances, foreign aid and foreign loans (1 mark)
    • Political instability (1 mark)

  17. Question 34 · 6 marks · Environment and Sustainable Development
    Read the following text carefully and answer the questions that follow.
    Kesarpur, a district in north India, has a growing number of brick kilns, small factories and farms. Every winter, smoke from burning crop stubble and from kiln chimneys covers its towns, and the local river carries untreated waste from the factories. A recent district report warned that wastes are being produced faster than the environment can absorb them.
    To change course, the district administration has started three programmes. Under the first, farmers get subsidised solar-powered pumps in place of diesel pumps for irrigation. Under the second, villages are helped to set up community biogas plants that use cattle dung to produce cooking gas; the slurry left over is used as manure. Under the third, farmers are trained to turn crop residue and kitchen waste into compost instead of burning it.

    (I) State the meaning of absorptive capacity of the environment. [1]

    (II) Identify the programme in the text that replaces a non-renewable source of energy with a renewable one. [1]

    (III) Explain how the second and the third programmes promote sustainable development. [4]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (I) State the meaning of absorptive capacity of the environment.

    Model answer: Absorptive capacity is the ability of the environment to absorb waste and degradation (pollution) without losing its quality.

    Marking scheme:

    • Ability of the environment to absorb waste/degradation without loss of quality (1 mark)

    (II) Identify the programme in the text that replaces a non-renewable source of energy with a renewable one.

    Model answer: The first programme: solar-powered irrigation pumps replace diesel pumps, so solar energy (renewable) replaces diesel (non-renewable).

    Marking scheme:

    • Solar-powered pumps replacing diesel pumps (1 mark)

    (III) Explain how the second and the third programmes promote sustainable development.

    Model answer: Biogas plants (second programme): cattle dung is used to produce cooking gas, a renewable and cleaner fuel. This reduces the burning of firewood and dung cakes, which saves forests and cuts indoor air pollution. The slurry left over is a good manure, so the use of chemical fertilisers falls. Resources are used without harming the ability of future generations to meet their needs.
    Composting (third programme): crop residue and kitchen waste are turned into compost instead of being burnt. This stops the smoke from stubble burning and improves air quality. Compost adds nutrients and organic matter to the soil, maintains its fertility and reduces dependence on chemical fertilisers, which pollute soil and water.

    Marking scheme:

    • Biogas: uses dung to replace firewood/dung cakes, saving forests and reducing indoor pollution (1 mark)
    • Biogas: slurry used as manure, reducing use of chemical fertilisers (1 mark)
    • Composting: avoids stubble burning, so air pollution falls (1 mark)
    • Composting: maintains soil fertility and reduces dependence on chemical fertilisers (1 mark)
