Section A: Accounting for Partnership Firms and Companies

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Accounting for Partnership: Basic Concepts
    Sunita, a partner, withdrew ₹5,000 at the end of every month during the year ended 31st March 2026. Interest on drawings is charged @ 12% p.a. The interest on her drawings will be:

    1. ₹3,900
    2. ₹3,600
    3. ₹3,300
    4. ₹7,200
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) ₹3,300

    Total drawings = 5,000 × 12 = ₹60,000. For equal drawings at the end of every month, the average period is 5.5 months. Interest = 60,000 × 12/100 × 5.5/12 = ₹3,300.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Accounting for Partnership: Basic Concepts
    Seema, a partner, is entitled to a commission of 5% of the net profit after charging such commission. The net profit of the firm before charging the commission is ₹2,52,000. Seema's commission will be:

    1. ₹12,600
    2. ₹12,000
    3. ₹11,400
    4. ₹13,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) ₹12,000

    Commission after charging such commission = 2,52,000 × 5/105 = ₹12,000. (5% of ₹2,52,000 = ₹12,600 would be commission before charging it.)

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Issue of Debentures
    Read the following statements, Assertion (A) and Reason (R), and choose the correct option.
    Assertion (A): A debentureholder is an owner of the company.
    Reason (R): Debentureholders receive interest at a fixed rate on the face value of their debentures.

    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is correct explanation to (A).
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not correct explanation of (A).
    3. (A) is false but (R) is correct.
    4. (A) is correct but (R) is false.
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) (A) is false but (R) is correct.

    A debenture is an acknowledgement of a debt, so a debentureholder is a creditor of the company, not an owner. (A) is false. (R) is correct: debentures carry a fixed rate of interest.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Accounting for Share Capital
    Tara Ltd. issued 20,000 equity shares of ₹10 each, and the full amount was called up. All the money was received except the final call of ₹2 per share on 1,500 shares. The amount shown as 'Subscribed but not fully paid' capital in the Notes to Accounts will be:

    1. ₹15,000
    2. ₹3,000
    3. ₹1,97,000
    4. ₹12,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) ₹12,000

    Subscribed but not fully paid = 1,500 shares × ₹10 = ₹15,000, less calls in arrears 1,500 × 2 = ₹3,000, giving ₹12,000.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Goodwill: Nature and Valuation
    The average profit of a firm is ₹1,50,000. Its capital employed is ₹10,00,000 and the normal rate of return is 10%. The goodwill of the firm by capitalising the super profit will be:

    1. ₹15,00,000
    2. ₹5,00,000
    3. ₹1,50,000
    4. ₹50,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) ₹5,00,000

    Normal profit = 10,00,000 × 10% = ₹1,00,000. Super profit = 1,50,000 − 1,00,000 = ₹50,000. Goodwill = 50,000 × 100/10 = ₹5,00,000.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Change in Profit Sharing Ratio
    P and Q share profits in the ratio of 3 : 2. They decide to share future profits in the ratio of 2 : 3. The Balance Sheet shows a debit balance of ₹30,000 in Profit and Loss Account (accumulated loss). The partners do not want to disturb this balance in the books. The adjusting entry will be:

    1. P's Capital A/c Dr. ₹6,000; To Q's Capital A/c ₹6,000
    2. Q's Capital A/c Dr. ₹6,000; To P's Capital A/c ₹6,000
    3. P's Capital A/c Dr. ₹18,000 and Q's Capital A/c Dr. ₹12,000; To Profit and Loss A/c ₹30,000
    4. Profit and Loss A/c Dr. ₹30,000; To P's Capital A/c ₹18,000 and To Q's Capital A/c ₹12,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) P's Capital A/c Dr. ₹6,000; To Q's Capital A/c ₹6,000

    The loss belongs to the old ratio: P ₹18,000, Q ₹12,000. If it stays in the books it will later be borne in the new ratio: P ₹12,000, Q ₹18,000. So P must bear ₹6,000 more now and Q ₹6,000 less: P's Capital A/c Dr. ₹6,000 To Q's Capital A/c ₹6,000.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Admission of a Partner
    A and B are partners sharing profits in the ratio of 5 : 3. They admit C, and the new profit sharing ratio of A, B and C is 4 : 2 : 2. The sacrificing ratio of A and B is:

    1. 5 : 3
    2. 2 : 1
    3. 1 : 1
    4. 2 : 3
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) 1 : 1

    A's sacrifice = 5/8 − 4/8 = 1/8; B's sacrifice = 3/8 − 2/8 = 1/8. Sacrificing ratio = 1 : 1.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Admission of a Partner
    X and Y are partners sharing profits in the ratio of 3 : 2. They admit Z for 1/4th share in profits. Goodwill of the firm is valued at ₹1,60,000. Z cannot bring his share of goodwill premium in cash, so it is adjusted through his Current Account. The amount credited to X's Capital Account for goodwill will be:

    1. ₹24,000
    2. ₹40,000
    3. ₹96,000
    4. ₹20,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) ₹24,000

    Z's share of goodwill = 1,60,000 × 1/4 = ₹40,000. It is credited to X and Y in their sacrificing ratio, which is the old ratio 3 : 2 (nothing else is given). X gets 40,000 × 3/5 = ₹24,000.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Retirement and Death of a Partner
    A, B and C are partners sharing profits in the ratio of 2 : 2 : 1. B retires, and his share is taken over by A and C in the ratio of 3 : 2. The new profit sharing ratio of A and C will be:

    1. 2 : 1
    2. 16 : 9
    3. 3 : 2
    4. 13 : 12
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) 16 : 9

    B's share 2/5 is taken 3 : 2: A gets 6/25 and C gets 4/25. A = 2/5 + 6/25 = 16/25; C = 1/5 + 4/25 = 9/25. New ratio = 16 : 9.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Retirement and Death of a Partner
    D, a partner, died during the year. The following items appear in his capital account at the time of settlement: opening capital ₹1,50,000, share of goodwill ₹30,000, share of loss on revaluation ₹6,000, share of general reserve ₹12,000 and drawings ₹8,000. The amount payable to D's executors will be:

    1. ₹1,90,000
    2. ₹1,86,000
    3. ₹1,62,000
    4. ₹1,78,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) ₹1,78,000

    Amount payable = 1,50,000 + 30,000 (goodwill) + 12,000 (reserve) − 6,000 (revaluation loss) − 8,000 (drawings) = ₹1,78,000.

  11. Question 11 · 1 mark · Dissolution of Partnership Firm
    On the dissolution of a firm, assets (other than cash) of ₹3,00,000 and creditors of ₹80,000 were transferred to the Realisation Account. The assets realised ₹2,40,000, creditors were paid ₹76,000 in full settlement and realisation expenses were ₹4,000. The result of realisation is:

    1. Loss ₹60,000
    2. Loss ₹56,000
    3. Profit ₹60,000
    4. Loss ₹64,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Loss ₹60,000

    Debit side: assets 3,00,000 + creditors paid 76,000 + expenses 4,000 = ₹3,80,000. Credit side: creditors 80,000 + assets realised 2,40,000 = ₹3,20,000. Loss = 3,80,000 − 3,20,000 = ₹60,000.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Accounting for Share Capital
    Equity shares of ₹10 each, fully called up, were forfeited after the holder had paid ₹7 per share. The minimum price per share at which these shares can be reissued as fully paid up is:

    1. ₹7
    2. ₹10
    3. ₹4
    4. ₹3
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) ₹3

    The discount allowed on reissue cannot exceed the amount forfeited (₹7 per share). Minimum reissue price = 10 − 7 = ₹3 per share.

  13. Question 13 · 1 mark · Accounting for Share Capital
    Shares issued by a company to its employees or directors at a discount, or for consideration other than cash, for providing know-how or making available rights such as intellectual property rights are called:

    1. Sweat equity shares
    2. Employee Stock Option Plan shares
    3. Bonus shares
    4. Right shares
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Sweat equity shares

    These are sweat equity shares. Under ESOP, employees get an option to buy shares later at a fixed price; bonus shares are issued free out of reserves; right shares are offered to existing shareholders.

  14. Question 14 · 1 mark · Issue of Debentures
    A company issued 2,000 debentures of ₹100 each at a discount of 6%, redeemable at a premium of 5%. The amount of loss on issue of these debentures is:

    1. ₹12,000
    2. ₹10,000
    3. ₹22,000
    4. ₹2,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) ₹22,000

    Loss on issue = discount on issue (2,000 × 6 = ₹12,000) + premium payable on redemption (2,000 × 5 = ₹10,000) = ₹22,000.

  15. Question 15 · 1 mark · Dissolution of Partnership Firm
    On dissolution, Asha, a partner, agreed to take over the work of dissolution and bear all realisation expenses for a remuneration of ₹10,000. The actual realisation expenses of ₹12,000 were paid by the firm on her behalf. The entry for payment of these expenses will be:

    1. Realisation A/c Dr. ₹12,000; To Bank A/c ₹12,000
    2. Asha's Capital A/c Dr. ₹12,000; To Bank A/c ₹12,000
    3. Realisation A/c Dr. ₹10,000; To Bank A/c ₹10,000
    4. Realisation A/c Dr. ₹2,000; To Asha's Capital A/c ₹2,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Asha's Capital A/c Dr. ₹12,000; To Bank A/c ₹12,000

    Asha has to bear the expenses, so the amount paid by the firm on her behalf is debited to her capital account. (Her remuneration of ₹10,000 is recorded separately: Realisation A/c Dr. To Asha's Capital A/c.)

  16. Question 16 · 1 mark · Accounting for Partnership: Basic Concepts
    When the capitals of partners are fixed, which of the following is recorded in the partners' Current Accounts?

    1. Additional capital introduced
    2. Permanent withdrawal of capital
    3. Interest on drawings
    4. Opening balance of capital
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Interest on drawings

    Under the fixed capital method, the capital account records only the opening capital, additional capital introduced and permanent withdrawal of capital. Interest on drawings, like drawings, salary and share of profit, goes to the current account.

  18. Question 18 · 3 marks · Goodwill: Nature and Valuation
    A firm earned profits of ₹1,40,000, ₹1,50,000 and ₹1,70,000 in the years ended 31st March 2024, 2025 and 2026 respectively. You are told that:
    (i) the profit of 2024-25 is after charging an abnormal loss of ₹10,000 due to a fire;
    (ii) the profit of 2025-26 includes a non-recurring income of ₹20,000.
    The capital employed in the firm is ₹10,00,000 and the normal rate of return is 12%. Calculate the goodwill of the firm at three years' purchase of super profits.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Adjusted profits: 2023-24 ₹1,40,000; 2024-25 1,50,000 + 10,000 = ₹1,60,000; 2025-26 1,70,000 − 20,000 = ₹1,50,000
    Average profit = 4,50,000/3 = ₹1,50,000
    Normal profit = 10,00,000 × 12% = ₹1,20,000
    Super profit = 1,50,000 − 1,20,000 = ₹30,000
    Goodwill = 30,000 × 3 = ₹90,000

    Marking scheme:

    • Adjusted profits (abnormal loss added back, non-recurring income deducted) and average profit ₹1,50,000 (1 mark)
    • Normal profit ₹1,20,000 (½ marks)
    • Super profit ₹30,000 (½ marks)
    • Goodwill ₹90,000 (1 mark)

  2. Question 28 · 1 mark · Accounting Ratios
    Read the following statements, Assertion (A) and Reason (R), and choose the correct option.
    Assertion (A): A high inventory turnover ratio generally shows efficient management of inventory.
    Reason (R): Inventory turnover ratio is calculated by dividing revenue from operations by average trade receivables.

    1. Both A and R are correct, and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are correct, but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is correct but R is incorrect
    4. A is incorrect but R is correct

    • Adjustments: depreciation ₹1,10,000, patents ₹10,000, interest ₹12,000 added; profit on sale ₹12,000 deducted; operating profit ₹3,70,000 (1 mark)
    • Working capital changes and tax paid; net cash from operating activities ₹2,80,000 (1 mark)
    • Investing: PPE ₹3,50,000 and patents ₹30,000 purchased, investments sold ₹62,000; net cash used ₹3,18,000 (1 mark)
    • Financing: shares ₹2,00,000, loan repaid ₹1,00,000, overdraft ₹30,000, interest ₹12,000, dividend ₹60,000; net ₹58,000 (1½ marks)
    • Net increase ₹20,000 reconciled with cash ₹40,000 to ₹60,000 (½ marks)
