Section A: Accounting for Partnership Firms and Companies

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Accounting for Partnership: Basic Concepts
    Kiran, a partner, withdrew ₹6,000 at the beginning of each quarter during the year ended 31st March 2026. The partnership deed allows interest on drawings @ 10% p.a. The interest on Kiran's drawings will be:

    1. ₹900
    2. ₹1,200
    3. ₹1,500
    4. ₹2,400
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) ₹1,500

    Drawings made at the beginning of each quarter carry interest for an average period of 7.5 months. Total drawings = ₹24,000. Interest = 24,000 × 10/100 × 7.5/12 = ₹1,500.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Accounting for Partnership: Basic Concepts
    P, Q and R are partners sharing profits and losses in the ratio of 2 : 2 : 1. R is guaranteed a minimum profit of ₹60,000 a year, and any deficiency is to be borne by P and Q in the ratio of 3 : 2. The firm's profit for the year ended 31st March 2026 was ₹2,00,000. P's share of profit after meeting the guarantee will be:

    1. ₹68,000
    2. ₹70,000
    3. ₹72,000
    4. ₹80,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) ₹68,000

    R's share = 2,00,000 × 1/5 = ₹40,000, so the deficiency = 60,000 − 40,000 = ₹20,000. P bears 3/5 of it = ₹12,000. P's share = 2,00,000 × 2/5 − 12,000 = 80,000 − 12,000 = ₹68,000.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Accounting for Share Capital
    Read the following statements, Assertion (A) and Reason (R), and choose the correct option.
    Assertion (A): Securities premium received on the issue of shares cannot be used by a company to pay dividend to its shareholders.
    Reason (R): Section 52 of the Companies Act, 2013 lists the purposes for which securities premium may be used, and payment of dividend is not one of them.

    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is correct explanation to (A).
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not correct explanation of (A).
    3. (A) is false but (R) is correct.
    4. (A) is correct but (R) is false.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is correct explanation to (A).

    Both statements are true. Securities premium can be used only for the purposes allowed by Section 52 (for example, issue of fully paid bonus shares, writing off preliminary expenses or discount/loss on issue of debentures, premium on redemption, buy-back). Dividend is not among them, which is exactly why (A) holds.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Accounting for Share Capital
    Neel Ltd. invited applications for 40,000 equity shares of ₹10 each, payable ₹3 on application, ₹4 on allotment and ₹3 on first and final call. Applications were received for 60,000 shares and shares were allotted to all applicants on a pro-rata basis. Excess application money was adjusted towards allotment. How much will be received on allotment from Mohan, who had applied for 1,500 shares?

    1. ₹4,000
    2. ₹2,500
    3. ₹1,500
    4. ₹2,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) ₹2,500

    Mohan is allotted 1,500 × 40,000/60,000 = 1,000 shares. He paid 1,500 × 3 = ₹4,500 on application against ₹3,000 required, so ₹1,500 is excess. Allotment due = 1,000 × 4 = ₹4,000; amount received = 4,000 − 1,500 = ₹2,500.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Goodwill: Nature and Valuation
    The capital employed by a firm is ₹5,00,000 and the normal rate of return in the industry is 12%. The average profit of the firm is ₹84,000. The goodwill of the firm at three years' purchase of super profits will be:

    1. ₹1,80,000
    2. ₹2,52,000
    3. ₹72,000
    4. ₹24,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) ₹72,000

    Normal profit = 5,00,000 × 12% = ₹60,000. Super profit = 84,000 − 60,000 = ₹24,000. Goodwill = 24,000 × 3 = ₹72,000.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Change in Profit Sharing Ratio
    X, Y and Z are partners sharing profits in the ratio of 5 : 3 : 2. From 1st April 2026 they decide to share profits in the ratio of 2 : 3 : 5. Which of the following is correct?

    1. X sacrifices 3/10 and Z gains 3/10
    2. X gains 3/10 and Z sacrifices 3/10
    3. X sacrifices 3/10, Y gains 1/10 and Z gains 2/10
    4. X sacrifices 1/2 and Z gains 1/2
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) X sacrifices 3/10 and Z gains 3/10

    Old share − new share: X = 5/10 − 2/10 = 3/10 (sacrifice); Y = 3/10 − 3/10 = 0 (no change); Z = 2/10 − 5/10 = −3/10 (gain of 3/10).

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Admission of a Partner
    A and B are partners sharing profits in the ratio of 3 : 2. They admit C for 1/4th share in profits, which C acquires 1/6th from A and 1/12th from B. The new profit sharing ratio of A, B and C will be:

    1. 9 : 6 : 5
    2. 19 : 26 : 15
    3. 26 : 19 : 15
    4. 13 : 19 : 15
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) 26 : 19 : 15

    A = 3/5 − 1/6 = 13/30 = 26/60; B = 2/5 − 1/12 = 19/60; C = 1/4 = 15/60. New ratio = 26 : 19 : 15.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Admission of a Partner
    M and N are partners sharing profits in the ratio of 3 : 2 with capitals of ₹2,40,000 and ₹1,60,000 respectively (after all adjustments). They admit O for 1/5th share in profits. O brings ₹1,20,000 as his capital but nothing for goodwill. The hidden goodwill of the firm is:

    1. ₹2,00,000
    2. ₹80,000
    3. ₹1,20,000
    4. ₹20,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) ₹80,000

    Total capital of the firm on the basis of O's share = 1,20,000 × 5/1 = ₹6,00,000. Actual combined capital = 2,40,000 + 1,60,000 + 1,20,000 = ₹5,20,000. Hidden goodwill = 6,00,000 − 5,20,000 = ₹80,000.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Retirement and Death of a Partner
    P, Q and R are partners sharing profits in the ratio of 4 : 3 : 2. Q retires, and P and R decide to share future profits in the ratio of 5 : 4. The gaining ratio of P and R is:

    1. 2 : 1
    2. 5 : 4
    3. 1 : 1
    4. 1 : 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) 1 : 2

    Old shares: P = 4/9, R = 2/9. New shares: P = 5/9, R = 4/9. Gain: P = 1/9, R = 2/9. Gaining ratio = 1 : 2.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Retirement and Death of a Partner
    D, a partner entitled to 1/4th share of profits, died on 30th June 2025. His share of profit up to the date of death is to be calculated on the basis of sales. For the year ended 31st March 2025, sales were ₹20,00,000 and profit was ₹3,00,000. Sales from 1st April 2025 to 30th June 2025 were ₹6,00,000. D's share of profit up to the date of death is:

    1. ₹18,750
    2. ₹22,500
    3. ₹75,000
    4. ₹90,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) ₹22,500

    Rate of profit on sales = 3,00,000/20,00,000 × 100 = 15%. Profit up to date of death = 6,00,000 × 15% = ₹90,000. D's share = 90,000 × 1/4 = ₹22,500.

  11. Question 11 · 1 mark · Dissolution of Partnership Firm
    On the dissolution of a firm, Ravi, a partner, took over stock of book value ₹50,000 at 10% less than book value. The journal entry for this will be:

    1. Ravi's Capital A/c Dr. ₹45,000; To Realisation A/c ₹45,000
    2. Realisation A/c Dr. ₹45,000; To Ravi's Capital A/c ₹45,000
    3. Ravi's Capital A/c Dr. ₹50,000; To Stock A/c ₹50,000
    4. Realisation A/c Dr. ₹5,000; To Ravi's Capital A/c ₹5,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Ravi's Capital A/c Dr. ₹45,000; To Realisation A/c ₹45,000

    Stock has already been transferred to the Realisation Account at book value. When a partner takes over an asset, his capital account is debited and the Realisation Account credited with the agreed value, 50,000 − 10% = ₹45,000.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Accounting for Share Capital
    Gopal Ltd. issued equity shares of ₹10 each at a premium of ₹3 per share. The first and final call of ₹4 per share was not paid on 500 shares, and these shares were forfeited. Application and allotment money (including premium) had been received on them. The amount credited to Share Forfeiture Account will be:

    1. ₹2,000
    2. ₹4,500
    3. ₹5,000
    4. ₹3,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) ₹3,000

    Amount received towards share capital on each share = 10 − 4 = ₹6 (the premium was received and stays in Securities Premium). Share Forfeiture A/c is credited with 500 × 6 = ₹3,000.

  13. Question 13 · 1 mark · Issue of Debentures
    Sagar Ltd. purchased machinery for ₹4,50,000 and paid the vendor by issuing 9% debentures of ₹100 each at a premium of 25%. The number of debentures issued will be:

    1. 4,500
    2. 3,600
    3. 5,000
    4. 3,750
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) 3,600

    Issue price of one debenture = 100 + 25 = ₹125. Number of debentures = 4,50,000/125 = 3,600.

  14. Question 14 · 1 mark · Accounting for Share Capital
    Raj Ltd. forfeited 1,000 equity shares of ₹10 each, ₹8 called up, on which the holder had paid ₹5 per share. Out of these, 600 shares were reissued at ₹7 per share as ₹8 paid up. The amount transferred to Capital Reserve will be:

    1. ₹3,000
    2. ₹4,400
    3. ₹2,400
    4. ₹1,800
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) ₹2,400

    Amount forfeited on the 600 reissued shares = 600 × 5 = ₹3,000. Discount on reissue = 600 × (8 − 7) = ₹600. Capital Reserve = 3,000 − 600 = ₹2,400. (Forfeited amount on the other 400 shares stays in Share Forfeiture A/c.)

  15. Question 15 · 1 mark · Dissolution of Partnership Firm
    On the dissolution of a partnership firm, which of the following is debited to the Realisation Account?

    1. Repayment of a partner's loan
    2. Cash received from sale of assets
    3. Balance of accumulated profits
    4. Payment made to creditors
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Payment made to creditors

    Payment to creditors is recorded as Realisation A/c Dr. To Cash/Bank A/c. A partner's loan is paid by debiting the Partner's Loan A/c, cash from assets sold is credited to Realisation A/c, and accumulated profits are credited to partners' capital accounts.

  16. Question 16 · 1 mark · Accounting for Partnership: Basic Concepts
    There is no partnership deed. On 1st October 2025, partner Vimal gave a loan of ₹50,000 to the firm. The interest payable to Vimal on this loan for the year ended 31st March 2026 will be:

    1. Nil
    2. ₹3,000
    3. ₹2,500
    4. ₹1,500
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) ₹1,500

    In the absence of a deed, the Indian Partnership Act, 1932 allows interest on a partner's loan @ 6% p.a. For 6 months: 50,000 × 6/100 × 6/12 = ₹1,500. It is a charge against profits.

  18. Question 18 · 3 marks · Goodwill: Nature and Valuation
    The profits of a firm for the last three years were: 2023-24 ₹1,60,000; 2024-25 ₹1,90,000; 2025-26 ₹2,00,000. The profit of 2025-26 includes a non-recurring gain of ₹10,000. The firm's assets are ₹18,00,000 and its outside liabilities are ₹5,00,000. The normal rate of return in the business is 12%.
    Calculate the goodwill of the firm by the capitalisation of average profits method.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Adjusted profit of 2025-26 = 2,00,000 − 10,000 = ₹1,90,000
    Average profit = (1,60,000 + 1,90,000 + 1,90,000)/3 = 5,40,000/3 = ₹1,80,000
    Capitalised value of average profit = 1,80,000 × 100/12 = ₹15,00,000
    Net assets (capital employed) = 18,00,000 − 5,00,000 = ₹13,00,000
    Goodwill = 15,00,000 − 13,00,000 = ₹2,00,000

    Marking scheme:

    • Average profit ₹1,80,000 after deducting the non-recurring gain of ₹10,000 (1 mark)
    • Capitalised value = 1,80,000 × 100/12 = ₹15,00,000 (1 mark)
    • Net assets = ₹13,00,000 (½ marks)
    • Goodwill = ₹2,00,000 (½ marks)

    2. Both A and R are correct, but R is not the correct explanation of A
