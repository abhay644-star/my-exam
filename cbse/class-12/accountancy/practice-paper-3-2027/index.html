Section A: Accounting for Partnership Firms and Companies

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Accounting for Partnership: Basic Concepts
    Anil and Bimal are partners sharing profits and losses in the ratio of 3 : 2. Their fixed capitals are ₹4,00,000 and ₹2,00,000 respectively. The partnership deed allows interest on capital @ 10% p.a., treated as an appropriation of profit. The profit for the year ended 31st March 2026, before interest on capital, was ₹48,000. The interest on capital credited to Anil will be:

    1. ₹40,000
    2. ₹28,800
    3. ₹32,000
    4. ₹24,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) ₹32,000

    Interest due: Anil ₹40,000, Bimal ₹20,000, total ₹60,000. Interest on capital is an appropriation, so it cannot exceed the profit of ₹48,000. The profit is shared in the ratio of interest due (2 : 1): Anil gets 48,000 × 2/3 = ₹32,000.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Accounting for Partnership: Basic Concepts
    Vikram, a partner, withdrew ₹12,000 on 1st July 2025 and ₹8,000 on 1st December 2025. Interest on drawings is charged @ 9% p.a. The interest on his drawings for the year ended 31st March 2026 will be:

    1. ₹1,800
    2. ₹900
    3. ₹810
    4. ₹1,050
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) ₹1,050

    ₹12,000 for 9 months: 12,000 × 9/100 × 9/12 = ₹810. ₹8,000 for 4 months: 8,000 × 9/100 × 4/12 = ₹240. Total = ₹1,050.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Admission of a Partner
    Read the following statements, Assertion (A) and Reason (R), and choose the correct option.
    Assertion (A): When a new partner brings his share of goodwill in cash, the amount is credited to the capital accounts of the old partners in their sacrificing ratio.
    Reason (R): The sacrificing ratio is always the same as the old profit sharing ratio of the old partners.

    1. Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is correct explanation to (A).
    2. Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not correct explanation of (A).
    3. (A) is false but (R) is correct.
    4. (A) is correct but (R) is false.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) (A) is correct but (R) is false.

    (A) is correct: the premium for goodwill compensates the old partners for the share of profit they give up, so it is shared in the sacrificing ratio. (R) is false: the sacrificing ratio equals the old ratio only when nothing else is agreed; if the new partner takes his share in some other proportion, the sacrificing ratio differs from the old ratio.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Accounting for Share Capital
    Arun Ltd. offered 1,00,000 equity shares to the public. Applications were received for 88,000 shares. As per the SEBI guidelines on minimum subscription, the company:

    1. can allot 88,000 shares and keep the application money
    2. can allot 88,000 shares only after the next call is paid
    3. must refund the entire application money, as the minimum subscription has not been received
    4. must allot all 1,00,000 shares to the 88,000 applicants
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) must refund the entire application money, as the minimum subscription has not been received

    The minimum subscription is 90% of the issue, i.e. 90,000 shares. Applications for only 88,000 shares were received, so no shares can be allotted and the entire application money must be refunded.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Goodwill: Nature and Valuation
    A firm's profits for the years ended 31st March 2024, 2025 and 2026 were ₹90,000, ₹1,20,000 and ₹1,50,000 respectively. The weights assigned to these years are 1, 2 and 3 respectively. The goodwill of the firm at two years' purchase of the weighted average profit will be:

    1. ₹2,40,000
    2. ₹2,60,000
    3. ₹1,30,000
    4. ₹3,90,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) ₹2,60,000

    Weighted average profit = (90,000 × 1 + 1,20,000 × 2 + 1,50,000 × 3)/6 = 7,80,000/6 = ₹1,30,000. Goodwill = 1,30,000 × 2 = ₹2,60,000. (₹2,40,000 uses the simple average.)

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Change in Profit Sharing Ratio
    X and Y share profits in the ratio of 3 : 2. They decide to share future profits equally. On that date, the Balance Sheet shows Investments at ₹2,50,000 and an Investment Fluctuation Reserve of ₹30,000. The market value of the investments is ₹2,35,000. The amount of Investment Fluctuation Reserve credited to X's Capital Account will be:

    1. ₹9,000
    2. ₹18,000
    3. ₹7,500
    4. ₹15,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) ₹9,000

    Fall in value of investments = 2,50,000 − 2,35,000 = ₹15,000, which is met from the reserve. The balance of ₹15,000 is credited to the partners in the old ratio 3 : 2: X gets 15,000 × 3/5 = ₹9,000.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Admission of a Partner
    After all adjustments on the admission of C, the capitals of A and B are ₹3,00,000 and ₹1,80,000 respectively. C is admitted for 1/4th share in profits and is to bring capital in proportion to his share, on the basis of the total adjusted capital of A and B. C's capital will be:

    1. ₹1,20,000
    2. ₹1,92,000
    3. ₹2,00,000
    4. ₹1,60,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) ₹1,60,000

    A and B together hold 3/4th share with capital ₹4,80,000. Total capital of the new firm = 4,80,000 × 4/3 = ₹6,40,000. C's capital = 6,40,000 × 1/4 = ₹1,60,000.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Admission of a Partner
    On the admission of a new partner it was found that: (i) investments of ₹10,000 were not recorded in the books; (ii) a creditor of ₹6,000 is not likely to claim the amount and is to be written back; (iii) stock is overvalued by ₹8,000. The result of revaluation will be:

    1. Profit ₹12,000
    2. Profit ₹8,000
    3. Loss ₹8,000
    4. Profit ₹24,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Profit ₹8,000

    Gains: unrecorded investments ₹10,000 and creditor written back ₹6,000 = ₹16,000. Loss: stock ₹8,000. Profit on revaluation = 16,000 − 8,000 = ₹8,000.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Retirement and Death of a Partner
    A, B and C are partners sharing profits in the ratio of 3 : 2 : 1. B retires. The goodwill of the firm is valued at ₹1,80,000 and A and C decide to share future profits equally. The journal entry for B's share of goodwill will be:

    1. A's Capital A/c Dr. ₹45,000 and C's Capital A/c Dr. ₹15,000; To B's Capital A/c ₹60,000
    2. C's Capital A/c Dr. ₹60,000; To B's Capital A/c ₹60,000
    3. A's Capital A/c Dr. ₹30,000 and C's Capital A/c Dr. ₹30,000; To B's Capital A/c ₹60,000
    4. C's Capital A/c Dr. ₹1,80,000; To B's Capital A/c ₹1,80,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) C's Capital A/c Dr. ₹60,000; To B's Capital A/c ₹60,000

    B's share of goodwill = 1,80,000 × 2/6 = ₹60,000. Gain: A = 1/2 − 3/6 = nil; C = 1/2 − 1/6 = 2/6. Only C gains, so C alone compensates B: C's Capital A/c Dr. ₹60,000 To B's Capital A/c ₹60,000.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Retirement and Death of a Partner
    D, a partner entitled to 1/4th share of profits, died on 31st December 2025. The firm closes its books on 31st March every year. His share of profit up to the date of death is to be calculated on the basis of the average profit of the last three years, which were ₹1,40,000, ₹1,60,000 and ₹1,80,000. D's share of profit will be:

    1. ₹40,000
    2. ₹33,750
    3. ₹30,000
    4. ₹10,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) ₹30,000

    Average profit = 4,80,000/3 = ₹1,60,000. Profit for 1st April to 31st December 2025 (9 months) = 1,60,000 × 9/12 = ₹1,20,000. D's share = 1,20,000 × 1/4 = ₹30,000.

  11. Question 11 · 1 mark · Dissolution of Partnership Firm
    On the dissolution of a firm, the cash balance was ₹20,000 and the other assets realised ₹1,80,000. Creditors of ₹90,000 were paid ₹85,000 in full settlement, realisation expenses were ₹5,000 and a bank loan of ₹30,000 was repaid. The amount available for paying the partners' capitals will be:

    1. ₹1,10,000
    2. ₹75,000
    3. ₹60,000
    4. ₹80,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) ₹80,000

    Cash available = 20,000 + 1,80,000 = ₹2,00,000. Less creditors ₹85,000, realisation expenses ₹5,000 and bank loan ₹30,000 = ₹1,20,000. Balance for partners' capitals = 2,00,000 − 1,20,000 = ₹80,000.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Accounting for Share Capital
    When shares issued at par are forfeited, the Share Capital Account is debited with:

    1. the amount paid by the shareholder
    2. the nominal (face) value of the shares
    3. the amount called up on the shares
    4. the amount not paid by the shareholder
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) the amount called up on the shares

    On forfeiture, the share capital recorded on those shares is cancelled. That is the amount called up, so Share Capital A/c is debited with the called-up amount (the amount paid goes to Share Forfeiture A/c and the unpaid calls are credited).

  14. Question 14 · 1 mark · Issue of Debentures
    Discount or loss on issue of debentures is written off in the year in which the debentures are issued:

    1. first from Securities Premium (if any), and the balance from the Statement of Profit and Loss
    2. from General Reserve only
    3. from Capital Reserve
    4. only from the Statement of Profit and Loss, in equal instalments over the life of the debentures
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) first from Securities Premium (if any), and the balance from the Statement of Profit and Loss

    Discount or loss on issue of debentures is a capital loss. It is written off in the year of issue, first from the Securities Premium balance (if any) and the balance from the Statement of Profit and Loss.

  15. Question 15 · 1 mark · Accounting for Share Capital
    A company forfeited 400 equity shares of ₹10 each, fully called up, for non-payment of the first and final call of ₹4 per share. All these shares were reissued at ₹11 per share as fully paid up. The amount transferred to Capital Reserve will be:

    1. ₹2,400
    2. ₹2,800
    3. ₹400
    4. ₹1,600
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) ₹2,400

    Amount forfeited = 400 × (10 − 4) = ₹2,400. The reissue price of ₹11 is above the face value, so no discount is allowed: ₹1 per share (₹400) is credited to Securities Premium, and the whole ₹2,400 forfeited is transferred to Capital Reserve.

  16. Question 16 · 1 mark · Dissolution of Partnership Firm
    On the dissolution of a firm, a loan given to the firm by a partner is paid:

    1. before the outside liabilities
    2. after the outside liabilities but before the partners' capitals
    3. after the partners' capitals have been paid
    4. along with the partners' capitals, in the profit sharing ratio
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) after the outside liabilities but before the partners' capitals

    Under Section 48 of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932, the assets are applied first to pay outside liabilities, then partners' loans (advances), then partners' capitals.

  2. Question 28 · 1 mark · Accounting Ratios
    Read the following statements, Assertion (A) and Reason (R), and choose the correct option.
    Assertion (A): Issue of equity shares for cash reduces the debt to equity ratio.
    Reason (R): Issue of equity shares increases shareholders' funds, while long-term debt remains unchanged.

    1. Both A and R are correct, and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are correct, but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is correct but R is incorrect
    4. A is incorrect but R is correct
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both A and R are correct, and R is the correct explanation of A

    Debt to equity ratio = Long-term debt ÷ Shareholders' funds. Issuing shares increases shareholders' funds without changing debt, so the ratio falls. A and R are both correct and R explains A.

  3. Question 29 · 1 mark · Cash Flow Statement

    3. Investing activities
    4. Cash and cash equivalents
