Section A: Reading Skills

  1. Question 1 · 12 marks
    Read the following passage.

    1. On a summer afternoon, the air above a crowded city market can be several degrees warmer than the air above fields only a short drive away. Scientists call this difference the urban heat island effect. It is not a new discovery: it was first described in London more than two hundred years ago. Yet it has become far more serious as Indian cities have grown taller, denser and more thickly paved.

    2. The main cause lies in the materials we build with. Concrete, asphalt and dark roofing sheets absorb most of the sunlight that falls on them and release the stored heat slowly through the evening and night. Trees and open soil behave differently. They cool their surroundings by giving off water vapour, a process known as evapotranspiration. When a neighbourhood replaces its trees with parking lots, it loses its natural cooler and gains, in effect, a giant storage heater.

    3. Human activity adds to the burden. Vehicles, factories and the very air-conditioners that people install to escape the heat all release warm air into the streets. This sets up a cruel cycle: the hotter the city becomes, the more air-conditioners are switched on, and the more heat is pumped outside.

    4. The effects are not shared equally. The heat is often fiercest in crowded settlements with tin roofs, narrow lanes and little room for greenery. Here, nights that never cool down deny the body its chance to recover from the day. Doctors point out that it is the warm night, more than the hot afternoon, that raises the risk of heatstroke, especially for elderly people, young children and those who work outdoors.

    5. The remedies are surprisingly simple. A coat of white, reflective paint on a roof can make the room below noticeably cooler. Rows of shade trees along streets, protected lakes and wetlands, and paving that lets rainwater soak into the ground all help a city shed its heat. Some Indian cities have also begun to issue heat warnings in advance and to open cool shelters for people who have no fans or electricity at home.

    6. Technology alone, however, will not solve the problem. Town planners argue that cooling a city calls for a change in thinking. Shade, water and green space must be seen not as luxuries but as public health infrastructure, as essential as roads and drains. A city that keeps itself cool, they say, is a city that keeps its people well.

  4. Question 6 · 5 marks
    Every year many lives are lost because no one nearby knows what to do in the first few minutes after an accident or a sudden illness. You are Sana/Sahil Verma, a Class XII student of Modern Public School, Indore. Write an article in 120-150 words for your school magazine on why every student should learn basic first aid.
    You may use the following cues:
    - the 'golden hour' after an accident or emergency
    - common situations: burns, cuts, fainting, choking
    - confidence and calm in a crisis
    - how schools can teach first aid
    - a trained student as a helper in the community

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: FIRST AID: A SKILL EVERY STUDENT NEEDS
    by Sana Verma, Class XII
    An accident on the road, a classmate who faints in the assembly, a younger brother who chokes on a sweet: emergencies arrive without warning. The first few minutes, often called the golden hour, can decide whether a person survives.
    Basic first aid is easy to learn. Cooling a burn under running water, pressing a clean cloth on a bleeding cut, placing a person who has fainted in a safe position, or helping someone who is choking are simple steps that can save a life before the doctor arrives.
    First aid also teaches us to stay calm instead of panicking. Schools can hold short courses with doctors and the Red Cross, keep first-aid kits in every block and run regular practice drills.
    A trained student becomes a helper wherever he or she goes. Learning first aid is not just a lesson; it is a responsibility.

    Marking scheme:

    • Format: a suitable title and the writer's name (byline) (1 mark)
    • Organisation of ideas: an engaging introduction, ideas developed in paragraphs, a conclusion (1 mark)
    • Content: why first aid matters (golden hour, common emergencies with examples) (1 mark)
    • Content: benefits and suggestions (staying calm; how schools can teach it; helping the community) (1 mark)
    • Accuracy of spelling and grammar (1 mark)

  11. Question 17 · 5 marks · Flamingo: The Last Lesson
    Answer in about 120-150 words.
    'The Last Lesson' shows how people value something only when they are about to lose it. Discuss with reference to Franz, the villagers and M. Hamel.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: In 'The Last Lesson', the order from Berlin that only German is to be taught in the schools of Alsace makes everyone value French just as they are losing it.
    Franz, who had not learnt his participles and was tempted to spend the day out of doors, now feels bad about his neglect. M. Hamel does not scold him; he says that all of them have a great deal to reproach themselves with.
    The village elders sit on the back benches. Their presence shows that they too have realised, too late, what they are losing.
    M. Hamel, dressed in his formal clothes for the last lesson, calls French the most beautiful language in the world, the clearest and the most logical. He asks them to guard it, because for an enslaved people their language is the key to their prison.
    The story teaches us to value our language before it is taken away.

    Marking scheme:

    • Content: the order from Berlin; Franz's change - from neglecting his lesson on participles to feeling bad about it (1 mark)
    • Content: the villagers on the back benches - their late realisation of the value of French (1 mark)
    • Content: M. Hamel - formal dress for the last lesson; French the most beautiful, clearest, most logical language, to be guarded as the key to their prison; value it in time (1 mark)
    • Expression: organisation and coherence of ideas (a clear introduction, ideas developed in logical order, a conclusion) (1 mark)

  12. Question 18 · 5 marks · Vistas: The Tiger King
    Answer in about 120-150 words.
    'The Tiger King' is a satire on the arrogance of people who hold absolute power. Discuss with reference to the Maharaja's actions.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Kalki's 'The Tiger King' mocks the vanity of the Maharaja of Pratibandapuram, who believes his power can defeat fate.
    Told that his hundredth tiger will cause his death, he bans tiger hunting by anyone except himself, threatens to confiscate the property of anyone who harms a tiger, and vows to attend to other matters only after killing a hundred tigers.
    He refuses a high-ranking British officer permission to hunt and, to save his throne, sends fifty diamond rings to the officer's wife at a cost of three lakh rupees. He even marries a princess from a state with a large tiger population.
    Yet fate wins. His hundredth tiger, an old animal brought by the dewan, only faints and is killed by one of the hunters. Finally, a wooden toy tiger bought for his son's birthday causes his death.
    The satire shows that absolute power cannot conquer destiny.

    Marking scheme:

    • Content: the prophecy and his arrogant response - ban on tiger hunting for all others, threat of confiscation, vow to kill a hundred tigers (1 mark)
    • Content: misuse of power and wealth - refusing the British officer, fifty diamond rings costing three lakh rupees / marriage for tigers (1 mark)
    • Content: the ironic ending - the hundredth tiger only fainted and was killed by a hunter; death caused by the wooden toy tiger; power cannot defeat fate (1 mark)
    • Expression: organisation and coherence of ideas (a clear introduction, ideas developed in logical order, a conclusion) (1 mark)
    • Expression: accuracy of grammar, spelling and punctuation, within about 120-150 words (1 mark)
