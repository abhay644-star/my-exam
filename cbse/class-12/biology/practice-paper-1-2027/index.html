Section A: 1 mark each

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants
    In a plant species, every plant bears either only male flowers or only female flowers, never both. Which type of pollination is possible in this species?

    1. Autogamy and geitonogamy only
    2. Geitonogamy only
    3. Xenogamy only
    4. Autogamy, geitonogamy and xenogamy
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Xenogamy only

    The plants are dioecious. Pollen of a plant can never reach a stigma of the same flower (autogamy) or of the same plant (geitonogamy), because each plant has flowers of one sex only. Only transfer of pollen to a stigma of a different plant (xenogamy) is possible.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Human Reproduction
    In a woman, ovulation takes place normally in a cycle, but the ruptured follicle fails to change into a corpus luteum. Which change is most likely in that cycle?

    1. The LH surge does not take place.
    2. Progesterone level remains low, so the endometrium is not maintained for implantation.
    3. Growing follicles stop secreting oestrogen during the follicular phase.
    4. The secondary oocyte is not released from the ovary.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Progesterone level remains low, so the endometrium is not maintained for implantation.

    The corpus luteum secretes large amounts of progesterone, which maintains the endometrium for implantation. Without a corpus luteum, progesterone stays low. Ovulation (and so the LH surge and release of the oocyte) has already happened, and oestrogen secretion by follicles happens before ovulation.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Reproductive Health
    A newly married couple wants a reversible method of contraception that also lowers the risk of sexually transmitted infections. Which method best meets both needs?

    1. Lippes loop
    2. Vasectomy
    3. Condoms
    4. Combined oral pills
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Condoms

    Condoms are a barrier method: they stop semen from reaching the female tract, can be stopped at any time, and also protect the user from STIs. The Lippes loop and oral pills give no protection from STIs; vasectomy is a terminal (permanent) method.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Principles of Inheritance and Variation
    In snapdragon, a cross between a red-flowered plant (RR) and a white-flowered plant (rr) gives pink-flowered $\mathrm{F_1}$ plants. The $\mathrm{F_1}$ plants are self-pollinated and 400 $\mathrm{F_2}$ plants are raised. How many $\mathrm{F_2}$ plants are expected to have pink flowers?

    1. 100
    2. 200
    3. 300
    4. 400
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) 200

    This is incomplete dominance. Rr $\times$ Rr gives RR : Rr : rr = 1 : 2 : 1, i.e. red : pink : white = 1 : 2 : 1. Pink (Rr) = $\frac{2}{4}\times 400 = 200$.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Molecular Basis of Inheritance
    In a sample of double-stranded DNA, 18% of the bases are cytosine. What percentage of the bases are adenine?

    1. 18%
    2. 36%
    3. 64%
    4. 32%
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) 32%

    By Chargaff's rule, C = G = 18%, so C + G = 36%. Then A + T = 100 $-$ 36 = 64%, and since A = T, adenine = 32%.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Molecular Basis of Inheritance
    E. coli cells are transferred to a medium in which lactose is the only sugar. What allows the structural genes of the lac operon to be transcribed?

    1. Lactose binds to the repressor protein, so the repressor cannot bind to the operator.
    2. Lactose binds to the operator and directly activates RNA polymerase.
    3. The i gene stops being transcribed in the presence of lactose.
    4. $\beta$-galactosidase binds to the promoter and starts transcription.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Lactose binds to the repressor protein, so the repressor cannot bind to the operator.

    Lactose (the inducer) binds to the repressor and inactivates it. The repressor can then no longer bind to the operator, so RNA polymerase can reach the promoter and transcribe the z, y and a genes. The i gene is transcribed all the time (it is constitutive).

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Evolution
    Which early human had a brain capacity of about 900 cc and probably ate meat?

    1. Australopithecus
    2. Homo habilis
    3. Homo erectus
    4. Neanderthal man
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Homo erectus

    Homo erectus (about 1.5 million years ago) had a large brain of around 900 cc and probably ate meat. Homo habilis had 650-800 cc and probably did not eat meat; Neanderthal man had about 1400 cc.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Human Health and Disease
    A person is bitten by an infected female Anopheles mosquito. Which form of Plasmodium enters the person's body and first multiplies in the liver cells?

    1. Sporozoite
    2. Gametocyte
    3. Merozoite
    4. Zygote
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Sporozoite

    Plasmodium enters the human body as sporozoites (the infectious form) through the bite of an infected female Anopheles. The sporozoites first multiply in liver cells and then attack red blood cells. Gametocytes are taken up by the mosquito; the zygote forms inside the mosquito.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Microbes in Human Welfare
    Milk is turned into curd with the help of lactic acid bacteria (LAB). Besides curdling the milk, these bacteria improve its nutritional quality by increasing the content of:

    1. vitamin A
    2. vitamin C
    3. vitamin K
    4. vitamin $\mathrm{B_{12}}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) vitamin $\mathrm{B_{12}}$

    LAB produce acids that coagulate and partially digest milk proteins, and they also increase the vitamin $\mathrm{B_{12}}$ content of curd.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Biotechnology: Principles and Processes
    The plasmid pBR322 carries genes for resistance to ampicillin and tetracycline. A foreign DNA is ligated at the BamH I site, which lies inside the tetracycline resistance gene. E. coli cells that take up this recombinant plasmid will:

    1. grow on both ampicillin and tetracycline media
    2. grow on ampicillin medium but not on tetracycline medium
    3. grow on tetracycline medium but not on ampicillin medium
    4. grow on neither medium
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) grow on ampicillin medium but not on tetracycline medium

    Insertion of the foreign DNA inactivates the tetracycline resistance gene (insertional inactivation), while the ampicillin resistance gene is still intact. So recombinant cells grow on ampicillin but not on tetracycline.

  11. Question 11 · 1 mark · Biotechnology and its Applications
    Bt cotton plants are protected from bollworms because the Bt toxin protein:

    1. is active inside the bacterium and kills the insect by blocking its respiration
    2. is converted to its active form in the alkaline pH of the insect's gut and creates pores in the midgut cells
    3. is converted to its active form in the acidic pH of the insect's gut and digests the gut wall
    4. is made by the insect itself after it feeds on the cotton leaves
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) is converted to its active form in the alkaline pH of the insect's gut and creates pores in the midgut cells

    The toxin is made as an inactive protoxin. In the alkaline pH of the insect's gut it is solubilised and activated; it binds to the surface of midgut epithelial cells and creates pores, causing the cells to swell and burst, so the insect dies.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Ecosystem
    In a grassland, the producers trap 10,000 J of energy. Following the 10 per cent law, how much of this energy will be available to the secondary consumers?

    1. 100 J
    2. 1000 J
    3. 10 J
    4. 1 J
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) 100 J

    Only about 10% passes to each next trophic level: producers 10,000 J $\rightarrow$ primary consumers 1000 J $\rightarrow$ secondary consumers 100 J.

  13. Question 13 · 1 mark · Human Reproduction
    Assertion (A): In humans, fertilisation can take place only if the ovum and the sperms are transported at the same time to the ampullary region of the fallopian tube.
    Reason (R): The ovum released from the ovary is at the secondary oocyte stage.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.

    Both statements are true. Fertilisation needs the ovum and sperms to meet in the ampulla at the same time, which is why not every coitus leads to fertilisation. The fact that the ovum is released as a secondary oocyte does not explain why they must arrive together, so R is not the correct explanation of A.

  14. Question 14 · 1 mark · Principles of Inheritance and Variation
    Assertion (A): Turner's syndrome is caused by the presence of an extra copy of chromosome 21.
    Reason (R): Failure of segregation of chromatids during cell division can lead to the gain or loss of a chromosome.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) A is false but R is true.

    A is false: Turner's syndrome is caused by the absence of one X chromosome (45, XO); an extra copy of chromosome 21 causes Down's syndrome. R is true: failure of chromatid segregation causes aneuploidy (gain or loss of a chromosome).

  15. Question 15 · 1 mark · Biotechnology: Principles and Processes
    Assertion (A): During agarose gel electrophoresis, DNA fragments move towards the anode.
    Reason (R): DNA is a negatively charged molecule.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.

    Both are true. DNA carries a negative charge (because of its phosphate groups), so in an electric field it moves towards the positive electrode, the anode. R correctly explains A.

  16. Question 16 · 1 mark · Biodiversity and Conservation
    Assertion (A): Sacred groves help in the conservation of biodiversity.
    Reason (R): Sacred groves are areas declared as national parks by the government under wildlife protection laws.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) A is true but R is false.

    A is true: in sacred groves, the trees and wildlife are venerated and given total protection by local communities, so many rare species survive there. R is false: sacred groves are forest patches protected by communities for religious and cultural reasons, not national parks declared by the government.

Section B: 2 marks each

  1. Question 17 · 2 marks · Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants
    A farmer sowed hybrid maize seeds and got a very good crop. The next year he sowed seeds collected from this crop, but the plants were not uniform and the yield fell.
    (a) Explain why the yield fell.
    (b) How would apomixis help the farmer if it could be introduced into the hybrid?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) The seeds collected from a hybrid crop give progeny in which the characters segregate, so the next generation does not keep the hybrid characters; the plants are not uniform and the yield falls. The farmer has to buy fresh, costly hybrid seed every year.
    (b) In apomixis, seeds are formed without fertilisation, so the embryo is genetically the same as the parent. If the hybrid is made apomictic, there is no segregation of characters in the progeny, and the farmer can keep using seeds from his own hybrid crop year after year.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Characters segregate in the progeny of hybrid seeds, so hybrid characters (uniformity, high yield) are not maintained (1 mark)
    • (b) Apomictic seeds form without fertilisation, so there is no segregation; the hybrid seed can be reused year after year (1 mark)

  2. Question 18 · 2 marks · Evolution
    The forelimbs of whales, bats, cheetahs and humans have the same basic pattern of bones but are used for different functions. The wings of a butterfly and of a bird are used for the same function but have different structures.
    Name the type of organs and the type of evolution shown in each case, and state what each case indicates.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Forelimbs of whales, bats, cheetahs and humans: homologous organs, showing divergent evolution. Same structure developed along different directions due to adaptation to different needs; it indicates common ancestry.
    Wings of butterfly and bird: analogous organs, showing convergent evolution. Different structures perform similar functions because of similar habitat or needs; it does not indicate common ancestry.

    Marking scheme:

    • Forelimbs: homologous organs / divergent evolution (0.5); indicate common ancestry (0.5) (1 mark)
    • Wings: analogous organs / convergent evolution (0.5); similar function due to similar habitat or needs, not common ancestry (0.5) (1 mark)

  3. Question 19 · 2 marks · Principles of Inheritance and Variation
    A man with blood group A marries a woman with blood group B. Their first child has blood group O.
    (a) Write the genotypes of the man and the woman.
    (b) What is the probability that their second child will have blood group AB? Show the cross.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) The child (O) has genotype $ii$, so each parent must carry the allele $i$. Man: $I^Ai$; woman: $I^Bi$.
    (b) $I^Ai \times I^Bi$. Gametes of man: $I^A$, $i$; gametes of woman: $I^B$, $i$.
    Offspring: $I^AI^B$ (AB), $I^Ai$ (A), $I^Bi$ (B), $ii$ (O), each $\frac{1}{4}$.
    Probability of blood group AB = $\frac{1}{4}$ (25%).

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Man $I^Ai$ (½ marks)
    • (a) Woman $I^Bi$ (½ marks)
    • (b) Correct cross showing the four genotypes $I^AI^B$, $I^Ai$, $I^Bi$, $ii$ (½ marks)
    • (b) Probability of AB = $\frac{1}{4}$ or 25% (½ marks)

  4. Question 20 · 2 marks · Biotechnology and its Applications
    A four-year-old child with adenosine deaminase (ADA) deficiency is treated by gene therapy. Lymphocytes from the child's blood are grown in culture, a functional ADA cDNA is introduced into them, and the cells are returned to the child.
    (a) Why does the child need this treatment again and again?
    (b) Suggest how this disorder could be cured permanently.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) The genetically engineered lymphocytes are not immortal; they die after some time, so the child needs periodic infusions of such lymphocytes.
    (b) If the gene for ADA, isolated from marrow cells producing ADA, is introduced into cells at an early embryonic stage, it could be a permanent cure.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Engineered lymphocytes are not immortal / have a limited life, so periodic infusion is needed (1 mark)
    • (b) Introducing the ADA gene (from marrow cells producing ADA) into cells at an early embryonic stage (1 mark)

  5. Question 21 · 2 marks · Biotechnology: Principles and Processes
    A student prepared two cloning vectors. Vector P has a selectable marker but no origin of replication (ori). Vector Q has an ori but no selectable marker. State the problem the student will face with each vector after inserting a gene of interest and transforming E. coli.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Vector P: ori is the sequence from where replication starts. Without it, the recombinant DNA cannot replicate in the host cell, so the gene of interest will not be multiplied (copies will not be made).
    Vector Q: a selectable marker helps to identify and eliminate non-transformants and to let only transformants grow. Without it, the student cannot tell transformed cells from non-transformed cells.

    Marking scheme:

    • P: without ori, the recombinant DNA (gene of interest) cannot replicate / be multiplied in the host (1 mark)
    • Q: without a selectable marker, transformants cannot be identified and separated from non-transformants (1 mark)

Section C: 3 marks each

  1. Question 22 · 3 marks · Reproductive Health
    Suggest the most suitable birth control method for each of the following, giving one reason for each choice.
    (a) A newly married woman wants to delay her first pregnancy by about two years.
    (b) A couple with two children has decided not to have any more children.
    (c) A woman had unprotected intercourse 24 hours ago and wants to avoid pregnancy.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) An intra-uterine device such as Copper-T (or oral pills). IUDs are ideal for women who want to delay pregnancy or space children; they are effective and reversible (Cu ions suppress sperm motility and fertilising capacity).
    (b) A surgical (terminal) method: vasectomy in the male or tubectomy in the female. A small part of the vas deferens or fallopian tube is removed or tied, blocking gamete transport; it is a permanent method.
    (c) Emergency contraception: progestogens or progestogen-oestrogen combination pills, or an IUD, within 72 hours of coitus. These are very effective in preventing pregnancy after unprotected intercourse.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) IUD (e.g. Copper-T) or oral pills (0.5), with reason: reversible / ideal for delaying or spacing pregnancy (0.5) (1 mark)
    • (b) Vasectomy or tubectomy (0.5), with reason: permanent / blocks transport of gametes (0.5) (1 mark)
    • (c) Emergency contraceptive pills (progestogens or progestogen-oestrogen) or IUD (0.5), used within 72 hours of coitus (0.5) (1 mark)

  2. Question 23 · 3 marks · Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants
    A compatible pollen grain has germinated on the stigma of a flower.
    (a) Trace the path of the pollen tube from the stigma up to the embryo sac.
    (b) State the role of the filiform apparatus. Predict what would happen if the synergids had no filiform apparatus.
    (c) Name the two fusions that take place inside the embryo sac and state the ploidy of the product of each.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) The pollen tube comes out through a germ pore, grows through the tissues of the stigma and style, reaches the ovary, enters the ovule through the micropyle and then enters one of the synergids.
    (b) The filiform apparatus at the micropylar part of the synergids guides the entry of the pollen tube into the synergid. Without it, the pollen tube would not be guided into the synergid, the male gametes would not be released into the embryo sac and fertilisation (and seed formation) would not occur.
    (c) Syngamy: one male gamete fuses with the egg cell to form the diploid (2n) zygote. Triple fusion: the other male gamete fuses with the two polar nuclei (or the secondary nucleus) to form the triploid (3n) primary endosperm nucleus.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Germ pore $\rightarrow$ stigma and style $\rightarrow$ ovary $\rightarrow$ micropyle of ovule $\rightarrow$ synergid (1 mark)
    • (b) Filiform apparatus guides the pollen tube into the synergid (0.5); without it the male gametes are not delivered and fertilisation fails (0.5) (1 mark)
    • (c) Syngamy forms a diploid (2n) zygote (0.5); triple fusion forms a triploid (3n) primary endosperm nucleus (0.5) (1 mark)

  3. Question 24 · 3 marks · Principles of Inheritance and Variation
    In pea, round seed shape (R) is dominant over wrinkled (r), and yellow seed colour (Y) is dominant over green (y). A plant with round, yellow seeds was crossed with a plant with wrinkled, green seeds. The progeny were:

    PhenotypeNumber
    Round, yellow52
    Round, green49
    Wrinkled, yellow50
    Wrinkled, green49
    (a) Write the genotype of the round, yellow parent. Justify your answer.
    (b) What is this type of cross called, and why is it useful to a breeder?
    (c) Which law of Mendel is shown by this result? Explain.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) RrYy. The wrinkled green parent (rryy) gives only ry gametes. Since all four phenotypes appear in about equal numbers (about 1 : 1 : 1 : 1), the round yellow parent must form four types of gametes, RY, Ry, rY and ry, in equal numbers; so it is heterozygous for both genes.
    (b) Test cross: an organism showing the dominant phenotype is crossed with the recessive parent. It is used to find out whether the dominant-looking organism is homozygous or heterozygous (its genotype).
    (c) Law of independent assortment: when two pairs of traits are combined in a hybrid, segregation of one pair of characters is independent of the other pair. The 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 ratio shows that R/r and Y/y went into gametes independently, giving four kinds of gametes in equal proportion.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) RrYy (0.5), with justification: four phenotypes in about 1 : 1 : 1 : 1, so four types of gametes in equal number (0.5) (1 mark)
    • (b) Test cross (0.5); used to find the unknown genotype (homozygous or heterozygous) of a dominant-phenotype organism (0.5) (1 mark)
    • (c) Law of independent assortment (0.5); the two gene pairs segregate independently, giving four gamete types in equal proportion (0.5) (1 mark)

  4. Question 25 · 3 marks · Evolution
    The table shows hypothetical data on moths collected from tree trunks in an area of England before and after the area became industrialised.

    PeriodWhite-winged moths (per 100 collected)Dark-winged moths (per 100 collected)
    Before industrialisation9010
    After industrialisation1585
    (a) Explain why the number of dark-winged moths increased after industrialisation.
    (b) Name the type of natural selection shown here. Justify your answer.
    (c) Predict what would happen to the two types of moths if the air became clean again and lichens grew back on the tree trunks. Give a reason.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) After industrialisation, smoke and soot darkened the tree trunks and killed the lichens. Dark-winged moths were camouflaged on the dark trunks, while white-winged moths were easily seen and eaten by predators (birds). So more dark moths survived and reproduced.
    (b) Directional selection: one extreme phenotype (dark wings) is favoured, so the population shifts in one direction from white-winged to dark-winged.
    (c) White-winged moths would increase again (and dark-winged moths would decrease), because on light, lichen-covered trunks white moths are camouflaged while dark moths are easily picked out by predators.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Trunks darkened by soot (no lichens); dark moths camouflaged and escaped predators, so they survived and reproduced more (1 mark)
    • (b) Directional selection (0.5); one extreme form (dark) favoured, population shifts in one direction (0.5) (1 mark)
    • (c) White-winged moths increase (0.5); they are camouflaged on lichen-covered trunks while dark moths are eaten by predators (0.5) (1 mark)

  5. Question 26 · 3 marks · Microbes in Human Welfare
    A village cooperative sets up a biogas plant that uses cattle dung.
    (a) Name the group of bacteria that produce biogas and state the condition in which they work.
    (b) Why is cattle dung a good input for the plant?
    (c) State one use of the gas and one use of the spent slurry.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Methanogens, e.g. Methanobacterium. They grow anaerobically (in the absence of oxygen) on cellulosic material and produce large amounts of methane along with $\mathrm{CO_2}$ and $\mathrm{H_2}$.
    (b) Methanogens are present in the rumen of cattle, where they help to break down cellulose. So cattle dung (gobar) is rich in these bacteria, and it also supplies the organic matter they act on.
    (c) The gas (mainly methane) is used for cooking and lighting. The spent slurry is used as a fertiliser (manure).

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Methanogens / Methanobacterium (0.5); anaerobic conditions (0.5) (1 mark)
    • (b) Dung is rich in methanogens (from the cattle rumen) and supplies organic matter (1 mark)
    • (c) Gas for cooking or lighting (0.5); slurry as fertiliser (0.5) (1 mark)

  6. Question 27 · 3 marks · Human Health and Disease
    State whether each of the following gives active immunity or passive immunity. Give a reason in each case.
    (a) A newborn baby fed with colostrum.
    (b) A child given oral polio vaccine.
    (c) A snake-bite victim injected with antivenom.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Passive immunity: colostrum contains ready-made antibodies (IgA) that protect the baby; the baby's own body does not make them.
    (b) Active immunity: the vaccine introduces inactivated or weakened pathogens (antigens), and the child's body produces its own antibodies and memory B and T cells.
    (c) Passive immunity: antivenom contains preformed antibodies against the snake venom, which act at once, as a quick response is needed.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Passive (0.5); ready-made antibodies (IgA) in colostrum (0.5) (1 mark)
    • (b) Active (0.5); body makes its own antibodies and memory cells in response to the antigen (0.5) (1 mark)
    • (c) Passive (0.5); preformed antibodies given for a quick response (0.5) (1 mark)

  7. Question 28 · 3 marks · Organisms and Populations
    Name the type of population interaction in each case below and state its effect (benefit, harm or no effect) on each of the two species.
    (a) Cuscuta growing on hedge plants.
    (b) Clownfish living among the stinging tentacles of a sea anemone.
    (c) A fungus and a photosynthesising alga living together as a lichen.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Parasitism: Cuscuta benefits (gets nutrition from the host); the host plant is harmed.
    (b) Commensalism: the clownfish benefits (gets protection from predators); the sea anemone is neither harmed nor benefited.
    (c) Mutualism: both species benefit; the fungus helps the alga by absorbing nutrients and providing protection, while the alga prepares food for the fungus by photosynthesis.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Parasitism (0.5); Cuscuta benefits, host harmed (0.5) (1 mark)
    • (b) Commensalism (0.5); clownfish benefits, sea anemone unaffected (0.5) (1 mark)
    • (c) Mutualism (0.5); both fungus and alga benefit (0.5) (1 mark)

Section D: case-based, 4 marks each

  1. Question 29 · 4 marks · Human Reproduction
    A biology teacher shows her class the relative levels of four hormones in the blood of a woman with a regular 28-day menstrual cycle (Day 1 is the first day of menstruation).

    DayFSHLHOestrogenProgesterone
    3moderatelowlowlow
    12moderaterisinghighlow
    14peakpeakfallinglow
    21lowlowmoderatehigh
    27lowlowlowlow

    (i) On which day is ovulation most likely to take place? Name the hormone whose surge causes it. [1]

    (ii) Explain why the progesterone level is high around Day 21. What would happen to this level, and to the endometrium, if fertilisation and implantation took place? [2]

    (iii) Why does menstruation begin at the end of the cycle when the ovum is not fertilised? [1]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) On which day is ovulation most likely to take place? Name the hormone whose surge causes it.

    Model answer: Ovulation is most likely on about Day 14. It is caused by the LH surge (peak of LH in the middle of the cycle), which induces rupture of the Graafian follicle.

    Marking scheme:

    • Day 14 (middle of the cycle) (½ marks)
    • LH surge (½ marks)

    (ii) Explain why the progesterone level is high around Day 21. What would happen to this level, and to the endometrium, if fertilisation and implantation took place?

    Model answer: After ovulation, the remaining part of the Graafian follicle changes into the corpus luteum, which secretes large amounts of progesterone (luteal phase). Progesterone maintains the thickened endometrium, needed for implantation.
    If fertilisation and implantation take place, the corpus luteum is maintained, so progesterone stays high. The endometrium is maintained, menstruation does not occur and the pregnancy continues.

    Marking scheme:

    • Corpus luteum (formed from the ruptured follicle) secretes progesterone (½ marks)
    • Progesterone maintains the endometrium for implantation (½ marks)
    • In pregnancy the progesterone level stays high (corpus luteum maintained) (½ marks)
    • Endometrium is maintained, so menstruation does not occur (½ marks)

    (iii) Why does menstruation begin at the end of the cycle when the ovum is not fertilised?

    Model answer: In the absence of fertilisation, the corpus luteum degenerates and the progesterone level falls (as seen on Day 27). Without progesterone the endometrium cannot be maintained, so it breaks down and menstruation begins.

    Marking scheme:

    • Corpus luteum degenerates, so progesterone falls (½ marks)
    • Endometrium breaks down, causing menstrual flow (½ marks)

  2. Question 30 · 4 marks · Human Health and Disease
    Ravi, a 30-year-old man, has had fever, diarrhoea and weight loss for several months and catches one infection after another. A doctor advises an ELISA test, which shows that he is infected with HIV. The doctor explains that HIV is a retrovirus with an RNA genome, and that it slowly weakens the immune system over several years.

    (i) Name the type of white blood cells whose number falls steadily in a person infected with HIV. [1]

    (ii) Describe how HIV multiplies inside the human body after it enters. [2]

    (iii) Explain why Ravi suffers from infections by organisms such as Mycobacterium and Toxoplasma, which a healthy person can fight off easily. [1]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Name the type of white blood cells whose number falls steadily in a person infected with HIV.

    Model answer: Helper T-lymphocytes ($\mathrm{T_H}$ cells).

    Marking scheme:

    • Helper T-lymphocytes ($\mathrm{T_H}$ cells) (1 mark)

    (ii) Describe how HIV multiplies inside the human body after it enters.

    Model answer: The virus first enters macrophages. There, its RNA genome is copied into viral DNA by the enzyme reverse transcriptase. The viral DNA gets incorporated into the host cell's DNA and directs the infected cell to produce virus particles; the macrophages keep producing virus and act like an HIV factory. At the same time HIV enters helper T-lymphocytes, replicates and produces progeny viruses, which attack other helper T cells.

    Marking scheme:

    • Virus enters macrophages (½ marks)
    • RNA genome copied into viral DNA by reverse transcriptase (½ marks)
    • Viral DNA is incorporated into host DNA; infected cells (macrophages) produce virus particles and act as an HIV factory (½ marks)
    • HIV enters helper T cells, replicates and the progeny viruses attack more helper T cells (½ marks)

    (iii) Explain why Ravi suffers from infections by organisms such as Mycobacterium and Toxoplasma, which a healthy person can fight off easily.

    Model answer: As the number of helper T-lymphocytes falls, the person's immunity breaks down (immunodeficiency). He can no longer produce a proper immune response even against these organisms, so he suffers from these infections.

    Marking scheme:

    • Fall in helper T cells causes immunodeficiency, so the body cannot fight even common infections (1 mark)

  1. Question 31 · 5 marks · Molecular Basis of Inheritance
    A short stretch of the coding strand of a bacterial gene is given below (the triplets are separated by spaces for ease of reading):
    5'- ATG GCA TTC AAG CCG TGA -3'

    (i) Write the sequence of the template strand of this stretch, showing its polarity. [1]

    (ii) Write the sequence of the mRNA transcribed from this stretch, showing its polarity. [1]

    (iii) How many amino acids will the polypeptide coded by this mRNA have? Give a reason. [1]

    (iv) A single base substitution in the coding strand changes AAG to TAG. Predict its effect on the polypeptide. [1]

    (v) The coding strand does not code for anything, yet it is used as the reference strand. Towards which end of the coding strand is the promoter located, and why is this strand called the coding strand? [1]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Write the sequence of the template strand of this stretch, showing its polarity.

    Model answer: 3'- TAC CGT AAG TTC GGC ACT -5'

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct complementary bases TAC CGT AAG TTC GGC ACT (½ marks)
    • Polarity shown as 3' to 5' (½ marks)

    (ii) Write the sequence of the mRNA transcribed from this stretch, showing its polarity.

    Model answer: 5'- AUG GCA UUC AAG CCG UGA -3'

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct bases with U in place of T: AUG GCA UUC AAG CCG UGA (½ marks)
    • Polarity shown as 5' to 3' (½ marks)

    (iii) How many amino acids will the polypeptide coded by this mRNA have? Give a reason.

    Model answer: 5 amino acids. AUG is the start codon (codes for methionine) and the next four codons each code for one amino acid. UGA is a stop codon and does not code for any amino acid.

    Marking scheme:

    • 5 amino acids (½ marks)
    • UGA is a stop codon that codes for no amino acid (AUG start codes for the first) (½ marks)

    (iv) A single base substitution in the coding strand changes AAG to TAG. Predict its effect on the polypeptide.

    Model answer: The fourth codon of the mRNA becomes UAG, which is a stop codon. Translation stops early, so a shorter polypeptide of only 3 amino acids is formed (which is likely to be non-functional).

    Marking scheme:

    • Fourth mRNA codon becomes UAG, a stop codon (½ marks)
    • Translation ends early: polypeptide of only 3 amino acids (½ marks)

    (v) The coding strand does not code for anything, yet it is used as the reference strand. Towards which end of the coding strand is the promoter located, and why is this strand called the coding strand?

    Model answer: The promoter is located towards the 5' end (upstream) of the structural gene, with reference to the coding strand. The strand is called the coding strand because its sequence is the same as that of the mRNA (except that it has thymine in place of uracil).

    Marking scheme:

    • Promoter towards the 5' (upstream) end of the coding strand (½ marks)
    • Its sequence is the same as the mRNA (T in place of U) (½ marks)

  2. Question 32 · 5 marks · Biotechnology: Principles and Processes
    A forensic laboratory receives a hair root from a crime scene. It contains only a very small amount of DNA, too little for analysis. The scientists decide to make millions of copies of a chosen region of this DNA in the laboratory.

    (i) Name the technique the scientists will use. Starting from one double-stranded DNA molecule, how many copies of the target region will be obtained after 5 cycles of this technique? [1]

    (ii) Describe the three steps of one cycle of this technique. [3]

    (iii) Why is a DNA polymerase from the bacterium Thermus aquaticus used in this technique? [1]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Name the technique the scientists will use. Starting from one double-stranded DNA molecule, how many copies of the target region will be obtained after 5 cycles of this technique?

    Model answer: Polymerase chain reaction (PCR). The number of copies doubles in each cycle, so after 5 cycles: $2^5 = 32$ copies.

    Marking scheme:

    • Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) (½ marks)
    • $2^5 = 32$ copies (½ marks)

    (ii) Describe the three steps of one cycle of this technique.

    Model answer: 1. Denaturation: the double-stranded DNA is heated to a high temperature so that the two strands separate.
    2. Annealing: the reaction is cooled so that two sets of primers (small, chemically synthesised oligonucleotides complementary to the ends of the region) bind to the single strands.
    3. Extension: DNA polymerase extends the primers using the nucleotides provided and the DNA as template, making new complementary strands.
    The cycle is repeated many times to amplify the region.

    Marking scheme:

    • Denaturation: heating separates the two strands of DNA (1 mark)
    • Annealing: primers bind to the complementary regions of the single strands (1 mark)
    • Extension: DNA polymerase extends the primers using nucleotides, copying the template (1 mark)

    (iii) Why is a DNA polymerase from the bacterium Thermus aquaticus used in this technique?

    Model answer: Taq polymerase, isolated from Thermus aquaticus, is thermostable. It stays active during the high temperature used for denaturation in every cycle, so fresh enzyme need not be added after each cycle.

    Marking scheme:

    • Taq polymerase is thermostable / remains active at the high temperature of denaturation (1 mark)

  3. Question 33 · 5 marks · Biodiversity and Conservation
    An ecologist counted the resident bird species in isolated forest patches of different sizes, separated from one another by farmland, in a hilly region. The hypothetical data are given below.

    Area of forest patch ($\mathrm{km^2}$)1101001000
    Number of bird species204080160

    (i) Name the scientist who first described this kind of relationship between species richness and area, and write the relationship in its logarithmic form. [1]

    (ii) Using the data for the 1 $\mathrm{km^2}$ and 10 $\mathrm{km^2}$ patches, calculate the value of Z. (Take $\log 2 = 0.30$) [2]

    (iii) If the 1000 $\mathrm{km^2}$ forest is cut into many small, separate patches, predict what will happen to the number of bird species. Name this threat to biodiversity. [1]

    (iv) A plant species that the birds depend on for food becomes extinct in the region. Explain why some insects that live only on this plant may also become extinct. Name the phenomenon. [1]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Name the scientist who first described this kind of relationship between species richness and area, and write the relationship in its logarithmic form.

    Model answer: Alexander von Humboldt. $\log S = \log C + Z \log A$, where S = species richness, A = area, Z = slope of the line (regression coefficient) and C = Y-intercept.

    Marking scheme:

    • Alexander von Humboldt (½ marks)
    • $\log S = \log C + Z \log A$ (with S and A identified) (½ marks)

    (ii) Using the data for the 1 $\mathrm{km^2}$ and 10 $\mathrm{km^2}$ patches, calculate the value of Z. (Take $\log 2 = 0.30$)

    Model answer: $Z = \frac{\log S_2 - \log S_1}{\log A_2 - \log A_1} = \frac{\log 40 - \log 20}{\log 10 - \log 1}$
    $= \frac{\log 2}{1 - 0} = 0.30$
    (The same value is obtained for every pair of neighbouring patches, since species double for every tenfold increase in area.)

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct expression: $Z = \frac{\log 40 - \log 20}{\log 10 - \log 1}$ (1 mark)
    • Simplified to $\frac{\log 2}{1}$ giving Z = 0.30 (1 mark)

    (iii) If the 1000 $\mathrm{km^2}$ forest is cut into many small, separate patches, predict what will happen to the number of bird species. Name this threat to biodiversity.

    Model answer: The number of bird species will fall, because smaller patches support fewer species; species that need large territories (and migratory birds) are badly affected and their populations decline. The threat is habitat loss and fragmentation.

    Marking scheme:

    • Number of bird species decreases (species needing large territories decline) (½ marks)
    • Habitat loss and fragmentation (½ marks)

    (iv) A plant species that the birds depend on for food becomes extinct in the region. Explain why some insects that live only on this plant may also become extinct. Name the phenomenon.

    Model answer: When a species becomes extinct, the plant and animal species associated with it in an obligatory way also become extinct. The insects that depend only on this plant lose their food and habitat and die out. This is called co-extinction.

    Marking scheme:

    • Insects depend obligatorily on that plant, so they also die out (½ marks)
    • Co-extinction (½ marks)
