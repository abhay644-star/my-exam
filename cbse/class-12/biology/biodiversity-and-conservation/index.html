  1. The term 'biodiversity' was popularised by the sociobiologist ______.

    1. David Tilman
    2. Robert May
    3. Paul Ehrlich
    4. Edward Wilson
    Show answer

    Answer: Edward Wilson

    Edward Wilson popularised the term biodiversity to describe the combined diversity at all levels of biological organisation.

  2. The 'rivet popper hypothesis', which compares species in an ecosystem to rivets in an airplane, was proposed by the ecologist ______.

    1. Paul Ehrlich
    2. Edward Wilson
    3. Robert May
    4. David Tilman
    Show answer

    Answer: Paul Ehrlich

    Stanford ecologist Paul Ehrlich used the rivet popper analogy to explain how loss of species weakens an ecosystem.

  3. For a region the species–area relationship is S = C A^Z with Z = 0.2. If the area studied is increased 32 times, by what factor does the species richness increase?

    1. 4
    2. 2
    3. 32
    4. 6.4
    Show answer

    Answer: 2

    S2/S1 = (A2/A1)^Z = 32^0.2 = (2⁵)^0.2 = 2.

  4. The German naturalist and geographer who observed that within a region species richness increases with increasing explored area, but only up to a limit, was ______.

    1. Ernst Mayr
    2. Robert May
    3. Charles Darwin
    4. Alexander von Humboldt
    Show answer

    Answer: Alexander von Humboldt

    Alexander von Humboldt, during his explorations of South American jungles, gave the species–area relationship.

  5. The Amazon rain forest, called the 'lungs of the planet', produces roughly what percentage of the total oxygen in the Earth's atmosphere?

    1. 35 %
    2. 20 %
    3. 10 %
    4. 50 %
    Show answer

    Answer: 20 %

    NCERT states the Amazon forest produces, through photosynthesis, about 20 per cent of the total oxygen in the Earth's atmosphere.

  6. India has only 2.4% of the world's land area. What is its share (in %) of the global species diversity?

    1. 12.5 %
    2. 8.1 %
    3. 2.4 %
    4. 4.8 %
    Show answer

    Answer: 8.1 %

    India's share of global species diversity is an impressive 8.1%, making it one of the 12 mega diversity countries.

  7. According to the IUCN Red List (2004), how many species have been documented as becoming extinct in the last 500 years?

    1. 784
    2. 87
    3. 338
    4. 359
    Show answer

    Answer: 784

    The IUCN Red List (2004) documents 784 extinctions in the last 500 years: 338 vertebrates, 359 invertebrates and 87 plants.

  8. When a host fish species becomes extinct, the unique parasites associated with it also become extinct. This cause of biodiversity loss is called ______.

    1. habitat fragmentation
    2. co-extinction
    3. over-exploitation
    4. alien species invasion
    Show answer

    Answer: co-extinction

    Co-extinction: when a species becomes extinct, the plant and animal species obligatorily associated with it (e.g. its parasites) also become extinct.
