A few questions from this chapter. Open each one to see the answer.

  1. The hard outer layer of the pollen grain wall, made of sporopollenin, is called the ______.

    1. endothecium
    2. tapetum
    3. intine
    4. exine
    Show answer

    Answer: exine

    Exine is made of sporopollenin, one of the most resistant organic materials known; the inner wall is the intine.

  2. The innermost wall layer of the microsporangium, which nourishes the developing pollen grains, is the ______.

    1. endothecium
    2. epidermis
    3. middle layer
    4. tapetum
    Show answer

    Answer: tapetum

    Tapetal cells are dense, often multinucleate, and nourish the developing pollen grains.

  3. The megaspore mother cell of a flowering plant has 24 chromosomes. How many chromosomes are present in a synergid cell?

    1. 36
    2. 6
    3. 12
    4. 24
    Show answer

    Answer: 12

    The MMC is diploid; after meiosis the embryo sac cells, including synergids, are haploid, so 24 ÷ 2 = 12.

  4. The part of the pistil that serves as the landing platform for pollen grains is the ______.

    1. style
    2. ovary
    3. anther
    4. stigma
    Show answer

    Answer: stigma

    Each pistil has a stigma, a style and an ovary; pollen grains land on the stigma.

  5. Development of fruits without fertilisation, as in banana, is called ______.

    1. parthenogenesis
    2. apomixis
    3. parthenocarpy
    4. polyembryony
    Show answer

    Answer: parthenocarpy

    Parthenocarpic fruits are seedless; parthenocarpy can also be induced by growth hormones.

  6. The occurrence of more than one embryo in a seed, as in many Citrus and mango varieties, is called ______.

    1. parthenocarpy
    2. apomixis
    3. polyspermy
    4. polyembryony
    Show answer

    Answer: polyembryony

    Some nucellar cells divide and protrude into the embryo sac to form extra embryos, giving polyembryony.

  7. Pollination by wind is called ______.

    1. entomophily
    2. hydrophily
    3. anemophily
    4. ornithophily
    Show answer

    Answer: anemophily

    Wind-pollinated flowers produce light, non-sticky pollen in large numbers and often have feathery stigmas.

  8. Transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of the same flower is called ______.

    1. geitonogamy
    2. xenogamy
    3. autogamy
    4. apomixis
    Show answer

    Answer: autogamy

    In autogamy pollination is achieved within the same flower.
