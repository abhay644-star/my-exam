  1. In the Geiger–Marsden alpha-particle scattering experiment, alpha particles were directed at a thin foil of ______.

    1. gold
    2. copper
    3. silver
    4. aluminium
    Show answer

    Answer: gold

    A thin gold foil (about 2.1 × 10^-7 m thick) was used; most alpha particles passed through but a few were scattered through large angles.

  2. According to Bohr's postulate, the angular momentum of the electron in a stationary orbit is an integral multiple of ______.

    1. 2πh
    2. h/2π
    3. h/π
    4. h
    Show answer

    Answer: h/2π

    Bohr's second postulate: L = nh/(2π), i.e. an integral multiple of h/2π.

  3. What is the energy of the photon emitted when the electron in a hydrogen atom jumps from n = 2 to n = 1?

    1. 10.2 eV
    2. 3.4 eV
    3. 13.6 eV
    4. 12.09 eV
    Show answer

    Answer: 10.2 eV

    ΔE = E₂ − E₁ = (−3.4) − (−13.6) = 10.2 eV.

  4. Using the Rydberg constant R = 1.097 × 10^7 m^-1, find the wavelength of the first line (Hα) of the Balmer series of hydrogen, to the nearest nm.

    1. 122 nm
    2. 656 nm
    3. 486 nm
    4. 434 nm
    Show answer

    Answer: 656 nm

    1/λ = R(1/2² − 1/3²) = 5R/36, so λ = 36/(5 × 1.097 × 10^7) ≈ 6.56 × 10^-7 m = 656 nm.

  5. What is the energy of the electron in the ground state of a hydrogen atom?

    1. 13.6 eV
    2. -13.6 eV
    3. -3.4 eV
    4. -1.51 eV
    Show answer

    Answer: -13.6 eV

    E_n = −13.6/n² eV; for n = 1, E = −13.6 eV (negative because the electron is bound).

  6. The radius of the first Bohr orbit of hydrogen is 0.53 Å. What is the radius of the n = 3 orbit?

    1. 4.77 Å
    2. 1.59 Å
    3. 0.53 Å
    4. 2.12 Å
    Show answer

    Answer: 4.77 Å

    r_n = n² a₀, so r₃ = 9 × 0.53 Å = 4.77 Å.

  7. Hydrogen atoms are excited to the n = 4 level. What is the maximum number of different spectral lines that can be emitted as they return to the ground state?

    1. 6
    2. 3
    3. 4
    4. 10
    Show answer

    Answer: 6

    Number of possible transitions = n(n − 1)/2 = 4 × 3/2 = 6.

  8. What is the ratio of the longest wavelength of the Lyman series to the longest wavelength of the Balmer series of hydrogen? Give your answer to three decimal places.

    1. 0.75
    2. 0.185
    3. 0.25
    4. 5.4
    Show answer

    Answer: 0.185

    Lyman longest: 1/λ = R(1 − 1/4) = 3R/4; Balmer longest: 1/λ = 5R/36. Ratio λ_L/λ_B = (5/36)/(3/4) = 5/27 ≈ 0.185.
