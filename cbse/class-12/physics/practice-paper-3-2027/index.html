Questions

Section A: 1 mark each

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Electric Charges and Fields
    An electric dipole of dipole moment $4\times10^{-9}$ C m is placed in a uniform electric field of $5\times10^{4}$ N C$^{-1}$. Its axis makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the field. The magnitude of the torque on the dipole is

    1. $2\times10^{-4}$ N m
    2. $1\times10^{-4}$ N m
    3. $1.7\times10^{-4}$ N m
    4. zero
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) $1\times10^{-4}$ N m

    $\tau = pE\sin\theta = 4\times10^{-9}\times5\times10^{4}\times\sin30^\circ = 2\times10^{-4}\times0.5 = 1\times10^{-4}$ N m.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
    A point charge of $+2$ nC is fixed at a point O. How much work must an external agent do to move a charge of $+1\ \mu$C slowly from a point 20 cm from O to a point 10 cm from O?

    1. $-9\times10^{-5}$ J
    2. zero
    3. $9\times10^{-5}$ J
    4. $1.8\times10^{-4}$ J
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $9\times10^{-5}$ J

    $V_{20} = \frac{9\times10^9\times2\times10^{-9}}{0.2} = 90$ V and $V_{10} = 180$ V. $W = q(V_{10}-V_{20}) = 10^{-6}\times90 = 9\times10^{-5}$ J (positive, since a positive charge is pushed towards a positive charge).

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Current Electricity
    The free electrons in a metal wire have a drift speed of $1.5\times10^{-4}$ m s$^{-1}$ when the electric field in the wire is 0.05 V m$^{-1}$. The mobility of the electrons is

    1. $3\times10^{-3}$ m$^2$ V$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$
    2. $7.5\times10^{-6}$ m$^2$ V$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$
    3. $3.3\times10^{2}$ m$^2$ V$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$
    4. $3\times10^{-5}$ m$^2$ V$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $3\times10^{-3}$ m$^2$ V$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$

    Mobility $\mu = \frac{v_d}{E} = \frac{1.5\times10^{-4}}{0.05} = 3\times10^{-3}$ m$^2$ V$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Current Electricity
    A wire of resistance $R$ is stretched uniformly until its length becomes twice its original length. Its resistance now is

    1. $R/2$
    2. $2R$
    3. $R$
    4. $4R$
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) $4R$

    The volume stays the same, so when the length doubles the area becomes half. $R=\rho l/A$, so $R' = \rho(2l)/(A/2) = 4R$.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Moving Charges and Magnetism
    A proton moves in a region where a uniform electric field of $3\times10^{4}$ V m$^{-1}$ and a uniform magnetic field of 0.02 T are perpendicular to each other and to the velocity of the proton. The proton passes through without any deflection. Its speed is

    1. $6\times10^{2}$ m s$^{-1}$
    2. $1.5\times10^{6}$ m s$^{-1}$
    3. $6.7\times10^{-7}$ m s$^{-1}$
    4. $1.5\times10^{4}$ m s$^{-1}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) $1.5\times10^{6}$ m s$^{-1}$

    No deflection when the electric and magnetic forces balance: $qE = qvB$, so $v = E/B = 3\times10^{4}/0.02 = 1.5\times10^{6}$ m s$^{-1}$.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Electromagnetic Induction
    The mutual inductance of a pair of coils placed close to each other does NOT depend on

    1. the number of turns in each coil
    2. the distance between the coils and their relative orientation
    3. the current flowing in the coils
    4. the material of the core on which the coils are wound
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) the current flowing in the coils

    Mutual inductance depends only on the geometry of the coils (number of turns, size, separation and relative orientation) and on the permeability of the core, for example $M = \mu_r\mu_0n_1n_2\pi r_1^2l$ for two coaxial solenoids. It does not depend on the current in the coils.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Alternating Current
    A capacitor of capacitance $\frac{100}{\pi}\ \mu$F is connected to a 220 V, 50 Hz a.c. supply. The capacitive reactance is

    1. $10^{-2}\ \Omega$
    2. $50\ \Omega$
    3. $200\ \Omega$
    4. $100\ \Omega$
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) $100\ \Omega$

    $X_C = \frac{1}{2\pi fC} = \frac{1}{2\pi\times50\times\frac{100}{\pi}\times10^{-6}} = \frac{1}{10^{-2}} = 100\ \Omega$.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Magnetism and Matter
    Which of the following statements about the magnetic field lines of a bar magnet is correct?

    1. They start from the north pole and end at the south pole, and do not exist inside the magnet.
    2. They form continuous closed loops; inside the magnet they run from the south pole to the north pole.
    3. Two field lines can cross each other at a neutral point.
    4. Inside the magnet they run from the north pole to the south pole.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) They form continuous closed loops; inside the magnet they run from the south pole to the north pole.

    Magnetic field lines always form closed loops (there are no isolated magnetic poles). Outside the magnet they run from N to S and inside it from S to N. They never cross, since the field has only one direction at a point.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Electromagnetic Waves
    Some water purifiers use a lamp that gives out radiation which kills germs in the water. This radiation is

    1. Ultraviolet rays
    2. Infrared rays
    3. Microwaves
    4. Radio waves
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Ultraviolet rays

    Ultraviolet radiation is absorbed by and damages the genetic material of bacteria and other germs, so UV lamps are used in water purifiers.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Ray Optics and Optical Instruments
    A coin lies at the bottom of a tank filled with water (refractive index $\frac{4}{3}$) to a depth of 20 cm. When viewed from directly above, the coin appears to be raised by

    1. 15 cm
    2. 26.7 cm
    3. 5 cm
    4. 6.7 cm
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) 5 cm

    Apparent depth $= \frac{\text{real depth}}{n} = \frac{20}{4/3} = 15$ cm. The coin appears raised by $20-15 = 5$ cm.

  11. Question 11 · 1 mark · Wave Optics
    In Young's double-slit experiment, the monochromatic light is replaced by white light. What is seen on the screen?

    1. All the fringes are white.
    2. The central fringe is dark and the other fringes are coloured.
    3. No fringes are seen at all.
    4. The central fringe is white, and a few coloured fringes are seen on either side of it.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) The central fringe is white, and a few coloured fringes are seen on either side of it.

    At the centre the path difference is zero for every colour, so all colours form a bright fringe there and the central fringe is white. Away from the centre the fringe width differs for different colours, so a few coloured fringes are seen, after which the colours overlap and the screen looks uniformly lit.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Wave Optics
    In Young's double-slit experiment with light of wavelength 500 nm, the path difference between the light reaching a point P on the screen from the two slits is 0.75 $\mu$m. The point P lies on the

    1. third bright fringe
    2. second dark fringe
    3. first dark fringe
    4. second bright fringe
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) second dark fringe

    Path difference $= \frac{750\text{ nm}}{500\text{ nm}}\lambda = 1.5\lambda$. Dark fringes occur at $0.5\lambda, 1.5\lambda, 2.5\lambda, \ldots$, so P is on the second dark fringe.

  13. Question 13 · 1 mark · Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter
    Given below are two statements: one labelled Assertion (A) and the other labelled Reason (R). Select the correct answer from the options given below.
    Assertion (A): The matter waves associated with a moving electron are not electromagnetic in nature.
    Reason (R): Matter waves are associated only with charged particles.

    1. Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
    2. Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation of Assertion.
    3. Assertion is true but Reason is false.
    4. Both Assertion and Reason are false.
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Assertion is true but Reason is false.

    A is true: matter waves are not produced by oscillating electric and magnetic fields; they describe the moving particle itself. R is false: every moving particle, charged or neutral (for example a neutron), has a de Broglie wavelength $\lambda = h/p$.

  14. Question 14 · 1 mark · Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
    Given below are two statements: one labelled Assertion (A) and the other labelled Reason (R). Select the correct answer from the options given below.
    Assertion (A): At a point where the electric field is zero, the electric potential must also be zero.
    Reason (R): The electric field is the negative gradient of the potential, so a zero field means a zero potential.

    1. Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
    2. Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation of Assertion.
    3. Assertion is true but Reason is false.
    4. Both Assertion and Reason are false.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Both Assertion and Reason are false.

    Both are false. $E = -\frac{dV}{dr}$, so a zero field means the potential does not change near that point (it is constant), not that it is zero. For example, inside a charged hollow conducting sphere $E=0$ but $V = \frac{kQ}{R} \neq 0$.

  15. Question 15 · 1 mark · Ray Optics and Optical Instruments
    Given below are two statements: one labelled Assertion (A) and the other labelled Reason (R). Select the correct answer from the options given below.
    Assertion (A): The focal length of a concave mirror does not change when the mirror is immersed in water.
    Reason (R): The focal length of a spherical mirror depends only on its radius of curvature, $f = R/2$.

    1. Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
    2. Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation of Assertion.
    3. Assertion is true but Reason is false.
    4. Both Assertion and Reason are false.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.

    Reflection does not depend on the medium in front of the mirror; $f = R/2$ involves no refractive index. So the focal length stays the same in water, and R explains A.

  16. Question 16 · 1 mark · Semiconductor Electronics
    Given below are two statements: one labelled Assertion (A) and the other labelled Reason (R). Select the correct answer from the options given below.
    Assertion (A): At 0 K, a pure semiconductor such as silicon behaves as an insulator.
    Reason (R): At 0 K, the valence band of a pure semiconductor is completely filled and its conduction band is completely empty.

    1. Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
    2. Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation of Assertion.
    3. Assertion is true but Reason is false.
    4. Both Assertion and Reason are false.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.

    At 0 K no electron has enough thermal energy to cross the band gap, so the conduction band is empty and there are no free charge carriers. Hence the semiconductor behaves as an insulator, and R explains A.

Section B: 2 marks each

  1. Question 17 · 2 marks · Electromagnetic Waves
    (i) Name the part of the electromagnetic spectrum that (a) is given out by radioactive nuclei, and (b) is used for AM and FM radio broadcasting.
    (ii) What is the source of electromagnetic waves? Explain briefly how this source produces them.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) (a) Gamma rays. (b) Radio waves.
    (ii) An accelerated (for example an oscillating) charge. An oscillating charge produces an oscillating electric field. This changing electric field produces an oscillating magnetic field, which in turn produces an oscillating electric field, and so on. The two fields keep producing each other and travel through space as an electromagnetic wave whose frequency equals the frequency of oscillation of the charge.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i)(a) Gamma rays (½ marks)
    • (i)(b) Radio waves (½ marks)
    • (ii) Source: an accelerated (oscillating) charge (½ marks)
    • (ii) Oscillating electric and magnetic fields produce each other and travel through space (½ marks)

  2. Question 18 · 2 marks · Semiconductor Electronics
    Draw the energy band diagram, at a temperature above 0 K, of (i) an n-type semiconductor and (ii) a p-type semiconductor. Show the position of the donor or acceptor energy level in each.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) n-type: the conduction band (above) and the valence band (below) are separated by the band gap $E_g$ (about 1.1 eV for Si). A donor energy level $E_D$ is drawn just below the bottom of the conduction band (about 0.01 to 0.05 eV below it). Electrons from the donor level move up into the conduction band, so the conduction band has many electrons and the valence band has a few holes.
    (ii) p-type: same bands, with an acceptor energy level $E_A$ drawn just above the top of the valence band. Electrons from the valence band move up into the acceptor level, leaving many holes in the valence band; the conduction band has only a few electrons.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Conduction band, valence band and band gap labelled (½ marks)
    • (i) Donor level $E_D$ shown just below the conduction band (½ marks)
    • (ii) Conduction band, valence band and band gap labelled (½ marks)
    • (ii) Acceptor level $E_A$ shown just above the valence band (½ marks)

  3. Question 19 · 2 marks · Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter
    In an experiment on the photoelectric effect, the photocurrent is measured while the potential of the collector plate (anode) is changed. Draw graphs showing how the photocurrent varies with the collector plate potential
    (i) for two different intensities of light of the same frequency, and
    (ii) for two different frequencies of light of the same intensity.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Two curves with the photocurrent rising with anode potential and then becoming constant (saturation current). Both curves meet the potential axis at the same negative potential $-V_0$ (same stopping potential), but the curve for the higher intensity has the higher saturation current.
    (ii) Two curves with the same saturation current. The curve for the higher frequency $\nu_1$ meets the potential axis at a larger negative potential ($|-V_{01}| > |-V_{02}|$), i.e. the stopping potential is larger for the higher frequency.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Saturation current higher for higher intensity (½ marks)
    • (i) Same stopping potential for both intensities (½ marks)
    • (ii) Same saturation current for both frequencies (½ marks)
    • (ii) Larger stopping potential (in magnitude) for the higher frequency (½ marks)

  4. Question 20 · 2 marks · Magnetism and Matter
    A rod of a diamagnetic material and a rod of a paramagnetic material are placed, one at a time, in a uniform magnetic field with their lengths along the field.
    (i) Describe (or sketch) how the magnetic field lines are changed by each rod.
    (ii) Give one example each of a diamagnetic and a paramagnetic material.

    OR (i) A straight solenoid carrying a current behaves like a bar magnet. Explain why.
    (ii) How can you find which end of such a solenoid behaves as its north pole?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Diamagnetic rod: the field lines are pushed out of the rod, so fewer lines pass through it (the field inside is slightly reduced). Paramagnetic rod: the field lines are drawn into the rod, so more lines pass through it (the field inside is slightly increased).
    (ii) Diamagnetic: bismuth (or copper, water, lead). Paramagnetic: aluminium (or sodium, oxygen, platinum).

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Diamagnetic: field lines pushed out / fewer lines inside (½ marks)
    • (i) Paramagnetic: field lines drawn in / more lines inside (½ marks)
    • (ii) Correct diamagnetic example (½ marks)
    • (ii) Correct paramagnetic example (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (i) Each turn of the solenoid is a small current loop, which behaves as a magnetic dipole. The magnetic moments of all the turns point along the axis, and their fields add up. So the field outside the solenoid looks like that of a bar magnet, with one end acting as a north pole and the other as a south pole.
    (ii) Look at one end of the solenoid. If the current in the turns flows anticlockwise as seen from that end, that end is the north pole; if it flows clockwise, it is the south pole. (Equivalently, curl the fingers of the right hand along the current: the thumb points towards the north pole.)

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Each turn acts as a current loop / magnetic dipole (½ marks)
    • (i) Fields of the turns add along the axis, giving a pattern like a bar magnet with N and S ends (½ marks)
    • (ii) Anticlockwise current seen from an end means that end is N (or right-hand thumb rule) (1 mark)

  5. Question 21 · 2 marks · Atoms
    When the electron of a hydrogen atom is in the orbit $n = 3$, its total energy is $-1.51$ eV.
    (i) What is the kinetic energy of the electron in this orbit?
    (ii) What is its potential energy in this orbit?

    OR (i) According to classical electromagnetic theory, what should happen to an electron revolving round the nucleus in Rutherford's model of the atom?
    (ii) Which two observed facts about atoms does this prediction contradict?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: In a Bohr orbit, $K = \frac{e^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 r}$ and $U = -\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r}$, so $E = K + U = -K$ and $U = 2E$.
    (i) $K = -E = 1.51$ eV.
    (ii) $U = 2E = -3.02$ eV.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Using $K = -E$: kinetic energy $= 1.51$ eV (1 mark)
    • (ii) Using $U = 2E$ (or $U = -2K$): potential energy $= -3.02$ eV (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (i) The revolving electron is accelerated (it has a centripetal acceleration), so it should radiate energy continuously. Its orbit would then shrink and it would spiral into the nucleus.
    (ii) Atoms are stable, but the prediction says they would collapse. Also, the frequency of the radiation would change continuously, giving a continuous spectrum, but atoms actually give line spectra.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Accelerated electron radiates energy continuously (½ marks)
    • (i) Electron spirals into the nucleus (½ marks)
    • (ii) Contradicts the stability of atoms (½ marks)
    • (ii) Contradicts the line spectra of atoms (prediction: continuous spectrum) (½ marks)

Section C: 3 marks each

  1. Question 22 · 3 marks · Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
    Two capacitors of 3 $\mu$F and 6 $\mu$F are connected in series. This combination is connected in parallel with a 4 $\mu$F capacitor, and the whole arrangement is connected across a 12 V battery. Find
    (i) the equivalent capacitance of the arrangement,
    (ii) the charge on each capacitor,
    (iii) the total energy stored in the arrangement.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Series part: $\frac{1}{C_s} = \frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$, so $C_s = 2\ \mu$F. Equivalent capacitance $C = 2 + 4 = 6\ \mu$F.
    (ii) Each branch has 12 V across it. 4 $\mu$F capacitor: $Q = 4\times12 = 48\ \mu$C. Series branch: $Q = C_sV = 2\times12 = 24\ \mu$C, and the 3 $\mu$F and 6 $\mu$F capacitors each carry 24 $\mu$C.
    (iii) $U = \frac{1}{2}CV^2 = \frac{1}{2}\times6\times10^{-6}\times12^2 = 4.32\times10^{-4}$ J.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Series combination $= 2\ \mu$F (½ marks)
    • (i) Equivalent capacitance $= 6\ \mu$F (½ marks)
    • (ii) Charge on 4 $\mu$F capacitor $= 48\ \mu$C (½ marks)
    • (ii) Charge on 3 $\mu$F and on 6 $\mu$F capacitors $= 24\ \mu$C each (½ marks)
    • (iii) $U = \frac{1}{2}CV^2$ used (½ marks)
    • (iii) $U = 4.32\times10^{-4}$ J (½ marks)

  2. Question 23 · 3 marks · Current Electricity
    A copper coil has a resistance of 20.0 $\Omega$ at 20 $^\circ$C. After a current has flowed through it for some time, its resistance is found to be 25.0 $\Omega$. The temperature coefficient of resistance of copper is $4.0\times10^{-3}\ ^\circ$C$^{-1}$ (taken at 20 $^\circ$C).
    (i) Find the temperature of the coil.
    (ii) Why does the resistivity of a metal increase when its temperature rises?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) $R_T = R_0[1+\alpha(T-T_0)]$
    $25.0 = 20.0[1+4.0\times10^{-3}(T-20)]$
    $T - 20 = \frac{25.0-20.0}{20.0\times4.0\times10^{-3}} = \frac{5}{0.08} = 62.5\ ^\circ$C
    $T = 82.5\ ^\circ$C
    (ii) $\rho = \frac{m}{ne^2\tau}$. In a metal the number density $n$ of free electrons hardly changes with temperature. At a higher temperature the ions vibrate more, so the electrons collide with them more often and the relaxation time $\tau$ decreases. Hence $\rho$ increases.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) $R_T = R_0[1+\alpha(T-T_0)]$ (½ marks)
    • (i) Correct substitution: $25.0 = 20.0[1+4.0\times10^{-3}(T-20)]$ (½ marks)
    • (i) Rise in temperature $= 62.5\ ^\circ$C (½ marks)
    • (i) $T = 82.5\ ^\circ$C (½ marks)
    • (ii) More frequent collisions, so relaxation time $\tau$ decreases (½ marks)
    • (ii) $n$ nearly constant, so $\rho = m/(ne^2\tau)$ increases (½ marks)

  3. Question 24 · 3 marks · Ray Optics and Optical Instruments
    A convex lens forms a real image of an object on a screen. The distance between the object and the screen is 90 cm, and the image is twice the size of the object.
    (i) Find the focal length of the lens.
    (ii) Keeping the object and the screen fixed, the lens is moved to another position between them where a sharp image is again formed on the screen. Find the distance of the lens from the object in this position and the magnification of the image.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Real image, twice the size: $m = \frac{v}{u} = -2$, so $v = 2|u|$. Also $|u| + v = 90$ cm, so $|u| = 30$ cm and $v = 60$ cm.
    With $u = -30$ cm, $v = +60$ cm: $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v}-\frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{60}+\frac{1}{30} = \frac{3}{60}$, so $f = 20$ cm.
    (ii) By the reversibility of light, the object and image distances can be exchanged: $u = -60$ cm, $v = +30$ cm. Check: $\frac{1}{30}+\frac{1}{60} = \frac{1}{20}$. So the lens is 60 cm from the object, and $m = \frac{v}{u} = \frac{30}{-60} = -\frac{1}{2}$ (real, inverted, half the size of the object).
    (Alternatively, with $|u| + v = 90$ cm and $f = 20$ cm: $\frac{1}{90-|u|}+\frac{1}{|u|} = \frac{1}{20}$ gives $|u|^2 - 90|u| + 1800 = 0$, so $|u| = 30$ cm or $60$ cm; the second position is $|u| = 60$ cm.)

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) $|u| = 30$ cm and $v = 60$ cm from $m=-2$ and $|u|+v = 90$ cm (1 mark)
    • (i) Lens formula used: $f = 20$ cm (1 mark)
    • (ii) Lens 60 cm from the object (½ marks)
    • (ii) Magnification $= -\frac{1}{2}$ (½ marks)

  4. Question 25 · 3 marks · Wave Optics
    Using Huygens' principle, show how a plane wavefront is reflected from a plane reflecting surface. Hence verify the laws of reflection.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Let a plane wavefront AB be incident at angle $i$ on the reflecting surface MN, with A touching the surface first. Let $v$ be the speed of light and $t$ the time taken by the disturbance at B to reach the point C on the surface, so BC $= vt$.
    In this time, the secondary wavelet from A spreads out (in the same medium) as a hemisphere of radius AE $= vt$. Draw the tangent plane CE from C to this hemisphere; CE is the reflected wavefront, and the angle it makes with the surface is the angle of reflection $r$.
    In triangles ABC and CEA: AC is common, BC = AE $= vt$, and the angles at B and E are both $90^\circ$. So the triangles are congruent, which gives $\angle$BAC = $\angle$ECA, i.e. $i = r$.
    Also, the incident ray, the normal and the reflected ray all lie in the same plane (the plane of the figure). These are the laws of reflection.

    Marking scheme:

    • Diagram: incident wavefront AB, surface MN, wavelet of radius AE = vt from A, reflected wavefront CE (1 mark)
    • BC = AE = vt and triangles ABC and CEA shown congruent (1 mark)
    • Conclusion $i = r$ (and rays and normal in one plane) (1 mark)

  5. Question 26 · 3 marks · Nuclei
    (i) The radius of a nucleus of mass number $A$ is $R = R_0A^{1/3}$, where $R_0$ is a constant. Show that the density of nuclear matter is independent of $A$.
    (ii) Two nuclei have mass numbers in the ratio 1 : 8. Find the ratio of (a) their volumes and (b) their densities.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Mass of nucleus $\approx Am$, where $m$ is the mass of a nucleon. Volume $= \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi R_0^3A$.
    Density $\rho = \frac{Am}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R_0^3A} = \frac{3m}{4\pi R_0^3}$, which does not contain $A$. So all nuclei have nearly the same density.
    (ii) (a) $V \propto R^3 \propto A$, so $V_1:V_2 = 1:8$. (b) Densities are equal: $1:1$.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Mass $= Am$ (½ marks)
    • (i) Volume $= \frac{4}{3}\pi R_0^3A$ (½ marks)
    • (i) $\rho = \frac{3m}{4\pi R_0^3}$ (½ marks)
    • (i) Conclusion: independent of $A$ (½ marks)
    • (ii)(a) Volumes 1 : 8 (since $V \propto A$) (½ marks)
    • (ii)(b) Densities 1 : 1 (½ marks)

  6. Question 27 · 3 marks · Moving Charges and Magnetism
    (i) Using the Biot-Savart law, obtain an expression for the magnetic field at the centre of a circular loop of radius $R$ carrying a current $I$.
    (ii) Two circular loops of radii 10 cm and 20 cm lie in the same plane with a common centre. They carry currents of 2 A and 4 A respectively, in opposite senses. Find the net magnetic field at their common centre.

    OR (i) State Ampere's circuital law. Use it to obtain an expression for the magnetic field at a distance $r$ from an infinitely long straight wire carrying a current $I$.
    (ii) A long straight wire carries a current of 10 A. Find the magnetic field at a distance of 5 cm from it.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) By the Biot-Savart law, $dB = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{I\,dl\sin\theta}{R^2}$. For every element of the loop, $dl$ is perpendicular to the line joining it to the centre ($\theta = 90^\circ$), and all the contributions $dB$ point in the same direction, perpendicular to the plane of the loop. So
    $B = \frac{\mu_0I}{4\pi R^2}\oint dl = \frac{\mu_0I}{4\pi R^2}(2\pi R) = \frac{\mu_0I}{2R}$.
    (ii) $B_1 = \frac{\mu_0\times2}{2\times0.10} = 10\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-6}$ T. $B_2 = \frac{\mu_0\times4}{2\times0.20} = 10\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-6}$ T. The currents are in opposite senses, so the fields are opposite. Net field $= 0$.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Biot-Savart law: $dB = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{I\,dl\sin\theta}{R^2}$ (½ marks)
    • (i) $\theta = 90^\circ$ and all $dB$ in the same direction (½ marks)
    • (i) $\oint dl = 2\pi R$ used (½ marks)
    • (i) $B = \frac{\mu_0I}{2R}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) Each field $= 4\pi\times10^{-6}$ T (½ marks)
    • (ii) Net field zero (fields opposite) (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (i) The line integral of the magnetic field around any closed loop equals $\mu_0$ times the total current passing through the surface bounded by the loop: $\oint\vec{B}\cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0I$.
    Take a circle of radius $r$ centred on the wire, in a plane perpendicular to it. By symmetry, $B$ has the same magnitude at every point of the circle and is along the tangent. So $\oint\vec{B}\cdot d\vec{l} = B(2\pi r) = \mu_0I$, giving $B = \frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}$.
    (ii) $B = \frac{4\pi\times10^{-7}\times10}{2\pi\times0.05} = 4\times10^{-5}$ T.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Statement: $\oint\vec{B}\cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0I$ (I = current enclosed) (½ marks)
    • (i) Circular loop chosen; B constant in magnitude and tangential by symmetry (½ marks)
    • (i) $B(2\pi r) = \mu_0I$ (½ marks)
    • (i) $B = \frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) Substitution: $\frac{4\pi\times10^{-7}\times10}{2\pi\times0.05}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) $B = 4\times10^{-5}$ T (½ marks)

  7. Question 28 · 3 marks · Electromagnetic Induction
    (i) State Lenz's law. Show that it follows from the law of conservation of energy.
    (ii) The north pole of a bar magnet is moved towards a closed circular coil along its axis. What is the direction of the induced current in the coil (clockwise or anticlockwise) as seen from the side of the magnet?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) The polarity of the induced emf is such that it tends to produce a current which opposes the change in magnetic flux that produced it.
    When the N pole of a magnet is pushed towards a coil, the induced current makes the near face of the coil a north pole, which repels the magnet. Work has to be done against this repulsion to move the magnet, and this mechanical work appears as electrical energy (and then heat) in the coil. If the induced current helped the motion instead, the magnet would speed up and electrical energy would be produced without any work being done, which would violate the conservation of energy.
    (ii) The face of the coil near the magnet must become a north pole to oppose the approach, so the induced current is anticlockwise as seen from the magnet's side.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Correct statement of Lenz's law (1 mark)
    • (i) Induced current opposes the motion, so work must be done (½ marks)
    • (i) This work is converted into electrical energy; otherwise energy would be created (½ marks)
    • (ii) Near face becomes a north pole (½ marks)
    • (ii) Anticlockwise as seen from the magnet (½ marks)

  1. Question 29 · 4 marks · Semiconductor Electronics
    A mobile phone charger must change the 50 Hz a.c. mains supply into a steady direct voltage. First, a transformer lowers the voltage. Then a rectifier made of p-n junction diodes changes the a.c. into a current that flows in only one direction.
    In a full-wave rectifier using a centre-tap transformer, two diodes D1 and D2 are connected to the two ends of the secondary coil, and the load resistor is connected between the joined output of the diodes and the centre tap. During one half of each cycle, D1 is forward biased and D2 is reverse biased; during the next half, the roles are exchanged. So the current through the load flows in the same direction in both halves of the cycle. A silicon diode conducts noticeably only when the forward voltage across it is more than about 0.7 V; while it conducts, the voltage across it stays at about 0.7 V.

    (i) What is the purpose of the centre tap of the secondary coil in this rectifier? [1]

    (ii) During the half cycle in which D1 conducts, why does D2 not conduct? [1]

    (iii) Each half of the secondary coil gives a peak voltage of 10 V. Estimate the peak voltage across the load. [1]

    (iv) State one advantage of a full-wave rectifier over a half-wave rectifier. [1]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) What is the purpose of the centre tap of the secondary coil in this rectifier?

    Model answer: The centre tap is the common reference (zero) point of the secondary. With respect to it, the two ends of the secondary are always at opposite potentials (opposite in phase), so the two diodes get forward biased in turn, one in each half cycle.

    Marking scheme:

    • Centre tap acts as the common reference point for the load (½ marks)
    • The two ends are at opposite potentials with respect to it, so the diodes conduct in alternate half cycles (½ marks)

    (ii) During the half cycle in which D1 conducts, why does D2 not conduct?

    Model answer: During that half cycle, the end of the secondary connected to D2 is at a negative potential with respect to the centre tap, so D2 is reverse biased and does not conduct.

    Marking scheme:

    • Its end of the secondary is negative with respect to the centre tap (½ marks)
    • So D2 is reverse biased (½ marks)

    (iii) Each half of the secondary coil gives a peak voltage of 10 V. Estimate the peak voltage across the load.

    Model answer: The conducting diode takes about 0.7 V, so the peak voltage across the load is about $10 - 0.7 = 9.3$ V.

    Marking scheme:

    • 0.7 V dropped across the conducting diode (½ marks)
    • Peak load voltage about 9.3 V (½ marks)

    (iv) State one advantage of a full-wave rectifier over a half-wave rectifier.

    Model answer: A full-wave rectifier uses both halves of each a.c. cycle, so it gives twice as many output pulses and a larger average (d.c.) output; it is more efficient.

    Marking scheme:

    • Uses both half cycles / larger average output / more efficient (1 mark)

  2. Question 30 · 4 marks · Atoms
    In 1911, following experiments by Geiger and Marsden, Rutherford proposed the nuclear model of the atom. A narrow beam of alpha particles was directed at a very thin gold foil. Most of the alpha particles passed straight through the foil. A small fraction was deflected through small angles, and about one in 8000 was deflected through more than $90^\circ$. The perpendicular distance of the initial velocity of an alpha particle from the centre of the nucleus is called its impact parameter.
    Rutherford's model could not explain why atoms are stable. In 1913, Bohr proposed that the electron of a hydrogen atom can revolve only in certain orbits in which its angular momentum is an integral multiple of $h/2\pi$. The atom emits a photon when the electron jumps from a higher orbit to a lower one, which explains the line spectrum of hydrogen.

    (i) What did the deflection of a few alpha particles through more than $90^\circ$ show about the structure of the atom? [1]

    (ii) What is the impact parameter of an alpha particle that rebounds straight back (scattering angle $180^\circ$)? Will an alpha particle with a large impact parameter be deflected much or little? [1]

    (iii) Find the angular momentum of the electron in the third orbit of the hydrogen atom. [1]

    (iv) Which spectral series of hydrogen lies in the visible region? To which orbit does the electron jump in this series? [1]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) What did the deflection of a few alpha particles through more than $90^\circ$ show about the structure of the atom?

    Model answer: It showed that the positive charge and nearly all the mass of the atom are concentrated in a very small central region, the nucleus, which can repel an alpha particle strongly enough to turn it back.

    Marking scheme:

    • Positive charge and almost all the mass concentrated (½ marks)
    • in a very small central region (nucleus) (½ marks)

    (ii) What is the impact parameter of an alpha particle that rebounds straight back (scattering angle $180^\circ$)? Will an alpha particle with a large impact parameter be deflected much or little?

    Model answer: For a head-on collision (rebounds back) the impact parameter is zero. An alpha particle with a large impact parameter passes far from the nucleus and is deflected very little.

    Marking scheme:

    • Impact parameter zero (½ marks)
    • Large impact parameter: very small deflection (½ marks)

    (iii) Find the angular momentum of the electron in the third orbit of the hydrogen atom.

    Model answer: $L = \frac{nh}{2\pi} = \frac{3\times6.63\times10^{-34}}{2\times3.14} \approx 3.17\times10^{-34}$ J s.

    Marking scheme:

    • $L = \frac{nh}{2\pi}$ with $n = 3$ (½ marks)
    • $L \approx 3.17\times10^{-34}$ J s (½ marks)

    (iv) Which spectral series of hydrogen lies in the visible region? To which orbit does the electron jump in this series?

    Model answer: The Balmer series; the electron jumps from higher orbits to the orbit $n = 2$.

    Marking scheme:

    • Balmer series (½ marks)
    • Final orbit $n = 2$ (½ marks)

  1. Question 31 · 5 marks · Electric Charges and Fields
    (i) Obtain an expression for the electric field at a point on the axis of an electric dipole, at a distance $r$ from its centre. Write the expression when $r \gg a$, where $2a$ is the length of the dipole.
    (ii) Charges of $+2$ nC and $-2$ nC are placed 2 mm apart. Find the magnitude and direction of the electric field at a point on the axis of this dipole, 20 cm from its centre.

    OR (i) State Gauss's law in electrostatics. Use it to obtain the electric field due to a uniformly charged thin spherical shell of radius $R$ carrying charge $q$, at a point (a) outside the shell and (b) inside the shell.
    (ii) A thin spherical shell of radius 10 cm carries a charge of 4 nC spread uniformly over it. Find the electric field at a point (a) 5 cm and (b) 20 cm from its centre.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Let $-q$ be at A and $+q$ at B, with AB $= 2a$, and let P be on the axis at distance $r$ from the centre O, on the side of $+q$. Let $\hat{p}$ be the unit vector from $-q$ to $+q$.
    Field due to $+q$: $\vec{E}_{+} = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0(r-a)^2}\hat{p}$. Field due to $-q$: $\vec{E}_{-} = -\frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0(r+a)^2}\hat{p}$.
    $\vec{E} = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left[\frac{1}{(r-a)^2}-\frac{1}{(r+a)^2}\right]\hat{p} = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\cdot\frac{4ar}{(r^2-a^2)^2}\hat{p} = \frac{2pr}{4\pi\varepsilon_0(r^2-a^2)^2}\hat{p}$, where $p = 2qa$.
    For $r \gg a$: $\vec{E} = \frac{2\vec{p}}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^3}$, directed along $\vec{p}$.
    (ii) $p = q(2a) = 2\times10^{-9}\times2\times10^{-3} = 4\times10^{-12}$ C m. Since $r = 0.2$ m $\gg a$:
    $E = \frac{2kp}{r^3} = \frac{2\times9\times10^{9}\times4\times10^{-12}}{(0.2)^3} = \frac{7.2\times10^{-2}}{8\times10^{-3}} = 9$ N C$^{-1}$, directed along the dipole moment (from the $-2$ nC charge towards the $+2$ nC charge).

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Field due to $+q$: $\frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0(r-a)^2}$ along $\hat{p}$ (½ marks)
    • (i) Field due to $-q$: $\frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0(r+a)^2}$ opposite to $\hat{p}$ (½ marks)
    • (i) Net field as the difference of the two (½ marks)
    • (i) $E = \frac{2pr}{4\pi\varepsilon_0(r^2-a^2)^2}$ (1 mark)
    • (i) For $r \gg a$: $E = \frac{2p}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^3}$ along $\vec{p}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) $p = 4\times10^{-12}$ C m (½ marks)
    • (ii) $E = \frac{2kp}{r^3}$ used (½ marks)
    • (ii) $E = 9$ N C$^{-1}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii) Direction: along the dipole moment, from the negative to the positive charge (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (i) Gauss's law: the total electric flux through any closed surface is $\frac{1}{\varepsilon_0}$ times the charge enclosed by it: $\oint\vec{E}\cdot d\vec{S} = \frac{q_{enc}}{\varepsilon_0}$.
    (a) Outside ($r > R$): take a concentric spherical Gaussian surface of radius $r$. By symmetry $\vec{E}$ is radial and has the same magnitude everywhere on it. $E(4\pi r^2) = \frac{q}{\varepsilon_0}$, so $E = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}$, as if the whole charge were at the centre.
    (b) Inside ($r \lt R$): a concentric Gaussian sphere of radius $r$ encloses no charge, so $E(4\pi r^2) = 0$ and $E = 0$.
    (ii) (a) 5 cm is inside the shell: $E = 0$.
    (b) $E = \frac{9\times10^{9}\times4\times10^{-9}}{(0.2)^2} = \frac{36}{0.04} = 900$ N C$^{-1}$, directed radially outward.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Statement: $\oint\vec{E}\cdot d\vec{S} = q_{enc}/\varepsilon_0$ (½ marks)
    • (i) Concentric spherical Gaussian surface; E radial and uniform on it by symmetry (½ marks)
    • (i)(a) $E(4\pi r^2) = q/\varepsilon_0$, so $E = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}$ (1 mark)
    • (i)(b) No charge enclosed, so $E = 0$ inside (1 mark)
    • (ii)(a) E = 0 at 5 cm (inside the shell) (1 mark)
    • (ii)(b) Substitution $\frac{9\times10^9\times4\times10^{-9}}{(0.2)^2}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii)(b) $E = 900$ N C$^{-1}$ (½ marks)

  2. Question 32 · 5 marks · Ray Optics and Optical Instruments
    (i) Draw a labelled ray diagram of a compound microscope forming the final image at the least distance of distinct vision. Write the expression for its magnifying power in this case.
    (ii) A compound microscope has an objective of focal length 1.25 cm and an eyepiece of focal length 5 cm. An object is placed 1.5 cm from the objective, and the microscope is adjusted so that the final image is formed at infinity. Find (a) the distance of the image formed by the objective from the objective, (b) the magnifying power of the microscope, and (c) the distance between the objective and the eyepiece. (Take the least distance of distinct vision $D = 25$ cm.)

    OR (i) Draw a labelled ray diagram of a refracting astronomical telescope in normal adjustment (final image at infinity). Define its magnifying power and write its expression in this case.
    (ii) A refracting telescope has an objective of focal length 150 cm and an eyepiece of focal length 5 cm. Find (a) its magnifying power in normal adjustment and (b) the distance between the objective and the eyepiece.
    (iii) Give two advantages of a reflecting telescope over a refracting telescope.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Diagram: the object AB is placed just outside the focus $F_o$ of the objective. The objective forms a real, inverted, magnified image A$'$B$'$ just inside the focus $F_e$ of the eyepiece. The eyepiece acts as a simple magnifier and forms a virtual, magnified final image A$''$B$''$ at distance $D$ from the eye. Objective, eyepiece, both foci, intermediate image and final image are labelled.
    Magnifying power $m = m_om_e = \frac{v_o}{|u_o|}\left(1+\frac{D}{f_e}\right)$.
    (ii) (a) $\frac{1}{v_o} = \frac{1}{f_o}+\frac{1}{u_o} = \frac{1}{1.25}-\frac{1}{1.5} = \frac{4}{5}-\frac{2}{3} = \frac{2}{15}$, so $v_o = 7.5$ cm.
    (b) $m_o = \frac{v_o}{|u_o|} = \frac{7.5}{1.5} = 5$; final image at infinity, so $m_e = \frac{D}{f_e} = \frac{25}{5} = 5$. Magnifying power $m = 5\times5 = 25$.
    (c) For the final image at infinity, the intermediate image is at the focus of the eyepiece, so the separation $L = v_o + f_e = 7.5 + 5 = 12.5$ cm.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Objective forms a real, inverted, magnified image just inside the focus of the eyepiece (½ marks)
    • (i) Eyepiece forms a virtual, magnified final image at D (½ marks)
    • (i) Diagram labels: objective, eyepiece, intermediate image, final image (½ marks)
    • (i) $m = \frac{v_o}{|u_o|}\left(1+\frac{D}{f_e}\right)$ (½ marks)
    • (ii)(a) Lens formula with $u_o = -1.5$ cm (½ marks)
    • (ii)(a) $v_o = 7.5$ cm (½ marks)
    • (ii)(b) $m_o = 5$ and $m_e = 5$ (½ marks)
    • (ii)(b) $m = 25$ (½ marks)
    • (ii)(c) $L = v_o + f_e = 12.5$ cm (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (i) Diagram: parallel rays from a distant object, making angle $\alpha$ with the axis, fall on the objective. The objective forms a real, inverted image at its focus, which is also the focus of the eyepiece. The rays leave the eyepiece parallel to each other, making angle $\beta$ with the axis, so the final image is at infinity. Objective, eyepiece, common focus, intermediate image, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ labelled.
    Magnifying power is the ratio of the angle $\beta$ subtended at the eye by the final image to the angle $\alpha$ subtended at the eye by the object: $m = \frac{\beta}{\alpha} = \frac{f_o}{f_e}$.
    (ii) (a) $m = \frac{150}{5} = 30$. (b) $L = f_o + f_e = 150 + 5 = 155$ cm.
    (iii) Any two: a mirror has no chromatic aberration; a paraboloidal mirror removes spherical aberration; a mirror is lighter and can be supported over its whole back, so very large apertures are possible; only one surface has to be ground and polished.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Diagram: image formed by the objective at the common focus (½ marks)
    • (i) Diagram: rays leave the eyepiece parallel; final image at infinity; labels (½ marks)
    • (i) Definition: $m = \beta/\alpha$ (½ marks)
    • (i) $m = f_o/f_e$ (½ marks)
    • (ii)(a) Magnifying power = 30 (1 mark)
    • (ii)(b) Separation = 155 cm (1 mark)
    • (iii) First advantage (½ marks)
    • (iii) Second advantage (½ marks)

  3. Question 33 · 5 marks · Alternating Current
    (i) State the principle of an a.c. generator. Describe its working and show that the emf produced is $e = NBA\omega\sin\omega t$, where the symbols have their usual meanings.
    (ii) The coil of an a.c. generator has 50 turns, each of area 0.02 m$^2$. It rotates at 50 revolutions per second in a uniform magnetic field of 0.5 T. Find (a) the peak emf and (b) the rms emf produced.

    OR (i) An a.c. voltage $v = v_m\sin\omega t$ is applied across a pure inductor of inductance $L$. Obtain an expression for the current, and show that the current lags the voltage by $\frac{\pi}{2}$. Show also that the average power consumed by the inductor over a complete cycle is zero.
    (ii) A pure inductor of inductance $\frac{0.5}{\pi}$ H is connected to a 220 V, 50 Hz supply. Find (a) the inductive reactance, (b) the rms current, and (c) the average power consumed.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Principle: electromagnetic induction. When a coil rotates in a magnetic field, the magnetic flux through it changes, and an emf is induced in it.
    Working: a coil of $N$ turns and area $A$ is rotated with constant angular speed $\omega$ about an axis perpendicular to a uniform field $B$. The ends of the coil are connected to slip rings, and carbon brushes pressing on the rings take the current to the external circuit.
    If the normal to the coil makes angle $\theta = \omega t$ with $B$ at time $t$, the flux is $\Phi = NBA\cos\omega t$. The induced emf is $e = -\frac{d\Phi}{dt} = NBA\omega\sin\omega t$, which varies sinusoidally with peak value $e_0 = NBA\omega$.
    (ii) (a) $\omega = 2\pi\times50 = 100\pi$ rad s$^{-1}$. $e_0 = NBA\omega = 50\times0.5\times0.02\times100\pi = 50\pi \approx 157$ V.
    (b) $e_{rms} = \frac{e_0}{\sqrt{2}} = 157\times0.707 \approx 111$ V.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Principle: electromagnetic induction (changing flux through a rotating coil) (½ marks)
    • (i) Working: coil rotated in uniform field; slip rings and brushes (1 mark)
    • (i) $\Phi = NBA\cos\omega t$ (½ marks)
    • (i) $e = -\frac{d\Phi}{dt} = NBA\omega\sin\omega t$ (1 mark)
    • (ii)(a) $\omega = 100\pi$ rad s$^{-1}$ and $e_0 = NBA\omega$ (½ marks)
    • (ii)(a) $e_0 = 50\pi \approx 157$ V (½ marks)
    • (ii)(b) $e_{rms} = e_0/\sqrt{2}$ (½ marks)
    • (ii)(b) $e_{rms} \approx 111$ V (accept 110 to 111 V) (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (i) By Kirchhoff's loop rule, $v_m\sin\omega t - L\frac{di}{dt} = 0$, so $\frac{di}{dt} = \frac{v_m}{L}\sin\omega t$.
    Integrating: $i = -\frac{v_m}{\omega L}\cos\omega t = i_m\sin\left(\omega t-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)$, where $i_m = \frac{v_m}{X_L}$ and $X_L = \omega L$.
    So the current reaches its maximum a quarter cycle after the voltage: it lags the voltage by $\frac{\pi}{2}$ (in the phasor diagram, the current phasor is $90^\circ$ behind the voltage phasor).
    Power: $p = vi = v_m\sin\omega t\times(-i_m\cos\omega t) = -\frac{v_mi_m}{2}\sin2\omega t$. The average of $\sin2\omega t$ over a complete cycle is zero, so the average power is zero.
    (ii) (a) $X_L = 2\pi fL = 2\pi\times50\times\frac{0.5}{\pi} = 50\ \Omega$. (b) $I = \frac{V}{X_L} = \frac{220}{50} = 4.4$ A. (c) Zero, since the phase difference is $90^\circ$ and $\cos90^\circ = 0$ (the current is wattless).

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) $L\frac{di}{dt} = v_m\sin\omega t$ (½ marks)
    • (i) $i = i_m\sin(\omega t-\pi/2)$ with $i_m = v_m/\omega L$ (1 mark)
    • (i) Current lags voltage by $\pi/2$ (½ marks)
    • (i) $p = -\frac{v_mi_m}{2}\sin2\omega t$, average zero (1 mark)
    • (ii)(a) $X_L = 50\ \Omega$ (½ marks)
    • (ii)(b) I = 4.4 A (½ marks)
    • (ii)(c) Average power zero (½ marks)
    • (ii)(c) Reason: phase difference $90^\circ$ / $\cos\phi = 0$ (½ marks)
