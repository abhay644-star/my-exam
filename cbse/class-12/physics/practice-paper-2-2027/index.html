Section A: 1 mark each

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
    At a point near an isolated point charge, the electric potential is 600 V and the magnitude of the electric field is 200 N C$^{-1}$. The distance of the point from the charge and the magnitude of the charge are

    1. 3 m and $2\times10^{-7}$ C
    2. 3 m and $6\times10^{-7}$ C
    3. $\frac{1}{3}$ m and $2\times10^{-8}$ C
    4. 9 m and $6\times10^{-7}$ C
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) 3 m and $2\times10^{-7}$ C

    $V=\frac{kq}{r}$ and $E=\frac{kq}{r^2}$, so $r=\frac{V}{E}=\frac{600}{200}=3$ m. Then $q=\frac{Vr}{k}=\frac{600\times3}{9\times10^{9}}=2\times10^{-7}$ C.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Current Electricity
    A cell of emf 2 V and internal resistance 0.5 $\Omega$ is connected across a resistor of 3.5 $\Omega$. The potential difference across the terminals of the cell is

    1. 2.0 V
    2. 1.5 V
    3. 0.25 V
    4. 1.75 V
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) 1.75 V

    $I=\frac{\varepsilon}{R+r}=\frac{2}{4}=0.5$ A, so $V=\varepsilon-Ir=2-0.5\times0.5=1.75$ V.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Moving Charges and Magnetism
    A circular coil of 50 turns and radius 10 cm carries a current of 2 A. The magnetic field at its centre is

    1. $2\pi\times10^{-4}$ T
    2. $\pi\times10^{-4}$ T
    3. $4\pi\times10^{-4}$ T
    4. $2\pi\times10^{-5}$ T
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $2\pi\times10^{-4}$ T

    $B=\frac{\mu_0NI}{2R}=\frac{4\pi\times10^{-7}\times50\times2}{2\times0.10}=2\pi\times10^{-4}$ T.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Magnetism and Matter
    A small piece of a diamagnetic substance is placed in a non-uniform magnetic field. It tends to move

    1. from the weaker part of the field to the stronger part
    2. from the stronger part of the field to the weaker part
    3. at right angles to the field lines
    4. nowhere, as it is not affected by the field
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) from the stronger part of the field to the weaker part

    A diamagnetic substance is weakly magnetised opposite to the applied field, so it is repelled and moves from stronger to weaker regions of the field.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Electromagnetic Induction
    The current in a coil falls uniformly from 5 A to 1 A in 0.1 s, and an emf of 8 V is induced in the coil. The self-inductance of the coil is

    1. 0.2 H
    2. 0.1 H
    3. 2 H
    4. 0.02 H
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) 0.2 H

    $|\varepsilon|=L\frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t}$, so $L=\frac{8}{(5-1)/0.1}=\frac{8}{40}=0.2$ H.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Alternating Current
    For a series LCR circuit at resonance, which of the following statements is correct?

    1. The impedance of the circuit is maximum.
    2. The current in the circuit is minimum.
    3. The voltages across the inductor and the capacitor are equal in magnitude and opposite in phase.
    4. The power factor of the circuit is zero.
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) The voltages across the inductor and the capacitor are equal in magnitude and opposite in phase.

    At resonance $X_L=X_C$, so $V_L=IX_L$ and $V_C=IX_C$ are equal and $180^\circ$ out of phase. Then $Z=R$ (minimum), the current is maximum and the power factor is 1.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Electromagnetic Waves
    While a capacitor is being charged, a displacement current exists in the gap between its plates because

    1. charges flow across the gap between the plates
    2. the magnetic field between the plates stays constant
    3. the medium between the plates becomes a conductor
    4. the electric field between the plates changes with time
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) the electric field between the plates changes with time

    Displacement current $i_d=\varepsilon_0\frac{d\Phi_E}{dt}$ arises from a changing electric field (flux), not from a flow of charges.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Ray Optics and Optical Instruments
    The critical angle for light going from a transparent medium to air is 30°. The refractive index of the medium is

    1. 1.5
    2. $\sqrt{2}$
    3. 2
    4. $\sqrt{3}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) 2

    $\mu=\frac{1}{\sin C}=\frac{1}{\sin30^\circ}=2$.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter
    In a photoelectric experiment, the stopping potential $V_0$ is plotted against the frequency $\nu$ of the incident light for a metal. The graph is a straight line whose slope is

    1. $h$
    2. $\frac{h}{e}$
    3. $\frac{e}{h}$
    4. $\frac{\phi_0}{e}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) $\frac{h}{e}$

    $eV_0=h\nu-\phi_0$ gives $V_0=\frac{h}{e}\nu-\frac{\phi_0}{e}$, so the slope is $\frac{h}{e}$.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Atoms
    The energy needed to remove the electron completely from a hydrogen atom that is in its first excited state is

    1. 13.6 eV
    2. 10.2 eV
    3. 3.4 eV
    4. 1.51 eV
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) 3.4 eV

    First excited state is $n=2$: $E_2=-\frac{13.6}{4}=-3.4$ eV, so 3.4 eV is needed to remove the electron.

  11. Question 11 · 1 mark · Semiconductor Electronics
    In an n-type semiconductor,

    1. electrons are majority carriers and the impurity atoms are pentavalent
    2. electrons are minority carriers and the impurity atoms are pentavalent
    3. holes are majority carriers and the impurity atoms are trivalent
    4. holes are majority carriers and the impurity atoms are pentavalent
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) electrons are majority carriers and the impurity atoms are pentavalent

    Pentavalent (donor) impurity gives extra free electrons, so electrons are the majority carriers in n-type material.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Semiconductor Electronics
    The input to a half-wave rectifier is an a.c. voltage of frequency 50 Hz. The frequency of the pulses in the output is

    1. 25 Hz
    2. 100 Hz
    3. 50 Hz
    4. 0 Hz
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) 50 Hz

    A half-wave rectifier passes one half of each cycle, so it gives one output pulse per input cycle: 50 Hz.

  13. Question 13 · 1 mark · Electric Charges and Fields
    Given below are two statements: one labelled Assertion (A) and the other labelled Reason (R). Select the correct answer from the options given below.
    Assertion (A): Two electric field lines never cross each other.
    Reason (R): If two field lines crossed, the electric field at the point of crossing would have two directions, which is not possible.

    1. Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
    2. Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation of Assertion.
    3. Assertion is true but Reason is false.
    4. Both Assertion and Reason are false.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.

    The field at a point has a single direction (along the tangent to the field line). Crossing would give two tangents. Both true; the Reason explains the Assertion.

  14. Question 14 · 1 mark · Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter
    Given below are two statements: one labelled Assertion (A) and the other labelled Reason (R). Select the correct answer from the options given below.
    Assertion (A): Photoelectric emission from a metal takes place only when the frequency of the incident light is above a certain minimum value.
    Reason (R): The number of photoelectrons emitted per second is directly proportional to the frequency of the incident light.

    1. Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
    2. Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation of Assertion.
    3. Assertion is true but Reason is false.
    4. Both Assertion and Reason are false.
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Assertion is true but Reason is false.

    Emission needs $h\nu\geq\phi_0$, so there is a threshold frequency: the Assertion is true. The number of photoelectrons depends on the intensity, not on the frequency, so the Reason is false.

  15. Question 15 · 1 mark · Wave Optics
    Given below are two statements: one labelled Assertion (A) and the other labelled Reason (R). Select the correct answer from the options given below.
    Assertion (A): In Young's double-slit experiment, if the light used is replaced by light of a longer wavelength, the fringes come closer together.
    Reason (R): The fringe width is inversely proportional to the wavelength of the light used.

    1. Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
    2. Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation of Assertion.
    3. Assertion is true but Reason is false.
    4. Both Assertion and Reason are false.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Both Assertion and Reason are false.

    $\beta=\frac{\lambda D}{d}$, so the fringe width is directly proportional to $\lambda$; a longer wavelength gives wider fringes. Both statements are false.

  16. Question 16 · 1 mark · Ray Optics and Optical Instruments
    Given below are two statements: one labelled Assertion (A) and the other labelled Reason (R). Select the correct answer from the options given below.
    Assertion (A): A ray of light passing through the optical centre of a thin lens emerges without deviation.
    Reason (R): The power of a lens is the reciprocal of its focal length expressed in metres.

    1. Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
    2. Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation of Assertion.
    3. Assertion is true but Reason is false.
    4. Both Assertion and Reason are false.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation of Assertion.

    Both statements are true. The ray through the optical centre is undeviated because the two faces there are parallel, like a thin slab; the definition of power does not explain this.

Section B: 2 marks each

  1. Question 17 · 2 marks · Current Electricity
    A copper wire of cross-sectional area $1.0\times10^{-6}$ m$^2$ carries a current of 1.6 A. The number density of free electrons in copper is $8\times10^{28}$ m$^{-3}$.
    (i) Calculate the drift speed of the electrons.
    (ii) The drift speed is very small, yet a bulb lights up almost as soon as the switch is closed. Why?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) $I=neAv_d$, so $v_d=\frac{I}{neA}=\frac{1.6}{8\times10^{28}\times1.6\times10^{-19}\times1.0\times10^{-6}}=\frac{1.6}{1.28\times10^{4}}=1.25\times10^{-4}$ m s$^{-1}$
    (ii) When the switch is closed, the electric field is set up throughout the circuit almost at the speed of light. Free electrons everywhere in the wire, including those in the bulb filament, start drifting at once, so current flows almost immediately.

    Marking scheme:

    • $v_d=\frac{I}{neA}$ (½ marks)
    • Substitution $v_d=\frac{1.6}{8\times10^{28}\times1.6\times10^{-19}\times1.0\times10^{-6}}$ (½ marks)
    • $v_d=1.25\times10^{-4}$ m s$^{-1}$ (½ marks)
    • Electric field is set up throughout the circuit almost instantly, so electrons everywhere start drifting at once (½ marks)

  2. Question 18 · 2 marks · Semiconductor Electronics
    Draw the I-V characteristics of a silicon p-n junction diode for both forward bias and reverse bias. Mark the threshold (knee) voltage. Why is the current in reverse bias very small?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: The graph has voltage on the x-axis and current on the y-axis.
    Forward bias (first quadrant): current is almost zero until about 0.7 V (the threshold or knee voltage for silicon), after which it rises steeply (current in mA).
    Reverse bias (third quadrant): a very small, nearly constant current (in $\mu$A) flows, almost independent of voltage, until breakdown.
    Reason: in reverse bias the current is due only to minority carriers (holes in n-side and electrons in p-side), which are very few.

    Marking scheme:

    • Forward characteristic: very small current till the knee, then a steep rise (½ marks)
    • Knee (threshold) voltage marked, about 0.7 V for silicon (½ marks)
    • Reverse characteristic: very small, nearly constant current (microampere range) (½ marks)
    • Reason: reverse current is due to the few minority carriers (½ marks)

  3. Question 19 · 2 marks · Magnetism and Matter
    Explain how the magnetisation of (i) a paramagnetic substance and (ii) a ferromagnetic substance is affected by a rise in temperature. Name the law or the temperature involved in each case.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Paramagnetic: the atomic dipoles tend to align with the field, but thermal agitation disturbs the alignment. As the temperature rises, the alignment and hence the magnetisation decrease. The susceptibility varies inversely with absolute temperature, $\chi\propto\frac{1}{T}$ (Curie's law).
    (ii) Ferromagnetic: as the temperature rises the magnetisation decreases, and above a certain temperature called the Curie temperature the domain structure breaks down and the substance becomes paramagnetic.

    Marking scheme:

    • Paramagnetic: thermal agitation disturbs alignment, so magnetisation decreases with rise in temperature (½ marks)
    • Curie's law $\chi\propto\frac{1}{T}$ (½ marks)
    • Ferromagnetic: magnetisation decreases with rise in temperature (½ marks)
    • Above the Curie temperature it becomes paramagnetic (½ marks)

  4. Question 20 · 2 marks · Electromagnetic Waves
    (i) Arrange the following in increasing order of frequency: gamma rays, microwaves, ultraviolet rays, infrared rays.
    (ii) Give one use each of microwaves and X-rays.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Microwaves, infrared rays, ultraviolet rays, gamma rays.
    (ii) Microwaves: radar systems / microwave ovens / mobile and satellite communication.
    X-rays: diagnosis in medicine (detecting fractures) / studying crystal structure.

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct order: microwaves, infrared, ultraviolet, gamma rays (1 mark)
    • One correct use of microwaves (½ marks)
    • One correct use of X-rays (½ marks)

  5. Question 21 · 2 marks · Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter
    An electron, starting from rest, is accelerated through a potential difference of 100 V.
    (i) Find its de Broglie wavelength.
    (ii) Through what potential difference must an electron be accelerated from rest so that its de Broglie wavelength is half of the value found in (i)?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) $\lambda=\frac{h}{\sqrt{2meV}}$. For an electron this gives $\lambda=\frac{1.227}{\sqrt{V}}$ nm, so $\lambda=\frac{1.227}{\sqrt{100}}$ nm $=0.1227$ nm $\approx1.23\times10^{-10}$ m.
    (ii) $\lambda\propto\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}$, so $\frac{\lambda'}{\lambda}=\sqrt{\frac{V}{V'}}=\frac{1}{2}$, which gives $V'=4V=4\times100=400$ V.

    Marking scheme:

    • $\lambda=\frac{h}{\sqrt{2meV}}$ (or $\lambda=\frac{1.227}{\sqrt{V}}$ nm) with $V=100$ V substituted (½ marks)
    • $\lambda\approx0.123$ nm ($1.23\times10^{-10}$ m) (½ marks)
    • $\lambda\propto\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}$, so halving $\lambda$ needs $V'=4V$ (½ marks)
    • $V'=400$ V (½ marks)

Section C: 3 marks each

  1. Question 22 · 3 marks · Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
    A point charge of $+3\ \mu$C is placed at the origin and a point charge of $-1\ \mu$C is placed at $x=40$ cm on the $x$-axis. Find all the points on the $x$-axis (at a finite distance) where the electric potential is zero.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: For zero potential at a point at distances $r_1$ and $r_2$ from the charges: $\frac{k(3\times10^{-6})}{r_1}+\frac{k(-1\times10^{-6})}{r_2}=0$, so $r_1=3r_2$.
    Between the charges, at $x$: $r_1=x$, $r_2=40-x$. Then $x=3(40-x)$, so $x=30$ cm.
    Beyond the negative charge, at $x>40$: $r_1=x$, $r_2=x-40$. Then $x=3(x-40)$, so $x=60$ cm.
    To the left of the origin, $r_1$ is less than $r_2$ always, so $r_1=3r_2$ is impossible: there is no such point.
    Potential is zero at $x=30$ cm and $x=60$ cm.

    Marking scheme:

    • Condition $\frac{kq_1}{r_1}+\frac{kq_2}{r_2}=0$, giving $r_1=3r_2$ (½ marks)
    • Point between the charges: x = 30 cm (1 mark)
    • Point beyond the negative charge: x = 60 cm (1 mark)
    • No point to the left of the origin (with reason) (½ marks)

  2. Question 23 · 3 marks · Current Electricity
    The four arms of a Wheatstone bridge are AB = $P$, BC = $Q$, AD = $R$ and DC = $S$. A cell is connected between A and C and a galvanometer between B and D. The values are $P=10\ \Omega$, $Q=20\ \Omega$, $R=15\ \Omega$ and $S=40\ \Omega$.
    (i) Is the bridge balanced? Give a reason.
    (ii) What resistance should be connected across $S$ (in parallel with it) to balance the bridge?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Balance condition: $\frac{P}{Q}=\frac{R}{S}$.
    $\frac{P}{Q}=\frac{10}{20}=0.5$ but $\frac{R}{S}=\frac{15}{40}=0.375$. These are not equal, so the bridge is not balanced.
    (ii) For balance, $S$ should be $S'=\frac{QR}{P}=\frac{20\times15}{10}=30\ \Omega$.
    A resistance $X$ in parallel with 40 $\Omega$: $\frac{40X}{40+X}=30$
    $40X=1200+30X$, so $X=120\ \Omega$.

    Marking scheme:

    • Balance condition $\frac{P}{Q}=\frac{R}{S}$ (½ marks)
    • Not balanced, with the ratios compared (0.5 and 0.375) (½ marks)
    • Required value of the arm $=30\ \Omega$ (½ marks)
    • Parallel combination $\frac{40X}{40+X}=30$ (½ marks)
    • $X=120\ \Omega$ (1 mark)

  3. Question 24 · 3 marks · Alternating Current
    An ideal step-down transformer reduces a supply voltage of 2200 V to 220 V. The primary coil has 3000 turns.
    (i) Find the number of turns in the secondary coil.
    (ii) If the current in the secondary is 10 A, find the current in the primary.
    (iii) Why is the core of a transformer laminated?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) $\frac{N_s}{N_p}=\frac{V_s}{V_p}$, so $N_s=3000\times\frac{220}{2200}=300$ turns.
    (ii) For an ideal transformer $V_pI_p=V_sI_s$, so $I_p=\frac{220\times10}{2200}=1$ A.
    (iii) The core is made of thin insulated sheets (laminations) to reduce eddy currents in it, and so reduce the energy lost as heat.

    Marking scheme:

    • $\frac{N_s}{N_p}=\frac{V_s}{V_p}$ (½ marks)
    • Ns = 300 (½ marks)
    • $V_pI_p=V_sI_s$ (½ marks)
    • Ip = 1 A (½ marks)
    • Lamination reduces eddy currents and hence energy loss as heat (1 mark)

  4. Question 25 · 3 marks · Atoms
    Using Bohr's postulates, obtain an expression for the radius of the $n$th orbit of the electron in a hydrogen atom.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: For the electron of mass $m$ and charge $e$ moving with speed $v$ in an orbit of radius $r$:
    Centripetal force is provided by the Coulomb force: $\frac{mv^2}{r}=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{e^2}{r^2}$ ...(1)
    Bohr's quantisation condition: $mvr=\frac{nh}{2\pi}$, so $v=\frac{nh}{2\pi mr}$ ...(2)
    Substituting (2) in (1): $m\frac{n^2h^2}{4\pi^2m^2r^2}\cdot\frac{1}{r}=\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}$
    $\frac{n^2h^2}{4\pi^2mr}=\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}$
    $r_n=\frac{\varepsilon_0n^2h^2}{\pi me^2}$, so $r_n\propto n^2$.

    Marking scheme:

    • Coulomb force provides centripetal force: $\frac{mv^2}{r}=\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}$ (½ marks)
    • Quantisation condition $mvr=\frac{nh}{2\pi}$ (½ marks)
    • Substituting $v=\frac{nh}{2\pi mr}$ in the force equation: $\frac{n^2h^2}{4\pi^2mr}=\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}$ (1 mark)
    • Final result $r_n=\frac{\varepsilon_0n^2h^2}{\pi me^2}$ (1 mark)

  5. Question 26 · 3 marks · Semiconductor Electronics
    With the help of a labelled circuit diagram, explain how a p-n junction diode works as a half-wave rectifier. Draw the input and output voltage-time graphs.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Circuit: the a.c. supply is applied to the primary of a transformer; the secondary is connected in series with a p-n junction diode and a load resistor $R_L$. The output is taken across $R_L$.
    Working: during the half cycle in which the diode is forward biased, it conducts and current flows through $R_L$, so a voltage appears across it. During the other half cycle the diode is reverse biased, practically no current flows and the output is zero. So current flows through the load in one direction only, during alternate half cycles.
    Graphs: input is a sinusoidal voltage; output consists of only the positive half cycles (one pulse per cycle), with zero output in the negative half cycles.

    Marking scheme:

    • Labelled circuit: transformer, diode and load resistor in series, output across the load (1 mark)
    • Working: diode conducts in forward-biased half cycle, blocks in reverse-biased half cycle (1 mark)
    • Input graph: sinusoidal a.c. voltage (½ marks)
    • Output graph: only alternate half cycles, zero in between (½ marks)

  6. Question 27 · 3 marks · Electromagnetic Induction
    A square coil of side 10 cm has 200 turns and a total resistance of 4 $\Omega$. It is placed with its plane perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field of 0.4 T. The field is reduced uniformly to zero in 0.2 s. Find
    (i) the emf induced in the coil,
    (ii) the current in the coil,
    (iii) the total charge that flows through the coil.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Initial flux linkage $N\Phi=NBA=200\times0.4\times(0.10)^2=0.8$ Wb (turns)
    (i) $|\varepsilon|=\frac{\Delta(N\Phi)}{\Delta t}=\frac{0.8}{0.2}=4$ V
    (ii) $I=\frac{\varepsilon}{R}=\frac{4}{4}=1$ A
    (iii) $q=I\Delta t=1\times0.2=0.2$ C (or $q=\frac{\Delta(N\Phi)}{R}=\frac{0.8}{4}=0.2$ C)

    Marking scheme:

    • Initial flux linkage NBA = 0.8 Wb (½ marks)
    • $|\varepsilon|=\frac{\Delta(N\Phi)}{\Delta t}$ (½ marks)
    • $\varepsilon=4$ V (½ marks)
    • I = 1 A (½ marks)
    • q = 0.2 C (1 mark)

  7. Question 28 · 3 marks · Ray Optics and Optical Instruments
    A small air bubble is inside a solid glass sphere of radius 10 cm and refractive index 1.5. The bubble is 4 cm from the centre of the sphere. Where does the bubble appear to be when it is seen from the side of the sphere nearest to it?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Distance of the bubble from the nearest surface $=10-4=6$ cm. Light goes from glass ($\mu_1=1.5$) to air ($\mu_2=1$).
    Taking the direction of light (towards the observer) as positive: $u=-6$ cm, and the centre of curvature lies on the glass side, so $R=-10$ cm.
    $\frac{\mu_2}{v}-\frac{\mu_1}{u}=\frac{\mu_2-\mu_1}{R}$
    $\frac{1}{v}-\frac{1.5}{-6}=\frac{1-1.5}{-10}$
    $\frac{1}{v}+0.25=0.05$, so $\frac{1}{v}=-0.2$ and $v=-5$ cm.
    The bubble appears 5 cm inside the sphere from the nearest surface (i.e. 1 cm closer to the surface than it really is).

    Marking scheme:

    • Formula $\frac{\mu_2}{v}-\frac{\mu_1}{u}=\frac{\mu_2-\mu_1}{R}$ (½ marks)
    • $u=-6$ cm (½ marks)
    • $R=-10$ cm with $\mu_1=1.5$, $\mu_2=1$ (½ marks)
    • $v=-5$ cm (1 mark)
    • Interpretation: appears 5 cm inside from the nearest surface (½ marks)

Section D: case-based, 4 marks each

  1. Question 29 · 4 marks · Ray Optics and Optical Instruments
    High-speed internet reaches many homes through optical fibres. An optical fibre has a thin core of glass surrounded by a layer called the cladding, which has a slightly lower refractive index. Light entering one end of the core strikes the core-cladding boundary at angles larger than the critical angle, so it is totally reflected again and again and travels along the fibre with very little loss, even when the fibre is gently bent. In a certain fibre, the refractive index of the core is 1.50 and that of the cladding is 1.20.

    (i) State the two conditions needed for total internal reflection. [1]

    (ii) Find the sine of the critical angle at the core-cladding boundary. [1]

    (iii) Find the speed of light in the core. [1]

    (iv) Why must the refractive index of the cladding be less than that of the core? [1]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) State the two conditions needed for total internal reflection.

    Model answer: 1. Light must travel from an optically denser medium to an optically rarer medium.
    2. The angle of incidence in the denser medium must be greater than the critical angle.

    Marking scheme:

    • Light goes from denser to rarer medium (½ marks)
    • Angle of incidence greater than the critical angle (½ marks)

    (ii) Find the sine of the critical angle at the core-cladding boundary.

    Model answer: $\sin C=\frac{\mu_{cladding}}{\mu_{core}}=\frac{1.20}{1.50}=0.8$ (so $C\approx53^\circ$).

    Marking scheme:

    • $\sin C=\frac{\mu_{cladding}}{\mu_{core}}$ (½ marks)
    • $\sin C=0.8$ (½ marks)

    (iii) Find the speed of light in the core.

    Model answer: $v=\frac{c}{\mu}=\frac{3\times10^{8}}{1.50}=2\times10^{8}$ m s$^{-1}$

    Marking scheme:

    • $v=\frac{c}{\mu}$ (½ marks)
    • $v=2\times10^{8}$ m s$^{-1}$ (½ marks)

    (iv) Why must the refractive index of the cladding be less than that of the core?

    Model answer: Total internal reflection can take place only when light goes from a denser to a rarer medium. So the cladding must be optically rarer than the core for light to be totally reflected at the core-cladding boundary and stay inside the core.

    Marking scheme:

    • Total internal reflection needs light going from denser (core) to rarer (cladding) medium, so light stays in the core (1 mark)

  2. Question 30 · 4 marks · Nuclei
    Nuclear power stations obtain energy from the fission of heavy nuclei such as uranium-235, while the Sun obtains its energy from the fusion of light nuclei such as hydrogen. In both processes the total mass of the products is slightly less than the total mass of the reacting nuclei, and the missing mass appears as energy according to Einstein's relation $E=mc^2$. The graph of binding energy per nucleon against mass number rises steeply for light nuclei, has a broad maximum of about 8.7 MeV near mass number 56, and falls slowly to about 7.6 MeV for very heavy nuclei. The nucleons are held together by the nuclear force, which is very strong but acts only over very short distances.

    (i) Using the binding energy curve, explain why the fission of a heavy nucleus releases energy. [1]

    (ii) Calculate the energy that would be obtained if 1 g of matter were completely converted into energy. [1]

    (iii) The mass defect of a certain nucleus is 0.1 u. Find its binding energy in MeV. [1]

    (iv) Protons in a nucleus repel each other. Which property of the nuclear force keeps the nucleus stable? [1]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Using the binding energy curve, explain why the fission of a heavy nucleus releases energy.

    Model answer: The fission fragments are medium-mass nuclei, which have a higher binding energy per nucleon (about 8.5 MeV) than the heavy nucleus (about 7.6 MeV). So the nucleons become more tightly bound, and the difference in total binding energy is released.

    Marking scheme:

    • Fragments have higher binding energy per nucleon than the heavy nucleus, so the difference is released (1 mark)

    (ii) Calculate the energy that would be obtained if 1 g of matter were completely converted into energy.

    Model answer: $E=mc^2=1\times10^{-3}\times(3\times10^{8})^2=9\times10^{13}$ J

    Marking scheme:

    • $E=mc^2$ with $m=10^{-3}$ kg (½ marks)
    • $E=9\times10^{13}$ J (½ marks)

    (iii) The mass defect of a certain nucleus is 0.1 u. Find its binding energy in MeV.

    Model answer: $B=\Delta m\times931.5$ MeV $=0.1\times931.5=93.15$ MeV

    Marking scheme:

    • $B=\Delta m\times931.5$ MeV (½ marks)
    • B = 93.15 MeV (½ marks)

    (iv) Protons in a nucleus repel each other. Which property of the nuclear force keeps the nucleus stable?

    Model answer: The nuclear force is a strong attractive force between nucleons (proton-proton, neutron-neutron and proton-neutron). Within nuclear distances (a few femtometres) it is much stronger than the Coulomb repulsion between protons, so it holds the nucleus together.

    Marking scheme:

    • Nuclear force is attractive and, at short range, much stronger than the Coulomb repulsion (1 mark)

  1. Question 31 · 5 marks · Electric Charges and Fields
    (i) State Gauss's law in electrostatics. Use it to obtain an expression for the electric field at a distance $r$ from an infinitely long straight wire with uniform linear charge density $\lambda$.
    (ii) A long straight wire carries a uniform linear charge density of $2\ \mu$C m$^{-1}$. Find the electric field at a distance of 9 cm from the wire, and the magnitude of the force on an electron placed at that point.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Gauss's law: the total electric flux through a closed surface is $\frac{1}{\varepsilon_0}$ times the total charge enclosed by it: $\oint\vec{E}\cdot d\vec{S}=\frac{q_{enc}}{\varepsilon_0}$.
    Take a cylindrical Gaussian surface of radius $r$ and length $l$, coaxial with the wire. By symmetry $\vec{E}$ is radial and has the same magnitude at all points of the curved surface.
    Flux through the flat ends is zero, since $\vec{E}$ is parallel to them. Flux through the curved surface $=E\times2\pi rl$.
    Charge enclosed $=\lambda l$. So $E\times2\pi rl=\frac{\lambda l}{\varepsilon_0}$, giving $E=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0r}$.
    (ii) $E=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0r}=\frac{2\times9\times10^{9}\times2\times10^{-6}}{0.09}=4\times10^{5}$ N C$^{-1}$
    $F=eE=1.6\times10^{-19}\times4\times10^{5}=6.4\times10^{-14}$ N (directed towards the wire).

    Marking scheme:

    • Statement of Gauss's law (1 mark)
    • Cylindrical Gaussian surface coaxial with the wire; E radial and uniform on curved surface (½ marks)
    • Zero flux through the flat ends; flux through curved surface = E(2πrl) (½ marks)
    • Charge enclosed $\lambda l$; $E\cdot2\pi rl=\frac{\lambda l}{\varepsilon_0}$ (½ marks)
    • $E=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0r}$ (½ marks)
    • Substitution in $E=\frac{2k\lambda}{r}$ (½ marks)
    • $E=4\times10^{5}$ N C$^{-1}$ (½ marks)
    • $F=eE$ (½ marks)
    • $F=6.4\times10^{-14}$ N (½ marks)

  2. Question 32 · 5 marks · Wave Optics
    (i) Define a wavefront. What is the shape of the wavefront (a) of light diverging from a nearby point source and (b) of light emerging from a convex lens when a point source is at its focus?
    (ii) Using Huygens' principle, draw the refracted wavefront for a plane wavefront travelling from an optically denser medium to an optically rarer medium. Hence obtain Snell's law of refraction.
    (iii) Light of wavelength 600 nm in air enters glass of refractive index 1.5. Find its wavelength and frequency in glass.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) A wavefront is the locus of all points that oscillate in the same phase. (a) Spherical (diverging). (b) Plane.
    (ii) Diagram: plane wavefront AB meets the surface PP' at A at angle of incidence $i$. Speeds are $v_1$ in the denser medium and $v_2$ in the rarer medium ($v_2>v_1$). In time $t$, B reaches C on the surface, with $BC=v_1t$. Meanwhile a secondary wavelet from A spreads into the rarer medium with radius $AE=v_2t$. The tangent CE from C to this wavelet is the refracted wavefront, at angle $r$ to the surface; since $v_2>v_1$, $r>i$ (the ray bends away from the normal).
    From triangles ABC and AEC: $\sin i=\frac{BC}{AC}=\frac{v_1t}{AC}$ and $\sin r=\frac{AE}{AC}=\frac{v_2t}{AC}$.
    So $\frac{\sin i}{\sin r}=\frac{v_1}{v_2}$. With $n_1=\frac{c}{v_1}$ and $n_2=\frac{c}{v_2}$: $\frac{v_1}{v_2}=\frac{n_2}{n_1}$, so $n_1\sin i=n_2\sin r$, which is Snell's law.
    (iii) Frequency is unchanged: $\nu=\frac{c}{\lambda}=\frac{3\times10^{8}}{600\times10^{-9}}=5\times10^{14}$ Hz.
    Wavelength in glass $=\frac{\lambda}{\mu}=\frac{600}{1.5}=400$ nm.

    Marking scheme:

    • Definition of wavefront (½ marks)
    • (a) spherical and (b) plane (½ marks)
    • Diagram: incident wavefront, wavelet of radius v2t from A, refracted wavefront CE bending away from normal (1 mark)
    • $\sin i=\frac{v_1t}{AC}$ and $\sin r=\frac{v_2t}{AC}$ (1 mark)
    • $\frac{\sin i}{\sin r}=\frac{v_1}{v_2}=\frac{n_2}{n_1}$, giving $n_1\sin i=n_2\sin r$ (1 mark)
    • Frequency in glass $=5\times10^{14}$ Hz (unchanged) (½ marks)
    • Wavelength in glass = 400 nm (½ marks)

  3. Question 33 · 5 marks · Moving Charges and Magnetism
    (i) A rectangular coil of $N$ turns and area $A$ carries a current $I$ in a uniform magnetic field $B$. The normal to the plane of the coil makes an angle $\theta$ with the field. Obtain an expression for the torque on the coil.
    (ii) In a moving coil galvanometer, why is (a) the magnetic field made radial and (b) a soft iron core placed inside the coil? Define the current sensitivity of a galvanometer.
    (iii) A rectangular coil of 100 turns and area $2\times10^{-3}$ m$^2$ carries a current of 0.5 A. It is placed in a uniform magnetic field of 0.2 T with its plane parallel to the field. Find the torque on the coil.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) Let the coil have sides $a$ and $b$, with the sides of length $b$ perpendicular to $\vec{B}$. The forces on the two sides of length $a$ are equal, opposite and along the same line, so they cancel. Each side of length $b$ experiences a force $F=IbB$; these two forces are equal and opposite but along different lines, forming a couple.
    Perpendicular distance between them $=a\sin\theta$. Torque on one turn $\tau=IbB\times a\sin\theta=IAB\sin\theta$.
    For $N$ turns: $\tau=NIAB\sin\theta$, or $\vec{\tau}=\vec{m}\times\vec{B}$ with $m=NIA$.
    (ii) (a) In a radial field the plane of the coil is always parallel to the field, so $\theta=90^\circ$ and the torque $NIAB$ is proportional to $I$. The deflection is then proportional to the current, giving a linear (uniform) scale.
    (b) The soft iron core increases the strength of the magnetic field (and helps make it radial), which increases the sensitivity.
    Current sensitivity is the deflection per unit current: $\frac{\phi}{I}=\frac{NAB}{k}$, where $k$ is the torsional constant of the spring.
    (iii) Plane parallel to the field means $\theta=90^\circ$:
    $\tau=NIAB=100\times0.5\times2\times10^{-3}\times0.2=0.02$ N m

    Marking scheme:

    • Forces on the two sides of length a cancel; forces IbB on the two sides of length b form a couple (½ marks)
    • Arm of the couple $=a\sin\theta$ (½ marks)
    • $\tau=IAB\sin\theta$ for one turn (½ marks)
    • $\tau=NIAB\sin\theta$ (or $\vec{\tau}=\vec{m}\times\vec{B}$) (½ marks)
    • Radial field keeps torque proportional to current, giving a linear scale (½ marks)
    • Soft iron core increases the field strength and hence the sensitivity (½ marks)
    • Current sensitivity = deflection per unit current, $\frac{\phi}{I}$ (the expression $\frac{NAB}{k}$ is not required) (1 mark)
    • $\tau=NIAB$ with $\theta=90^\circ$ (½ marks)
    • Torque = 0.02 N m (½ marks)
