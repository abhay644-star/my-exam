Practice questions with answers

A few questions from this chapter. Open each one to see the answer.

  1. Amoeba reproduces asexually by ______.

    1. fragmentation
    2. spore formation
    3. budding
    4. binary fission
    Show answer

    Answer: binary fission

    Amoeba splits into two daughter cells by binary fission.

  2. The fusion of a male gamete with a female gamete is called ______.

    1. germination
    2. pollination
    3. fission
    4. fertilisation
    Show answer

    Answer: fertilisation

    Fertilisation forms a zygote that develops into a new organism.

  3. After fertilisation in a flower, the ovary develops into the ______.

    1. stamen
    2. fruit
    3. seed
    4. petal
    Show answer

    Answer: fruit

    The ovary grows into a fruit, and the ovules inside it become seeds.

  4. Yeast reproduces asexually by ______.

    1. budding
    2. fragmentation
    3. binary fission
    4. spore formation
    Show answer

    Answer: budding

    A small bud grows out of the parent yeast cell and separates.

  5. The malaria parasite, Plasmodium, divides into many daughter cells at the same time. This is called ______ fission.

    1. binary
    2. simple
    3. longitudinal
    4. multiple
    Show answer

    Answer: multiple

    In multiple fission one cell divides into many; in binary fission it divides into two.

  6. In human males, the sperm are produced in the ______.

    1. seminal vesicles
    2. vas deferens
    3. prostate gland
    4. testes
    Show answer

    Answer: testes

    The testes also make the hormone testosterone.

  7. Bread mould (Rhizopus) reproduces by ______ formation.

    1. spore
    2. bud
    3. seed
    4. gamete
    Show answer

    Answer: spore

    Spores are formed in sporangia; they have thick walls and grow when they reach a moist surface.

  8. Spirogyra breaks up into smaller pieces, each of which grows into a new individual. This is called ______.

    1. regeneration
    2. budding
    3. fission
    4. fragmentation
    Show answer

    Answer: fragmentation

    Fragmentation is common in simple multicellular organisms such as Spirogyra.
