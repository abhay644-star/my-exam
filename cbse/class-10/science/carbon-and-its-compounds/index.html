A few questions from this chapter. Open each one to see the answer.

  1. Compounds made up of only carbon and hydrogen are called ______.

    1. esters
    2. alcohols
    3. hydrocarbons
    4. carbohydrates
    Show answer

    Answer: hydrocarbons

    Methane, ethane and ethene are hydrocarbons.

  2. What is the general formula of alkanes?

    1. C2nHn
    2. CnHn
    3. CnH2n
    4. CnH2n+2
    Show answer

    Answer: CnH2n+2

    Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons with the general formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.

  3. How many single covalent bonds are present in a molecule of methane (CH4)?

    1. 2
    2. 5
    3. 4
    4. 8
    Show answer

    Answer: 4

    Carbon shares one electron pair with each of the four hydrogen atoms.

  4. A carbon atom has four electrons in its outermost shell. What is the valency of carbon?

    1. 6
    2. 8
    3. 4
    4. 2
    Show answer

    Answer: 4

    Carbon shares its four valence electrons to form four covalent bonds, so it is tetravalent.

  5. The –OH functional group is present in which class of carbon compounds?

    1. carboxylic acids
    2. alcohols
    3. aldehydes
    4. ketones
    Show answer

    Answer: alcohols

    Ethanol (C₂H₅OH) is an alcohol containing the –OH group.

  6. What is the IUPAC name of CH3COOH?

    1. methanoic acid
    2. ethanoic acid
    3. ethanal
    4. ethanol
    Show answer

    Answer: ethanoic acid

    It has two carbon atoms (eth-) and the carboxylic acid group (-oic acid). Its common name is acetic acid.

  7. Compounds that have the same molecular formula but different structures are called ______.

    1. allotropes
    2. isotopes
    3. isomers
    4. homologues
    Show answer

    Answer: isomers

    For example, butane (C4H10) has a straight-chain and a branched-chain isomer.

  8. The ability of carbon atoms to bond with other carbon atoms and form long chains is called ______.

    1. isomerism
    2. catenation
    3. hydrogenation
    4. tetravalency
    Show answer

    Answer: catenation

    Catenation and tetravalency together explain the very large number of carbon compounds.
