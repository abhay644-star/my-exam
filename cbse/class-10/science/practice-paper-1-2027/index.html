Section A: Biology

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Life Processes
    The stomach of a person produces very little hydrochloric acid. Which step of digestion is most directly affected?

    1. Breakdown of starch by salivary amylase
    2. Action of pepsin on proteins
    3. Emulsification of fats by bile
    4. Absorption of water in the large intestine
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Action of pepsin on proteins

    Pepsin, the protein-digesting enzyme of the gastric juice, works only in the acidic medium created by hydrochloric acid. With little acid, protein digestion in the stomach is affected.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Life Processes
    On a bright, breezy morning, a leafy plant loses a large amount of water vapour from its leaves. What is the main effect of this water loss on the plant?

    1. It stops the upward movement of water in the xylem.
    2. It moves food from the leaves to the roots through the phloem.
    3. It makes root pressure the main force for the movement of water.
    4. It creates a suction that pulls water up through the xylem from the roots.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) It creates a suction that pulls water up through the xylem from the roots.

    Loss of water vapour from the leaves (transpiration) creates a suction (transpiration pull) that draws water up through the xylem. During the day this is the main force for the upward movement of water.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · How Do Organisms Reproduce
    Which of the following plants has lost the capacity to produce seeds and is therefore grown by vegetative propagation?

    1. Banana
    2. Mustard
    3. Pea
    4. Wheat
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Banana

    Banana plants have lost the capacity to produce seeds, so they are grown from vegetative parts. Mustard, pea and wheat are grown from seeds.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · How Do Organisms Reproduce
    A Planaria is cut into three pieces. After some days, each piece grows into a complete Planaria. This process is called:

    1. fragmentation
    2. regeneration
    3. budding
    4. binary fission
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) regeneration

    When a fully differentiated organism is cut into pieces and each piece grows into a complete organism, the process is regeneration. In fragmentation the organism breaks up on its own.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · How Do Organisms Reproduce
    Which of the following methods of preventing pregnancy also helps to protect against sexually transmitted diseases?

    1. Oral pills
    2. Copper-T
    3. Condom
    4. Tubectomy
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Condom

    A condom is a mechanical barrier that stops the sperm from reaching the egg and also prevents the transfer of infections during sexual contact. The other methods do not give this protection.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Heredity
    A couple already has two daughters. What is the chance that their third child will be a son?

    1. 0%
    2. 25%
    3. 50%
    4. 100%
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) 50%

    Each child is decided separately. The mother always gives an X chromosome; the father gives an X or a Y with equal chance. So the chance of a son (XY) is 50% for every child, whatever the earlier children are.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Our Environment
    Which of the following groups contains only biodegradable substances?

    1. Paper, cotton cloth, fruit peels
    2. Glass, paper, cow dung
    3. Polythene, wood, wool
    4. Aluminium foil, cow dung, grass
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Paper, cotton cloth, fruit peels

    Paper, cotton cloth and fruit peels are broken down by bacteria and other decomposers. Glass, polythene and aluminium foil are non-biodegradable.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Life Processes
    The following question consists of two statements: Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Answer it by selecting the appropriate option.
    Assertion (A): Arteries have thick, elastic walls.
    Reason (R): Blood flows in the arteries under high pressure as it is pumped out of the heart.

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

    Both statements are true. Arteries carry blood away from the heart under high pressure, which is why they need thick, elastic walls. So R correctly explains A.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Our Environment
    The following question consists of two statements: Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Answer it by selecting the appropriate option.
    Assertion (A): Ozone present in the upper atmosphere is harmful to living organisms.
    Reason (R): Ozone in the upper atmosphere shields the surface of the Earth from the ultraviolet radiation of the Sun.

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) A is false but R is true.

    Ozone at ground level is poisonous, but the ozone layer in the upper atmosphere is useful because it absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation. So A is false and R is true.

  10. Question 10 · 2 marks · Life Processes
    (a) What is the role of guard cells in a leaf?
    (b) Why do the stomata of many plants close on a hot, dry afternoon?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Guard cells surround each stomatal pore and control its opening and closing. When water flows into the guard cells they swell and the pore opens; when they lose water they shrink and the pore closes.
    (b) On a hot, dry afternoon the plant would lose a large amount of water by transpiration through open stomata. Closing the stomata reduces this water loss when the plant does not need the pores open for gaseous exchange.

    Marking scheme:

    • Guard cells open and close the stomatal pore (½ marks)
    • Swelling of guard cells (water flows in) opens the pore; shrinking (water lost) closes it (½ marks)
    • Closing the stomata reduces loss of water by transpiration in hot, dry conditions (1 mark)

  11. Question 11 · 2 marks · Control and Coordination
    A person touches a very hot plate and pulls the hand back even before feeling the pain.
    (a) Name this kind of action and the part of the nervous system where it is controlled.
    (b) Write, in the correct order, the path followed by the message in this action.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) It is a reflex action. It is controlled by the spinal cord.
    (b) Receptor in the skin of the hand $\rightarrow$ sensory neuron $\rightarrow$ relay neuron in the spinal cord $\rightarrow$ motor neuron $\rightarrow$ effector (muscles of the arm), which pull the hand away.

    Marking scheme:

    • Names it as a reflex action (½ marks)
    • Spinal cord (½ marks)
    • Correct order: receptor, sensory neuron, relay neuron in spinal cord, motor neuron, effector muscle (1 mark)

  12. Question 12 · 2 marks · How Do Organisms Reproduce
    (a) State two functions of the testes in human males.
    (b) Why are the testes located in the scrotum, outside the abdominal cavity?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) The testes produce the male germ cells (sperms). They also secrete the hormone testosterone, which brings about the changes in appearance seen in boys at puberty.
    (b) Sperm formation needs a temperature lower than the normal body temperature. The scrotum, outside the abdominal cavity, keeps the testes at this lower temperature.

    Marking scheme:

    • Production of sperms (male germ cells) (½ marks)
    • Secretion of the hormone testosterone (½ marks)
    • Sperm formation needs a temperature lower than normal body temperature; the scrotum provides it (1 mark)

  13. Question 13 · 3 marks · Control and Coordination
    A potted seedling is kept in a room where light enters only through one window.
    (a) In which direction will the shoot bend? Name this kind of movement.
    (b) Explain the role of auxin in this bending.
    (c) Name the plant hormone that (i) promotes cell division and (ii) inhibits growth and causes wilting of leaves.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) The shoot bends towards the window (towards light). This movement is (positive) phototropism.
    (b) Auxin is made at the tip of the shoot. When light falls from one side, auxin diffuses towards the shaded side of the shoot. The higher concentration of auxin makes the cells on the shaded side grow longer, so the shoot bends towards the light.
    (c) (i) Cytokinins (ii) Abscisic acid

    Marking scheme:

    • Shoot bends towards the window (light) (½ marks)
    • Phototropism (½ marks)
    • Auxin moves to the shaded side; cells there elongate more, so the shoot bends towards light (1 mark)
    • (i) Cytokinin (½ marks)
    • (ii) Abscisic acid (½ marks)

  14. Question 14 · 3 marks · Our Environment
    Study the food chain in a grassland:
    Grass $\rightarrow$ Grasshopper $\rightarrow$ Frog $\rightarrow$ Snake $\rightarrow$ Hawk
    (a) The grass stores 20,000 J of energy. Using the 10 per cent law, find the energy available to the snake.
    (b) Why is the number of hawks in the grassland much smaller than the number of grasshoppers?
    (c) A non-biodegradable pesticide is sprayed on the grass. Which organism in this chain will have the highest concentration of the pesticide? Name the phenomenon.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Grass 20,000 J $\rightarrow$ grasshopper 2,000 J $\rightarrow$ frog 200 J $\rightarrow$ snake 20 J. Energy available to the snake = 20 J.
    (b) Only about 10% of the energy is passed on from one trophic level to the next. The hawk is at the top level, so very little energy reaches it and this can support only a small number of hawks.
    (c) The hawk. The phenomenon is biological magnification.

    Marking scheme:

    • Energy available to the snake = 20 J (1 mark)
    • Only about 10% of energy passes to the next level, so less energy at the top level supports fewer hawks (1 mark)
    • Hawk (½ marks)
    • Biological magnification (½ marks)

  15. Question 15 · 4 marks · Heredity
    Rohit studied two traits of pea seeds. Round seed shape (R) is dominant over wrinkled seed shape (r), and yellow seed colour (Y) is dominant over green seed colour (y). He crossed a pure-breeding plant having round green seeds (RRyy) with a pure-breeding plant having wrinkled yellow seeds (rrYY). The F1 plants were then self-pollinated to obtain the F2 generation.

    (i) Write the genotype and the phenotype of the F1 plants. [1]

    (ii) Out of 160 F2 plants, how many are expected to have round green seeds? [1]

    (iii) Some F2 plants had wrinkled green seeds, and some had round yellow seeds, though neither of the two original (pure-breeding) parent plants had these combinations. What does this show? Explain. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Write the genotype and the phenotype of the F1 plants.

    Model answer: Genotype: RrYy. Phenotype: round seeds with yellow colour.

    Marking scheme:

    • Genotype RrYy (½ marks)
    • Phenotype: round and yellow seeds (½ marks)

    (ii) Out of 160 F2 plants, how many are expected to have round green seeds?

    Model answer: In a dihybrid cross the F2 ratio is 9 round yellow : 3 round green : 3 wrinkled yellow : 1 wrinkled green. Round green = 3/16 of 160 = 30 plants.

    Marking scheme:

    • Fraction of round green = 3/16 (½ marks)
    • Number = 30 plants (½ marks)

    (iii) Some F2 plants had wrinkled green seeds, and some had round yellow seeds, though neither of the two original (pure-breeding) parent plants had these combinations. What does this show? Explain.

    Model answer: It shows that the traits of seed shape and seed colour are inherited independently of each other. During gamete formation the factors for shape and the factors for colour separate independently, so in the F2 generation they come together in new combinations such as round yellow and wrinkled green, which were not present in the original parent plants.

    Marking scheme:

    • Seed shape and seed colour are inherited independently (1 mark)
    • Factors separate independently in gametes, giving new combinations (round yellow, wrinkled green) in F2 (1 mark)

  16. Question 16 · 5 marks · Life Processes
    (a) Name the filtration unit of the human kidney.
    (b) Explain how urine is formed in this unit and how it leaves the kidney.
    (c) State any two ways in which plants get rid of their waste products.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Nephron.
    (b) 1. Filtration: In the glomerulus (a cluster of thin-walled blood capillaries) inside the cup-shaped Bowman's capsule, blood is filtered. Water, glucose, amino acids, salts and urea pass into the capsule; blood cells and proteins stay in the blood.
    2. Reabsorption: As the filtrate flows along the tubule, useful substances such as glucose, amino acids, salts and a major amount of water are selectively reabsorbed into the surrounding blood capillaries. The amount of water reabsorbed depends on how much excess water the body has.
    3. The remaining fluid, urine (water with urea and other wastes), passes from the tubules into the collecting duct, then through the ureter to the urinary bladder, where it is stored until it is passed out through the urethra.
    (c) Any two: waste stored in leaves that fall off; waste stored in cell vacuoles; waste stored as resins and gums in old xylem; some waste excreted into the soil around the roots; excess water removed by transpiration.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Nephron (1 mark)
    • (b) Filtration of blood in the glomerulus into Bowman's capsule (water, glucose, salts, urea pass) (1 mark)
    • (b) Selective reabsorption of glucose, amino acids, salts and much water in the tubule (1 mark)
    • (b) Urine goes through the collecting duct and ureter to the urinary bladder (1 mark)
    • (c) First correct way (for example, stored in leaves that fall off) (½ marks)
    • (c) Second correct way (for example, resins and gums in old xylem, or vacuoles) (½ marks)

Section B: Chemistry

  1. Question 17 · 1 mark · Chemical Reactions and Equations
    A strip of zinc is dipped in blue copper sulphate solution. After some time the blue colour fades and a reddish-brown deposit forms on the zinc. Which option correctly gives the type of reaction and the substance deposited?

    1. Combination reaction; copper
    2. Double displacement reaction; zinc sulphate
    3. Displacement reaction; zinc
    4. Displacement reaction; copper
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Displacement reaction; copper

    Zinc is more reactive than copper, so it displaces copper: $\mathrm{Zn + CuSO_4 \rightarrow ZnSO_4 + Cu}$. The reddish-brown deposit is copper and the reaction is a displacement reaction.

  2. Question 18 · 1 mark · Chemical Reactions and Equations
    In the reaction $\mathrm{Fe_2O_3 + 2Al \rightarrow 2Fe + Al_2O_3}$, which substance is oxidised and which is reduced?

    1. Al is reduced and $\mathrm{Fe_2O_3}$ is oxidised.
    2. Al is oxidised and $\mathrm{Fe_2O_3}$ is reduced.
    3. Fe is oxidised and $\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$ is reduced.
    4. $\mathrm{Fe_2O_3}$ is both oxidised and reduced.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Al is oxidised and $\mathrm{Fe_2O_3}$ is reduced.

    Aluminium gains oxygen to form $\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$, so it is oxidised. $\mathrm{Fe_2O_3}$ loses oxygen to form Fe, so it is reduced.

  3. Question 19 · 1 mark · Acids, Bases and Salts
    Four solutions P, Q, R and S have pH values 2, 6, 9 and 13 respectively. Which option arranges them in increasing order of hydroxide ion concentration?

    1. S, R, Q, P
    2. Q, P, R, S
    3. P, Q, S, R
    4. P, Q, R, S
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) P, Q, R, S

    The higher the pH, the higher the hydroxide ion concentration. Increasing pH: P (2), Q (6), R (9), S (13).

  4. Question 20 · 1 mark · Acids, Bases and Salts
    A compound X is used as an antacid. On heating, it gives off a gas that turns lime water milky. X is:

    1. washing soda
    2. bleaching powder
    3. plaster of Paris
    4. baking soda
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) baking soda

    Baking soda (sodium hydrogencarbonate) is mildly basic, so it is used as an antacid. On heating it gives carbon dioxide: $\mathrm{2NaHCO_3 \rightarrow Na_2CO_3 + H_2O + CO_2}$.

  5. Question 21 · 1 mark · Metals and Non-metals
    A metal M does not react with cold water or hot water, but it reacts with steam to form a metal oxide and hydrogen. M is most likely:

    1. sodium
    2. potassium
    3. iron
    4. copper
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) iron

    Sodium and potassium react violently even with cold water, and copper does not react even with steam. Iron reacts only with steam: $\mathrm{3Fe + 4H_2O \rightarrow Fe_3O_4 + 4H_2}$.

  6. Question 22 · 1 mark · Carbon and Its Compounds
    Which of the following hydrocarbons will undergo an addition reaction with hydrogen in the presence of a nickel catalyst?

    1. $\mathrm{C_2H_6}$
    2. $\mathrm{C_3H_8}$
    3. $\mathrm{C_2H_4}$
    4. $\mathrm{CH_4}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $\mathrm{C_2H_4}$

    Addition of hydrogen takes place only in unsaturated hydrocarbons. $\mathrm{C_2H_4}$ (ethene) has a carbon-carbon double bond; the other three are saturated alkanes.

  7. Question 23 · 1 mark · Chemical Reactions and Equations
    Which of the following is an endothermic process?

    1. Respiration
    2. Burning of natural gas
    3. Decomposition of calcium carbonate into lime and carbon dioxide on heating
    4. Reaction of quicklime with water
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Decomposition of calcium carbonate into lime and carbon dioxide on heating

    Decomposition of calcium carbonate needs a continuous supply of heat, so it is endothermic. Respiration, burning of natural gas and the reaction of quicklime with water all release heat.

  12. Question 28 · 4 marks · Acids, Bases and Salts
    A farmer gets the soil of his field tested. The report shows that the soil has a pH of 5.0, and the agriculture officer advises him to treat the soil before sowing. At home, his daughter reads that tooth enamel starts to get corroded when the pH in the mouth falls below 5.5, and that the sting of a honey-bee injects an acid into the skin, which causes pain and irritation.

    (i) Is the soil of the field acidic or basic? Name one substance the farmer can add to correct it. [1]

    (ii) How does brushing the teeth with toothpaste help to prevent tooth decay? [1]

    (iii) Which of these will give relief from a bee sting: baking soda solution or vinegar? Give a reason and name the type of reaction involved. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Is the soil of the field acidic or basic? Name one substance the farmer can add to correct it.

    Model answer: The soil is acidic (pH less than 7). He can add quicklime (calcium oxide), slaked lime (calcium hydroxide) or chalk (calcium carbonate).

    Marking scheme:

    • Acidic (½ marks)
    • Any one: quicklime, slaked lime or chalk (½ marks)

    (ii) How does brushing the teeth with toothpaste help to prevent tooth decay?

    Model answer: Bacteria in the mouth produce acids from food particles left in the mouth. Toothpastes are generally basic, so they neutralise the excess acid and keep the pH from falling below 5.5.

    Marking scheme:

    • Toothpaste is basic and neutralises the acid produced in the mouth (1 mark)

    (iii) Which of these will give relief from a bee sting: baking soda solution or vinegar? Give a reason and name the type of reaction involved.

    Model answer: Baking soda solution. It is a mild base and neutralises the acid injected by the bee, which reduces the pain. The reaction is a neutralisation reaction: acid + base $\rightarrow$ salt + water.

    Marking scheme:

    • Baking soda solution (½ marks)
    • Reason: it is a (mild) base and neutralises the acid of the sting (½ marks)
    • Neutralisation reaction (acid + base gives salt + water) (1 mark)

  13. Question 29 · 5 marks · Metals and Non-metals
    (a) Show the formation of sodium oxide ($\mathrm{Na_2O}$) from sodium and oxygen atoms by the transfer of electrons. (Atomic numbers: Na = 11, O = 8)
    (b) Why do ionic compounds conduct electricity in the molten state but not in the solid state?
    (c) Zinc blende (ZnS) is an ore of zinc. Name the process used to convert it into zinc oxide, and write the chemical equations for this process and for the reduction of zinc oxide with carbon.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Electronic configurations: Na 2, 8, 1; O 2, 6.
    Each of the two sodium atoms loses its one outermost electron to the oxygen atom: $\mathrm{2Na \rightarrow 2Na^+ + 2e^-}$ and $\mathrm{O + 2e^- \rightarrow O^{2-}}$. The oxygen atom gains two electrons to complete its octet (2, 8) and each sodium ion has the configuration 2, 8. The oppositely charged ions are held together by electrostatic attraction: $\mathrm{2Na^+ + O^{2-} \rightarrow Na_2O}$, written $\mathrm{(Na^+)_2O^{2-}}$.
    (b) Electricity is carried by the movement of ions. In the solid state the ions are held in fixed positions by strong forces and cannot move. In the molten state the ions are free to move, so the compound conducts electricity.
    (c) Roasting (heating strongly in excess air).
    $\mathrm{2ZnS + 3O_2 \xrightarrow{\text{heat}} 2ZnO + 2SO_2}$
    $\mathrm{ZnO + C \rightarrow Zn + CO}$

    Marking scheme:

    • Electronic configurations Na (2, 8, 1) and O (2, 6) (½ marks)
    • Two Na atoms each transfer one electron to one O atom (1 mark)
    • Ions $\mathrm{Na^+}$ and $\mathrm{O^{2-}}$ formed and combine as $\mathrm{Na_2O}$ (½ marks)
    • Ions cannot move in the solid; ions are free to move in the molten state (1 mark)
    • Roasting (½ marks)
    • Balanced equation: $\mathrm{2ZnS + 3O_2 \rightarrow 2ZnO + 2SO_2}$ (1 mark)
    • Equation: $\mathrm{ZnO + C \rightarrow Zn + CO}$ (½ marks)

Section C: Physics

  1. Question 30 · 1 mark · Light Reflection and Refraction
    Using a concave mirror of focal length 15 cm, a student obtains on a screen a sharp image of the same size as the object. What is the distance of the object from the mirror?

    1. 15 cm
    2. 30 cm
    3. 7.5 cm
    4. 45 cm
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) 30 cm

    A concave mirror forms a real image of the same size when the object is at the centre of curvature C. Distance of C = 2f = 2 $\times$ 15 cm = 30 cm.

  2. Question 31 · 1 mark · Electricity
    Three resistors of 6 $\Omega$ each are available. How should they be connected to get an equivalent resistance of 9 $\Omega$?

    1. All three in series
    2. All three in parallel
    3. Two in parallel, and this combination in series with the third
    4. Two in series, and this combination in parallel with the third
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Two in parallel, and this combination in series with the third

    Two 6 $\Omega$ resistors in parallel give 3 $\Omega$; adding the third in series gives 3 + 6 = 9 $\Omega$. The other options give 18 $\Omega$, 2 $\Omega$ and 4 $\Omega$.

  3. Question 32 · 1 mark · Human Eye and Colourful World
    The following question consists of two statements: Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Answer it by selecting the appropriate option.
    Assertion (A): A beam of white light splits into its component colours on passing through a glass prism.
    Reason (R): Different colours of light bend through different angles with respect to the incident ray as they pass through the prism.

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

    Both statements are true. Because each colour bends through a different angle, the colours separate (dispersion). So R correctly explains A.

  4. Question 33 · 2 marks · Light Reflection and Refraction
    The refractive index of glass is 1.5 and that of water is 1.33. The speed of light in vacuum is $3 \times 10^8$ m/s.
    (a) Find the speed of light in glass.
    (b) In which of the two, glass or water, does light travel faster? Give a reason.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) $n = \dfrac{c}{v}$, so $v = \dfrac{c}{n} = \dfrac{3 \times 10^8}{1.5} = 2 \times 10^8$ m/s.
    (b) Light travels faster in water, because water has a lower refractive index (it is optically rarer than glass).

    Marking scheme:

    • Uses $v = c/n$ with correct substitution (½ marks)
    • Speed in glass $= 2 \times 10^8$ m/s (½ marks)
    • Water (½ marks)
    • Reason: lower refractive index (optically rarer) (½ marks)

  5. Question 34 · 2 marks · Magnetic Effects of Current
    (a) Why do two magnetic field lines never intersect each other?
    (b) How is the relative strength of a magnetic field shown in a field-line diagram?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) If two field lines crossed, a compass needle placed at the point of intersection would point in two directions at once, which is not possible. So field lines never intersect.
    (b) By how close the field lines are. The field is stronger where the lines are crowded (closer together) and weaker where they are farther apart.

    Marking scheme:

    • At an intersection the field (compass needle) would have two directions, which is not possible (1 mark)
    • Closer (crowded) field lines show a stronger field (1 mark)

  6. Question 35 · 3 marks · Light Reflection and Refraction
    An object 2 cm tall is placed 30 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 20 cm. Find the position, size and nature of the image.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Using the sign convention: $u = -30$ cm, $f = -20$ cm.
    Mirror formula: $\dfrac{1}{v} + \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{f}$, so $\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f} - \dfrac{1}{u} = -\dfrac{1}{20} + \dfrac{1}{30} = \dfrac{-3 + 2}{60} = -\dfrac{1}{60}$
    $v = -60$ cm: the image is 60 cm in front of the mirror.
    Magnification $m = -\dfrac{v}{u} = -\dfrac{-60}{-30} = -2$
    Height of image $h' = m \times h = -2 \times 2 = -4$ cm.
    The image is real, inverted and magnified (4 cm tall).

    Marking scheme:

    • Mirror formula with correct signs: $\frac{1}{v} = -\frac{1}{20} + \frac{1}{30}$ (1 mark)
    • $v = -60$ cm (60 cm in front of the mirror) (½ marks)
    • $m = -v/u = -2$ (½ marks)
    • Image height $= -4$ cm (4 cm, inverted) (½ marks)
    • Nature: real, inverted, magnified (½ marks)

  7. Question 36 · 3 marks · Magnetic Effects of Current
    (a) State Fleming's left-hand rule.
    (b) A straight horizontal wire carries current from west to east. It is placed in a uniform magnetic field that points vertically upwards. Find the direction of the force on the wire.
    (c) State two ways of increasing the force on the wire.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the left hand so that they are mutually perpendicular. If the forefinger points in the direction of the magnetic field and the middle finger in the direction of the current, then the thumb points in the direction of the force on the conductor.
    (b) Forefinger upwards (field), middle finger towards east (current): the thumb points towards the south. The force is towards the south.
    (c) Increase the current in the wire; use a stronger magnetic field (a longer length of wire in the field also works).

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct statement of Fleming's left-hand rule (field, current, force on mutually perpendicular fingers) (1 mark)
    • Force towards the south (1 mark)
    • Increase the current (½ marks)
    • Use a stronger magnetic field (or longer wire in the field) (½ marks)

  8. Question 37 · 3 marks · Electricity
    A 4 $\Omega$ resistor and a 12 $\Omega$ resistor are connected in parallel. This combination is connected in series with a 3 $\Omega$ resistor and a 12 V battery.
    (a) Find the equivalent resistance of the circuit.
    (b) Find the current drawn from the battery.
    (c) Find the current through the 12 $\Omega$ resistor.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Parallel part: $\dfrac{1}{R_p} = \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{12} = \dfrac{3 + 1}{12} = \dfrac{1}{3}$, so $R_p = 3\ \Omega$.
    Total $R = 3 + 3 = 6\ \Omega$.
    (b) $I = \dfrac{V}{R} = \dfrac{12}{6} = 2$ A.
    (c) Potential difference across the parallel part $= I R_p = 2 \times 3 = 6$ V.
    Current through 12 $\Omega$ $= \dfrac{6}{12} = 0.5$ A.

    Marking scheme:

    • $R_p = 3\ \Omega$ and total resistance $= 6\ \Omega$ (1 mark)
    • Current from battery $= 2$ A (1 mark)
    • P.d. across parallel part $= 6$ V (½ marks)
    • Current through 12 $\Omega$ $= 0.5$ A (½ marks)

  9. Question 38 · 4 marks · Electricity
    Meena's family uses the following appliances every day on the 220 V mains supply:

    AppliancePowerNumberTime used per day
    Electric iron1100 W11 hour
    LED bulb10 W45 hours each
    Geyser2200 W11 hour
    The electricity board charges Rs 6 per unit (kWh).

    (i) What current does the geyser draw from the mains? [1]

    (ii) Find the total electrical energy used by these appliances in one day, in kWh. [1]

    (iii) (a) Find the cost of electricity for these appliances for 30 days.
    (b) Why is the heating element of the geyser made of an alloy such as nichrome and not of copper? [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) What current does the geyser draw from the mains?

    Model answer: $I = \dfrac{P}{V} = \dfrac{2200}{220} = 10$ A.

    Marking scheme:

    • Uses $I = P/V$ (½ marks)
    • $I = 10$ A (½ marks)

    (ii) Find the total electrical energy used by these appliances in one day, in kWh.

    Model answer: Iron: 1.1 kW $\times$ 1 h = 1.1 kWh. Bulbs: 4 $\times$ 0.01 kW $\times$ 5 h = 0.2 kWh. Geyser: 2.2 kW $\times$ 1 h = 2.2 kWh.
    Total = 1.1 + 0.2 + 2.2 = 3.5 kWh.

    Marking scheme:

    • Energy of each appliance worked out correctly (1.1, 0.2 and 2.2 kWh) (½ marks)
    • Total = 3.5 kWh (½ marks)

    (iii) (a) Find the cost of electricity for these appliances for 30 days.
    (b) Why is the heating element of the geyser made of an alloy such as nichrome and not of copper?

    Model answer: (a) Energy in 30 days = 3.5 $\times$ 30 = 105 kWh (units). Cost = 105 $\times$ Rs 6 = Rs 630.
    (b) An alloy such as nichrome has a much higher resistivity than copper, so it produces more heat, and it does not oxidise (burn) readily at high temperatures.

    Marking scheme:

    • 105 units in 30 days (½ marks)
    • Cost = Rs 630 (½ marks)
    • Alloy has high resistivity (½ marks)
    • Alloy does not oxidise (burn) readily at high temperature (½ marks)

  10. Question 39 · 5 marks · Human Eye and Colourful World
    (a) Name the part of the human eye that (i) controls the size of the pupil and (ii) acts as the screen on which the image is formed.
    (b) What is hypermetropia? State two possible causes of this defect.
    (c) The near point of a person's eye is 50 cm. Name the type of lens needed to correct the defect, and find the power of the lens that will let the person read a book held at 25 cm.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) (i) Iris (ii) Retina
    (b) Hypermetropia (far-sightedness) is the defect in which a person can see distant objects clearly but cannot see nearby objects distinctly; the near point is farther than 25 cm. Causes: (1) the focal length of the eye lens is too long, (2) the eyeball has become too small (short).
    (c) A convex (converging) lens is needed.
    The lens must form a virtual image of the book (at 25 cm) at the near point (50 cm): $u = -25$ cm, $v = -50$ cm.
    $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{v} - \dfrac{1}{u} = -\dfrac{1}{50} + \dfrac{1}{25} = \dfrac{1}{50}$, so $f = +50$ cm $= +0.5$ m.
    Power $P = \dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{0.5} = +2$ D.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Iris (½ marks)
    • (ii) Retina (½ marks)
    • Hypermetropia: distant objects seen clearly, nearby objects not seen distinctly (near point beyond 25 cm) (1 mark)
    • Cause: focal length of eye lens too long (½ marks)
    • Cause: eyeball too small (½ marks)
    • Convex lens (½ marks)
    • Lens formula with $u = -25$ cm, $v = -50$ cm (½ marks)
    • $f = +50$ cm (½ marks)
    • $P = +2$ D (½ marks)
