Section A: Biology

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Life Processes
    A child gets a small cut on a finger, but the bleeding does not stop for a long time. A blood test is most likely to show a shortage of:

    1. red blood corpuscles
    2. white blood corpuscles
    3. haemoglobin
    4. platelets
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) platelets

    Platelets circulate in the blood and help it to clot at a wound, which plugs the leak. A shortage of platelets makes bleeding continue for a long time.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Life Processes
    Plants need nitrogen to make proteins. In which form do most plants take up nitrogen?

    1. Nitrogen gas from the air, through the stomata
    2. Inorganic nitrates or nitrites from the soil
    3. Proteins absorbed from dead organisms in the soil
    4. Ammonia gas from the air, through the leaves
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Inorganic nitrates or nitrites from the soil

    Most plants take up nitrogen from the soil in the form of inorganic nitrates or nitrites (or as organic compounds made by bacteria from atmospheric nitrogen). They cannot use nitrogen gas from the air directly.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Control and Coordination
    Which of the following pairs of a hormone and its function is correctly matched?

    1. Insulin - lowers the level of sugar in the blood
    2. Growth hormone - prepares the body to deal with an emergency
    3. Thyroxine - brings about the changes in boys at puberty
    4. Adrenaline - controls the growth and development of the body
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Insulin - lowers the level of sugar in the blood

    Insulin from the pancreas lowers the blood sugar level. An emergency is handled by adrenaline, puberty changes in boys by testosterone, and growth by growth hormone.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · How Do Organisms Reproduce
    In Plasmodium, the nucleus divides several times and the cell then splits into many daughter cells at the same time. This method of reproduction is called:

    1. binary fission
    2. budding
    3. multiple fission
    4. fragmentation
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) multiple fission

    When a single-celled organism divides into many daughter cells at the same time, it is multiple fission. Binary fission gives only two daughter cells.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Our Environment
    Which of the following is the most environment-friendly way to dispose of kitchen waste such as vegetable peels?

    1. Burning it in the open
    2. Turning it into compost in a pit
    3. Dumping it into a nearby river
    4. Packing it in plastic bags and sending it to a landfill
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Turning it into compost in a pit

    Vegetable peels are biodegradable. In a compost pit they are broken down by decomposers into manure that returns nutrients to the soil. Burning pollutes the air and dumping pollutes water and land.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Heredity
    The height of a pea plant depends on the amount of a growth hormone it makes, and this hormone is made with the help of an enzyme. A change in the gene for this enzyme makes the enzyme work less efficiently. What is the most likely result?

    1. The plant will be shorter.
    2. The plant will be taller.
    3. The plant will not produce flowers.
    4. The plant will produce wrinkled seeds.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) The plant will be shorter.

    A gene controls a trait by making a protein, here an enzyme. If the enzyme works less efficiently, less growth hormone is made, so the plant will be shorter.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Our Environment
    The flow of energy in an ecosystem is said to be unidirectional. This means that:

    1. energy flows from the consumers back to the producers
    2. the same energy is used again and again by the organisms at all levels
    3. energy increases at each level as it moves up the food chain
    4. energy captured by the producers does not go back to the Sun, and energy passed to the herbivores does not come back to the producers
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) energy captured by the producers does not go back to the Sun, and energy passed to the herbivores does not come back to the producers

    Energy moves step by step from the Sun to producers and then to consumers. It never moves back to an earlier level, so the flow is in one direction only.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Life Processes
    The following question consists of two statements: Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Answer it by selecting the appropriate option.
    Assertion (A): Haemoglobin is found dissolved in the plasma of human blood.
    Reason (R): Haemoglobin has a very high affinity for oxygen.

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) A is false but R is true.

    Haemoglobin is present in the red blood corpuscles, not dissolved in the plasma, so A is false. It does have a very high affinity for oxygen, so R is true.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Control and Coordination
    The following question consists of two statements: Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Answer it by selecting the appropriate option.
    Assertion (A): The pancreas acts as an endocrine gland.
    Reason (R): The pancreas secretes pancreatic juice containing digestive enzymes into the small intestine.

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.

    Both statements are true. The pancreas is an endocrine gland because it secretes insulin directly into the blood. Pancreatic juice is poured into the intestine through a duct, so R does not explain A.

  10. Question 10 · 2 marks · Life Processes
    (a) How do the cells of a plant get the oxygen they need for respiration?
    (b) A green plant gives out oxygen during the day but carbon dioxide at night. Explain why.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Plants exchange gases through the stomata, and the large intercellular spaces ensure that all the cells are in contact with air. Oxygen enters the cells from this air by diffusion.
    (b) During the day, photosynthesis takes place. The carbon dioxide produced in respiration is used up in photosynthesis, and the release of oxygen is the major event. At night there is no photosynthesis, so the carbon dioxide produced in respiration is given out.

    Marking scheme:

    • Gases are exchanged through the stomata (½ marks)
    • Large intercellular spaces keep all cells in contact with air; oxygen enters by diffusion (½ marks)
    • Day: carbon dioxide from respiration is used in photosynthesis; release of oxygen is the major event (½ marks)
    • Night: no photosynthesis, so carbon dioxide from respiration is given out (½ marks)

  11. Question 11 · 2 marks · Control and Coordination
    (a) Name the part of the brain that controls involuntary actions such as blood pressure, salivation and vomiting. In which region of the brain is it located?
    (b) How is the brain protected from injury?

    OR Animals use both electrical impulses and hormones for control and coordination. State two limitations of using only electrical impulses, and two ways in which hormones overcome them.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) The medulla. It is located in the hind-brain.
    (b) The brain is placed inside a bony box (the skull or cranium). Inside this box it is surrounded by a fluid-filled balloon (cerebrospinal fluid), which absorbs shocks.

    Marking scheme:

    • Medulla (½ marks)
    • Hind-brain (½ marks)
    • Enclosed in a bony box (skull/cranium) (½ marks)
    • Surrounded by fluid that acts as a shock absorber (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: Limitations of electrical impulses: (1) they reach only those cells that are connected by nervous tissue; (2) after an impulse is generated and sent, a cell takes some time to reset before it can send a new impulse.
    Hormones are released into the blood, so they can reach all the cells of the body whatever the nervous connections, and they can act steadily and persistently.

    Marking scheme:

    • Impulses reach only cells connected by nervous tissue (½ marks)
    • A cell needs time to reset before it can send a new impulse (½ marks)
    • Hormones travel in the blood and reach all cells of the body (½ marks)
    • Hormones can act steadily and persistently (½ marks)

  12. Question 12 · 2 marks · Life Processes
    (a) Describe how Amoeba takes in and digests its food.
    (b) How does Paramecium take in its food differently from Amoeba?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Amoeba takes in food using temporary finger-like extensions of the cell surface, which fuse over the food particle and form a food vacuole. Inside the food vacuole, complex substances are broken down into simpler ones, which then diffuse into the cytoplasm. The undigested material is moved to the surface of the cell and thrown out.
    (b) Paramecium has a definite shape, and food is taken in only at a specific spot. The food is moved to this spot by the movement of the cilia that cover the entire surface of the cell.

    Marking scheme:

    • Temporary finger-like extensions fuse over the food particle to form a food vacuole (½ marks)
    • Food broken down in the vacuole; simpler substances diffuse into the cytoplasm; undigested material thrown out (½ marks)
    • Paramecium: definite shape; food taken in at a specific spot (½ marks)
    • Food moved to this spot by the beating of cilia (½ marks)

  13. Question 13 · 3 marks · Life Processes
    (a) The heart of a fish has two chambers, but birds and mammals have a four-chambered heart in which oxygenated and deoxygenated blood do not mix. Why is this separation useful to birds and mammals?
    (b) What is the role of the valves in the heart?
    (c) State two functions of lymph.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Separating the two kinds of blood gives a highly efficient supply of oxygen to the body. Birds and mammals need a lot of energy because they keep their body temperature constant, so this is useful to them.
    (b) Valves make sure that blood does not flow backwards when the atria or the ventricles contract.
    (c) Lymph (tissue fluid) is a colourless fluid, formed from plasma, proteins and blood cells that escape through the pores in the walls of capillaries into the intercellular spaces. Functions: it carries digested and absorbed fat from the intestine, and it drains excess fluid from the intercellular spaces back into the blood.

    Marking scheme:

    • Efficient supply of oxygen, needed for high energy use to keep body temperature constant (1 mark)
    • Valves prevent backward flow of blood when the chambers contract (1 mark)
    • Carries digested and absorbed fat from the intestine (½ marks)
    • Drains excess fluid from intercellular spaces back into the blood (½ marks)

  14. Question 14 · 3 marks · Our Environment
    After a school fair, the waste collected includes fruit peels, used paper plates, plastic wrappers and broken glass bottles.
    (a) Sort these into biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste.
    (b) Why are plastic and glass not broken down by bacteria and other decomposers?
    (c) Why is the build-up of non-biodegradable substances harmful to the environment?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Biodegradable: fruit peels and used paper plates. Non-biodegradable: plastic wrappers and broken glass bottles.
    (b) Decomposers break down substances with the help of enzymes, and enzymes are specific in their action. Bacteria and other decomposers have no enzymes that can break down plastic or glass.
    (c) Non-biodegradable substances are not broken down, so they persist in the environment for a long time. They may harm various members of the ecosystem, for example animals that swallow plastic, and they pollute land and water.

    Marking scheme:

    • Biodegradable: fruit peels, paper plates (½ marks)
    • Non-biodegradable: plastic wrappers, glass bottles (½ marks)
    • Enzymes of decomposers are specific in action; none can break down plastic or glass (1 mark)
    • They persist in the environment for a long time (½ marks)
    • They may harm members of the ecosystem (e.g. animals swallow plastic) (½ marks)

  15. Question 15 · 4 marks · Heredity
    Asha studied the position of flowers in pea plants. Axial flowers (A) are dominant over terminal flowers (a). She crossed a pure-breeding plant with axial flowers with a pure-breeding plant with terminal flowers and raised the F1 plants. She then self-pollinated the F1 plants and raised 240 F2 plants.

    (i) What will be the genotype of the F1 plants, and what type of flowers will they bear? [1]

    (ii) Of the 240 F2 plants that Asha raised, about how many would bear terminal flowers? [1]

    (iii) An F2 plant has axial flowers. How can Asha find out whether its genotype is AA or Aa? Explain with the expected results. [2]

    OR (iii) None of the F1 plants had terminal flowers, but some F2 plants did. Explain why. What does this show about how traits are inherited?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) What will be the genotype of the F1 plants, and what type of flowers will they bear?

    Model answer: Genotype: Aa. They will bear axial flowers.

    Marking scheme:

    • Genotype Aa (½ marks)
    • Phenotype: axial flowers (½ marks)

    (ii) Of the 240 F2 plants that Asha raised, about how many would bear terminal flowers?

    Model answer: The F2 ratio is 3 axial : 1 terminal. Terminal = 1/4 of 240 = 60 plants.

    Marking scheme:

    • Fraction with terminal flowers = 1/4 (½ marks)
    • 60 plants (½ marks)

    (iii) An F2 plant has axial flowers. How can Asha find out whether its genotype is AA or Aa? Explain with the expected results.

    Model answer: She should cross the plant with a plant having terminal flowers (aa) (or self-pollinate it). If all the progeny have axial flowers, the plant is AA. If about half the progeny have terminal flowers (or, on self-pollination, about one-fourth have terminal flowers), the plant is Aa.

    Marking scheme:

    • Cross with a terminal-flowered (aa) plant, or self-pollinate it (1 mark)
    • All progeny axial: the plant is AA (½ marks)
    • Some progeny terminal (about half with aa; about one-fourth on selfing): the plant is Aa (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: The F1 plants are Aa. They carry the factor for terminal flowers (a), but it does not show because axial (A) is dominant. When the F1 plants form gametes, the two factors separate, so each gamete gets either A or a. When two gametes carrying a fuse, an aa plant with terminal flowers is formed (about one-fourth of the F2). This shows that a trait is controlled by a pair of factors, one from each parent, which separate when gametes are formed.

    Marking scheme:

    • F1 is Aa: the factor for terminal flowers is present but hidden by the dominant factor (½ marks)
    • The two factors separate during gamete formation; each gamete gets A or a (½ marks)
    • Fusion of two gametes with a gives aa (terminal) plants in F2 (½ marks)
    • Traits are controlled by a pair of factors, one from each parent, that separate in gametes (½ marks)

  16. Question 16 · 5 marks · How Do Organisms Reproduce
    (a) Name the part of the human female reproductive system where (i) eggs are produced, (ii) fertilisation takes place and (iii) the embryo gets implanted.
    (b) Describe how the developing embryo gets nourishment from the mother's blood.
    (c) What happens to the egg and to the lining of the uterus if the egg is not fertilised?

    OR (a) Describe how Rhizopus (bread mould) reproduces by spore formation. How are the spores protected?
    (b) Describe how Hydra reproduces by budding.
    (c) State two advantages of vegetative propagation.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) (i) Ovary (ii) Oviduct (fallopian tube) (iii) Uterus
    (b) The embryo gets nutrition through the placenta, a disc-like tissue embedded in the wall of the uterus. It has villi on the embryo's side, which give a large surface area, and blood spaces on the mother's side that surround the villi. Glucose and oxygen pass from the mother's blood to the embryo through the placenta, and the wastes made by the embryo are removed into the mother's blood through it.
    (c) The egg lives for about one day and then dies. The thick and spongy lining of the uterus, prepared to receive a fertilised egg, is not needed. It slowly breaks down and comes out through the vagina as blood and mucus. This is menstruation; it takes place roughly every month and lasts for about two to eight days.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Ovary (½ marks)
    • (ii) Oviduct (fallopian tube) (½ marks)
    • (iii) Uterus (½ marks)
    • Placenta: disc-like tissue embedded in the uterine wall (½ marks)
    • Villi on the embryo's side give a large surface area (½ marks)
    • Glucose and oxygen pass from the mother's blood to the embryo (½ marks)
    • Wastes of the embryo are removed through the placenta (½ marks)
    • Egg lives about one day; the unused uterine lining breaks down and comes out as blood and mucus (1 mark)
    • This is menstruation, about every month, lasting 2 to 8 days (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (a) Rhizopus has thread-like hyphae. Tiny blob-on-a-stick structures called sporangia grow up from them. Each sporangium contains cells called spores, which can develop into new Rhizopus individuals. The spores are covered by thick walls that protect them until they come into contact with a moist surface and can begin to grow.
    (b) In Hydra, a bud develops as an outgrowth at one specific site of the body because of repeated cell division. The bud grows into a tiny individual, and when it is fully mature it detaches from the parent and becomes a new independent individual.
    (c) Any two: plants raised in this way bear flowers and fruits earlier than those produced from seeds; plants that have lost the capacity to produce seeds (such as banana, orange, rose and jasmine) can be propagated; all the plants produced are genetically similar to the parent plant, so desirable features are kept.

    Marking scheme:

    • Sporangia (blob-on-a-stick structures) grow from the hyphae and contain spores (1 mark)
    • Spores develop into new individuals (½ marks)
    • Thick walls protect the spores until they reach a moist surface (½ marks)
    • Bud forms as an outgrowth at a specific site by repeated cell division (1 mark)
    • Bud grows into a tiny individual and detaches when mature (1 mark)
    • First advantage of vegetative propagation (½ marks)
    • Second advantage of vegetative propagation (½ marks)

Section B: Chemistry

  1. Question 17 · 1 mark · Chemical Reactions and Equations
    Water containing a few drops of dilute sulphuric acid is electrolysed. Which gas is collected at the cathode (negative electrode), and how does its volume compare with that of the gas collected at the anode?

    1. Hydrogen; double the volume
    2. Oxygen; double the volume
    3. Hydrogen; half the volume
    4. Oxygen; half the volume
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Hydrogen; double the volume

    Water decomposes as $\mathrm{2H_2O \rightarrow 2H_2 + O_2}$. Hydrogen is collected at the cathode, and its volume is double that of the oxygen collected at the anode.

  2. Question 18 · 1 mark · Chemical Reactions and Equations
    Before a magnesium ribbon is burnt in air, it is cleaned by rubbing it with sandpaper. Why?

    1. To make the ribbon thinner so that it melts easily
    2. To remove moisture that would put out the flame
    3. To produce heat by friction to start the burning
    4. To remove the protective layer that forms on its surface in air, so that the metal burns readily
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) To remove the protective layer that forms on its surface in air, so that the metal burns readily

    In air, magnesium gets coated with a protective layer (basic magnesium carbonate / oxide) that keeps the metal from burning readily. Rubbing with sandpaper removes this layer.

  3. Question 19 · 1 mark · Acids, Bases and Salts
    Blue crystals of copper sulphate turn white on heating and become blue again when a few drops of water are added. This shows that the blue crystals:

    1. decompose into copper oxide on heating
    2. are acidic in nature
    3. contain water of crystallisation
    4. absorb carbon dioxide from the air
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) contain water of crystallisation

    Blue crystals are $\mathrm{CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O}$. On heating they lose their water of crystallisation and become white anhydrous copper sulphate; adding water gives back the blue colour.

  4. Question 20 · 1 mark · Acids, Bases and Salts
    A solution of an acid has a pH of 3. It is diluted by adding a large amount of water. What happens to its pH?

    1. It decreases below 3.
    2. It increases and moves towards 7.
    3. It becomes more than 7.
    4. It stays the same.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) It increases and moves towards 7.

    Dilution lowers the concentration of $\mathrm{H^+}$ (hydronium) ions per unit volume, so the solution becomes less acidic and its pH rises towards 7. Adding water alone cannot make an acid solution basic (pH above 7).

  5. Question 21 · 1 mark · Metals and Non-metals
    Which of the following oxides reacts with both hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide to form a salt and water?

    1. $\mathrm{Na_2O}$
    2. $\mathrm{MgO}$
    3. $\mathrm{CO_2}$
    4. $\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) $\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$

    Aluminium oxide is amphoteric: it reacts with acids as well as bases to give salt and water. $\mathrm{Na_2O}$ and $\mathrm{MgO}$ are basic; $\mathrm{CO_2}$ is acidic.

  6. Question 22 · 1 mark · Carbon and Its Compounds
    Which of the following molecular formulae fits the general formula of an alkyne?

    1. $\mathrm{C_3H_4}$
    2. $\mathrm{C_3H_6}$
    3. $\mathrm{C_3H_8}$
    4. $\mathrm{C_4H_8}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\mathrm{C_3H_4}$

    Alkynes have the general formula $\mathrm{C_nH_{2n-2}}$. For n = 3 this gives $\mathrm{C_3H_4}$ (propyne). $\mathrm{C_3H_6}$ and $\mathrm{C_4H_8}$ fit $\mathrm{C_nH_{2n}}$ and $\mathrm{C_3H_8}$ fits $\mathrm{C_nH_{2n+2}}$.

  7. Question 23 · 1 mark · Metals and Non-metals
    Hydrogen gas is usually not given off when a metal reacts with nitric acid. This is because nitric acid:

    1. is a weak acid
    2. does not contain hydrogen
    3. is a strong oxidising agent and oxidises the hydrogen produced to water
    4. reacts only with non-metals
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) is a strong oxidising agent and oxidises the hydrogen produced to water

    Nitric acid is a strong oxidising agent. It oxidises the hydrogen produced to water and is itself reduced to oxides of nitrogen.

  8. Question 24 · 1 mark · Carbon and Its Compounds
    The following question consists of two statements: Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Answer it by selecting the appropriate option.
    Assertion (A): Both ethanol and ethanoic acid react with sodium metal to give hydrogen gas.
    Reason (R): Both ethanol and ethanoic acid are acids that turn blue litmus red.

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) A is true but R is false.

    A is true: sodium reacts with ethanol (giving sodium ethoxide) and with ethanoic acid (giving sodium ethanoate), and hydrogen is given off in both. R is false: ethanol is neutral and does not turn blue litmus red.

  9. Question 25 · 2 marks · Carbon and Its Compounds
    Carbon has four electrons in its outermost shell.
    (a) Why does carbon not form $\mathrm{C^{4-}}$ or $\mathrm{C^{4+}}$ ions?
    (b) How, then, does carbon attain a noble gas configuration?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) To form $\mathrm{C^{4-}}$, carbon would have to gain four electrons; it would be difficult for a nucleus with six protons to hold on to ten electrons. To form $\mathrm{C^{4+}}$, it would have to lose four electrons, which needs a large amount of energy.
    (b) Carbon shares its four valence electrons with other atoms of carbon or of other elements, forming covalent bonds.

    Marking scheme:

    • $\mathrm{C^{4-}}$: a nucleus with 6 protons cannot easily hold 10 electrons (½ marks)
    • $\mathrm{C^{4+}}$: removing 4 electrons needs a large amount of energy (½ marks)
    • Carbon shares its valence electrons and forms covalent bonds (1 mark)

  10. Question 26 · 3 marks · Carbon and Its Compounds
    (a) Write the structural formulae and names of two isomers of butane, $\mathrm{C_4H_{10}}$.
    (b) A saturated and an unsaturated hydrocarbon are burnt in air. How will their flames differ?
    (c) Write the name of the compound $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2Cl}$ and name its functional group.

    OR (a) What is a soap molecule made of? Explain how soap removes oily dirt from clothes.
    (b) Why does soap not form lather easily in hard water, while a detergent does?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) $\mathrm{CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-CH_3}$: n-butane (butane).
    $\mathrm{CH_3-CH(CH_3)-CH_3}$: isobutane (2-methylpropane).
    (b) The saturated hydrocarbon burns with a clean (blue) flame. The unsaturated hydrocarbon burns with a yellow flame with lots of black smoke (sooty flame).
    (c) Chloroethane; the functional group is the halo (chloro) group, $\mathrm{-Cl}$.

    Marking scheme:

    • n-Butane, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_3}$ (½ marks)
    • Isobutane (2-methylpropane), $\mathrm{CH_3CH(CH_3)CH_3}$ (½ marks)
    • Saturated: clean (blue) flame (½ marks)
    • Unsaturated: yellow, sooty flame (½ marks)
    • Chloroethane (½ marks)
    • Halo (chloro) group, $\mathrm{-Cl}$ (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (a) A soap molecule is the sodium or potassium salt of a long-chain carboxylic acid. It has an ionic end, which dissolves in water, and a long hydrocarbon chain, which dissolves in oil. In water, the soap molecules arrange themselves in clusters called micelles: the hydrocarbon tails point inwards and stick to the oily dirt, and the ionic ends face the water outside. The oily dirt is collected in the centre of the micelles, which stay in the water as an emulsion, and the dirt is washed away on rinsing.
    (b) Hard water contains calcium and magnesium salts. Soap reacts with these to form an insoluble substance (scum), so a lot of soap is wasted and little lather forms. The charged ends of detergent molecules do not form insoluble precipitates with calcium and magnesium ions, so detergents lather well in hard water.

    Marking scheme:

    • Soap: sodium/potassium salt of a long-chain carboxylic acid with an ionic end and a hydrocarbon tail (½ marks)
    • Tails dissolve in oil, ionic ends face water: micelles form (½ marks)
    • Oily dirt is trapped in the centre of the micelles and washed away on rinsing (½ marks)
    • Hard water has calcium and magnesium salts that react with soap to form insoluble scum (1 mark)
    • Detergents do not form insoluble precipitates with these ions, so they lather (½ marks)

  11. Question 27 · 3 marks · Chemical Reactions and Equations
    (a) Copper powder is heated in an open china dish. What change is seen? Write the chemical equation for the reaction.
    (b) Hydrogen gas is then passed over the heated product. What change is seen? Write the chemical equation, and name the substance oxidised and the substance reduced.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) The surface of the copper powder becomes coated with a black substance, copper(II) oxide.
    $\mathrm{2Cu + O_2 \xrightarrow{\text{heat}} 2CuO}$
    (b) The black coating turns brown, as copper is formed again.
    $\mathrm{CuO + H_2 \xrightarrow{\text{heat}} Cu + H_2O}$
    Hydrogen is oxidised (it gains oxygen to form water) and copper(II) oxide is reduced (it loses oxygen).

    Marking scheme:

    • Black coating (copper oxide) forms (½ marks)
    • $\mathrm{2Cu + O_2 \rightarrow 2CuO}$ (½ marks)
    • Black coating turns brown (copper) (½ marks)
    • $\mathrm{CuO + H_2 \rightarrow Cu + H_2O}$ (½ marks)
    • Hydrogen is oxidised (½ marks)
    • Copper(II) oxide is reduced (½ marks)

  12. Question 28 · 4 marks · Metals and Non-metals
    For a project on metals, Kavya reads that metals high in the activity series, such as sodium, cannot be obtained by heating their oxides with carbon. Mercury, which is low in the activity series, is obtained from its sulphide ore, cinnabar (HgS), by heating alone. Copper obtained by reduction is not pure enough for electric wires, so it is purified by electrolysis. She also notes that a goldsmith makes jewellery from 22 carat gold and not from pure gold.

    (i) Why can sodium not be obtained by heating its oxide with carbon? How is it obtained? [1]

    (ii) How is mercury obtained from cinnabar? Write the chemical equations for the two steps. [1]

    (iii) Describe how copper is purified by electrolytic refining. Name the anode, the cathode and the electrolyte, and state what happens to the impurities. [2]

    OR (iii) (a) Why is pure (24 carat) gold not used for making jewellery? What is 22 carat gold?
    (b) How do the electrical conductivity and melting point of an alloy compare with those of the pure metals in it? Give one example.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Why can sodium not be obtained by heating its oxide with carbon? How is it obtained?

    Model answer: Sodium has more affinity for oxygen than carbon has, so carbon cannot reduce sodium oxide. Sodium is obtained by the electrolysis of molten sodium chloride (electrolytic reduction).

    Marking scheme:

    • Sodium has more affinity for oxygen than carbon (½ marks)
    • Electrolysis of molten sodium chloride (½ marks)

    (ii) How is mercury obtained from cinnabar? Write the chemical equations for the two steps.

    Model answer: Cinnabar is first heated in air, which changes it into mercuric oxide. The mercuric oxide is then reduced to mercury on further heating.
    $\mathrm{2HgS + 3O_2 \xrightarrow{\text{heat}} 2HgO + 2SO_2}$
    $\mathrm{2HgO \xrightarrow{\text{heat}} 2Hg + O_2}$

    Marking scheme:

    • $\mathrm{2HgS + 3O_2 \rightarrow 2HgO + 2SO_2}$ (heating cinnabar in air) (½ marks)
    • $\mathrm{2HgO \rightarrow 2Hg + O_2}$ (further heating) (½ marks)

    (iii) Describe how copper is purified by electrolytic refining. Name the anode, the cathode and the electrolyte, and state what happens to the impurities.

    Model answer: Anode: a thick block of impure copper. Cathode: a thin strip of pure copper. Electrolyte: a solution of acidified copper sulphate. When current is passed, pure copper from the anode dissolves into the electrolyte and an equal amount of pure copper is deposited on the cathode. Soluble impurities go into the solution, and insoluble impurities settle at the bottom of the anode as anode mud.

    Marking scheme:

    • Anode: impure copper (½ marks)
    • Cathode: thin strip of pure copper (½ marks)
    • Electrolyte: acidified copper sulphate solution (½ marks)
    • Pure copper deposits on the cathode; soluble impurities go into solution, insoluble ones settle as anode mud (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (a) Pure gold (24 carat) is very soft, so it is not suitable for jewellery. 22 carat gold is an alloy of 22 parts of pure gold with 2 parts of silver or copper.
    (b) The electrical conductivity and the melting point of an alloy are lower than those of the pure metals. For example, solder, an alloy of lead and tin, has a low melting point and is used for welding electrical wires together (or: brass and bronze are not good conductors of electricity, though copper is).

    Marking scheme:

    • Pure gold is very soft (½ marks)
    • 22 carat: 22 parts gold alloyed with 2 parts silver or copper (½ marks)
    • Alloys have lower electrical conductivity and lower melting point (½ marks)
    • Correct example (solder, brass or bronze) (½ marks)

  13. Question 29 · 5 marks · Acids, Bases and Salts
    (a) A student adds zinc granules to dilute sulphuric acid. Write the balanced chemical equation and describe how the gas formed can be tested.
    (b) Write the balanced chemical equation for the reaction of sodium carbonate with dilute hydrochloric acid, and describe a test for the gas formed.
    (c) Why does a solution of hydrochloric acid in water conduct electricity, while a solution of glucose in water does not?
    (d) Solutions of hydrochloric acid and acetic acid of the same concentration are taken. Which has the lower pH? Give a reason.

    OR (a) State whether the aqueous solution of each of the following salts is acidic, basic or neutral, giving a reason in each case: (i) sodium chloride, (ii) sodium carbonate, (iii) ammonium chloride.
    (b) Write the chemical equation for the preparation of plaster of Paris from gypsum, and give one use of plaster of Paris.
    (c) What is baking powder? Write the reaction that takes place when it is heated or mixed with water, and explain how it makes a cake soft and spongy.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) $\mathrm{Zn + H_2SO_4 \rightarrow ZnSO_4 + H_2}$
    Test: bring a burning candle (or splinter) near the mouth of the tube; the gas burns with a pop sound, showing it is hydrogen.
    (b) $\mathrm{Na_2CO_3 + 2HCl \rightarrow 2NaCl + H_2O + CO_2}$
    Test: pass the gas through lime water; the lime water turns milky, showing it is carbon dioxide.
    (c) In water, hydrochloric acid produces hydrogen ions ($\mathrm{H^+}$, as $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$) and chloride ions, which carry the current. Glucose does not form ions in water, so its solution does not conduct.
    (d) Hydrochloric acid has the lower pH. It is a strong acid and gives a much higher concentration of $\mathrm{H^+}$ ions than acetic acid, which is a weak acid.

    Marking scheme:

    • $\mathrm{Zn + H_2SO_4 \rightarrow ZnSO_4 + H_2}$ (1 mark)
    • Hydrogen burns with a pop sound (½ marks)
    • $\mathrm{Na_2CO_3 + 2HCl \rightarrow 2NaCl + H_2O + CO_2}$ (1 mark)
    • Carbon dioxide turns lime water milky (½ marks)
    • HCl gives ions ($\mathrm{H^+}$) in water; glucose does not form ions (1 mark)
    • Hydrochloric acid; strong acid, gives more $\mathrm{H^+}$ ions (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (a) (i) Neutral (pH 7): it is a salt of a strong acid (HCl) and a strong base (NaOH).
    (ii) Basic (pH more than 7): it is a salt of a strong base (NaOH) and a weak acid (carbonic acid).
    (iii) Acidic (pH less than 7): it is a salt of a strong acid (HCl) and a weak base (ammonium hydroxide).
    (b) $\mathrm{CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O \xrightarrow{373\ K} CaSO_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}H_2O + 1\frac{1}{2}H_2O}$
    Use (any one): plaster for supporting fractured bones; making toys and decorative materials; making surfaces smooth.
    (c) Baking powder is a mixture of baking soda (sodium hydrogencarbonate) and a mild edible acid such as tartaric acid. When it is heated or mixed with water, carbon dioxide is produced: $\mathrm{NaHCO_3 + H^+ \rightarrow CO_2 + H_2O}$ + sodium salt of the acid. The carbon dioxide makes the cake rise and become soft and spongy.

    Marking scheme:

    • Sodium chloride: neutral, salt of strong acid and strong base (½ marks)
    • Sodium carbonate: basic, salt of strong base and weak acid (½ marks)
    • Ammonium chloride: acidic, salt of strong acid and weak base (½ marks)
    • $\mathrm{CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O \rightarrow CaSO_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}H_2O + 1\frac{1}{2}H_2O}$ (at 373 K) (1 mark)
    • One correct use of plaster of Paris (½ marks)
    • Baking powder: baking soda + a mild edible acid (tartaric acid) (½ marks)
    • $\mathrm{NaHCO_3 + H^+ \rightarrow CO_2 + H_2O}$ + sodium salt of the acid (½ marks)
    • Carbon dioxide produced makes the cake rise, soft and spongy (1 mark)

Section C: Physics

  1. Question 30 · 1 mark · Light Reflection and Refraction
    A dentist wants to see a large, erect image of a tooth. Which mirror should be used, and where should the tooth be?

    1. Convex mirror; tooth beyond the centre of curvature
    2. Concave mirror; tooth between the pole and the principal focus
    3. Concave mirror; tooth at the centre of curvature
    4. Plane mirror; tooth very close to the mirror
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Concave mirror; tooth between the pole and the principal focus

    A concave mirror gives a virtual, erect and enlarged image only when the object is between the pole and the focus. A convex mirror gives diminished images, a plane mirror gives same-size images, and an object at C gives a real, inverted image.

  2. Question 31 · 1 mark · Electricity
    A current of 0.4 A flows through a bulb for 5 minutes. What is the amount of charge that flows through it?

    1. 2 C
    2. 12 C
    3. 120 C
    4. 1200 C
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) 120 C

    $Q = I \times t = 0.4\ \mathrm{A} \times (5 \times 60)\ \mathrm{s} = 120$ C.

  3. Question 32 · 1 mark · Human Eye and Colourful World
    The following question consists of two statements: Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Answer it by selecting the appropriate option.
    Assertion (A): Planets do not twinkle the way stars do.
    Reason (R): Planets are much closer to the Earth and are seen as extended sources, so the variations in the light from their many point-sized parts average out.

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

    Both statements are true. A planet behaves like a collection of many point sources; the changes caused by atmospheric refraction in the light from each of them cancel out on average, so the planet does not twinkle. So R correctly explains A.

  4. Question 33 · 2 marks · Light Reflection and Refraction
    (a) A spherical lens always forms a virtual, erect and diminished image, wherever the object is placed in front of it. Name the lens and state where the image is formed.
    (b) A convex lens of focal length 12 cm forms a real image of an object on a screen placed 36 cm from the lens. Find the distance of the object from the lens.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) A concave lens. The image is formed on the same side of the lens as the object, between the focus (F1) and the optical centre.
    (b) Lens formula: $\dfrac{1}{v} - \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{f}$, with $v = +36$ cm and $f = +12$ cm.
    $\dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{v} - \dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{36} - \dfrac{1}{12} = \dfrac{1 - 3}{36} = -\dfrac{1}{18}$, so $u = -18$ cm.
    The object is 18 cm in front of the lens.

    Marking scheme:

    • Concave lens (½ marks)
    • Image between the focus and the optical centre, on the same side as the object (½ marks)
    • Correct substitution in the lens formula: $\dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{36} - \dfrac{1}{12}$ (½ marks)
    • $u = -18$ cm (object 18 cm in front of the lens) (½ marks)

  5. Question 34 · 2 marks · Electricity
    A student finds that the current through a resistor is 0.3 A when the potential difference across it is 6 V.
    (a) State Ohm's law.
    (b) Find the resistance of the resistor, and the current through it when the potential difference is 9 V (temperature unchanged).

    OR (a) How is an ammeter connected in a circuit to measure the current through a resistor, and how is a voltmeter connected to measure the potential difference across it?
    (b) Define 1 volt.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) The potential difference across the ends of a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the current through it, provided its temperature remains the same ($V \propto I$, or $V = IR$).
    (b) $R = \dfrac{V}{I} = \dfrac{6}{0.3} = 20\ \Omega$.
    At 9 V: $I = \dfrac{V}{R} = \dfrac{9}{20} = 0.45$ A.

    Marking scheme:

    • Ohm's law: V proportional to I at constant temperature (1 mark)
    • $R = 20\ \Omega$ (½ marks)
    • Current at 9 V = 0.45 A (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (a) The ammeter is connected in series with the resistor, so that the current through the resistor also passes through the ammeter. The voltmeter is connected in parallel across the resistor, that is, across the two points between which the potential difference is to be measured.
    (b) One volt is the potential difference between two points in a current-carrying conductor when 1 joule of work is done to move a charge of 1 coulomb from one point to the other ($1\ \mathrm{V} = 1\ \mathrm{J\,C^{-1}}$).

    Marking scheme:

    • Ammeter in series with the resistor (½ marks)
    • Voltmeter in parallel across the resistor (½ marks)
    • 1 V: 1 J of work is done to move 1 C of charge from one point to the other (1 mark)

  6. Question 35 · 3 marks · Human Eye and Colourful World
    (a) What is a rainbow? What acts like tiny prisms in its formation?
    (b) Describe what happens to sunlight inside a raindrop to produce the colours of a rainbow.
    (c) In which direction, with respect to the Sun, should you look to see a rainbow?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) A rainbow is a natural spectrum of colours seen in the sky after a rain shower. Tiny water droplets present in the atmosphere act like small prisms.
    (b) Sunlight is refracted and dispersed (split into its colours) as it enters a raindrop. It is then reflected from the inner surface of the drop (internal reflection), and is refracted again as it comes out of the drop, so the colours reach the observer separated.
    (c) A rainbow is always formed in the direction opposite to the Sun, so you must look with the Sun behind you.

    Marking scheme:

    • Natural spectrum in the sky after rain (½ marks)
    • Water droplets act as small prisms (½ marks)
    • Refraction and dispersion as light enters the drop (½ marks)
    • Internal reflection inside the drop (½ marks)
    • Refraction again as light leaves the drop (½ marks)
    • Opposite to the Sun (Sun behind the observer) (½ marks)

  7. Question 36 · 3 marks · Magnetic Effects of Current
    (a) How does an alternating current (AC) differ from a direct current (DC)?
    (b) What is the frequency of the AC supplied to homes in India? How many times does this current change its direction in one second?
    (c) State one important advantage of AC over DC.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) A direct current always flows in one direction. An alternating current reverses its direction at equal intervals of time (periodically).
    (b) The frequency is 50 Hz. The current changes direction after every 1/100 second, that is, 100 times in one second.
    (c) Electric power can be transmitted over long distances as AC without much loss of energy.

    Marking scheme:

    • DC flows in one direction only (½ marks)
    • AC reverses its direction periodically (½ marks)
    • 50 Hz (½ marks)
    • Changes direction 100 times in one second (½ marks)
    • AC can be transmitted over long distances without much loss of energy (1 mark)

  8. Question 37 · 3 marks · Electricity
    Two electric bulbs are rated 100 W, 220 V and 25 W, 220 V.
    (a) Find the resistance of each bulb.
    (b) The two bulbs are connected in series to a 220 V supply. Which bulb will glow brighter? Justify by calculating the power used by each bulb.
    (c) Which bulb will glow brighter if they are connected in parallel to the 220 V supply?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) $R = \dfrac{V^2}{P}$. For the 100 W bulb: $R_1 = \dfrac{220 \times 220}{100} = 484\ \Omega$. For the 25 W bulb: $R_2 = \dfrac{220 \times 220}{25} = 1936\ \Omega$.
    (b) In series, $R = 484 + 1936 = 2420\ \Omega$ and $I = \dfrac{220}{2420} = \dfrac{1}{11}$ A, the same in both bulbs.
    Power in the 100 W bulb $= I^2R_1 = \dfrac{484}{121} = 4$ W; power in the 25 W bulb $= I^2R_2 = \dfrac{1936}{121} = 16$ W.
    The 25 W bulb glows brighter, because it has the higher resistance and so uses more power when the same current flows.
    (c) In parallel, each bulb gets 220 V and uses its rated power, so the 100 W bulb glows brighter.

    Marking scheme:

    • $R_1 = 484\ \Omega$ and $R_2 = 1936\ \Omega$ (1 mark)
    • Series current $= \frac{1}{11}$ A (½ marks)
    • Powers 4 W (100 W bulb) and 16 W (25 W bulb) (½ marks)
    • 25 W bulb glows brighter in series (½ marks)
    • 100 W bulb glows brighter in parallel (½ marks)

  9. Question 38 · 4 marks · Magnetic Effects of Current
    For a science fair, Riya winds 50 turns of insulated copper wire into a circular coil and fixes it vertically through a horizontal cardboard sheet. She passes a steady current through the coil, sprinkles iron filings on the sheet and taps it gently. The filings arrange themselves in a pattern, and a small compass needle placed at the centre of the coil is deflected.

    (i) Describe the magnetic field lines near the centre of the coil. [1]

    (ii) What will happen to the magnetic field at the centre if Riya uses a coil of 100 turns with the same current and size? Give a reason. [1]

    (iii) (a) Without changing the coil, how can Riya make the magnetic field at its centre stronger? Give a reason.
    (b) Riya now reverses the direction of the current in the coil. What change will she see in the compass needle? Why? [2]

    OR (iii) (a) State the right-hand thumb rule.
    (b) Looking at the coil from the front, Riya sees the current flowing anticlockwise. What is the direction of the magnetic field at the centre of the coil?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Describe the magnetic field lines near the centre of the coil.

    Model answer: Near the centre of the coil, the field lines are nearly straight and parallel, and they are perpendicular to the plane of the coil.

    Marking scheme:

    • Nearly straight (parallel) lines (½ marks)
    • Perpendicular to the plane of the coil (½ marks)

    (ii) What will happen to the magnetic field at the centre if Riya uses a coil of 100 turns with the same current and size? Give a reason.

    Model answer: The field becomes twice as strong. The fields of the turns add up, so a coil of n turns produces a field n times that of a single turn.

    Marking scheme:

    • Field doubles (½ marks)
    • Fields of all the turns add up (field is n times that of one turn) (½ marks)

    (iii) (a) Without changing the coil, how can Riya make the magnetic field at its centre stronger? Give a reason.
    (b) Riya now reverses the direction of the current in the coil. What change will she see in the compass needle? Why?

    Model answer: (a) By increasing the current through the coil. The magnetic field produced by a current-carrying wire at a given point depends directly on the current passing through it.
    (b) The compass needle is now deflected in the opposite direction, because reversing the direction of the current reverses the direction of the magnetic field at the centre of the coil.

    Marking scheme:

    • Increase the current (½ marks)
    • Field is directly proportional to the current (½ marks)
    • Needle is deflected in the opposite direction (½ marks)
    • Reversing the current reverses the direction of the magnetic field (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (a) Imagine holding a current-carrying straight conductor in the right hand so that the thumb points in the direction of the current. Then the fingers wrapped around the conductor give the direction of the magnetic field lines.
    (b) Applying the rule to each part of the coil, the field at the centre is perpendicular to the plane of the coil and points out of the coil towards Riya.

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct statement of the right-hand thumb rule (1 mark)
    • Field at the centre points towards Riya (out of the plane of the coil) (1 mark)

  10. Question 39 · 5 marks · Light Reflection and Refraction
    (a) Why is a convex mirror preferred as a rear-view mirror in vehicles?
    (b) An object 5 cm tall is placed 10 cm in front of a convex mirror of radius of curvature 30 cm. Find the position, size and nature of the image.
    (c) Which type of mirror is used in solar furnaces? Give a reason.

    OR (a) State the two laws of refraction of light.
    (b) Define the absolute refractive index of a medium.
    (c) The speed of light is $2.0 \times 10^8$ m/s in medium X and $1.5 \times 10^8$ m/s in medium Y. (i) Find the refractive index of medium Y with respect to medium X. (ii) A ray of light passes obliquely from X into Y. Will it bend towards or away from the normal? Give a reason.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) A convex mirror always gives an erect, diminished image, and it has a wider field of view, so the driver can see a large area behind the vehicle.
    (b) $R = +30$ cm, so $f = \dfrac{R}{2} = +15$ cm; $u = -10$ cm; $h = +5$ cm.
    $\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f} - \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{15} + \dfrac{1}{10} = \dfrac{2 + 3}{30} = \dfrac{1}{6}$, so $v = +6$ cm: the image is 6 cm behind the mirror.
    $m = -\dfrac{v}{u} = -\dfrac{6}{-10} = +0.6$; $h' = m \times h = 0.6 \times 5 = 3$ cm.
    The image is virtual, erect and diminished, 3 cm tall.
    (c) A large concave mirror. It converges (concentrates) the sunlight falling on it to a small region (its focus), producing a lot of heat there.

    Marking scheme:

    • Erect, diminished image and wider field of view (1 mark)
    • f = +15 cm with correct signs (u = -10 cm) (½ marks)
    • v = +6 cm (behind the mirror) (1 mark)
    • m = +0.6 (½ marks)
    • Image height 3 cm (½ marks)
    • Virtual, erect, diminished (½ marks)
    • Concave mirror (½ marks)
    • It concentrates sunlight at its focus to produce heat (½ marks)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: (a) (1) The incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal to the interface of two transparent media at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.
    (2) For light of a given colour and for a given pair of media, the ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is a constant: $\dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$ = constant (Snell's law).
    (b) The absolute refractive index of a medium is the ratio of the speed of light in air (vacuum) to the speed of light in the medium: $n_m = \dfrac{c}{v}$.
    (c) (i) $n_{YX} = \dfrac{v_X}{v_Y} = \dfrac{2.0 \times 10^8}{1.5 \times 10^8} = \dfrac{4}{3} \approx 1.33$.
    (ii) Towards the normal, because light slows down on entering Y; Y is optically denser than X.

    Marking scheme:

    • First law: incident ray, refracted ray and normal lie in one plane (1 mark)
    • Second law: $\sin i / \sin r$ = constant (Snell's law) (1 mark)
    • Absolute refractive index = speed of light in air (vacuum) / speed of light in the medium, $n_m = c/v$ (1 mark)
    • $n_{YX} = 4/3$ (about 1.33) (1 mark)
    • Bends towards the normal: Y is optically denser (light slows down) (1 mark)
