Section A: Biology

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Life Processes
    Which option shows the correct sequence of steps in the nutrition of human beings?

    1. Ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation, egestion
    2. Ingestion, absorption, digestion, assimilation, egestion
    3. Digestion, ingestion, absorption, egestion, assimilation
    4. Ingestion, digestion, assimilation, absorption, egestion
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation, egestion

    Food is first taken in (ingestion), broken down (digestion), the digested food is absorbed into the blood (absorption), used by the cells (assimilation), and the undigested remains are removed (egestion).

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Life Processes
    Oxygen-rich blood from the lungs enters the human heart through the:

    1. vena cava, into the right atrium
    2. pulmonary artery, into the right ventricle
    3. aorta, into the left ventricle
    4. pulmonary vein, into the left atrium
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) pulmonary vein, into the left atrium

    The pulmonary veins bring oxygenated blood from the lungs to the left atrium. The vena cava brings deoxygenated blood to the right atrium.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Life Processes
    A blood test shows that a patient has a very high level of urea in the blood. Which organ is most likely not working properly?

    1. Lungs
    2. Stomach
    3. Kidneys
    4. Small intestine
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Kidneys

    Urea is removed from the blood by filtration in the kidneys. If the kidneys do not work properly, urea builds up in the blood.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Control and Coordination
    A person has a swollen neck. The doctor advises the person to use iodised salt. Which gland is affected and which hormone does it make?

    1. Thyroid gland; thyroxine
    2. Pituitary gland; growth hormone
    3. Adrenal gland; adrenaline
    4. Pancreas; insulin
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Thyroid gland; thyroxine

    Iodine is needed by the thyroid gland to make thyroxine. Lack of iodine causes goitre, a swelling of the thyroid gland in the neck.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Heredity
    A tall pea plant (Tt) is crossed with a dwarf pea plant (tt). What fraction of the offspring is expected to be dwarf?

    1. 1/4
    2. 1/2
    3. 3/4
    4. 0
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) 1/2

    Tt gives T and t gametes in equal numbers; tt gives only t. Offspring: Tt (tall) and tt (dwarf) in the ratio 1 : 1, so half are dwarf.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Our Environment
    Which of the following is a human-made ecosystem?

    1. Forest
    2. Pond
    3. Crop field
    4. Lake
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Crop field

    Crop fields and gardens are ecosystems created and maintained by human beings. Forests, ponds and lakes are natural ecosystems.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Our Environment
    If all the decomposers in an ecosystem disappeared, what would happen?

    1. Producers would get more nutrients from the soil.
    2. Energy would flow faster to the top consumers.
    3. The number of herbivores would increase at once.
    4. Dead remains would pile up and nutrients would not be returned to the soil.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Dead remains would pile up and nutrients would not be returned to the soil.

    Decomposers break down dead plants and animals and return the nutrients to the soil. Without them, dead remains would collect and nutrients would not be recycled for producers.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Life Processes
    The following question consists of two statements: Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Answer it by selecting the appropriate option.
    Assertion (A): Water and minerals absorbed by the roots reach the leaves through the xylem.
    Reason (R): Food made in the leaves is carried to other parts of the plant through the phloem.

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.

    Both statements are true, but R is about the transport of food in the phloem. It does not explain how water and minerals move up through the xylem.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Our Environment
    The following question consists of two statements: Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Answer it by selecting the appropriate option.
    Assertion (A): Green plants in an ecosystem are called producers.
    Reason (R): Green plants capture about 10% of the solar energy that falls on their leaves.

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) A is true but R is false.

    Green plants are producers because they make food from sunlight, so A is true. They capture only about 1% of the solar energy falling on the leaves, so R is false.

  10. Question 10 · 2 marks · Life Processes
    Glucose is broken down in the absence of oxygen in yeast and also in human muscle cells during heavy exercise.
    (a) Name the end products of this breakdown in each case.
    (b) In which part of the cell does the first step, the breakdown of glucose into pyruvate, take place?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) In yeast: ethanol and carbon dioxide (with release of energy). In human muscle cells: lactic acid (with release of energy).
    (b) In the cytoplasm.

    Marking scheme:

    • Yeast: carbon dioxide (½ marks)
    • Muscle cells: lactic acid (½ marks)
    • Cytoplasm (½ marks)

  11. Question 11 · 2 marks · Our Environment
    (a) How is ozone formed in the upper atmosphere?
    (b) Why is the depletion of the ozone layer a cause of concern?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) High-energy ultraviolet radiation splits some oxygen molecules ($\mathrm{O_2}$) into free oxygen atoms ($\mathrm{O}$). These atoms combine with oxygen molecules to form ozone: $\mathrm{O_2 \rightarrow O + O}$; $\mathrm{O + O_2 \rightarrow O_3}$.
    (b) The ozone layer shields the surface of the Earth from the harmful ultraviolet radiation of the Sun. If it becomes thinner, more ultraviolet radiation reaches the Earth, which can cause skin cancer in human beings and harm other organisms.

    Marking scheme:

    • UV radiation splits $\mathrm{O_2}$ into oxygen atoms (½ marks)
    • Oxygen atoms combine with $\mathrm{O_2}$ to form $\mathrm{O_3}$ (½ marks)
    • Ozone layer shields the Earth from harmful UV radiation; depletion lets more UV in, causing harm such as skin cancer (1 mark)

  12. Question 12 · 2 marks · Life Processes
    State two features of the small intestine that help it absorb digested food efficiently. Give the role of each feature.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. The inner lining of the small intestine has numerous finger-like projections called villi. They greatly increase the surface area for absorption.
    2. The villi are richly supplied with blood vessels, which take the absorbed food to every cell of the body.
    (The great length of the small intestine, which gives food more time and area for absorption, is also accepted.)

    Marking scheme:

    • Villi increase the surface area for absorption (1 mark)
    • Villi have a rich supply of blood vessels that carry absorbed food away (or great length of intestine) (1 mark)

  13. Question 13 · 3 marks · Life Processes
    (a) Write the balanced chemical equation for photosynthesis.
    (b) Desert plants keep their stomata closed during the day. How do they still carry out photosynthesis?
    (c) A potted plant is kept in a dark room for two days, and then a leaf is tested with iodine solution. The leaf does not turn blue-black. Explain why.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) $\mathrm{6CO_2 + 12H_2O \xrightarrow[\text{chlorophyll}]{\text{sunlight}} C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2 + 6H_2O}$
    (b) Desert plants open their stomata at night and take in carbon dioxide, which is stored in the form of an intermediate (an acid). During the day, when the leaves absorb light energy, this stored carbon dioxide is used to make food.
    (c) In the dark, no photosynthesis takes place, so no new starch is made. The starch already present in the leaf is used up during the two days, so the leaf gives a negative starch test.

    Marking scheme:

    • Balanced equation with sunlight and chlorophyll (1 mark)
    • $\mathrm{CO_2}$ taken in at night and stored as an intermediate, used during the day (1 mark)
    • No photosynthesis in the dark and stored starch is used up, so no starch is present (1 mark)

  14. Question 14 · 3 marks · Heredity
    (a) Explain how the sex of a child is determined in human beings.
    (b) A family blames the mother for giving birth only to daughters. Is this correct? Justify your answer.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) A woman has a perfect pair of sex chromosomes, XX, so all her eggs carry an X chromosome. A man has a mismatched pair, XY, so half of his sperms carry an X chromosome and half carry a Y chromosome. If a sperm carrying X fertilises the egg, the child is XX, a girl. If a sperm carrying Y fertilises the egg, the child is XY, a boy.
    (b) No, it is not correct. The mother contributes only an X chromosome. The sex of the child depends on whether the sperm from the father carries an X or a Y chromosome.

    Marking scheme:

    • Female XX: all eggs carry X (½ marks)
    • Male XY: sperms carry X or Y (half each) (½ marks)
    • X sperm + egg gives XX (girl) (½ marks)
    • Y sperm + egg gives XY (boy) (½ marks)
    • Not correct: mother gives only X; sex depends on X or Y from the father's sperm (1 mark)

  15. Question 15 · 4 marks · Control and Coordination
    Arjun is the opening batsman of his school cricket team. Just before the final match, he feels nervous: his heart beats fast and he breathes quickly. During his innings he has to watch a fast-moving ball, move his bat at the right moment and keep his balance while running between the wickets. Messages travel rapidly through his nervous system from his eyes to his brain and from his brain to his muscles.

    (i) Name the hormone that is released when Arjun feels nervous, and the gland that secretes it. [1]

    (ii) State one effect of this hormone that prepares the body to deal with the situation. [1]

    (iii) (a) Name the part of the brain that maintains posture and balance of the body.
    (b) How is a message passed from one neuron to the next neuron? [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Name the hormone that is released when Arjun feels nervous, and the gland that secretes it.

    Model answer: Adrenaline, secreted by the adrenal glands.

    Marking scheme:

    • Adrenaline (½ marks)
    • Adrenal gland (½ marks)

    (ii) State one effect of this hormone that prepares the body to deal with the situation.

    Model answer: Any one: the heart beats faster, so more oxygen is supplied to the muscles; blood supply to the digestive system and skin is reduced and diverted to the skeletal muscles; the breathing rate increases.

    Marking scheme:

    • Any one correct effect (faster heartbeat supplying more oxygen to muscles, blood diverted to skeletal muscles, or faster breathing) (1 mark)

    (iii) (a) Name the part of the brain that maintains posture and balance of the body.
    (b) How is a message passed from one neuron to the next neuron?

    Model answer: (a) Cerebellum (part of the hind-brain).
    (b) The electrical impulse reaches the end of the axon of the first neuron and causes the release of chemicals. These chemicals cross the small gap (synapse) and start a similar electrical impulse in the dendrite of the next neuron.

    Marking scheme:

    • Cerebellum (1 mark)
    • Impulse at axon end releases chemicals that cross the synapse and start an impulse in the next neuron's dendrite (1 mark)

  16. Question 16 · 5 marks · How Do Organisms Reproduce
    (a) Distinguish between self-pollination and cross-pollination.
    (b) Describe the events that take place after a pollen grain lands on a suitable stigma, up to the formation of the zygote.
    (c) After fertilisation, what does each of the following develop into: (i) zygote, (ii) ovule, (iii) ovary? What happens to the petals and sepals?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Self-pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of the same flower (or another flower of the same plant). Cross-pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther of a flower of one plant to the stigma of a flower of another plant of the same kind.
    (b) The pollen grain on the stigma grows a pollen tube, which travels down through the style and reaches the ovule in the ovary. The male germ cell moves through the tube and fuses with the female germ cell (egg) in the ovule. This fusion forms the zygote.
    (c) (i) The zygote divides several times to form the embryo. (ii) The ovule develops a tough coat and becomes the seed. (iii) The ovary grows rapidly and ripens to form the fruit. The petals and sepals (with the stamens, style and stigma) shrivel and fall off.

    Marking scheme:

    • Self-pollination: pollen to stigma of same flower/plant (½ marks)
    • Cross-pollination: pollen to stigma of a flower on another plant (½ marks)
    • Pollen tube grows from the pollen grain through the style to the ovule (1 mark)
    • Male germ cell fuses with the egg in the ovule, forming the zygote (1 mark)
    • (i) Zygote: embryo (½ marks)
    • (ii) Ovule: seed (½ marks)
    • (iii) Ovary: fruit (½ marks)
    • Petals and sepals shrivel and fall off (½ marks)

Section B: Chemistry

  1. Question 17 · 1 mark · Chemical Reactions and Equations
    When solutions of barium chloride and sodium sulphate are mixed, a white precipitate is formed. Which option correctly names the precipitate and the type of reaction?

    1. Sodium chloride; combination reaction
    2. Barium sulphate; displacement reaction
    3. Barium sulphate; double displacement reaction
    4. Sodium chloride; double displacement reaction
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Barium sulphate; double displacement reaction

    $\mathrm{BaCl_2 + Na_2SO_4 \rightarrow BaSO_4 + 2NaCl}$. The ions are exchanged (double displacement) and insoluble white barium sulphate precipitates; sodium chloride stays in solution.

  2. Question 18 · 1 mark · Acids, Bases and Salts
    A few drops of phenolphthalein are added to each of the following. Which one turns pink?

    1. Vinegar
    2. Lemon juice
    3. Soda water
    4. Lime water
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Lime water

    Phenolphthalein turns pink only in a basic solution. Lime water (calcium hydroxide solution) is basic; vinegar, lemon juice and soda water are acidic.

  3. Question 19 · 1 mark · Acids, Bases and Salts
    Plaster of Paris should be stored in a moisture-proof container because it:

    1. reacts with water and sets into a hard mass of gypsum
    2. absorbs carbon dioxide and becomes washing soda
    3. changes into bleaching powder
    4. decomposes into lime
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) reacts with water and sets into a hard mass of gypsum

    Plaster of Paris ($\mathrm{CaSO_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}H_2O}$) combines with water to form gypsum ($\mathrm{CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O}$), a hard solid, so moisture would spoil it.

  4. Question 20 · 1 mark · Metals and Non-metals
    In which of the following will a displacement reaction NOT take place?

    1. Zinc granules added to copper sulphate solution
    2. Iron nail dipped in copper sulphate solution
    3. Copper wire dipped in iron sulphate solution
    4. Magnesium ribbon dipped in zinc sulphate solution
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Copper wire dipped in iron sulphate solution

    A metal displaces only a less reactive metal from its salt solution. Copper is less reactive than iron, so it cannot displace iron from iron sulphate.

  5. Question 21 · 1 mark · Carbon and Its Compounds
    How many covalent bonds are present in a molecule of ethane ($\mathrm{C_2H_6}$)?

    1. 6
    2. 7
    3. 8
    4. 9
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) 7

    Ethane has one C-C single bond and six C-H single bonds: 1 + 6 = 7 covalent bonds.

  6. Question 22 · 1 mark · Metals and Non-metals
    Thin foils of silver used to decorate sweets are made by hammering silver. Which property of metals does this use?

    1. Ductility
    2. Lustre
    3. Sonority
    4. Malleability
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Malleability

    Malleability is the property of being beaten into thin sheets. Ductility is the property of being drawn into wires.

  7. Question 23 · 1 mark · Carbon and Its Compounds
    Which of the following pairs of compounds belongs to the same homologous series?

    1. $\mathrm{C_2H_6}$ and $\mathrm{C_3H_8}$
    2. $\mathrm{CH_4}$ and $\mathrm{C_2H_4}$
    3. $\mathrm{C_2H_4}$ and $\mathrm{C_3H_8}$
    4. $\mathrm{C_2H_2}$ and $\mathrm{C_2H_6}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\mathrm{C_2H_6}$ and $\mathrm{C_3H_8}$

    $\mathrm{C_2H_6}$ and $\mathrm{C_3H_8}$ are both alkanes ($\mathrm{C_nH_{2n+2}}$) and differ by one $\mathrm{CH_2}$ unit. The other pairs mix alkanes, alkenes and alkynes.

  8. Question 24 · 1 mark · Acids, Bases and Salts
    The following question consists of two statements: Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Answer it by selecting the appropriate option.
    Assertion (A): While diluting a concentrated acid, the acid should be added slowly to water and not water to the acid.
    Reason (R): Mixing a concentrated acid with water is a highly exothermic process.

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

    Both statements are true. Because a lot of heat is released, adding water to the acid can make the mixture splash out and cause burns; adding acid slowly to a large amount of water keeps it safe. So R correctly explains A.

  9. Question 25 · 2 marks · Carbon and Its Compounds
    Draw the electron dot structures of (i) a nitrogen molecule, $\mathrm{N_2}$, and (ii) an ethene molecule, $\mathrm{C_2H_4}$. Show all the valence electrons. (Atomic numbers: N = 7, C = 6, H = 1)

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: The diagrams must show the following.
    (i) Each nitrogen atom has 5 valence electrons. The two nitrogen atoms share three pairs of electrons, so there is a triple bond, N$\equiv$N. Each nitrogen atom also has one unshared (lone) pair of electrons, which completes its octet.
    (ii) In ethene, the two carbon atoms share two pairs of electrons (a double bond, C=C). Each carbon atom shares one pair of electrons with each of two hydrogen atoms, so every carbon has an octet and every hydrogen has a duplet.

    Marking scheme:

    • $\mathrm{N_2}$: three shared pairs of electrons between the two N atoms (triple bond) (½ marks)
    • $\mathrm{N_2}$: one unshared (lone) pair on each N atom, so each N has 8 electrons (½ marks)
    • Ethene: two shared pairs of electrons between the two C atoms (double bond) (½ marks)
    • Ethene: each C shares one pair with each of two H atoms (12 valence electrons shown in all) (½ marks)

  11. Question 27 · 3 marks · Carbon and Its Compounds
    (a) A cook notices that the bottoms of the cooking vessels are getting blackened on the gas stove. What does this show, and what should be done?
    (b) What is formed when ethanol is heated with alkaline potassium permanganate solution? What is the role of the potassium permanganate?
    (c) Write the chemical equation for the reaction of methane with chlorine in the presence of sunlight, and name the type of reaction.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) It shows incomplete combustion of the fuel, because the supply of air is not enough (the flame is yellow and sooty). The air holes of the burner should be cleaned (opened) so that more air enters.
    (b) Ethanoic acid is formed. Alkaline potassium permanganate acts as an oxidising agent (it adds oxygen to ethanol).
    (c) $\mathrm{CH_4 + Cl_2 \xrightarrow{\text{sunlight}} CH_3Cl + HCl}$. It is a substitution reaction.

    Marking scheme:

    • Incomplete combustion due to insufficient air (½ marks)
    • Clean the air holes of the burner to let in more air (½ marks)
    • Ethanoic acid is formed (½ marks)
    • Potassium permanganate is an oxidising agent (½ marks)
    • Equation $\mathrm{CH_4 + Cl_2 \rightarrow CH_3Cl + HCl}$ (½ marks)
    • Substitution reaction (½ marks)

  12. Question 28 · 4 marks · Metals and Non-metals
    To study rusting, a student set up four test tubes, each containing a clean iron nail:
    Tube A: nail in ordinary tap water, open to the air.
    Tube B: nail in boiled distilled water, with a layer of oil on top of the water.
    Tube C: nail in dry air, with some anhydrous calcium chloride in the tube.
    Tube D: galvanised iron nail in ordinary tap water, open to the air.
    The tubes were left for one week.

    (i) In which test tube will the nail rust? [1]

    (ii) Why is a layer of oil put on the water in tube B, and why is anhydrous calcium chloride kept in tube C? [1]

    (iii) (a) Why does a galvanised nail not rust even if its zinc coating gets scratched?
    (b) Stainless steel is an alloy that does not rust. Name the two metals mixed with iron to make it. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) In which test tube will the nail rust?

    Model answer: Tube A, because there the nail is in contact with both air (oxygen) and water.

    Marking scheme:

    • Tube A (1 mark)

    (ii) Why is a layer of oil put on the water in tube B, and why is anhydrous calcium chloride kept in tube C?

    Model answer: The oil layer stops air from dissolving again in the boiled water. Anhydrous calcium chloride absorbs moisture and keeps the air in tube C dry.

    Marking scheme:

    • Oil stops air from dissolving in the water (½ marks)
    • Calcium chloride absorbs moisture (keeps air dry) (½ marks)

    (iii) (a) Why does a galvanised nail not rust even if its zinc coating gets scratched?
    (b) Stainless steel is an alloy that does not rust. Name the two metals mixed with iron to make it.

    Model answer: (a) Zinc is more reactive than iron. Even when the coating is broken, zinc reacts with air and moisture in preference to iron, so the iron stays protected.
    (b) Nickel and chromium.

    Marking scheme:

    • Zinc is more reactive than iron and corrodes in preference, protecting the iron (1 mark)
    • Nickel (½ marks)
    • Chromium (½ marks)

  13. Question 29 · 5 marks · Chemical Reactions and Equations
    (a) A student heats a few crystals of ferrous sulphate in a dry boiling tube.
    (i) What colour change is seen in the solid?
    (ii) What smell is noticed?
    (iii) Write the balanced chemical equation and name the type of reaction.
    (b) Silver chloride is stored in dark-coloured bottles. Explain why, with a chemical equation.
    (c) Chips and other foods containing oils are often packed in bags filled with nitrogen. Explain why.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) (i) The green crystals first lose water and then change into a reddish-brown solid (ferric oxide).
    (ii) A smell of burning sulphur (due to sulphur dioxide and sulphur trioxide gases).
    (iii) $\mathrm{2FeSO_4 \xrightarrow{\text{heat}} Fe_2O_3 + SO_2 + SO_3}$. It is a (thermal) decomposition reaction.
    (b) In sunlight, silver chloride decomposes into silver and chlorine: $\mathrm{2AgCl \xrightarrow{\text{sunlight}} 2Ag + Cl_2}$. Dark bottles keep out light and prevent this decomposition.
    (c) Oils and fats get oxidised by air and become rancid, changing their smell and taste. Nitrogen does not react with the food and keeps oxygen away, so it prevents oxidation (rancidity).

    Marking scheme:

    • Green colour changes to reddish-brown (½ marks)
    • Smell of burning sulphur (½ marks)
    • Balanced equation $\mathrm{2FeSO_4 \rightarrow Fe_2O_3 + SO_2 + SO_3}$ (1 mark)
    • (Thermal) decomposition reaction (1 mark)
    • Equation $\mathrm{2AgCl \rightarrow 2Ag + Cl_2}$ in sunlight (½ marks)
    • Dark bottles keep out light and prevent decomposition (½ marks)
    • Oils get oxidised by air and become rancid (½ marks)
    • Nitrogen keeps oxygen away, preventing oxidation (½ marks)

Section C: Physics

  1. Question 30 · 1 mark · Human Eye and Colourful World
    A student sitting in the last row of the classroom cannot read the writing on the board clearly, but can read her book easily. Which option gives her defect of vision and the lens that will correct it?

    1. Hypermetropia; convex lens
    2. Myopia; convex lens
    3. Myopia; concave lens
    4. Presbyopia; concave lens
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Myopia; concave lens

    She cannot see distant objects clearly but sees nearby objects well, so she has myopia (near-sightedness). It is corrected with a concave (diverging) lens.

  2. Question 31 · 1 mark · Electricity
    A wire of length $l$ and area of cross-section $A$ has resistance $R$. What is the resistance of another wire of the same material with length $2l$ and area of cross-section $2A$?

    1. $R/2$
    2. $R$
    3. $2R$
    4. $4R$
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) $R$

    $R = \rho l / A$. For the new wire, $R' = \rho (2l)/(2A) = \rho l/A = R$.

  3. Question 32 · 1 mark · Light Reflection and Refraction
    The following question consists of two statements: Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Answer it by selecting the appropriate option.
    Assertion (A): The focal length of a concave mirror whose radius of curvature is 30 cm is 60 cm.
    Reason (R): For spherical mirrors of small aperture, the radius of curvature is equal to twice the focal length.

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true but R is false.
    4. A is false but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) A is false but R is true.

    Since $R = 2f$, the focal length is $f = R/2 = 30/2 = 15$ cm, not 60 cm. So A is false and R is true.

  4. Question 33 · 2 marks · Human Eye and Colourful World
    White light is passed through a glass prism and, separately, through a rectangular glass slab. A spectrum is seen with the prism but not with the slab. Explain why.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: In a glass prism, the two refracting surfaces are inclined to each other. The different colours bend through different angles, and at the second surface they bend further apart, so they spread out and form a spectrum.
    In a rectangular glass slab, the two refracting surfaces are parallel. The bending at the second surface is equal and opposite to that at the first, so all the colours come out parallel to the incident ray and overlap again. Hence no spectrum is seen.

    Marking scheme:

    • Prism: inclined surfaces; colours deviate through different angles and spread out (1 mark)
    • Slab: parallel surfaces; emergent colours are parallel to the incident ray and overlap, so no spectrum (1 mark)

  5. Question 34 · 2 marks · Electricity
    An electric heater of resistance 20 $\Omega$ draws a current of 5 A from the mains. Calculate the heat produced by it in 1 minute.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: By Joule's law of heating, $H = I^2 R t$.
    $H = 5^2 \times 20 \times 60 = 25 \times 20 \times 60 = 30000$ J $= 3 \times 10^4$ J.

    Marking scheme:

    • Uses $H = I^2 R t$ with $t = 60$ s (1 mark)
    • $H = 30000$ J ($3 \times 10^4$ J) (1 mark)

  6. Question 35 · 3 marks · Human Eye and Colourful World
    (a) How do the ciliary muscles help the eye to see a nearby object clearly?
    (b) What is presbyopia? Why does it occur?
    (c) A person with presbyopia uses bifocal lenses. Which type of lens forms the upper part of a bifocal lens and which forms the lower part?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) To see a nearby object, the ciliary muscles contract. The eye lens becomes thicker, its focal length decreases, and the image of the nearby object is focused on the retina.
    (b) Presbyopia is the defect in which the power of accommodation of the eye decreases with age, so the near point moves farther away and nearby objects are not seen clearly. It occurs because the ciliary muscles weaken and the eye lens becomes less flexible.
    (c) The upper part is a concave lens (for distant vision); the lower part is a convex lens (for near vision).

    Marking scheme:

    • Ciliary muscles contract, lens becomes thicker and its focal length decreases (1 mark)
    • Presbyopia: power of accommodation decreases with age (near point recedes) (½ marks)
    • Cause: weakening of ciliary muscles and reduced flexibility of the eye lens (½ marks)
    • Upper part concave (distant vision) (½ marks)
    • Lower part convex (near vision) (½ marks)

  7. Question 36 · 3 marks · Magnetic Effects of Current
    (a) Describe the pattern of the magnetic field lines (i) around a straight wire carrying current, and (ii) inside a long solenoid carrying current.
    (b) A vertical wire carries current upwards. Seen from above, what is the direction of the magnetic field lines around it?
    (c) Which material is used for the core of an electromagnet? Give one reason.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) (i) The field lines are concentric circles around the wire, with the wire at the centre, in a plane perpendicular to the wire.
    (ii) Inside a long solenoid the field lines are parallel straight lines, showing that the field is the same at all points inside (uniform).
    (b) By the right-hand thumb rule (thumb pointing upwards along the current), the fingers curl anticlockwise when seen from above. The field lines are anticlockwise.
    (c) Soft iron. It becomes a strong magnet when current flows in the coil and loses its magnetism as soon as the current is switched off.

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) Concentric circles around the wire (½ marks)
    • (ii) Parallel straight lines inside the solenoid (uniform field) (½ marks)
    • Anticlockwise (seen from above) (1 mark)
    • Soft iron (½ marks)
    • Reason: magnetised strongly when current flows and loses magnetism when current stops (½ marks)

  8. Question 37 · 3 marks · Electricity
    Two resistors of 10 $\Omega$ and 15 $\Omega$ are available.
    (a) Find the current drawn when they are connected in series to a 10 V supply.
    (b) Find the total current drawn when they are connected in parallel to a 6 V supply.
    (c) Give two reasons why electrical appliances in homes are connected in parallel and not in series.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Series: $R = 10 + 15 = 25\ \Omega$; $I = \dfrac{V}{R} = \dfrac{10}{25} = 0.4$ A.
    (b) Parallel: $\dfrac{1}{R_p} = \dfrac{1}{10} + \dfrac{1}{15} = \dfrac{3 + 2}{30} = \dfrac{1}{6}$, so $R_p = 6\ \Omega$; $I = \dfrac{6}{6} = 1$ A.
    (c) Any two: each appliance gets the full mains voltage; if one appliance fails or is switched off, the others keep working; each appliance can be switched on or off separately; each appliance draws the current it needs according to its own resistance.

    Marking scheme:

    • Series: $R = 25\ \Omega$ and $I = 0.4$ A (1 mark)
    • Parallel: $R_p = 6\ \Omega$ and $I = 1$ A (1 mark)
    • First correct reason (for example, each appliance gets full voltage) (½ marks)
    • Second correct reason (for example, one failing does not stop the others) (½ marks)

  9. Question 38 · 4 marks · Magnetic Effects of Current
    Ravi's house has a domestic circuit with three wires: a live wire with red insulation, a neutral wire with black insulation and an earth wire with green insulation. The kitchen circuit is protected by a 10 A fuse. One morning, Ravi switches on an electric kettle of 2200 W and a toaster of 1100 W together on the kitchen circuit. Later in the day, an electrician warns him that some old wiring in the kitchen is loose and could cause a short circuit.

    (i) What is the potential difference between the live wire and the neutral wire in Indian homes? [1]

    (ii) Will the 10 A fuse blow when the kettle and the toaster are used together? Show your working. [1]

    (iii) (a) Why is the metal body of the kettle connected to the earth wire?
    (b) What is meant by a short circuit? What happens to the current in the circuit when it occurs? [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) What is the potential difference between the live wire and the neutral wire in Indian homes?

    Model answer: 220 V.

    Marking scheme:

    • 220 V (1 mark)

    (ii) Will the 10 A fuse blow when the kettle and the toaster are used together? Show your working.

    Model answer: Total power = 2200 + 1100 = 3300 W. Current $I = \dfrac{P}{V} = \dfrac{3300}{220} = 15$ A. This is more than 10 A, so the fuse will blow (melt).

    Marking scheme:

    • Current $= 3300/220 = 15$ A (½ marks)
    • Yes, the fuse blows since 15 A is more than 10 A (½ marks)

    (iii) (a) Why is the metal body of the kettle connected to the earth wire?
    (b) What is meant by a short circuit? What happens to the current in the circuit when it occurs?

    Model answer: (a) The earth wire provides a low-resistance path for current. If the live wire touches the metal body due to a fault, the current leaks to the earth and the user does not get a severe electric shock.
    (b) A short circuit occurs when the live wire and the neutral wire come into direct contact. The resistance of the circuit becomes very small, so the current in the circuit increases suddenly to a very large value.

    Marking scheme:

    • Earth wire gives a low-resistance path for leakage current, so the user does not get a shock (1 mark)
    • Short circuit: live and neutral wires touch directly (½ marks)
    • Resistance becomes very small and current increases sharply (½ marks)

  10. Question 39 · 5 marks · Light Reflection and Refraction
    (a) Define the power of a lens and give its SI unit.
    (b) An object 3 cm high is placed 15 cm in front of a convex lens of focal length 10 cm. Find the position, size and nature of the image.
    (c) A concave lens has a power of $-2.5$ D. Find its focal length.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) The power of a lens is the reciprocal of its focal length (in metres): $P = \dfrac{1}{f}$. It shows the degree to which the lens converges or diverges light. SI unit: dioptre (D).
    (b) $u = -15$ cm, $f = +10$ cm.
    Lens formula: $\dfrac{1}{v} - \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{f}$, so $\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f} + \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{10} - \dfrac{1}{15} = \dfrac{3 - 2}{30} = \dfrac{1}{30}$
    $v = +30$ cm: the image is 30 cm from the lens on the other side.
    Magnification $m = \dfrac{v}{u} = \dfrac{30}{-15} = -2$
    Height of image $h' = m \times h = -2 \times 3 = -6$ cm.
    The image is real, inverted and magnified (6 cm tall).
    (c) $f = \dfrac{1}{P} = \dfrac{1}{-2.5}$ m $= -0.4$ m $= -40$ cm.

    Marking scheme:

    • Power is the reciprocal of focal length ($P = 1/f$, $f$ in metres) (½ marks)
    • SI unit: dioptre (D) (½ marks)
    • Lens formula with correct signs: $\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{10} - \frac{1}{15}$ (1 mark)
    • $v = +30$ cm (on the other side of the lens) (½ marks)
    • $m = v/u = -2$ (½ marks)
    • Image height $= -6$ cm (6 cm, inverted) (½ marks)
    • Nature: real, inverted, magnified (½ marks)
    • $f = 1/P = -0.4$ m $= -40$ cm (1 mark)
