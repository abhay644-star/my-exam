A few questions from this chapter. Open each one to see the answer.

  1. Identify the subordinating conjunction: 'I stayed indoors because it was snowing.'

    1. was
    2. stayed
    3. indoors
    4. because
    Show answer

    Answer: because

    'Because' introduces the subordinate clause of reason.

  2. What kind of clause is in italics? 'The place where we camped was beautiful.'

    1. main clause
    2. adjective clause
    3. adverb clause
    4. noun clause
    Show answer

    Answer: adjective clause

    The clause describes the noun 'place', so it is an adjective clause.

  3. Identify the noun clause: 'What he said surprised everyone.'

    1. what he said
    2. surprised everyone
    3. he said
    4. everyone
    Show answer

    Answer: what he said

    'What he said' is the subject of the verb 'surprised', so it is a noun clause.

  4. Identify the relative pronoun: 'The girl whom you met yesterday is my cousin.'

    1. met
    2. girl
    3. whom
    4. yesterday
    Show answer

    Answer: whom

    'Whom' joins the clause 'whom you met yesterday' to the noun 'girl'.

  5. What kind of clause is in italics? 'I know that he is honest.'

    1. adverb clause
    2. main clause
    3. adjective clause
    4. noun clause
    Show answer

    Answer: noun clause

    The clause is the object of the verb 'know', so it is a noun clause.

  6. What kind of clause is in italics? 'He ran fast so that he could catch the bus.'

    1. noun clause
    2. main clause
    3. adverb clause
    4. adjective clause
    Show answer

    Answer: adverb clause

    The clause shows purpose and modifies the verb 'ran', so it is an adverb clause.

  7. Identify the main clause: 'When the bell rang, the students left.'

    1. when
    2. the students left
    3. the bell rang
    4. when the bell rang
    Show answer

    Answer: the students left

    'The students left' makes complete sense on its own, so it is the main clause.

  8. What kind of clause is in italics? 'Although it was raining, we went out.'

    1. adverb clause
    2. adjective clause
    3. main clause
    4. noun clause
    Show answer

    Answer: adverb clause

    The clause shows contrast (concession) and modifies the verb 'went', so it is an adverb clause.
