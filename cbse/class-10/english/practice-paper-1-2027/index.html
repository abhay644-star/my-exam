Section A: Reading Skills

  1. Question 1 · 10 marks · Reading Comprehension
    Read the following passage.

    (1) Ask any parent what they dread most during the summer holidays, and many will give the same answer: a child who says, "I'm bored." Within minutes, a screen is switched on, a class is booked or a list of activities appears on the fridge. We treat boredom as a problem to be fixed as quickly as possible. But what if boredom is not an enemy at all? What if it is one of the most useful feelings a young person can have?

    (2) Boredom is, at heart, a signal. It tells us that what we are doing no longer holds our attention and that we need something new. Psychologists who study the feeling describe it as uncomfortable but not harmful. Like hunger, it pushes us to act. The real question is what we reach for when it arrives. A child who is handed a phone every time the signal sounds learns only one response: look for something outside yourself to fill the gap.

    (3) A child who is allowed to sit with the feeling a little longer often discovers something else. At first there is complaining, then restlessness, and then, surprisingly often, an idea. An old cardboard box becomes a shop counter. A few pebbles turn into a board game with rules invented on the spot. A notebook fills up with a story about a cat who runs a bus company. None of this is planned by an adult, and that is exactly the point.

    (4) In one well-known experiment, people who were first given a dull task, such as copying numbers from a telephone directory, later thought of more creative uses for everyday objects than people who had not been bored. The mind, it seems, wanders when it is under-stimulated, and a wandering mind makes unexpected connections. Daydreaming is not wasted time; it is the brain sorting, linking and imagining.

    (5) Boredom also teaches patience and self-reliance. In a world where entertainment is available at the touch of a button, many teenagers find it hard to wait in a queue or sit through a slow chapter of a book. Learning to handle a few empty minutes without panic is a skill, much like learning to swim. It is uncomfortable at first, but it builds a kind of inner strength that lasts.

    (6) None of this means that children should be left with nothing to do all day. Hobbies, sport and good books matter. But adults might try a small experiment: the next time a child complains of boredom, resist the urge to rescue. Offer a little time, a little space and perhaps a box of odds and ends. The empty afternoon may turn out to be a gift.

Section B: Grammar and Creative Writing Skills

  1. Question 3 · 1 mark · Verbs and Tenses
    Select the correct option to complete the line from a travel diary.
    When we reached the platform at 7:10, our train ________ already, so we had to wait for the next one.

    1. left
    2. had left
    3. has left
    4. was leaving
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) had left

    The past perfect 'had left' shows an action completed before another past action (reaching the platform); 'already' supports it.

    Marking scheme:

  3. Question 5 · 1 mark · Articles and Determiners
    Choose the correct option to fill in the blank.
    Don't worry, there are still ________ seats left for the evening show; you can book them after lunch.

    1. few
    2. little
    3. a few
    4. much
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) a few

    'A few' (some) is used with countable nouns and has a positive sense, which fits 'Don't worry'. 'Few' means hardly any; 'little' and 'much' are used with uncountable nouns.

  10. Question 12 · 1 mark · Reported Speech
    Select the option that correctly reports what Meera said.
    Meera said, "I am preparing for the debate tomorrow."

    1. Meera said that she is preparing for the debate tomorrow.
    2. Meera told that she was preparing for the debate the next day.
    3. Meera said that she was preparing for the debate the next day.
    4. Meera said that she had prepared for the debate the next day.

    Answer: (c) Meera said that she was preparing for the debate the next day.

    The Bhopal Herald
    Bhopal

  12. Question 14 · 5 marks
    The table shows how the students of Sunrise Public School travelled to school in 2022 and in 2026. Write an analytical paragraph in about 120 words analysing the changes.

    Mode of travel20222026
    Walking30%22%
    Bicycle12%20%
    School bus25%30%
    Private car18%10%
    Two-wheeler with a parent15%18%

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: The table compares how the students of Sunrise Public School travelled to school in 2022 and 2026. In 2022, walking was the most common mode, at 30%, but by 2026 it had fallen to 22%. The most striking change is the rise in cycling, which went up from 12% to 20%. The school bus also became more popular, rising from 25% to 30%, and was the most common mode in 2026. In contrast, the share of students coming by private car nearly halved, falling from 18% to 10%. Meanwhile, travel on two-wheelers with a parent rose slightly, from 15% to 18%. Overall, the data shows a clear shift towards shared and greener transport, although the fall in walking deserves attention.

    Marking scheme:

    • Content: the key changes are reported with correct figures (e.g. cycling 12% to 20%, car 18% to 10%, bus 25% to 30%, walking 30% to 22%) (1 mark)
    • Content: an overall trend or conclusion is drawn (e.g. a shift towards shared/greener transport), not just a list of figures (1 mark)
    • Organisation: an introductory sentence and a concluding statement; no title (1 mark)
    • Organisation: points in a logical order, linked with words of comparison (in contrast, meanwhile, overall) (1 mark)
    • Accuracy: grammar, spelling and punctuation are correct (1 mark)

  2. Question 16 · 5 marks
    Read the following extract and answer the questions that follow.

    He stalks in his vivid stripes
    The few steps of his cage,
    On pads of velvet quiet,
    In his quiet rage.
    (A Tiger in the Zoo, Leslie Norris)

    (i) Complete the sentence suitably.
    The phrase 'the few steps of his cage' emphasises the ________ of the tiger's living space. [1]

    (ii) The word 'stalks' suggests that the tiger moves ________.
    A. playfully and fast
    B. slowly and quietly, with a sense of threat
    C. clumsily and noisily
    D. lazily and without purpose [1]

    (iii) Fill in the blank with the correct option from the bracket.
    The phrase 'quiet rage' is an example of ________, because it joins two ideas that seem opposite. (oxymoron / simile) [1]

    (iv) How does the poet use the contrast between the tiger's 'vivid stripes' and his small cage to show his feelings about keeping wild animals in zoos? [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Complete the sentence suitably.
    The phrase 'the few steps of his cage' emphasises the ________ of the tiger's living space.

    Model answer: smallness / narrowness (how cramped and limited it is)

    Marking scheme:

    • smallness / limited size / confinement (1 mark)

    (ii) The word 'stalks' suggests that the tiger moves ________.
    A. playfully and fast
    B. slowly and quietly, with a sense of threat
    C. clumsily and noisily
    D. lazily and without purpose

    Model answer: B. slowly and quietly, with a sense of threat

    Marking scheme:

    • Option B (1 mark)

    (iii) Fill in the blank with the correct option from the bracket.
    The phrase 'quiet rage' is an example of ________, because it joins two ideas that seem opposite. (oxymoron / simile)

    Model answer: oxymoron

    Marking scheme:

    • oxymoron (1 mark)

    (iv) How does the poet use the contrast between the tiger's 'vivid stripes' and his small cage to show his feelings about keeping wild animals in zoos?

    Model answer: The 'vivid stripes' suggest the tiger's beauty, strength and wild energy, yet he can walk only 'the few steps of his cage'. The contrast shows that his power is wasted and that he is angry but helpless. Through it the poet expresses sympathy for the tiger and suggests that wild animals belong in their natural home, not in cages.

    Marking scheme:

    • Vivid stripes (beauty, power, wildness) set against the few steps of the cage: power wasted, silent anger (1 mark)
    • The poet's view: sympathy for the tiger; wild animals belong in the wild, not in cages (1 mark)

  9. Question 23 · 6 marks
    Valli's bus ride is both an adventure and a lesson. Discuss how her careful planning shows her independence, and what the sight of the dead cow on her return journey teaches her. (Madam Rides the Bus)

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Valli, an eight-year-old girl, longed to ride the bus that passed her village. She planned the trip carefully: she found out the fare and the journey time, and saved every coin she got, refusing peppermints, toys and a merry-go-round ride, until she had sixty paise. She slipped away alone during her mother's afternoon nap. On the bus she refused help, paid her own fare and behaved like a grown-up, showing independence and self-respect. The ride was a joyful adventure until, on the way back, she saw the young cow that had earlier run in front of the bus lying dead by the road. The sight showed her that death is real and sudden, and her joy turned to sadness. She returned a little wiser.

    Marking scheme:

    • Content: careful planning: finding out the fare and time, saving coins, giving up treats (1 mark)
    • Content: independence: travels alone, refuses help, pays her own fare, behaves like a grown-up (1 mark)
    • Content: the dead cow: her first close view of death; joy turns to sadness and she learns that life is uncertain (1 mark)
    • Expression: ideas are organised logically, with an opening and a conclusion (1 mark)
    • Expression: the answer is fluent, relevant and in about 100-120 words (1 mark)
    • Accuracy: grammar, spelling and punctuation are correct (1 mark)

  10. Question 24 · 6 marks
    Apprentice Noodle is a minor character, yet he plays an important part in the play. Discuss how Noodle deals with Think-Tank's mistakes and what finally happens to him. (The Book That Saved the Earth)

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Noodle is the apprentice of Think-Tank, the arrogant ruler of Mars who believes he is never wrong. Noodle always flatters him, calling him 'Great and Mighty Think-Tank' and 'your Cleverness', yet he quietly corrects his mistakes. When Think-Tank says the books are sandwiches to be eaten, Noodle mentions an 'insignificant bit of data': Earthlings did not eat them but used them to communicate. Later he recalls that they opened the books and watched them, and he reminds Think-Tank about the vitamins that increase intelligence. Each time, Think-Tank takes the idea as his own. Noodle's tact keeps him safe while he guides the action. In the end, Think-Tank flees in panic, and the Historian tells us that he was replaced by the wise and wonderful Noodle.

    Marking scheme:

    • Content: Noodle flatters Think-Tank ('Great and Mighty', 'your Cleverness') and never contradicts him openly (1 mark)
    • Content: he corrects mistakes tactfully (books not eaten but used to communicate; opened and watched; vitamins); Think-Tank takes the credit (1 mark)
    • Content: outcome: Think-Tank flees in panic and is later replaced by 'the wise and wonderful Noodle' (1 mark)
    • Expression: ideas are organised logically, with an opening and a conclusion (1 mark)
    • Expression: the answer is fluent, relevant and in about 100-120 words (1 mark)
    • Accuracy: grammar, spelling and punctuation are correct (1 mark)
