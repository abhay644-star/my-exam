General instructions

  1. This question paper contains 24 questions. All questions are compulsory.

Section A: Reading Skills

  1. Question 1 · 10 marks · Reading Comprehension
    Read the following passage.

    (1) Most of us remember the sinking feeling of a red mark on an answer sheet. From an early age, we learn to see a mistake as something to hide, a small stain on our record. Schools reward the right answer, parents celebrate full marks, and many students would rather leave a question blank than risk getting it wrong. But this fear of failure may be costing young people far more than a few marks.

    (2) Think of how a baby learns to walk. She stands, wobbles and falls, again and again. No one calls her a failure; everyone understands that each fall teaches her a little more about balance. Somewhere between the playground and the examination hall, we forget this simple lesson. We begin to believe that clever people get things right the first time, and that a mistake proves we are not good enough.

    (3) Studies of how people learn tell a different story. When students are asked to attempt a problem before they are taught how to solve it, they often struggle and make errors. Yet the same students frequently understand the topic more deeply later, because the struggle has prepared their minds to notice what matters. A mistake, looked at carefully, shows exactly where our understanding breaks down. It is less a verdict than a map.

    (4) Of course, not every failure is useful. A mistake teaches us something only if we stop to ask why it happened. A student who glances at a wrong answer, sighs and turns the page has gained nothing. One who goes back, finds the faulty step and tries again has gained a great deal. The difference lies not in the mistake itself but in what we do with it.

    (5) Many of the things we rely on every day came out of repeated failure. Engineers test materials until they find the point at which they break. Scientists run experiments that fail for years before one finally works. Even great writers fill wastepaper baskets with drafts before they produce a page they are proud of. In each case, failure is treated not as the end of the road but as useful information.

    (6) Perhaps the most valuable thing we can teach young people, then, is not how to avoid mistakes but how to recover from them. That means praising effort and improvement, not only results; giving students the chance to correct their work; and admitting our own errors openly. A classroom where it is safe to be wrong is, in the end, a classroom where people learn to be right.

Students were asked to bring food in reusable boxes, carry steel bottles and avoid packaged snacks. To see whether the drive was working, the school's eco-club weighed the waste collected from all the classrooms after lunch.

Section B: Grammar and Creative Writing Skills

  1. Question 3 · 1 mark · Active and Passive Voice
    Select the correct option to complete the line from a school announcement.
    The new library block ________ by the Chief Guest next Monday.

    1. will be inaugurated
    2. was inaugurated
    3. has inaugurated
    4. is inaugurating
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) will be inaugurated

    The block does not perform the action, so the passive is needed, and 'next Monday' needs the future: 'will be inaugurated'. 'Was inaugurated' is past; 'has inaugurated' and 'is inaugurating' are active.

  2. Question 4 · 1 mark · Reported Speech

    Rohan said to me, "I have lost my library card."
    Rohan told me ________.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Rohan told me that he had lost his library card.

    Marking scheme:

    • that he had lost his library card (1 mark)

  3. Question 5 · 1 mark · Articles and Determiners
    Choose the correct option to fill in the blank.
    There isn't ________ sugar in the jar, so please buy some on your way home.

    1. many
    2. any
    3. few
    4. a few
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) any

    'Sugar' is an uncountable noun, and in a negative sentence 'any' is used. 'Many', 'few' and 'a few' go with countable nouns.

  4. Question 6 · 1 mark · Subject–Verb Agreement
    Identify the error in the given line from a lost-and-found notice and supply the correction.
    The bunch of keys were found under a bench near the main gate.
    Use the format: Error | Correction

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Error: were | Correction: was

    Marking scheme:

    • Error: were; Correction: was (the subject is 'bunch', which is singular) (1 mark)

  5. Question 7 · 1 mark · Modals
    Select the correct option to complete the sentence from a sports report.
    When he was only five, Arjun ________ swim across the pool without any help.

    1. can
    2. could
    3. must
    4. should
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) could

    'Could' expresses ability in the past ('when he was only five'). 'Can' is present; 'must' and 'should' express obligation and advice, not ability.

  10. Question 12 · 1 mark · Reported Speech
    Select the option that correctly reports Neha's question.
    Neha said to her mother, "Can I go to the fair with my friends?"

    1. Neha asked her mother if she could go to the fair with her friends.
    2. Neha asked her mother can she go to the fair with her friends.
    3. Neha asked her mother that could she go to the fair with her friends.
    4. Neha told her mother if she can go to the fair with her friends.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Neha asked her mother if she could go to the fair with her friends.

    A yes/no question is reported with 'if' or 'whether', in statement order, and 'can' changes to 'could'. 'Told' does not introduce a question, and 'that' is not used before a reported question.

  12. Question 14 · 5 marks
    Attempt any one of the two, A or B.

    A. The table shows how the Class 10 students of Green Park School spent their pocket money in 2023 and in 2026. Write an analytical paragraph in about 120 words analysing the changes.

    Item20232026
    Snacks and fast food35%25%
    Mobile recharges and apps15%28%
    Stationery and books20%22%
    Savings20%15%
    Gifts and others10%10%

    OR B. Your school is discussing whether printed textbooks should be replaced with tablets. Study the notes below and write an analytical paragraph in about 120 words, evaluating whether this is a good idea.
    Advantages: lighter school bags; videos and animations explain difficult topics; books can be updated quickly; saves paper
    Disadvantages: tablets are costly; not all families have internet; strain on the eyes; distraction by games; a broken or uncharged tablet stops learning

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: The table shows how Class 10 students of Green Park School spent their pocket money in 2023 and in 2026. In 2023, snacks and fast food took the largest share, at 35%, but by 2026 this had fallen to 25%. The most striking change is the rise in spending on mobile recharges and apps, which nearly doubled, from 15% to 28%, making it the largest item in 2026. In contrast, savings fell from 20% to 15%. Spending on stationery and books rose slightly, from 20% to 22%, while gifts and other items stayed steady at 10%. Overall, the data shows a clear shift from food towards digital spending, and the fall in savings is a cause for concern.

    Marking scheme:

    • Content: the key changes are reported with correct figures (e.g. snacks 35% to 25%, mobile and apps 15% to 28%, savings 20% to 15%) (1 mark)
    • Content: an overall trend or conclusion is drawn (e.g. a shift from food to digital spending; falling savings), not just a list of figures (1 mark)
