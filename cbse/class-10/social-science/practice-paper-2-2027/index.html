Section A: History

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · History: Print Culture and the Modern World
    Why did the Roman Catholic Church begin to maintain an Index of Prohibited Books from 1558?

    1. To fix the prices of printed books sold in Europe
    2. To encourage the printing of the Bible in local languages
    3. To control the spread of printed ideas that questioned its beliefs
    4. To support the printing of Protestant writings
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) To control the spread of printed ideas that questioned its beliefs

    Printed books and tracts spread new readings of faith that the Church saw as heresy (for example, Menocchio's ideas). To curb such ideas, the Church imposed controls over publishers and booksellers and began the Index of Prohibited Books in 1558.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · History: The Making of a Global World
    In the pre-modern world, which of the following gradually moved the centre of world trade westwards and made Europe its centre?

    1. The opening of the silk routes by European traders
    2. The decline in the importance of the Americas
    3. A ban on foreign trade by the rulers of India
    4. China's reduced role in world trade and the rising importance of the Americas
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) China's reduced role in world trade and the rising importance of the Americas

    Until well into the eighteenth century, China and India were among the richest countries. But from the fifteenth century China restricted its overseas contacts. China's reduced role and the rising importance of the Americas moved the centre of world trade westwards to Europe.

  5. Question 5 · 2 marks · History: The Making of a Global World
    Explain two effects of the introduction of the potato in Europe.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. Europe's poor began to eat better and live longer once the humble potato became part of their diet.
    2. Ireland's poorest peasants became so dependent on potatoes that when disease destroyed the potato crop in the mid-1840s, hundreds of thousands died of starvation. In the Great Irish Potato Famine (1845-1849), around 1,000,000 people died of starvation and about double that number emigrated in search of work.

    Marking scheme:

    • The poor in Europe ate better and lived longer (1 mark)
    • Heavy dependence on potatoes in Ireland led to famine, deaths and emigration when the crop was destroyed by disease (1845-1849) (1 mark)

  6. Question 6 · 3 marks · History: Print Culture and the Modern World
    How did women in India become readers and writers with the growth of print in the nineteenth century? Explain with three examples.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. Liberal husbands and fathers began educating their womenfolk at home, and sent them to schools when women's schools were set up in the cities after the mid-nineteenth century. Many journals carried writings by women and explained why women should be educated.
    2. Rashsundari Debi, a young married girl in a very orthodox household, learnt to read in the secrecy of her kitchen. Later she wrote her autobiography, Amar Jiban, published in 1876, the first full-length autobiography in the Bengali language.
    3. From the 1860s, a few Bengali women such as Kailashbashini Debi wrote books highlighting the experiences of women: how they were imprisoned at home, kept in ignorance and forced to do hard domestic labour.
    4. In the 1880s, in present-day Maharashtra, Tarabai Shinde and Pandita Ramabai wrote with passionate anger about the miserable lives of upper-caste Hindu women, especially widows.

    Marking scheme:

    • First example, e.g. women educated at home and in new women's schools; journals carried women's writings (1 mark)
    • Second example, e.g. Rashsundari Debi learnt to read secretly and wrote Amar Jiban (1876) (1 mark)
    • Third example, e.g. Kailashbashini Debi, or Tarabai Shinde and Pandita Ramabai writing about the lives of women and widows (1 mark)

  7. Question 7 · 5 marks · History: Nationalism in India
    Describe how the peasants of Awadh and the tribal peasants of the Gudem Hills in Andhra Pradesh took part in the Non-Cooperation Movement. How did their idea of swaraj differ from that of the Congress? (2 + 2 + 1 = 5)

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Awadh:
    1. The peasants, led by Baba Ramchandra, a sanyasi who had earlier been an indentured labourer in Fiji, struggled against talukdars and landlords who demanded very high rents and other cesses and forced them to do begar.
    2. They demanded reduction of revenue, abolition of begar and social boycott of oppressive landlords; panchayats organised nai-dhobi bandhs. The Oudh Kisan Sabha was set up in October 1920, headed by Jawaharlal Nehru, Baba Ramchandra and others. In 1921, the houses of talukdars and merchants were attacked, bazaars were looted and grain hoards taken over.
    Gudem Hills:
    3. In the early 1920s a militant guerrilla movement spread here. The government had closed large forest areas, stopping people from grazing cattle and collecting fuelwood and fruits, and forced them to do begar for road building.
    4. Led by Alluri Sitaram Raju, who spoke of Gandhiji's greatness but believed India could be freed only by force, the rebels attacked police stations, tried to kill British officials and carried on guerrilla warfare. Raju was captured and executed in 1924.
    Idea of swaraj:
    5. Each group gave swaraj its own meaning. For the Awadh peasants it meant freedom from oppressive landlords and begar; for the tribals it meant the right to enter forests and use their resources freely. Many used violent methods, while the Congress wanted a non-violent struggle for self-rule.

    Marking scheme:

    • Awadh: Baba Ramchandra led peasants against talukdars and landlords over high rents, cesses and begar (1 mark)
    • Awadh: methods, e.g. nai-dhobi bandhs, Oudh Kisan Sabha (October 1920), or attacks on houses of talukdars and looting of bazaars in 1921 (1 mark)
    • Gudem Hills: anger over closure of forests and forced begar for road building (1 mark)
    • Gudem Hills: guerrilla movement under Alluri Sitaram Raju; attacks on police stations; Raju executed in 1924 (1 mark)
    • Swaraj meant freedom from local oppression (landlords, forest laws) to them, often by force, unlike the non-violent swaraj of the Congress (1 mark)

  8. Question 8 · 4 marks · History: The Rise of Nationalism in Europe
    Read the following text and answer the questions that follow:
    <b>The Frankfurt Parliament</b>
    In 1848, many political associations in the German regions, whose members were middle-class professionals, businessmen and prosperous artisans, met in the city of Frankfurt and decided to elect an all-German National Assembly. In May 1848, 831 elected representatives took their seats in the Church of St Paul. They drafted a constitution for a German nation that would be headed by a monarchy answerable to a parliament. The deputies offered the crown on these terms to Friedrich Wilhelm IV, the King of Prussia, but he refused it and joined other monarchs in opposing the elected assembly. The aristocracy and the army grew stronger in their opposition. The parliament was dominated by the middle classes, who resisted the demands of workers and artisans and so lost their support. Women were admitted only as observers in the visitors' gallery. In the end, troops were called in and the assembly was forced to break up.

    8.1 Who refused the crown offered by the Frankfurt Parliament? [1]

    8.2 Why did the Frankfurt Parliament lose the support of workers and artisans? [1]

    8.3 The liberal movements of 1848 were suppressed, yet the old order could not be fully restored. Evaluate two changes that took place in Central and Eastern Europe after 1848. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    8.1 Who refused the crown offered by the Frankfurt Parliament?

    Model answer: Friedrich Wilhelm IV, the King of Prussia.

    Marking scheme:

    • Friedrich Wilhelm IV, King of Prussia (1 mark)

    8.2 Why did the Frankfurt Parliament lose the support of workers and artisans?

    Model answer: It was dominated by the middle classes, who resisted the demands of workers and artisans.

    Marking scheme:

    • The middle classes who dominated it resisted the demands of workers and artisans (1 mark)

    8.3 The liberal movements of 1848 were suppressed, yet the old order could not be fully restored. Evaluate two changes that took place in Central and Eastern Europe after 1848.

    Model answer: Monarchs began to realise that the cycles of revolution and repression could be ended only by granting concessions to the liberal-nationalist revolutionaries. So, after 1848, the autocratic monarchies of Central and Eastern Europe introduced changes that had already taken place in Western Europe before 1815:
    1. Serfdom and bonded labour were abolished both in the Habsburg dominions and in Russia.
    2. The Habsburg rulers granted more autonomy to the Hungarians in 1867.

    Marking scheme:

    • First change: serfdom and bonded labour abolished in the Habsburg dominions and Russia (1 mark)
    • Second change: more autonomy granted to the Hungarians by the Habsburg rulers (1867), or a correct statement that monarchs granted concessions to end the cycle of revolution and repression (1 mark)

    Village ponds, public parks, picnic spots and grazing grounds that are open to all the people of a village are examples of:

    1. individual resources
    2. community owned resources
    3. national resources
    4. international resources

  3. Question 12 · 1 mark · Geography: Agriculture
    Which of the following groups contains only rabi crops?

    1. Paddy, maize, jowar, bajra
    2. Watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, fodder crops
    3. Wheat, barley, peas, gram
    4. Cotton, jute, groundnut, soyabean
    Show answer

  4. Question 13 · 1 mark · Geography: Minerals and Energy Resources
    Which one of the following statements about coal in India is correct?

    1. The major resources of Gondwana coal are located in the Damodar valley.
    2. Tertiary coal is found mainly in the Damodar valley.
    3. Lignite deposits are found mainly in the north-eastern states.
    4. Lignite is the main coal used in the metallurgical industry.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) The major resources of Gondwana coal are located in the Damodar valley.

    The major Gondwana coal resources (metallurgical coal) are in the Damodar valley (Jharia, Raniganj, Bokaro). Tertiary coal occurs in the north-eastern states, lignite is found mainly at Neyveli in Tamil Nadu, and high-grade bituminous coal is used in the metallurgical industry.

  5. Question 14 · 1 mark · Geography: Agriculture
    A crop grows well on well-drained fertile soils in the flood plains, where the soils are renewed every year, and needs high temperature during the time of its growth. It is used to make gunny bags, mats and ropes. Identify the crop.

    1. Cotton
    2. Jute
    3. Rubber
    4. Wheat
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Jute

    Jute, the golden fibre, grows well on well-drained fertile soils in the flood plains where soils are renewed every year, and needs high temperature during growth (West Bengal, Bihar, Assam, Odisha, Meghalaya). Cotton grows in the drier parts of the black soil of the Deccan plateau; rubber needs rainfall above 200 cm; wheat is a rabi crop of cool growing seasons.

  8. Question 17 · 5 marks · Geography: Manufacturing Industries
    Explain any three types of pollution caused by manufacturing industries. Suggest two measures to reduce industrial pollution. (3 + 2 = 5)

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Types of pollution (any three):
    1. Air pollution: a high proportion of undesirable gases such as sulphur dioxide and carbon monoxide, and smoke from chemical and paper factories, brick kilns, refineries and smelting plants, harm people, animals, plants and buildings.
    2. Water pollution: organic and inorganic effluents from paper, pulp, chemical, textile and dyeing industries, petroleum refineries, tanneries and electroplating units are discharged into rivers.
    3. Thermal pollution: hot water from factories and thermal plants is drained into rivers and ponds before cooling, harming aquatic life.
    4. Noise pollution: noise from industrial machines, generators and saws causes irritation, hearing loss, higher heart rate and blood pressure.
    Measures (any two):
    1. Reuse and recycle water in two or more stages, and harvest rainwater.
    2. Treat hot water and effluents before releasing them into rivers and ponds (primary, secondary and tertiary treatment).
    3. Fit smoke stacks with electrostatic precipitators, fabric filters, scrubbers and inertial separators; use oil or gas instead of coal.
    4. Redesign machinery to be energy-efficient and less noisy, use noise-absorbing material and ear plugs.

    Marking scheme:

    • First type of pollution correctly explained (air, water, thermal or noise) (1 mark)
    • Second type of pollution correctly explained (different from the first) (1 mark)
    • Third type of pollution correctly explained (different from the first two) (1 mark)
    • First measure, e.g. treating effluents and hot water before release, or reusing/recycling water (1 mark)
    • Second measure (different), e.g. electrostatic precipitators/scrubbers on smoke stacks, oil or gas instead of coal, or less noisy machinery (1 mark)

  9. Question 18 · 4 marks · Geography: Resources and Development
    Read the given text and answer the following questions:
    <b>The soil of the Deccan lava region</b>
    Over large parts of Maharashtra, Saurashtra, Malwa, Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh, the land is covered by a dark soil called regur. The region was once flooded by lava, and the soil has formed from this rock under the climate of the area. The soil is made of extremely fine, clayey material and holds moisture well. It is rich in calcium carbonate, magnesium, potash and lime but generally poor in phosphorus. In the hot season the soil develops deep cracks, which let air into it. When wet, it becomes very sticky. Farmers here have learnt from experience to plan their work around these features.

    18.1 Using the information in the text, give one reason why this soil is well suited to growing cotton. [1]

    18.2 Explain two features of this soil that a farmer must keep in mind while working on it. [2]

    18.3 Name the two important factors in the formation of this soil. [1]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    18.1 Using the information in the text, give one reason why this soil is well suited to growing cotton.

    Model answer: It is made of fine clayey material that holds moisture well, so a crop that needs light rainfall gets moisture for a long time; it is also rich in nutrients such as calcium carbonate, magnesium, potash and lime. (NCERT: black soil is ideal for growing cotton and is also called black cotton soil.)

    Marking scheme:

    • Any one reason from the text: holds moisture well, or rich in nutrients (calcium carbonate, magnesium, potash, lime) (1 mark)

    18.2 Explain two features of this soil that a farmer must keep in mind while working on it.

    Model answer: 1. It is sticky when wet and difficult to work on, so it must be tilled immediately after the first shower or during the pre-monsoon period.
    2. It is generally poor in phosphorus, so the farmer has to add phosphate fertilisers or manure to keep it productive.
    (Deep cracks in the hot season help in the aeration of the soil: also acceptable.)

    Marking scheme:

    • Sticky when wet, so it must be tilled right after the first shower or in the pre-monsoon period (1 mark)
    • Poor in phosphorus, so phosphate fertilisers/manure are needed (or deep cracks in summer help aeration) (1 mark)

    18.3 Name the two important factors in the formation of this soil.

    Model answer: Climatic conditions along with the parent rock material (lava flows of the Deccan trap, basalt).

    Marking scheme:

    Which of the following statements about Belgium are correct?

  4. Question 23 · 1 mark · Civics: Outcomes of Democracy
    Why do democracies often take more time to make decisions than non-democratic governments?

    2. They do not allow citizens to take part in decision-making.
    3. They do not hold elections at regular intervals.
    4. They allow only one party to take all decisions.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) They follow procedures based on deliberation and negotiation.

    Democracy is based on deliberation and negotiation, and decisions follow rules and procedures, so some delay is bound to take place. In return, decisions are more acceptable to the people and more effective.

  5. Question 24 · 2 marks · Civics: Gender, Religion and Caste

  6. Question 25 · 2 marks · Civics: Federalism
    How has the language policy of the Indian federation helped to strengthen national unity? Give two points.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. The Constitution did not give the status of national language to any one language. Hindi was identified as the official language, and besides Hindi there are 21 other languages recognised as Scheduled Languages; a candidate in a Central Government examination may take the examination in any of these languages, and States have their own official languages.

    Marking scheme:

    • Second point explained, e.g. women's struggles have made equal treatment of women a necessary part of a democratic society (1 mark)

  8. Question 27 · 5 marks · Civics: Power Sharing
    Explain any four elements of the Belgian model of accommodation. Why did this model work better than the approach taken in Sri Lanka? (4 + 1 = 5)

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Elements of the Belgian model (any four):
    1. The Constitution prescribes that the number of Dutch-speaking and French-speaking ministers in the central government shall be equal, so no single community can make decisions unilaterally.
    2. Many powers of the central government have been given to the state governments of the two regions; the state governments are not subordinate to the central government.
    3. Brussels has a separate government in which both communities have equal representation.
    4. A third kind of government, the community government, is elected by people belonging to one language community (Dutch, French or German-speaking) and has power over cultural, educational and language-related issues.
    5. Special laws require the support of a majority of members from each linguistic group.
    Why it worked better: Belgium shared power and accommodated the interests of all communities, which helped avoid civic strife and a division of the country on linguistic lines. Sri Lanka followed majoritarian measures that favoured the Sinhala majority; this alienated the Tamils and led to a long civil war.

    Marking scheme:

    • First element, e.g. equal number of Dutch and French-speaking ministers in the central government (1 mark)
    • Second element, e.g. powers given to the state governments of the two regions, not subordinate to the Centre (1 mark)
    • Third element, e.g. separate government for Brussels with equal representation of both communities (1 mark)
    • Fourth element, e.g. community government for cultural, educational and language issues, or special laws needing majority support of each linguistic group (1 mark)
    • Comparison: accommodation in Belgium avoided conflict and division, while majoritarianism in Sri Lanka alienated Tamils and led to civil war (1 mark)

  9. Question 28 · 4 marks · Civics: Political Parties
    Read the given text and answer the following questions:
    <b>Cleaning up party politics</b>
    Citizens often complain that political parties do not work democratically within their own organisations and that some candidates use money and muscle power to win elections. Some steps have been taken in recent years. Elected representatives used to leave the party on whose ticket they had won and join another, often for the lure of a ministerial post or money. The Constitution was amended to stop this practice. The Supreme Court has also made it compulsory for every candidate who contests an election to file an affidavit, and the Election Commission has issued an order requiring political parties to hold their organisational elections and file their income tax returns. Many people feel that these steps are not enough.

    28.1 What is meant by 'defection' in politics? [1]

    28.2 Explain how the affidavit ordered by the Supreme Court and the order of the Election Commission can help to improve party politics. [2]

    28.3 Suggest one more reform that could strengthen democracy within political parties. [1]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    28.1 What is meant by 'defection' in politics?

    Model answer: Defection means changing party allegiance from the party on whose ticket a person got elected (to a legislative body) to a different party.

    Marking scheme:

    • Changing party allegiance from the party on which one was elected to another party (1 mark)

    28.2 Explain how the affidavit ordered by the Supreme Court and the order of the Election Commission can help to improve party politics.

    Model answer: 1. The affidavit gives details of the candidate's property and of criminal cases pending against the candidate. This information is made public, so voters can make an informed choice, which reduces the influence of money and crime.
    2. The Election Commission's order makes parties hold regular organisational elections and file income tax returns, which encourages internal democracy and makes party finances more transparent.

    Marking scheme:

    • Affidavit gives public details of property and pending criminal cases, helping voters judge candidates (1 mark)
    • EC order: organisational elections and income tax returns promote internal democracy and financial transparency (1 mark)

    28.3 Suggest one more reform that could strengthen democracy within political parties.

    Model answer: Any one: a law to regulate the internal affairs of parties (keeping a register of members, following their own constitution, an independent authority to act as judge in disputes, open elections to the highest posts); giving a minimum number of tickets, about one-third, to women; state funding of elections; or pressure from people and the media on parties to reform.

    Marking scheme:

    • Any one valid reform, e.g. law to regulate internal affairs, one-third tickets to women, state funding of elections (1 mark)

  1. Question 29 · 1 mark · Economics: Development
    The Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) of a country indicates:

    1. the proportion of children aged 15-17 years attending school
    2. the proportion of literate people aged 7 years and above
    3. the number of children who die before the age of one year as a proportion of 1000 live children born in that year
    4. the average number of years a newborn child is expected to live
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) the number of children who die before the age of one year as a proportion of 1000 live children born in that year

    IMR is the number of children that die before the age of one year as a proportion of 1000 live children born in that particular year. Option A is the net attendance ratio, B is the literacy rate and D is life expectancy.

  2. Question 30 · 1 mark · Economics: Sectors of the Indian Economy
    Which of the following is an example of the public sector?

    1. Tata Iron and Steel Company Limited
    2. Reliance Industries Limited
    3. A privately owned courier company
    4. Indian Railways
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Indian Railways

    In the public sector the government owns most of the assets and provides all the services. Indian Railways and the post office are examples; TISCO and Reliance Industries are privately owned.

  3. Question 31 · 1 mark · Economics: Money and Credit
    Why are demand deposits with banks considered a form of money?

    1. They are printed and issued by the Reserve Bank of India.
    2. They can be withdrawn on demand and are widely accepted for payments by cheque.
    3. They always earn a very high rate of interest for the depositors.
    4. They can be used only for buying assets such as gold and land.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) They can be withdrawn on demand and are widely accepted for payments by cheque.

    Deposits can be withdrawn on demand, and the cheque facility lets people settle payments directly without cash. Since demand deposits are widely accepted as a means of payment along with currency, they are a form of money. Currency, not deposits, is issued by the RBI.

  4. Question 32 · 1 mark · Economics: Money and Credit
    Farida takes a loan from a bank to buy a house. The bank keeps the papers of the house until the loan is repaid. The house papers here are an example of:

    1. collateral
    2. interest
    3. a demand deposit
    4. a cheque
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) collateral

    Collateral is an asset that the borrower owns (such as land, a building or a vehicle) and uses as a guarantee to the lender until the loan is repaid. If the borrower fails to repay, the lender can sell it.

  5. Question 33 · 1 mark · Economics: Globalisation and the Indian Economy
    Which of the following has greatly reduced port handling costs and increased the speed with which exports reach markets?

    1. Increase in import duties
    2. Introduction of trade barriers
    3. Use of containers for transport of goods
    4. Restrictions on foreign investment
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) Use of containers for transport of goods

    Goods are placed in containers that can be loaded intact onto ships, railways, aeroplanes and trucks. This has led to a huge reduction in port handling costs and faster movement of exports. The other options restrict trade.

  6. Question 34 · 1 mark · Economics: Sectors of the Indian Economy
    In the following question, a statement of Assertion (A) is followed by a statement of Reason (R). Choose the appropriate answer from the options given below as the correct answer:
    Assertion (A): Workers in the unorganised sector often do not get paid leave, holidays or medical benefits.
    Reason (R): The unorganised sector is made up of small and scattered units that are largely outside the control of the government.

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true, but R is false
    4. A is false, but R is true
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A

    Both statements are true. Because these units are largely outside government control, rules and regulations exist but are often not followed, so workers do not get paid leave, holidays or medical benefits. R explains A.

  7. Question 35 · 3 marks · Economics: Sectors of the Indian Economy
    The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act 2005 (MGNREGA) was replaced in 2025 by the Viksit Bharat-Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Viksit Bharat-G RAM G 2025). Explain three provisions of MGNREGA 2005 that aimed to reduce unemployment in rural areas.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. All those in rural areas who were able to work and were in need of work were guaranteed 100 days of employment in a year by the government.
    2. If the government failed in its duty to provide employment, it had to give unemployment allowances to the people.
    3. Preference was given to the types of work that would help to increase production from land in future, so the work also created further employment and income in villages.

    Marking scheme:

    • Guarantee of 100 days of employment a year to those in rural areas able to and in need of work (1 mark)
    • Unemployment allowance if the government failed to provide work (1 mark)
    • Preference to work that would increase production from land in future (1 mark)

  8. Question 36 · 3 marks · Economics: Money and Credit
    Ramesh, a small farmer, borrows ₹20,000 from a moneylender at 3 per cent interest per month to buy seeds and fertilisers.
    (a) How much interest (simple interest) will he have to pay for one year? (1)
    (b) How can such a loan push him into a debt trap? (1)
    (c) Suggest one cheaper source of credit for him and say why it is better. (1)

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Interest for one year = ₹20,000 x 3/100 x 12 = ₹600 x 12 = ₹7,200 (that is, 36 per cent per year).
    (b) If the crop fails or prices are low, his earnings may not be enough to repay the loan with such high interest. He then has to sell part of his land or take a fresh loan to repay the old one, so his debt keeps growing. This situation is called a debt trap.
    (c) A bank or a cooperative society (or a self-help group). Formal lenders charge much lower rates of interest and are supervised by the Reserve Bank of India, so he will not be exploited.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) ₹7,200 (1 mark)
    • (b) Crop failure/low earnings leave him unable to repay, so he borrows again or sells land and his debt keeps growing (1 mark)
    • (c) Bank, cooperative or SHG, with a reason: lower interest / supervised by RBI (1 mark)

  9. Question 37 · 3 marks · Economics: Globalisation and the Indian Economy
    A garment company in Delhi designs clothes, gets them stitched by a factory in Tiruppur, Tamil Nadu, and sells them to a buyer in London. Explain three ways in which information and communication technology makes this possible.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: 1. Telecommunication facilities (telephone, mobile phones, fax) are used to contact others around the world, to place orders and to access information instantly at low cost.
    2. Designs can be prepared on a computer and sent by e-mail over the internet to the factory in Tiruppur and to the buyer in London within seconds, and changes can be agreed quickly.
    3. Payment can be made instantly from a bank in London to a bank in Delhi through the internet (e-banking), and people can talk (voice-mail) across the world at negligible cost.

    Marking scheme:

    • Telecommunication (telephone, mobile, fax) for instant contact and ordering at low cost (1 mark)
    • Computers and the internet (e-mail) to send designs and information instantly (1 mark)
    • E-banking (payment through the internet) or voice-mail at negligible cost across countries (1 mark)

  10. Question 38 · 5 marks · Economics: Development
    Study the table given below and answer the questions that follow.
    <b>Monthly incomes of the five citizens of two countries (in ₹, imaginary data)</b>

    CountryCitizen ICitizen IICitizen IIICitizen IVCitizen V
    P11,80012,40011,60012,00012,200
    Q1,0001,5001,0001,50055,000
    (a) Calculate the average monthly income of the citizens of each country. (1)
    (b) In which country would you prefer to live? Give two reasons. (2)
    (c) What limitation of the average income does this example show? Name one other indicator that should be used to compare the development of the two countries. (2)

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (a) Country P: (11,800 + 12,400 + 11,600 + 12,000 + 12,200) / 5 = 60,000 / 5 = ₹12,000.
    Country Q: (1,000 + 1,500 + 1,000 + 1,500 + 55,000) / 5 = 60,000 / 5 = ₹12,000.
    (b) Country P. 1. Incomes are distributed fairly equally, so every citizen earns close to the average. 2. In Country Q, four out of five citizens earn only ₹1,000-1,500 a month and are poor; one person is extremely rich.
    (c) The average is useful for comparison, but it hides the disparities (the distribution of income) among people. Two countries with the same average income can have very different levels of well-being. Other indicators such as the infant mortality rate, literacy rate, net attendance ratio, life expectancy or the Human Development Index should also be used.

    Marking scheme:

    • (a) Average income of both P and Q is ₹12,000 (1 mark)
    • (b) Country P, because incomes are distributed equally / everyone earns close to the average (1 mark)
    • (b) In Q, most citizens are poor and one person is very rich (unequal distribution) (1 mark)
    • (c) The average hides disparities in the distribution of income among people (1 mark)
    • (c) One other indicator, e.g. IMR, literacy rate, net attendance ratio, life expectancy or HDI (1 mark)
