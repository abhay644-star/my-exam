A few questions from this chapter. Open each one to see the answer.

  1. Assets such as land, buildings or livestock that a borrower pledges as a guarantee to a lender are called ______.

    1. Interest
    2. Collateral
    3. Deposit
    4. Principal
    Show answer

    Answer: Collateral

    The lender can sell the collateral if the loan is not repaid.

  2. Deposits in bank accounts that can be withdrawn whenever the account holder wants are called ______ deposits.

    1. Recurring
    2. Demand
    3. Term
    4. Fixed
    Show answer

    Answer: Demand

    Demand deposits can be withdrawn on demand and are used for cheque payments.

  3. A farmer borrows from a moneylender at high interest, his crop fails and he must sell part of his land to repay. This situation is called a ______.

    1. Collateral
    2. Credit boom
    3. Subsidy
    4. Debt trap
    Show answer

    Answer: Debt trap

    When repayment becomes impossible, credit pushes the borrower into a debt trap.

  4. Who founded the Grameen Bank of Bangladesh and won the Nobel Peace Prize in 2006?

    1. Kailash Satyarthi
    2. Abhijit Banerjee
    3. Muhammad Yunus
    4. Amartya Sen
    Show answer

    Answer: Muhammad Yunus

    Muhammad Yunus pioneered small loans to poor rural borrowers, mostly women.

  5. Which institution supervises the functioning of formal sources of loans in India?

    1. State Bank of India
    2. Reserve Bank of India
    3. NABARD
    4. SEBI
    Show answer

    Answer: Reserve Bank of India

    The RBI monitors how banks maintain cash balances and give loans.

  6. According to NCERT, banks in India these days hold about what percentage of their deposits as cash?

    1. 15 %
    2. 50 %
    3. 30 %
    4. 5 %
    Show answer

    Answer: 15 %

    Banks keep about 15 per cent as cash to pay depositors who may come to withdraw, and use most of the rest to extend loans.

  7. The exchange of goods for goods without the use of money is called the ______ system.

    1. Barter
    2. Cheque
    3. Currency
    4. Credit
    Show answer

    Answer: Barter

    Barter needs a double coincidence of wants, which money eliminates.

  8. In a barter system, both parties must want to sell what the other wishes to buy. This is called a double ______ of wants.

    1. Coincidence
    2. Exchange
    3. Supply
    4. Demand
    Show answer

    Answer: Coincidence

    Money acts as a medium of exchange and removes the need for a double coincidence of wants.
