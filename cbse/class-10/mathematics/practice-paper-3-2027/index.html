General instructions

  1. This question paper contains 38 questions. All questions are compulsory.
  2. This question paper is divided into five sections: A, B, C, D and E.
  3. In Section A, questions 1 to 18 are multiple choice questions (MCQs) and questions 19 and 20 are assertion-reason based questions of 1 mark each.
  4. In Section B, questions 21 to 25 are very short answer (VSA) type questions carrying 2 marks each.
  5. In Section C, questions 26 to 31 are short answer (SA) type questions carrying 3 marks each.
  6. In Section D, questions 32 to 35 are long answer (LA) type questions carrying 5 marks each.
  7. In Section E, questions 36 to 38 are case study based questions carrying 4 marks each. Each has three sub-parts (i), (ii) and (iii) of 1, 1 and 2 marks respectively.
  8. There is no overall choice. However, an internal choice has been provided in 2 questions in Section B, 2 questions in Section C and 2 questions in Section D. An internal choice has been provided in all the sub-parts having 2 marks of the questions in Section E.
  9. Draw neat and clean figures wherever required. Take $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$ wherever required, if not stated.
  10. Use of calculators is not allowed.

Questions

Try each question first, then open the model answer and the marking scheme.

Section A: 1 mark each

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Real Numbers
    The product of a non-zero rational number and an irrational number is:

    1. always rational
    2. always irrational
    3. rational or irrational
    4. always an integer
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) always irrational

    If $r \neq 0$ is rational and $r \times x = q$ were rational for an irrational $x$, then $x = \frac{q}{r}$ would be rational, which is not true. So the product is always irrational.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Polynomials
    If $-2$ is a zero of the polynomial $x^2 + kx - 6$, then its other zero is:

    1. $-3$
    2. 2
    3. 3
    4. 6
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) 3

    $p(-2) = 4 - 2k - 6 = 0 \Rightarrow k = -1$. The polynomial is $x^2 - x - 6 = (x - 3)(x + 2)$, so the other zero is 3. (Also, product of zeroes $= -6$, so the other zero $= \frac{-6}{-2} = 3$.)

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Pair of Linear Equations
    The pair of equations $x = 4$ and $y = -3$ represents, graphically, two lines which are:

    1. parallel
    2. coincident
    3. intersecting at the point $(-3, 4)$
    4. intersecting at the point $(4, -3)$
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) intersecting at the point $(4, -3)$

    $x = 4$ is a line parallel to the y-axis and $y = -3$ is a line parallel to the x-axis. They meet at the point $(4, -3)$.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Quadratic Equations
    The quadratic equation $kx^2 - 6x + 1 = 0$ has two distinct real roots if:

    1. $k \lt 9$ and $k \neq 0$
    2. $k \lt 9$ only
    3. $k \gt 9$
    4. $k = 9$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $k \lt 9$ and $k \neq 0$

    For a quadratic equation, $k \neq 0$. Two distinct real roots need $D = 36 - 4k \gt 0$, i.e. $k \lt 9$. So $k \lt 9$ and $k \neq 0$.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Arithmetic Progressions
    The sum of the first 20 odd natural numbers is:

    1. 400
    2. 420
    3. 210
    4. 200
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) 400

    1, 3, 5, ... is an A.P. with $a = 1$, $d = 2$. $S_{20} = \frac{20}{2}[2 \times 1 + 19 \times 2] = 10 \times 40 = 400$.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Triangles
    $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$. If AB = 4 cm, DE = 6 cm, EF = 9 cm and FD = 12 cm, then the perimeter of $\triangle ABC$ is:

    1. 13.5 cm
    2. 18 cm
    3. 27 cm
    4. 40.5 cm
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) 18 cm

    Perimeter of $\triangle DEF = 6 + 9 + 12 = 27$ cm. For similar triangles, the ratio of perimeters equals the ratio of corresponding sides: $\frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3}$. Perimeter of $\triangle ABC = \frac{2}{3} \times 27 = 18$ cm.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Coordinate Geometry
    The distance between the points $(a\cos\theta, 0)$ and $(0, a\sin\theta)$, where $a \gt 0$, is:

    1. $a$
    2. $2a$
    3. $a^2$
    4. $\sqrt{2}a$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $a$

    Distance $= \sqrt{a^2\cos^2\theta + a^2\sin^2\theta} = \sqrt{a^2(\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta)} = \sqrt{a^2} = a$.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Introduction to Trigonometry
    If $\sin\theta = \cos\theta$, where $0^\circ \lt \theta \lt 90^\circ$, then the value of $2\tan\theta + \cos^2\theta$ is:

    1. $\frac{3}{2}$
    2. 2
    3. $\frac{5}{2}$
    4. 3
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $\frac{5}{2}$

    $\sin\theta = \cos\theta \Rightarrow \tan\theta = 1 \Rightarrow \theta = 45^\circ$. Value $= 2 \times 1 + \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2 = 2 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{2}$.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Introduction to Trigonometry
    $(1 + \tan^2 A)(1 - \sin A)(1 + \sin A)$ is equal to:

    1. 0
    2. 1
    3. $\sec^2 A$
    4. $\cos^2 A$
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) 1

    $(1 + \tan^2 A) = \sec^2 A$ and $(1 - \sin A)(1 + \sin A) = 1 - \sin^2 A = \cos^2 A$. Product $= \sec^2 A \times \cos^2 A = 1$.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Circles
    TP and TQ are tangents drawn from an external point T to a circle with centre O and radius 5 cm. If TP and TQ are perpendicular to each other, then the length of each tangent is:

    1. 2.5 cm
    2. 5 cm
    3. $5\sqrt{2}$ cm
    4. 10 cm
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) 5 cm

    $\angle OPT = \angle OQT = 90^\circ$ and $\angle PTQ = 90^\circ$, so $\angle POQ = 90^\circ$ and OPTQ is a rectangle with OP = OQ. Hence it is a square, and TP = OP = 5 cm.

  11. Question 11 · 1 mark · Areas Related to Circles
    The circumference of a circle is equal to the perimeter of a square of side 11 cm. The area of the circle is:

    1. $77\ \text{cm}^2$
    2. $121\ \text{cm}^2$
    3. $154\ \text{cm}^2$
    4. $616\ \text{cm}^2$
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $154\ \text{cm}^2$

    $2\pi r = 4 \times 11 = 44 \Rightarrow r = 7$ cm. Area $= \frac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 = 154\ \text{cm}^2$.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Areas Related to Circles
    The area of a sector of a circle of radius 6 cm is $6\pi\ \text{cm}^2$. The angle of the sector is:

    1. $120^\circ$
    2. $90^\circ$
    3. $45^\circ$
    4. $60^\circ$
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) $60^\circ$

    $\frac{\theta}{360^\circ} \times \pi \times 6^2 = 6\pi \Rightarrow \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} = \frac{1}{6} \Rightarrow \theta = 60^\circ$.

  13. Question 13 · 1 mark · Surface Areas and Volumes
    A right circular cylinder, a right circular cone and a hemisphere have the same base radius $r$, and the heights of the cylinder and the cone are also equal to $r$. The ratio of their volumes is:

    1. $1 : 2 : 3$
    2. $2 : 1 : 3$
    3. $3 : 2 : 1$
    4. $3 : 1 : 2$
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) $3 : 1 : 2$

    Cylinder: $\pi r^3$; cone: $\frac{1}{3}\pi r^3$; hemisphere: $\frac{2}{3}\pi r^3$. Ratio $= 1 : \frac{1}{3} : \frac{2}{3} = 3 : 1 : 2$.

  14. Question 14 · 1 mark · Statistics
    In the formula $\bar{x} = a + \frac{\sum f_i d_i}{\sum f_i}$ for finding the mean of grouped data, the $d_i$ are the deviations from the assumed mean $a$ of the:

    1. lower class limits
    2. upper class limits
    3. frequencies
    4. class marks
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) class marks

    In the assumed mean method, $d_i = x_i - a$, where $x_i$ are the class marks (mid-points) of the classes.

  15. Question 15 · 1 mark · Statistics
    For the following distribution, the sum of the lower limits of the median class and the modal class is:

    Class0 - 1010 - 2020 - 3030 - 4040 - 50
    Frequency5710126

    1. 40
    2. 50
    3. 60
    4. 70
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) 50

    N = 40, $\frac{N}{2} = 20$. Cumulative frequencies: 5, 12, 22, 34, 40, so the median class is 20 - 30 (lower limit 20). The highest frequency is 12, so the modal class is 30 - 40 (lower limit 30). Sum $= 20 + 30 = 50$.

  16. Question 16 · 1 mark · Probability
    A letter is chosen at random from the letters of the word ASSOCIATION. The probability that the letter chosen is a vowel is:

    1. $\frac{3}{11}$
    2. $\frac{5}{11}$
    3. $\frac{6}{11}$
    4. $\frac{7}{11}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $\frac{6}{11}$

    ASSOCIATION has 11 letters, of which A, O, I, A, I, O, i.e. 6, are vowels. Probability $= \frac{6}{11}$.

  17. Question 17 · 1 mark · Probability
    Two dice are thrown together. The probability that the sum of the numbers on them is 10 or more is:

    1. $\frac{1}{12}$
    2. $\frac{5}{36}$
    3. $\frac{1}{6}$
    4. $\frac{1}{4}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $\frac{1}{6}$

    Favourable outcomes: (4, 6), (5, 5), (6, 4), (5, 6), (6, 5), (6, 6), i.e. 6 out of 36. Probability $= \frac{6}{36} = \frac{1}{6}$.

  18. Question 18 · 1 mark · Probability
    Tickets numbered 1 to 50 are mixed and one ticket is drawn at random. The probability that the number on it is a multiple of 3 or of 7 is:

    1. $\frac{21}{50}$
    2. $\frac{23}{50}$
    3. $\frac{8}{25}$
    4. $\frac{2}{25}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\frac{21}{50}$

    Multiples of 3: 16; multiples of 7: 7; multiples of both (21 and 42): 2. Favourable $= 16 + 7 - 2 = 21$. Probability $= \frac{21}{50}$.

  19. Question 19 · 1 mark · Coordinate Geometry
    Assertion (A): The point $(3, -4)$ is at a distance of 4 units from the x-axis and 5 units from the y-axis.
    Reason (R): The distance of a point from the x-axis is the absolute value of its ordinate.

    1. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    2. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    3. Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.
    4. Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.

    Distance of $(3, -4)$ from the x-axis $= |-4| = 4$ units, but its distance from the y-axis $= |3| = 3$ units, not 5. So A is false. R is true.

  20. Question 20 · 1 mark · Polynomials
    Assertion (A): The graph of $y = x^2 - 6x + 9$ meets the x-axis at exactly one point.
    Reason (R): $x^2 - 6x + 9 = (x - 3)^2$, which becomes zero only when $x = 3$.

    1. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    2. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    3. Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.
    4. Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

    The graph meets the x-axis where $y = 0$, i.e. at the zeroes of the polynomial. R shows that the only zero is 3, so the graph meets the x-axis only at $(3, 0)$. Both are true and R explains A.

Section B: 2 marks each

  1. Question 21 · 2 marks · Real Numbers
    Check whether $12^n$ can end with the digit 0 for any natural number $n$. Give a reason for your answer.

    OR The HCF of two numbers is 12 and their LCM is 2520. If one of the numbers is 72, find the other number and write it as a product of its prime factors.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $12^n = (2^2 \times 3)^n = 2^{2n} \times 3^n$
    A number ends with the digit 0 only if it is divisible by 10, i.e. by both 2 and 5, so 5 must appear in its prime factorisation.
    The prime factors of $12^n$ are only 2 and 3. By the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic this factorisation is unique, so 5 is not a factor of $12^n$.
    Hence $12^n$ cannot end with the digit 0 for any natural number $n$.

    Marking scheme:

    • Prime factorisation $12^n = 2^{2n} \times 3^n$ (1 mark)
    • Reason: 5 is not a prime factor (uniqueness of factorisation), so $12^n$ cannot end with 0 (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: HCF $\times$ LCM = product of the two numbers
    Other number $= \frac{12 \times 2520}{72} = \frac{30240}{72} = 420$
    $420 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7$

    Marking scheme:

    • Using HCF $\times$ LCM = product to get the other number = 420 (1 mark)
    • $420 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7$ (1 mark)

  2. Question 22 · 2 marks · Triangles
    The sides of $\triangle ABC$ are AB = 4 cm, BC = 6 cm and CA = 8 cm, and the sides of $\triangle PQR$ are PQ = 9 cm, QR = 12 cm and RP = 6 cm. Check whether the two triangles are similar. If they are, write the similarity correspondence.

    OR In $\triangle ABC$, AD and BE are altitudes, with D on BC and E on AC. Prove that $CD \times CB = CE \times CA$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Arranging the sides in increasing order: $\triangle ABC$: AB = 4, BC = 6, CA = 8; $\triangle PQR$: RP = 6, PQ = 9, QR = 12.
    $\frac{AB}{RP} = \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3}$, $\frac{BC}{PQ} = \frac{6}{9} = \frac{2}{3}$, $\frac{CA}{QR} = \frac{8}{12} = \frac{2}{3}$
    All three ratios are equal, so the triangles are similar (SSS similarity criterion).
    B is common to AB and BC, and P is common to RP and PQ, so B corresponds to P; similarly A corresponds to R and C to Q.
    $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle RPQ$

    Marking scheme:

    • Matching the sides in order and showing all three ratios equal $\frac{2}{3}$, so the triangles are similar (SSS) (1 mark)
    • Correct correspondence $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle RPQ$ (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: In $\triangle ADC$ and $\triangle BEC$:
    $\angle ADC = \angle BEC = 90^\circ$ (AD and BE are altitudes)
    $\angle ACD = \angle BCE$ (common angle C)
    So $\triangle ADC \sim \triangle BEC$ (AA similarity criterion).
    Hence $\frac{CD}{CE} = \frac{CA}{CB} \Rightarrow CD \times CB = CE \times CA$.

    Marking scheme:

    • $\triangle ADC \sim \triangle BEC$ by AA (right angles and common angle C) (1 mark)
    • Ratio $\frac{CD}{CE} = \frac{CA}{CB}$, giving $CD \times CB = CE \times CA$ (1 mark)

  3. Question 23 · 2 marks · Introduction to Trigonometry
    Evaluate: $\frac{2\sin^2 60^\circ - \tan^2 45^\circ + 3\cos^2 30^\circ}{\sec^2 45^\circ - \cot^2 45^\circ}$

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $\sin 60^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $\tan 45^\circ = 1$, $\cos 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $\sec 45^\circ = \sqrt{2}$, $\cot 45^\circ = 1$
    Numerator $= 2 \times \frac{3}{4} - 1 + 3 \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{3}{2} - 1 + \frac{9}{4} = \frac{11}{4}$
    Denominator $= 2 - 1 = 1$
    Value $= \frac{11}{4}$

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct values: $\sin 60^\circ = \cos 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $\tan 45^\circ = \cot 45^\circ = 1$, $\sec 45^\circ = \sqrt{2}$ (1 mark)
    • Numerator $\frac{11}{4}$, denominator 1; value $= \frac{11}{4}$ (1 mark)

  4. Question 24 · 2 marks · Areas Related to Circles
    A chord of a circle of radius 10.5 cm subtends an angle of $120^\circ$ at the centre. Find the areas of the minor sector and the major sector formed by the two radii through the ends of the chord.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Area of the circle $= \frac{22}{7} \times 10.5 \times 10.5 = 346.5\ \text{cm}^2$
    Area of minor sector $= \frac{120}{360} \times 346.5 = 115.5\ \text{cm}^2$
    Area of major sector $= 346.5 - 115.5 = 231\ \text{cm}^2$

    Marking scheme:

    • Area of minor sector $= 115.5\ \text{cm}^2$ (1 mark)
    • Area of major sector $= 231\ \text{cm}^2$ (1 mark)

  5. Question 25 · 2 marks · Probability
    In a class of 40 students, 25 play cricket, 20 play football and 10 play both games. A student of the class is chosen at random. Find the probability that the student plays (i) neither of the two games, (ii) exactly one of the two games.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Students who play only cricket $= 25 - 10 = 15$; only football $= 20 - 10 = 10$; both $= 10$.
    Students who play at least one game $= 15 + 10 + 10 = 35$, so neither $= 40 - 35 = 5$.
    (i) P(neither) $= \frac{5}{40} = \frac{1}{8}$
    (ii) Exactly one game: $15 + 10 = 25$ students. P(exactly one) $= \frac{25}{40} = \frac{5}{8}$

    Marking scheme:

    • (i) 5 students play neither; probability $\frac{1}{8}$ (1 mark)
    • (ii) 25 students play exactly one; probability $\frac{5}{8}$ (1 mark)

Section C: 3 marks each

  1. Question 26 · 3 marks · Real Numbers
    Find the largest number which divides 245 and 1029 leaving a remainder 5 in each case.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: The required number divides $245 - 5 = 240$ and $1029 - 5 = 1024$ exactly.
    $240 = 2^4 \times 3 \times 5$
    $1024 = 2^{10}$
    HCF(240, 1024) $= 2^4 = 16$
    The largest such number is 16. (Check: $245 = 16 \times 15 + 5$ and $1029 = 16 \times 64 + 5$.)

    Marking scheme:

    • Subtracting the remainders: 240 and 1024 (1 mark)
    • Prime factorisations $240 = 2^4 \times 3 \times 5$ and $1024 = 2^{10}$ (1 mark)
    • HCF = 16, the required number (1 mark)

  2. Question 27 · 3 marks · Polynomials
    If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the zeroes of the polynomial $3x^2 - 4x - 4$, find a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes are $\alpha^2$ and $\beta^2$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $\alpha + \beta = \frac{4}{3}$ and $\alpha\beta = -\frac{4}{3}$
    Sum of the new zeroes: $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 2\alpha\beta = \frac{16}{9} + \frac{8}{3} = \frac{40}{9}$
    Product of the new zeroes: $\alpha^2\beta^2 = (\alpha\beta)^2 = \frac{16}{9}$
    Required polynomial $= k\left(x^2 - \frac{40}{9}x + \frac{16}{9}\right)$; taking $k = 9$: $9x^2 - 40x + 16$.
    (Check: the zeroes of $3x^2 - 4x - 4$ are 2 and $-\frac{2}{3}$; their squares 4 and $\frac{4}{9}$ are the zeroes of $9x^2 - 40x + 16$.)

    Marking scheme:

    • $\alpha + \beta = \frac{4}{3}$ and $\alpha\beta = -\frac{4}{3}$ (1 mark)
    • $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = \frac{40}{9}$ and $\alpha^2\beta^2 = \frac{16}{9}$ (1 mark)
    • Polynomial $9x^2 - 40x + 16$ (or any non-zero multiple) (1 mark)

  3. Question 28 · 3 marks · Arithmetic Progressions
    The 5th term of an A.P. is 19, and its 12th term exceeds twice its 6th term by 1. Find the A.P. and the sum of its first 15 terms.

    OR Find the sum of all three-digit natural numbers which leave a remainder 3 when divided by 7.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Let the first term be $a$ and the common difference be $d$.
    $a + 4d = 19$ ...(1)
    $a + 11d = 2(a + 5d) + 1 \Rightarrow a + 11d = 2a + 10d + 1 \Rightarrow d - a = 1$ ...(2)
    From (2), $a = d - 1$. Substituting in (1): $5d - 1 = 19 \Rightarrow d = 4$, $a = 3$.
    The A.P. is 3, 7, 11, 15, ...
    $S_{15} = \frac{15}{2}[2 \times 3 + 14 \times 4] = \frac{15}{2} \times 62 = 465$

    Marking scheme:

    • Equations $a + 4d = 19$ and $a + 11d = 2(a + 5d) + 1$ (1 mark)
    • $a = 3$, $d = 4$; A.P. 3, 7, 11, ... (1 mark)
    • $S_{15} = 465$ (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: The numbers are 101, 108, 115, ..., 997 (since $101 = 7 \times 14 + 3$ and $997 = 7 \times 142 + 3$).
    This is an A.P. with $a = 101$, $d = 7$, $a_n = 997$.
    $997 = 101 + (n - 1) \times 7 \Rightarrow n - 1 = 128 \Rightarrow n = 129$
    $S_{129} = \frac{129}{2}(101 + 997) = \frac{129}{2} \times 1098 = 129 \times 549 = 70821$

    Marking scheme:

    • First term 101, last term 997, $d = 7$ (1 mark)
    • Number of terms $n = 129$ (1 mark)
    • Sum $= 70821$ (1 mark)

  4. Question 29 · 3 marks · Circles
    Two concentric circles have centre O and radii 13 cm and 5 cm. AB is a chord of the larger circle which touches the smaller circle at P. Prove that P is the mid-point of AB, and find the length of AB.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: AB touches the smaller circle at P, so AB is a tangent to the smaller circle and $OP \perp AB$ (radius is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact).
    OP is also a perpendicular from the centre O to the chord AB of the larger circle. In right triangles OPA and OPB, OA = OB (radii of the larger circle) and OP is common, so $\triangle OPA \cong \triangle OPB$ (RHS) and AP = PB. Hence P is the mid-point of AB.
    In right $\triangle OPA$: $AP = \sqrt{OA^2 - OP^2} = \sqrt{169 - 25} = \sqrt{144} = 12$ cm
    AB $= 2 \times AP = 24$ cm

    Marking scheme:

    • $OP \perp AB$ because AB is a tangent to the smaller circle at P (1 mark)
    • AP = PB (congruent right triangles OPA and OPB), so P is the mid-point (1 mark)
    • AP = 12 cm and AB = 24 cm (1 mark)

  5. Question 30 · 3 marks · Triangles
    In $\triangle PQR$, S is a point on the side QR such that $PS \perp QR$ and $PS^2 = QS \times SR$. Prove that $\angle QPR = 90^\circ$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $PS^2 = QS \times SR \Rightarrow \frac{PS}{SR} = \frac{QS}{PS}$
    In $\triangle PSQ$ and $\triangle RSP$: $\frac{QS}{PS} = \frac{PS}{SR}$ and $\angle PSQ = \angle RSP = 90^\circ$.
    So $\triangle PSQ \sim \triangle RSP$ (SAS similarity criterion), and hence $\angle QPS = \angle PRS$.
    In right $\triangle PSR$: $\angle PRS + \angle SPR = 90^\circ$.
    $\angle QPR = \angle QPS + \angle SPR = \angle PRS + \angle SPR = 90^\circ$

    Marking scheme:

    • Writing the ratio $\frac{QS}{PS} = \frac{PS}{SR}$ with the right angles at S (1 mark)
    • $\triangle PSQ \sim \triangle RSP$ (SAS), so $\angle QPS = \angle PRS$ (1 mark)
    • Using $\angle PRS + \angle SPR = 90^\circ$ to conclude $\angle QPR = 90^\circ$ (1 mark)

  6. Question 31 · 3 marks · Introduction to Trigonometry
    If $\sin\theta + \cos\theta = \frac{7}{5}$, where $\theta$ is an acute angle, find the value of $\tan\theta + \cot\theta$.

    OR Prove that $\sin\theta(1 + \tan\theta) + \cos\theta(1 + \cot\theta) = \sec\theta + \operatorname{cosec}\theta$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Squaring: $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta + 2\sin\theta\cos\theta = \frac{49}{25}$
    $1 + 2\sin\theta\cos\theta = \frac{49}{25} \Rightarrow \sin\theta\cos\theta = \frac{12}{25}$
    $\tan\theta + \cot\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} + \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} = \frac{\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta}{\sin\theta\cos\theta} = \frac{1}{\sin\theta\cos\theta} = \frac{25}{12}$

    Marking scheme:

    • Squaring and using $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ (1 mark)
    • $\sin\theta\cos\theta = \frac{12}{25}$ (1 mark)
    • $\tan\theta + \cot\theta = \frac{1}{\sin\theta\cos\theta} = \frac{25}{12}$ (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: LHS $= \sin\theta + \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\cos\theta} + \cos\theta + \frac{\cos^2\theta}{\sin\theta}$
    $= \left(\sin\theta + \frac{\cos^2\theta}{\sin\theta}\right) + \left(\cos\theta + \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\cos\theta}\right)$
    $= \frac{\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta}{\sin\theta} + \frac{\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta}{\cos\theta}$
    $= \frac{1}{\sin\theta} + \frac{1}{\cos\theta} = \operatorname{cosec}\theta + \sec\theta =$ RHS

    Marking scheme:

    • Expanding with $\tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$ and $\cot\theta = \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}$ (1 mark)
    • Regrouping to get $\frac{\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta}{\sin\theta} + \frac{\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta}{\cos\theta}$ (1 mark)
    • Using $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ to get $\operatorname{cosec}\theta + \sec\theta$ (1 mark)

    Solve the following pair of linear equations graphically: $2x - y = 2$ and $x + y = 7$.
    Also find the coordinates of the vertices of the triangle formed by these two lines and the x-axis, and find the area of this triangle.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: For $2x - y = 2$, i.e. $y = 2x - 2$:

    $x$013
    $y$$-2$04
    For $x + y = 7$, i.e. $y = 7 - x$:
    $x$037
    $y$740
    Plot these points and draw the two lines on the same graph. The lines intersect at $(3, 4)$, so the solution is $x = 3$, $y = 4$.
    The line $2x - y = 2$ meets the x-axis at $(1, 0)$ and the line $x + y = 7$ meets it at $(7, 0)$.
    Vertices of the triangle: $(1, 0)$, $(7, 0)$ and $(3, 4)$.

  2. Question 33 · 5 marks · Circles
    PA and PB are tangents drawn from an external point P to a circle with centre O and radius 6 cm, touching it at A and B. OP = 10 cm, and OP intersects AB at M.
    (i) Prove that OP is the perpendicular bisector of AB.
    (ii) Find the length of the tangent PA.
    (iii) Find the length of the chord AB.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: (i) In $\triangle OAP$ and $\triangle OBP$: OA = OB (radii), $\angle OAP = \angle OBP = 90^\circ$ (radius $\perp$ tangent) and OP is common. So $\triangle OAP \cong \triangle OBP$ (RHS), giving PA = PB and $\angle APO = \angle BPO$.
    In $\triangle PAM$ and $\triangle PBM$: PA = PB, $\angle APM = \angle BPM$ and PM is common. So $\triangle PAM \cong \triangle PBM$ (SAS).
    Hence AM = BM and $\angle AMP = \angle BMP$. As these angles form a linear pair, each is $90^\circ$. So OP is the perpendicular bisector of AB.
    (ii) In right $\triangle OAP$: $PA = \sqrt{OP^2 - OA^2} = \sqrt{100 - 36} = 8$ cm.
    (iii) AM is the altitude of right $\triangle OAP$ to its hypotenuse OP. Area of $\triangle OAP$: $\frac{1}{2} \times OA \times AP = \frac{1}{2} \times OP \times AM$
    $\Rightarrow 6 \times 8 = 10 \times AM \Rightarrow AM = 4.8$ cm
    AB $= 2 \times AM = 9.6$ cm.

    Marking scheme:

    • $\triangle OAP \cong \triangle OBP$ (RHS), so $\angle APO = \angle BPO$ (with figure) (1 mark)
    • $\triangle PAM \cong \triangle PBM$ (SAS), so AM = BM and $\angle AMP = 90^\circ$ (1 mark)
    • PA = 8 cm (1 mark)
    • AM = 4.8 cm (using area of $\triangle OAP$ or similar triangles) (1 mark)
    • AB = 9.6 cm (1 mark)

  3. Question 34 · 5 marks · Surface Areas and Volumes
    A solid model of a rocket is in the shape of a right circular cylinder of radius 3.5 cm and height 10 cm, with a right circular cone of the same radius and height 12 cm fixed on one end. Find the volume and the total surface area of the model.

    OR A solid toy is in the form of a hemisphere surmounted by a right circular cone of the same radius. The radius of the hemisphere is 10.5 cm and the height of the cone is 14 cm. Find the volume and the total surface area of the toy.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $r = 3.5$ cm, height of cylinder $h = 10$ cm, height of cone $H = 12$ cm.
    Slant height of cone $l = \sqrt{3.5^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{12.25 + 144} = \sqrt{156.25} = 12.5$ cm
    Volume of cylinder $= \pi r^2 h = \frac{22}{7} \times 3.5 \times 3.5 \times 10 = 385\ \text{cm}^3$
    Volume of cone $= \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 H = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 3.5 \times 3.5 \times 12 = 154\ \text{cm}^3$
    Volume of the model $= 385 + 154 = 539\ \text{cm}^3$
    Curved surface area of cylinder $= 2\pi rh = 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 3.5 \times 10 = 220\ \text{cm}^2$; area of the free base $= \pi r^2 = 38.5\ \text{cm}^2$
    Curved surface area of cone $= \pi rl = \frac{22}{7} \times 3.5 \times 12.5 = 137.5\ \text{cm}^2$
    Total surface area $= 220 + 38.5 + 137.5 = 396\ \text{cm}^2$

    Marking scheme:

    • Slant height $l = 12.5$ cm (½ marks)
    • Volume of cylinder $= 385\ \text{cm}^3$ (1 mark)
    • Volume of cone $= 154\ \text{cm}^3$ (1 mark)
    • Volume of model $= 539\ \text{cm}^3$ (½ marks)
    • CSA of cylinder $= 220\ \text{cm}^2$ and base $= 38.5\ \text{cm}^2$ (1 mark)
    • CSA of cone $= 137.5\ \text{cm}^2$ and total surface area $= 396\ \text{cm}^2$ (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: $r = 10.5$ cm, $h = 14$ cm. Slant height $l = \sqrt{10.5^2 + 14^2} = \sqrt{110.25 + 196} = \sqrt{306.25} = 17.5$ cm
    Volume of hemisphere $= \frac{2}{3}\pi r^3 = \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 10.5 \times 10.5 \times 10.5 = 2425.5\ \text{cm}^3$
    Volume of cone $= \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 10.5 \times 10.5 \times 14 = 1617\ \text{cm}^3$
    Volume of toy $= 2425.5 + 1617 = 4042.5\ \text{cm}^3$
    CSA of hemisphere $= 2\pi r^2 = 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 10.5 \times 10.5 = 693\ \text{cm}^2$
    CSA of cone $= \pi rl = \frac{22}{7} \times 10.5 \times 17.5 = 577.5\ \text{cm}^2$
    Total surface area $= 693 + 577.5 = 1270.5\ \text{cm}^2$

    Marking scheme:

    • Slant height $l = 17.5$ cm (½ marks)
    • Volume of hemisphere $= 2425.5\ \text{cm}^3$ (1 mark)
    • Volume of cone $= 1617\ \text{cm}^3$ (1 mark)
    • Volume of toy $= 4042.5\ \text{cm}^3$ (½ marks)
    • CSA of hemisphere $= 693\ \text{cm}^2$ (1 mark)
    • CSA of cone $= 577.5\ \text{cm}^2$ and total surface area $= 1270.5\ \text{cm}^2$ (1 mark)

  4. Question 35 · 5 marks · Applications of Trigonometry
    A drone is hovering at a point vertically above a straight road. Two cars are on the road, one on each side of the drone, and are 400 m apart. The angles of depression of the two cars as seen from the drone are $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$. Find the height of the drone above the road and the distance of each car from the point on the road directly below the drone.

    OR A balloon is rising vertically. At one instant, an observer standing on level ground sees the balloon at an angle of elevation of $30^\circ$. After the balloon has risen a further 200 m, the angle of elevation becomes $60^\circ$. Find the distance of the observer from the point on the ground directly below the balloon, and the height of the balloon at the first instant.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Let D be the drone, F the point on the road directly below it, and DF $= h$ m. Let A and B be the cars, with the angles of depression of A and B equal to $60^\circ$ and $30^\circ$. Then $\angle DAF = 60^\circ$ and $\angle DBF = 30^\circ$ (alternate angles), and AF + FB = 400 m.
    In right $\triangle DFA$: $\tan 60^\circ = \frac{h}{AF} \Rightarrow AF = \frac{h}{\sqrt{3}}$
    In right $\triangle DFB$: $\tan 30^\circ = \frac{h}{FB} \Rightarrow FB = \sqrt{3}h$
    $\frac{h}{\sqrt{3}} + \sqrt{3}h = 400 \Rightarrow \frac{4h}{\sqrt{3}} = 400 \Rightarrow h = 100\sqrt{3}$ m
    AF $= \frac{100\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}} = 100$ m and FB $= \sqrt{3} \times 100\sqrt{3} = 300$ m
    The drone is $100\sqrt{3}$ m (about 173.2 m) high; the cars are 100 m and 300 m from the point below it.

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct figure (1 mark)
    • $\tan 60^\circ = \frac{h}{AF}$, i.e. AF $= \frac{h}{\sqrt{3}}$ (1 mark)
    • $\tan 30^\circ = \frac{h}{FB}$, i.e. FB $= \sqrt{3}h$ (1 mark)
    • Height $h = 100\sqrt{3}$ m (1 mark)
    • Distances 100 m and 300 m (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: Let O be the observer and F the point on the ground directly below the balloon, with OF $= x$ m. Let the first height of the balloon be $h$ m, so its second height is $(h + 200)$ m.
    $\tan 30^\circ = \frac{h}{x} \Rightarrow h = \frac{x}{\sqrt{3}}$ ...(1)
    $\tan 60^\circ = \frac{h + 200}{x} \Rightarrow h + 200 = \sqrt{3}x$ ...(2)
    From (1) and (2): $\sqrt{3}x - \frac{x}{\sqrt{3}} = 200 \Rightarrow \frac{2x}{\sqrt{3}} = 200 \Rightarrow x = 100\sqrt{3}$ m
    $h = \frac{100\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}} = 100$ m
    The observer is $100\sqrt{3}$ m (about 173.2 m) from the point below the balloon, and the balloon was first at a height of 100 m.

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct figure (1 mark)
    • $\tan 30^\circ = \frac{h}{x}$ (1 mark)
    • $\tan 60^\circ = \frac{h + 200}{x}$ (1 mark)
    • Distance $x = 100\sqrt{3}$ m (1 mark)
    • First height $h = 100$ m (1 mark)

Section E: case-based, 4 marks each

  1. Question 36 · 4 marks · Quadratic Equations
    During a sports day, a ball is thrown vertically upwards from ground level. Its height $h$ (in metres) above the ground $t$ seconds after it is thrown is given by $h = 25t - 5t^2$.
    Based on the above information, answer the following questions:

    (i) Find the height of the ball 2 seconds after it is thrown. [1]

    (ii) Using the discriminant, check whether the ball can reach a height of 35 m. [1]

    (iii) At what times is the ball at a height of 20 m? [2]

    OR (iii) Using the condition for equal roots, find the greatest height reached by the ball.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Find the height of the ball 2 seconds after it is thrown.

    Model answer: $h = 25 \times 2 - 5 \times 2^2 = 50 - 20 = 30$ m

    Marking scheme:

    • Height = 30 m (1 mark)

    (ii) Using the discriminant, check whether the ball can reach a height of 35 m.

    Model answer: $25t - 5t^2 = 35 \Rightarrow t^2 - 5t + 7 = 0$. $D = (-5)^2 - 4 \times 1 \times 7 = 25 - 28 = -3 \lt 0$, so there is no real value of $t$. The ball cannot reach a height of 35 m.

    Marking scheme:

    • $t^2 - 5t + 7 = 0$ with $D = -3 \lt 0$, so the ball cannot reach 35 m (1 mark)

    (iii) At what times is the ball at a height of 20 m?

    Model answer: $25t - 5t^2 = 20 \Rightarrow t^2 - 5t + 4 = 0 \Rightarrow (t - 1)(t - 4) = 0 \Rightarrow t = 1$ or $t = 4$.
    The ball is at a height of 20 m after 1 second (going up) and after 4 seconds (coming down).

    Marking scheme:

    • Forming $t^2 - 5t + 4 = 0$ (1 mark)
    • $t = 1$ s and $t = 4$ s (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: Let the greatest height be $H$ m. The ball reaches $H$ at only one instant, so $25t - 5t^2 = H$, i.e. $5t^2 - 25t + H = 0$, has equal roots.
    $D = 625 - 20H = 0 \Rightarrow H = 31.25$ m (reached at $t = \frac{25}{10} = 2.5$ s).

    Marking scheme:

    • Equal roots condition: $625 - 20H = 0$ (1 mark)
    • Greatest height $H = 31.25$ m (1 mark)

  2. Question 37 · 4 marks · Coordinate Geometry
    On a map drawn with a coordinate grid, Ravi's house is at the point A$(-3, 2)$ and his school is at the point B$(5, 8)$. A straight road joins A and B. The main road of the town runs along the y-axis. One unit on the map represents 100 m on the ground.
    Based on the above information, answer the following questions:

    (i) Find the actual distance between Ravi's house and his school. [1]

    (ii) A bus stop is at the mid-point of AB. Find its coordinates. [1]

    (iii) The road AB crosses the main road (the y-axis) at a point P. Find the ratio in which P divides AB, and the coordinates of P. [2]

    OR (iii) A tea stall T is on the road AB such that AT : TB = 3 : 1. Find the coordinates of T.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Find the actual distance between Ravi's house and his school.

    Model answer: AB $= \sqrt{(5 + 3)^2 + (8 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{64 + 36} = 10$ units $= 10 \times 100$ m $= 1000$ m, i.e. 1 km.

    Marking scheme:

    • AB = 10 units, i.e. 1000 m (1 km) (1 mark)

    (ii) A bus stop is at the mid-point of AB. Find its coordinates.

    Model answer: Mid-point $= \left(\frac{-3 + 5}{2}, \frac{2 + 8}{2}\right) = (1, 5)$

    Marking scheme:

    • Bus stop at $(1, 5)$ (1 mark)

    (iii) The road AB crosses the main road (the y-axis) at a point P. Find the ratio in which P divides AB, and the coordinates of P.

    Model answer: Let P divide AB in the ratio $k : 1$. The x-coordinate of P is 0: $\frac{5k - 3}{k + 1} = 0 \Rightarrow k = \frac{3}{5}$, so the ratio is $3 : 5$.
    y-coordinate of P $= \frac{3 \times 8 + 5 \times 2}{3 + 5} = \frac{34}{8} = \frac{17}{4}$
    P $= \left(0, \frac{17}{4}\right)$

    Marking scheme:

    • Ratio $3 : 5$ (1 mark)
    • P $= \left(0, \frac{17}{4}\right)$ (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: By the section formula with $m : n = 3 : 1$:
    $x = \frac{3 \times 5 + 1 \times (-3)}{3 + 1} = \frac{12}{4} = 3$, $y = \frac{3 \times 8 + 1 \times 2}{3 + 1} = \frac{26}{4} = \frac{13}{2}$
    T $= \left(3, \frac{13}{2}\right)$

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct use of the section formula with ratio $3 : 1$ (1 mark)
    • T $= \left(3, \frac{13}{2}\right)$ (1 mark)

  3. Question 38 · 4 marks · Statistics
    A school surveyed 50 students about the time (in minutes) they take to travel from home to school. The data are given below:

    Time (in minutes)0 - 1010 - 2020 - 3030 - 4040 - 50
    Number of students61015127
    Based on the above information, answer the following questions:

    (i) Identify the modal class of the travel times. [1]

    (ii) How many students take less than 30 minutes to reach school? [1]

    (iii) Find the median time taken by the students. [2]

    OR (iii) Find the mode of the data.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Identify the modal class of the travel times.

    Model answer: The highest frequency is 15, so the modal class is 20 - 30.

    Marking scheme:

    • Modal class 20 - 30 (1 mark)

    (ii) How many students take less than 30 minutes to reach school?

    Model answer: $6 + 10 + 15 = 31$ students.

    Marking scheme:

    • 31 students (1 mark)

    (iii) Find the median time taken by the students.

    Model answer: $\frac{N}{2} = 25$. Cumulative frequencies: 6, 16, 31, 43, 50. The median class is 20 - 30.
    $l = 20$, $cf = 16$, $f = 15$, $h = 10$
    Median $= l + \frac{\frac{N}{2} - cf}{f} \times h = 20 + \frac{25 - 16}{15} \times 10 = 20 + 6 = 26$ minutes

    Marking scheme:

    • Median class 20 - 30 with $l = 20$, $cf = 16$, $f = 15$, $h = 10$ (1 mark)
    • Median = 26 minutes (1 mark)

    OR (the other choice)

    Model answer: Modal class 20 - 30: $l = 20$, $f_1 = 15$, $f_0 = 10$, $f_2 = 12$, $h = 10$
    Mode $= l + \frac{f_1 - f_0}{2f_1 - f_0 - f_2} \times h = 20 + \frac{15 - 10}{30 - 10 - 12} \times 10 = 20 + \frac{50}{8} = 26.25$ minutes

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct values $l = 20$, $f_1 = 15$, $f_0 = 10$, $f_2 = 12$, $h = 10$ in the formula (1 mark)
    • Mode = 26.25 minutes (1 mark)
