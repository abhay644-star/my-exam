General instructions

  1. This question paper contains 38 questions. All questions are compulsory.
  2. This question paper is divided into five sections: A, B, C, D and E.
  3. In Section A, questions 1 to 18 are multiple choice questions (MCQs) and questions 19 and 20 are assertion-reason based questions of 1 mark each.
  4. In Section B, questions 21 to 25 are very short answer (VSA) type questions carrying 2 marks each.
  5. In Section C, questions 26 to 31 are short answer (SA) type questions carrying 3 marks each.
  6. In Section D, questions 32 to 35 are long answer (LA) type questions carrying 5 marks each.
  7. In Section E, questions 36 to 38 are case study based questions carrying 4 marks each. Each has three sub-parts (i), (ii) and (iii) of 1, 1 and 2 marks respectively.
  8. There is no overall choice. Internal choices of the board paper are not included: each question has one version.
  9. Draw neat and clean figures wherever required. Take $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$ wherever required, if not stated.
  10. Use of calculators is not allowed.

Questions

Try each question first, then open the model answer and the marking scheme.

Section A: 1 mark each

  1. Question 1 · 1 mark · Real Numbers
    If $p = 2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5$ and $q = 2^2 \times 3^3 \times 7$, then LCM$(p, q)$ is:

    1. 36
    2. 1080
    3. 3780
    4. 7560
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) 7560

    LCM = product of the highest powers of all prime factors $= 2^3 \times 3^3 \times 5 \times 7 = 7560$.

  2. Question 2 · 1 mark · Polynomials
    If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the zeroes of the polynomial $x^2 - 5x + 6$, then the value of $\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta}$ is:

    1. $\frac{6}{5}$
    2. $\frac{5}{6}$
    3. $-\frac{5}{6}$
    4. $\frac{1}{6}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) $\frac{5}{6}$

    $\alpha + \beta = 5$, $\alpha\beta = 6$. $\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta} = \frac{\alpha + \beta}{\alpha\beta} = \frac{5}{6}$.

  3. Question 3 · 1 mark · Pair of Linear Equations
    The pair of linear equations $3x - 2y = 5$ and $6x - 4y = 7$ has:

    1. a unique solution
    2. exactly two solutions
    3. infinitely many solutions
    4. no solution
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) no solution

    $\frac{3}{6} = \frac{-2}{-4} = \frac{1}{2}$ but $\frac{5}{7} \neq \frac{1}{2}$. The lines are parallel, so there is no solution.

  4. Question 4 · 1 mark · Quadratic Equations
    The roots of the quadratic equation $2x^2 - 5x + 3 = 0$ are:

    1. $1, \frac{3}{2}$
    2. $-1, -\frac{3}{2}$
    3. $1, -\frac{3}{2}$
    4. $-1, \frac{3}{2}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $1, \frac{3}{2}$

    $2x^2 - 5x + 3 = (x - 1)(2x - 3) = 0$, so $x = 1$ or $x = \frac{3}{2}$.

  5. Question 5 · 1 mark · Arithmetic Progressions
    If $2k + 1$, 13 and $5k - 3$ are three consecutive terms of an A.P., then the value of $k$ is:

    1. 3
    2. 4
    3. 5
    4. 6
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) 4

    For consecutive terms of an A.P., $2 \times 13 = (2k + 1) + (5k - 3) \Rightarrow 26 = 7k - 2 \Rightarrow k = 4$.

  6. Question 6 · 1 mark · Circles
    PA and PB are tangents drawn from an external point P to a circle with centre O. If $\angle APB = 70^\circ$, then $\angle AOB$ is:

    1. $70^\circ$
    2. $90^\circ$
    3. $110^\circ$
    4. $140^\circ$
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $110^\circ$

    $\angle OAP = \angle OBP = 90^\circ$. In quadrilateral OAPB, $\angle AOB = 360^\circ - 90^\circ - 90^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ$.

  7. Question 7 · 1 mark · Coordinate Geometry
    If the distance between the points $(4, k)$ and $(1, 0)$ is 5 units, then a possible value of $k$ is:

    1. 2
    2. 3
    3. 4
    4. 5
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) 4

    $(4 - 1)^2 + k^2 = 25 \Rightarrow k^2 = 16 \Rightarrow k = \pm 4$. Of the options, only 4 is possible.

  8. Question 8 · 1 mark · Introduction to Trigonometry
    If $\sqrt{3}\tan\theta = 1$, where $\theta$ is an acute angle, then the value of $\sin^2\theta - \cos^2\theta$ is:

    1. $\frac{1}{2}$
    2. $-\frac{1}{2}$
    3. 0
    4. 1
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) $-\frac{1}{2}$

    $\tan\theta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \Rightarrow \theta = 30^\circ$. $\sin^2 30^\circ - \cos^2 30^\circ = \frac{1}{4} - \frac{3}{4} = -\frac{1}{2}$.

  9. Question 9 · 1 mark · Probability
    A card is drawn at random from a well-shuffled pack of 52 playing cards. The probability that it is a red face card is:

    1. $\frac{3}{26}$
    2. $\frac{3}{13}$
    3. $\frac{1}{13}$
    4. $\frac{1}{26}$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $\frac{3}{26}$

    Red face cards: king, queen and jack of hearts and of diamonds, i.e. 6 cards. Probability $= \frac{6}{52} = \frac{3}{26}$.

  10. Question 10 · 1 mark · Areas Related to Circles
    The perimeter of a quadrant of a circle of radius 14 cm is:

    1. 22 cm
    2. 36 cm
    3. 50 cm
    4. 72 cm
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) 50 cm

    Perimeter of a quadrant = arc + two radii $= \frac{1}{4} \times 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 14 + 2 \times 14 = 22 + 28 = 50$ cm.

  11. Question 11 · 1 mark · Surface Areas and Volumes
    A solid cylinder has radius 7 cm and height 10 cm. A hemisphere of radius 7 cm is scooped out from one of its ends. The total surface area of the remaining solid is:

    1. $594\ \text{cm}^2$
    2. $748\ \text{cm}^2$
    3. $902\ \text{cm}^2$
    4. $1056\ \text{cm}^2$
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $902\ \text{cm}^2$

    TSA = CSA of cylinder + area of the other base + inner surface of hemisphere $= 2\pi rh + \pi r^2 + 2\pi r^2 = 440 + 154 + 308 = 902\ \text{cm}^2$.

  12. Question 12 · 1 mark · Statistics
    For a frequency distribution, $\sum f_i = 25$, $\sum f_i x_i = 50 + 5k$ and the mean is 6. The value of $k$ is:

    1. 10
    2. 16
    3. 20
    4. 25
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) 20

    Mean $= \frac{\sum f_i x_i}{\sum f_i} \Rightarrow 6 = \frac{50 + 5k}{25} \Rightarrow 150 = 50 + 5k \Rightarrow k = 20$.

  13. Question 13 · 1 mark · Probability
    Which of the following cannot be the probability of an event?

    1. $\frac{2}{3}$
    2. 0.07
    3. 15%
    4. 1.03
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) 1.03

    The probability of an event lies between 0 and 1 (both included). 1.03 is greater than 1, so it cannot be a probability.

  14. Question 14 · 1 mark · Coordinate Geometry
    If $(2, 3)$ is the mid-point of the line segment joining the points $(-3, 8)$ and $(a, -2)$, then the value of $a$ is:

    1. $-1$
    2. 1
    3. 5
    4. 7
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) 7

    $\frac{-3 + a}{2} = 2 \Rightarrow a = 7$. (Check: $\frac{8 + (-2)}{2} = 3$.)

  15. Question 15 · 1 mark · Surface Areas and Volumes
    A hemispherical bowl has an inner radius of 10.5 cm. The volume of water it can hold is:

    1. $693\ \text{cm}^3$
    2. $1212.75\ \text{cm}^3$
    3. $2425.5\ \text{cm}^3$
    4. $4851\ \text{cm}^3$
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $2425.5\ \text{cm}^3$

    Volume $= \frac{2}{3}\pi r^3 = \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 10.5 \times 10.5 \times 10.5 = 2425.5\ \text{cm}^3$.

  16. Question 16 · 1 mark · Statistics
    The median class of the following distribution is:

    Class0 - 1010 - 2020 - 3030 - 4040 - 50
    Frequency5812105

    1. 10 - 20
    2. 20 - 30
    3. 30 - 40
    4. 40 - 50
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) 20 - 30

    N = 40, so $\frac{N}{2} = 20$. Cumulative frequencies: 5, 13, 25, ... The first cumulative frequency greater than 20 is 25, for 20 - 30.

  17. Question 17 · 1 mark · Applications of Trigonometry
    The length of the shadow of a vertical pole on level ground is $\sqrt{3}$ times the height of the pole. The angle of elevation of the Sun is:

    1. $30^\circ$
    2. $45^\circ$
    3. $60^\circ$
    4. $90^\circ$
    Show answer

    Answer: (a) $30^\circ$

    $\tan\theta = \frac{h}{\sqrt{3}h} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \Rightarrow \theta = 30^\circ$.

  18. Question 18 · 1 mark · Probability
    Two coins are tossed together. The probability of getting at most one head is:

    1. $\frac{1}{4}$
    2. $\frac{1}{2}$
    3. $\frac{3}{4}$
    4. 1
    Show answer

    Answer: (c) $\frac{3}{4}$

    Outcomes: HH, HT, TH, TT. At most one head: HT, TH, TT, i.e. 3 out of 4. Probability $= \frac{3}{4}$.

  19. Question 19 · 1 mark · Coordinate Geometry
    Assertion (A): The point P(1, 2) divides the line segment joining A($-1$, 0) and B(5, 6) internally in the ratio 2 : 1.
    Reason (R): The point dividing the line segment joining $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ internally in the ratio $m : n$ is $\left(\frac{mx_2 + nx_1}{m + n}, \frac{my_2 + ny_1}{m + n}\right)$.

    1. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    2. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    3. Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.
    4. Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (d) Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.

    R is the section formula, so R is true. For ratio 2 : 1 the point is $\left(\frac{10 - 1}{3}, \frac{12 + 0}{3}\right) = (3, 4)$, not (1, 2). (P actually divides AB in the ratio 1 : 2.) So A is false.

  20. Question 20 · 1 mark · Arithmetic Progressions
    Assertion (A): The 10th term of the A.P. 5, 9, 13, ... is 41.
    Reason (R): The common difference of an A.P. can be negative.

    1. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    2. Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
    3. Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.
    4. Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (b) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

    $a_{10} = 5 + 9 \times 4 = 41$, so A is true. R is also true (for example 10, 7, 4, ...), but it does not explain A.

Section B: 2 marks each

  1. Question 21 · 2 marks · Real Numbers
    Two buses leave a bus depot together at 6:00 a.m. One bus returns to the depot and leaves again every 40 minutes, and the other every 50 minutes. At what time will the two buses next leave the depot together?

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $40 = 2^3 \times 5$ and $50 = 2 \times 5^2$
    LCM(40, 50) $= 2^3 \times 5^2 = 200$ minutes = 3 hours 20 minutes
    The buses will next leave together at 6:00 a.m. + 3 h 20 min = 9:20 a.m.

    Marking scheme:

    • LCM(40, 50) = 200 minutes (with prime factorisation) (1 mark)
    • Time = 9:20 a.m. (1 mark)

  2. Question 22 · 2 marks · Triangles
    In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle PQR$, $\angle A = \angle P$, AB = 6 cm, AC = 8 cm, BC = 7 cm, PQ = 9 cm and PR = 12 cm. Show that $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR$ and find QR.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $\frac{AB}{PQ} = \frac{6}{9} = \frac{2}{3}$ and $\frac{AC}{PR} = \frac{8}{12} = \frac{2}{3}$, and $\angle A = \angle P$.
    So $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR$ (SAS similarity criterion).
    Hence $\frac{BC}{QR} = \frac{2}{3} \Rightarrow QR = \frac{3 \times 7}{2} = 10.5$ cm.

    Marking scheme:

    • Ratios $\frac{AB}{PQ} = \frac{AC}{PR} = \frac{2}{3}$ with included angle equal, so similar by SAS (1 mark)
    • QR = 10.5 cm (1 mark)

  3. Question 23 · 2 marks · Applications of Trigonometry
    A straight wheelchair ramp rises through a vertical height of 1.5 m and makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the level ground. Find the length of the ramp.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Let the length of the ramp be $L$ m. The ramp, the vertical rise and the ground form a right triangle.
    $\sin 30^\circ = \frac{1.5}{L} \Rightarrow \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1.5}{L} \Rightarrow L = 3$ m
    The ramp is 3 m long.

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct ratio $\sin 30^\circ = \frac{1.5}{L}$ (1 mark)
    • Length of ramp = 3 m (1 mark)

  4. Question 24 · 2 marks · Areas Related to Circles
    Find the area of the sector of a circle of radius 42 cm with central angle $60^\circ$. Also find the length of the arc of this sector.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Area of sector $= \frac{60}{360} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 42 \times 42 = \frac{1}{6} \times 5544 = 924\ \text{cm}^2$
    Length of arc $= \frac{60}{360} \times 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 42 = \frac{1}{6} \times 264 = 44$ cm

    Marking scheme:

    • Area of sector $= 924\ \text{cm}^2$ (1 mark)
    • Length of arc = 44 cm (1 mark)

  5. Question 25 · 2 marks · Statistics
    Find the mode of the following data:

    Class10 - 2020 - 3030 - 4040 - 5050 - 60
    Frequency5814103

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Modal class = 30 - 40 (highest frequency 14).
    $l = 30$, $f_1 = 14$, $f_0 = 8$, $f_2 = 10$, $h = 10$
    Mode $= l + \frac{f_1 - f_0}{2f_1 - f_0 - f_2} \times h = 30 + \frac{14 - 8}{28 - 8 - 10} \times 10 = 30 + 6 = 36$

    Marking scheme:

    • Modal class 30 - 40 with $l = 30$, $f_1 = 14$, $f_0 = 8$, $f_2 = 10$, $h = 10$ (1 mark)
    • Mode = 36 (1 mark)

Section C: 3 marks each

  1. Question 26 · 3 marks · Real Numbers
    Given that $\sqrt{5}$ is an irrational number, prove that $7 - 2\sqrt{5}$ is an irrational number.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Let us assume that $7 - 2\sqrt{5}$ is rational. Then $7 - 2\sqrt{5} = \frac{a}{b}$, where $a$ and $b$ are integers and $b \neq 0$.
    $\Rightarrow 2\sqrt{5} = 7 - \frac{a}{b} \Rightarrow \sqrt{5} = \frac{7b - a}{2b}$
    Since $a$ and $b$ are integers, $\frac{7b - a}{2b}$ is rational, so $\sqrt{5}$ is rational.
    This contradicts the given fact that $\sqrt{5}$ is irrational.
    Hence our assumption is wrong, and $7 - 2\sqrt{5}$ is irrational.

    Marking scheme:

    • Assuming $7 - 2\sqrt{5} = \frac{a}{b}$ ($a$, $b$ integers, $b \neq 0$) (1 mark)
    • Getting $\sqrt{5} = \frac{7b - a}{2b}$, a rational number (1 mark)
    • Contradiction with $\sqrt{5}$ irrational, and conclusion (1 mark)

  2. Question 27 · 3 marks · Pair of Linear Equations
    At a fruit shop, 5 kg of apples and 3 dozen bananas cost ₹680, while 3 kg of apples and 4 dozen bananas cost ₹540. Find the cost of 1 kg of apples and of 1 dozen bananas.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Let the cost of 1 kg of apples be ₹$x$ and of 1 dozen bananas be ₹$y$.
    $5x + 3y = 680$ ...(1)
    $3x + 4y = 540$ ...(2)
    (1) $\times 4$: $20x + 12y = 2720$; (2) $\times 3$: $9x + 12y = 1620$
    Subtracting: $11x = 1100 \Rightarrow x = 100$
    From (1): $500 + 3y = 680 \Rightarrow y = 60$
    1 kg of apples costs ₹100 and 1 dozen bananas cost ₹60.

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct equations $5x + 3y = 680$ and $3x + 4y = 540$ (1 mark)
    • Cost of 1 kg of apples = ₹100 (1 mark)
    • Cost of 1 dozen bananas = ₹60 (1 mark)

  3. Question 28 · 3 marks · Polynomials
    If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the zeroes of the polynomial $x^2 - 6x + k$ such that $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = 20$, find the value of $k$. Hence find the zeroes of the polynomial.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: $\alpha + \beta = 6$ and $\alpha\beta = k$
    $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 2\alpha\beta \Rightarrow 20 = 36 - 2k \Rightarrow k = 8$
    The polynomial is $x^2 - 6x + 8 = (x - 2)(x - 4)$, so its zeroes are 2 and 4.

    Marking scheme:

    • $\alpha + \beta = 6$ and $\alpha\beta = k$ (1 mark)
    • Using $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 2\alpha\beta$ to get $k = 8$ (1 mark)
    • Zeroes 2 and 4 (1 mark)

  4. Question 29 · 3 marks · Triangles
    S is a point on the side QR of $\triangle PQR$ such that $\angle PSR = \angle QPR$. If QR = 16 cm and SR = 9 cm, prove that $\triangle RSP \sim \triangle RPQ$ and find the length of PR.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: In $\triangle RSP$ and $\triangle RPQ$:
    $\angle PSR = \angle QPR$ (given)
    $\angle SRP = \angle PRQ$ (common angle R)
    So $\triangle RSP \sim \triangle RPQ$ (AA similarity criterion).
    Hence $\frac{RS}{RP} = \frac{RP}{RQ} \Rightarrow RP^2 = RS \times RQ = 9 \times 16 = 144 \Rightarrow PR = 12$ cm.

    Marking scheme:

    • Two pairs of equal angles stated, so $\triangle RSP \sim \triangle RPQ$ by AA (1½ marks)
    • Correct ratio $\frac{RS}{RP} = \frac{RP}{RQ}$, i.e. $RP^2 = RS \times RQ$ (½ marks)
    • PR = 12 cm (1 mark)

  5. Question 30 · 3 marks · Coordinate Geometry
    The points A($-2$, 1), B($a$, 0), C(4, $b$) and D(1, 2) are the vertices of a parallelogram ABCD, taken in order. Find the values of $a$ and $b$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, so the mid-point of AC = the mid-point of BD.
    Mid-point of AC $= \left(\frac{-2 + 4}{2}, \frac{1 + b}{2}\right) = \left(1, \frac{1 + b}{2}\right)$
    Mid-point of BD $= \left(\frac{a + 1}{2}, \frac{0 + 2}{2}\right) = \left(\frac{a + 1}{2}, 1\right)$
    $\frac{a + 1}{2} = 1 \Rightarrow a = 1$; $\frac{1 + b}{2} = 1 \Rightarrow b = 1$

    Marking scheme:

    • Using mid-point of AC = mid-point of BD, with both mid-points written (1 mark)
    • $a = 1$ (1 mark)
    • $b = 1$ (1 mark)

  6. Question 31 · 3 marks · Introduction to Trigonometry
    Prove that $\frac{\sin^4\theta - \cos^4\theta}{\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta} = \sec^2\theta - \operatorname{cosec}^2\theta$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: LHS $= \frac{(\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta)(\sin^2\theta - \cos^2\theta)}{\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta}$
    $= \frac{\sin^2\theta - \cos^2\theta}{\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta}$ (since $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$)
    $= \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta} - \frac{\cos^2\theta}{\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta} = \frac{1}{\cos^2\theta} - \frac{1}{\sin^2\theta}$
    $= \sec^2\theta - \operatorname{cosec}^2\theta =$ RHS

    Marking scheme:

    • Factorising the numerator and using $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ to get $\sin^2\theta - \cos^2\theta$ (1 mark)
    • Splitting into $\frac{1}{\cos^2\theta} - \frac{1}{\sin^2\theta}$ (1 mark)
    • Writing as $\sec^2\theta - \operatorname{cosec}^2\theta$ = RHS (1 mark)

Section D: 5 marks each

  1. Question 32 · 5 marks · Quadratic Equations
    The length of a rectangular field is 1 m more than twice its breadth, and the area of the field is $528\ \text{m}^2$. Find the length and breadth of the field. Also find the cost of fencing the field at the rate of ₹50 per metre.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Let the breadth be $x$ m. Then the length is $(2x + 1)$ m.
    $x(2x + 1) = 528 \Rightarrow 2x^2 + x - 528 = 0$
    $\Rightarrow 2x^2 - 32x + 33x - 528 = 0 \Rightarrow 2x(x - 16) + 33(x - 16) = 0 \Rightarrow (x - 16)(2x + 33) = 0$
    $\Rightarrow x = 16$ or $x = -\frac{33}{2}$. Breadth cannot be negative, so $x = 16$.
    Breadth = 16 m, length $= 2 \times 16 + 1 = 33$ m.
    Perimeter $= 2(33 + 16) = 98$ m; cost of fencing $= 98 \times 50 =$ ₹4900.

    Marking scheme:

    • Forming $x(2x + 1) = 528$ (1 mark)
    • Writing the quadratic $2x^2 + x - 528 = 0$ (1 mark)
    • Solving: $(x - 16)(2x + 33) = 0$ (1 mark)
    • Rejecting the negative value: breadth 16 m and length 33 m (1 mark)
    • Perimeter 98 m and cost ₹4900 (1 mark)

  2. Question 33 · 5 marks · Circles
    Prove that the lengths of the tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
    Using this result, solve the following: A circle is inscribed in $\triangle ABC$, touching the sides AB, BC and CA at P, Q and R respectively. If AB = 10 cm, BC = 12 cm and CA = 8 cm, find the lengths of AP, BQ and CR.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Given: PA and PB are tangents from an external point P to a circle with centre O, touching it at A and B.
    To prove: PA = PB.
    Construction: Join OA, OB and OP.
    Proof: $\angle OAP = \angle OBP = 90^\circ$ (radius is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact).
    In right triangles OAP and OBP: OA = OB (radii) and OP = OP (common).
    So $\triangle OAP \cong \triangle OBP$ (RHS congruence), and hence PA = PB (CPCT).

    Application: Let AP = AR = $x$, BP = BQ = $y$ and CQ = CR = $z$ (tangents from the same external point).
    $x + y = 10$, $y + z = 12$, $z + x = 8$. Adding: $2(x + y + z) = 30 \Rightarrow x + y + z = 15$.
    $x = 15 - 12 = 3$, $y = 15 - 8 = 7$, $z = 15 - 10 = 5$.
    AP = 3 cm, BQ = 7 cm, CR = 5 cm.

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct figure, given, to prove and construction (1 mark)
    • $\angle OAP = \angle OBP = 90^\circ$, OA = OB, OP common (1 mark)
    • $\triangle OAP \cong \triangle OBP$ (RHS), so PA = PB (1 mark)
    • Equations $x + y = 10$, $y + z = 12$, $z + x = 8$ using equal tangents (1 mark)
    • AP = 3 cm, BQ = 7 cm, CR = 5 cm (1 mark)

  3. Question 34 · 5 marks · Surface Areas and Volumes
    A solid metal piece is in the shape of a cylinder with a hemisphere attached at each end. The diameter of the piece is 21 cm and its total length is 45 cm. Find the volume of the metal and the total surface area of the piece. Also find the cost of polishing its surface at the rate of ₹10 per $100\ \text{cm}^2$.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Radius $r = 10.5$ cm; length of the cylindrical part $h = 45 - 2 \times 10.5 = 24$ cm.
    Volume of cylinder $= \pi r^2 h = \frac{22}{7} \times 10.5 \times 10.5 \times 24 = 8316\ \text{cm}^3$
    Volume of two hemispheres $= \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 = \frac{4}{3} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 10.5 \times 10.5 \times 10.5 = 4851\ \text{cm}^3$
    Volume of metal $= 8316 + 4851 = 13167\ \text{cm}^3$
    CSA of cylinder $= 2\pi rh = 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 10.5 \times 24 = 1584\ \text{cm}^2$
    CSA of two hemispheres $= 4\pi r^2 = 4 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 10.5 \times 10.5 = 1386\ \text{cm}^2$
    Total surface area $= 1584 + 1386 = 2970\ \text{cm}^2$
    Cost of polishing $= \frac{2970}{100} \times 10 =$ ₹297

    Marking scheme:

    • Length of cylindrical part = 24 cm (½ marks)
    • Volume of cylinder $= 8316\ \text{cm}^3$ (1 mark)
    • Volume of two hemispheres $= 4851\ \text{cm}^3$ (1 mark)
    • Volume of metal $= 13167\ \text{cm}^3$ (½ marks)
    • CSA of cylinder $= 1584\ \text{cm}^2$ (½ marks)
    • CSA of two hemispheres $= 1386\ \text{cm}^2$ (½ marks)
    • Total surface area $= 2970\ \text{cm}^2$ and cost ₹297 (1 mark)

  4. Question 35 · 5 marks · Applications of Trigonometry
    A man standing on one bank of a river observes that the angle of elevation of the top of a tree standing on the opposite bank, exactly across, is $60^\circ$. When he walks 40 m straight back from the bank, directly away from the tree, the angle of elevation becomes $30^\circ$. Find the height of the tree and the width of the river.

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    Model answer: Let AB be the tree of height $h$ m, C the first position of the man on the bank and D the second position, with B, C, D in a line. Let BC (width of the river) = $x$ m, so BD $= (x + 40)$ m.
    In right $\triangle ABC$: $\tan 60^\circ = \frac{h}{x} \Rightarrow h = \sqrt{3}x$ ...(1)
    In right $\triangle ABD$: $\tan 30^\circ = \frac{h}{x + 40} \Rightarrow h = \frac{x + 40}{\sqrt{3}}$ ...(2)
    From (1) and (2): $\sqrt{3}x = \frac{x + 40}{\sqrt{3}} \Rightarrow 3x = x + 40 \Rightarrow x = 20$
    $h = 20\sqrt{3}$ m
    Height of the tree $= 20\sqrt{3}$ m; width of the river = 20 m.

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct figure (1 mark)
    • $\tan 60^\circ = \frac{h}{x}$, i.e. $h = \sqrt{3}x$ (1 mark)
    • $\tan 30^\circ = \frac{h}{x + 40}$ (1 mark)
    • Width of the river $x = 20$ m (1 mark)
    • Height of the tree $= 20\sqrt{3}$ m (1 mark)

Section E: case-based, 4 marks each

  1. Question 36 · 4 marks · Arithmetic Progressions
    A school auditorium has seats arranged in rows. There are 20 seats in the first row, and each row has 2 seats more than the row in front of it. There are 30 rows in all.
    Based on the above information, answer the following questions:

    (i) How many seats are there in the 10th row? [1]

    (ii) How many more seats are there in the 25th row than in the 15th row? [1]

    (iii) Find the total number of seats in the auditorium. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) How many seats are there in the 10th row?

    Model answer: $a = 20$, $d = 2$. $a_{10} = 20 + 9 \times 2 = 38$ seats.

    Marking scheme:

    • $a_{10} = 38$ seats (1 mark)

    (ii) How many more seats are there in the 25th row than in the 15th row?

    Model answer: $a_{25} - a_{15} = (25 - 15)d = 10 \times 2 = 20$ seats more.

    Marking scheme:

    • 20 seats more (1 mark)

    (iii) Find the total number of seats in the auditorium.

    Model answer: $S_{30} = \frac{30}{2}[2 \times 20 + (30 - 1) \times 2] = 15 \times (40 + 58) = 15 \times 98 = 1470$ seats.

    Marking scheme:

    • Correct use of $S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d]$ with $n = 30$, $a = 20$, $d = 2$ (1 mark)
    • Total = 1470 seats (1 mark)

  2. Question 37 · 4 marks · Triangles
    On a sunny morning, Kabir measured that a vertical pole 6 m high casts a shadow 4 m long on level ground. At the same time, a nearby vertical tower casts a shadow 28 m long. The Sun's rays are parallel, so the pole with its shadow and the tower with its shadow form two right triangles.
    Based on the above information, answer the following questions:

    (i) Name the similarity criterion by which the two triangles are similar. [1]

    (ii) Find the height of the tower. [1]

    (iii) At the same time, a boy 1.5 m tall stands on the line of the tower's shadow so that the tip of his shadow falls exactly at the tip of the tower's shadow. Find the length of the boy's shadow and his distance from the foot of the tower. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) Name the similarity criterion by which the two triangles are similar.

    Model answer: Each triangle has a right angle at the foot of the object, and the angle of elevation of the Sun at the tip of each shadow is the same (parallel rays). So the triangles are similar by the AA criterion.

    Marking scheme:

    • AA similarity criterion (1 mark)

    (ii) Find the height of the tower.

    Model answer: $\frac{\text{height of tower}}{28} = \frac{6}{4} \Rightarrow$ height of tower $= 42$ m.

    Marking scheme:

    • Height of tower = 42 m (1 mark)

    (iii) At the same time, a boy 1.5 m tall stands on the line of the tower's shadow so that the tip of his shadow falls exactly at the tip of the tower's shadow. Find the length of the boy's shadow and his distance from the foot of the tower.

    Model answer: Boy's shadow: $\frac{1.5}{s} = \frac{6}{4} \Rightarrow s = 1$ m.
    Distance of the boy from the foot of the tower $= 28 - 1 = 27$ m.

    Marking scheme:

    • Length of the boy's shadow = 1 m (1 mark)
    • Distance from the foot of the tower = 27 m (1 mark)

  3. Question 38 · 4 marks · Probability
    At a school fair, a game stall has a bag containing 60 tokens, each of which is red, blue or green. A player draws one token at random. The probability of drawing a red token is $\frac{1}{4}$ and the probability of drawing a blue token is $\frac{1}{3}$.
    Based on the above information, answer the following questions:

    (i) How many red tokens are there in the bag? [1]

    (ii) Find the probability of drawing a green token. [1]

    (iii) The stall keeper adds 5 more blue tokens to the bag. Find the probability of drawing a blue token now. [2]

    Show model answer and marking scheme

    (i) How many red tokens are there in the bag?

    Model answer: Number of red tokens $= \frac{1}{4} \times 60 = 15$.

    Marking scheme:

    • 15 red tokens (1 mark)

    (ii) Find the probability of drawing a green token.

    Model answer: P(green) $= 1 - \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{5}{12}$.

    Marking scheme:

    • P(green) $= \frac{5}{12}$ (1 mark)

    (iii) The stall keeper adds 5 more blue tokens to the bag. Find the probability of drawing a blue token now.

    Model answer: Blue tokens at first $= \frac{1}{3} \times 60 = 20$. After adding 5: blue tokens = 25 and total tokens = 65.
    P(blue) $= \frac{25}{65} = \frac{5}{13}$.

    Marking scheme:

    • Blue tokens = 25 and total = 65 after adding (1 mark)
    • P(blue) $= \frac{5}{13}$ (1 mark)
