A few questions with their official answers and worked solutions. All 51 are in the online test.

  1. Question 1 · Mathematics · Differential Equations
    Let $f(x)$ be a continuously differentiable function on the interval $(0, \infty)$ such that $f(1) = 2$ and

    $\displaystyle \lim_{t \to x} \frac{t^{10} f(x) - x^{10} f(t)}{t^9 - x^9} = 1$
    for each $x > 0$. Then, for all $x > 0$, $f(x)$ is equal to

    1. $\dfrac{31}{11x} - \dfrac{9}{11}x^{10}$
    2. $\dfrac{9}{11x} + \dfrac{13}{11}x^{10}$
    3. $\dfrac{-9}{11x} + \dfrac{31}{11}x^{10}$
    4. $\dfrac{13}{11x} + \dfrac{9}{11}x^{10}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    The limit is of the form $\dfrac00$; by L'Hospital's rule (differentiating in $t$): $\dfrac{10x^9 f(x) - x^{10} f'(x)}{9x^8} = 1$.
    So $10x f(x) - x^2 f'(x) = 9$, i.e. $f'(x) - \dfrac{10}{x} f(x) = -\dfrac{9}{x^2}$.
    Integrating factor $x^{-10}$: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(x^{-10} f(x)\right) = -9x^{-12} \Rightarrow x^{-10} f(x) = \dfrac{9}{11}x^{-11} + K$.
    So $f(x) = \dfrac{9}{11x} + Kx^{10}$; $f(1) = 2 \Rightarrow K = \dfrac{13}{11}$.
    $f(x) = \dfrac{9}{11x} + \dfrac{13}{11}x^{10}$ → (B).

  2. Question 3 · Mathematics · Trigonometric Functions
    Let $\dfrac{\pi}{2} \lt x \lt \pi$ be such that $\cot x = \dfrac{-5}{\sqrt{11}}$. Then

    $\displaystyle \left(\sin\frac{11x}{2}\right)\left(\sin 6x - \cos 6x\right) + \left(\cos\frac{11x}{2}\right)\left(\sin 6x + \cos 6x\right)$
    is equal to

    1. $\dfrac{\sqrt{11}-1}{2\sqrt{3}}$
    2. $\dfrac{\sqrt{11}+1}{2\sqrt{3}}$
    3. $\dfrac{\sqrt{11}+1}{3\sqrt{2}}$
    4. $\dfrac{\sqrt{11}-1}{3\sqrt{2}}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Let $a = \dfrac{11x}{2}$. Regroup: $(\sin 6x\cos a - \cos 6x\sin a) + (\cos 6x\cos a + \sin 6x\sin a) = \sin(6x - a) + \cos(6x - a)$.
    $6x - a = \dfrac{x}{2}$, so the expression is $\sin\dfrac{x}{2} + \cos\dfrac{x}{2}$.
    $\csc^2 x = 1 + \dfrac{25}{11} = \dfrac{36}{11}$; $x$ in the second quadrant gives $\sin x = \dfrac{\sqrt{11}}{6}$, $\cos x = -\dfrac56$.
    $\dfrac{x}{2} \in \left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$: $\cos\dfrac{x}{2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{1+\cos x}{2}} = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt3}$, $\sin\dfrac{x}{2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{1-\cos x}{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{11}}{2\sqrt3}$.
    Sum $= \dfrac{\sqrt{11}+1}{2\sqrt3}$ → (B).

  3. Question 18 · Physics · Units and Measurements
    A dimensionless quantity is constructed in terms of electronic charge $e$, permittivity of free space $\varepsilon_0$, Planck's constant $h$, and speed of light $c$. If the dimensionless quantity is written as $e^{\alpha}\varepsilon_0^{\beta}h^{\gamma}c^{\delta}$ and $n$ is a non-zero integer, then $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta)$ is given by

    1. $(2n, -n, -n, -n)$
    2. $(n, -n, -2n, -n)$
    3. $(n, -n, -n, -2n)$
    4. $(2n, -n, -2n, -2n)$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $\dfrac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r}$ is an energy and $\dfrac{hc}{r}$ is an energy, so $\dfrac{e^2}{\varepsilon_0 hc}$ is dimensionless (it equals twice the fine-structure constant $\dfrac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar c}$).
    Any power of it is dimensionless: $\left(\dfrac{e^2}{\varepsilon_0 hc}\right)^n = e^{2n}\varepsilon_0^{-n}h^{-n}c^{-n}$.
    $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta) = (2n, -n, -n, -n)$ → (A).

  4. Question 19 · Physics · Moving Charges and Magnetism
    An infinitely long wire, located on the $z$-axis, carries a current $I$ along the $+z$-direction and produces the magnetic field $\vec{B}$. The magnitude of the line integral $\int \vec{B} \cdot \vec{dl}$ along a straight line from the point $(-\sqrt{3}a, a, 0)$ to $(a, a, 0)$ is given by
    [$\mu_0$ is the magnetic permeability of free space.]

    1. $7\mu_0 I/24$
    2. $7\mu_0 I/12$
    3. $\mu_0 I/8$
    4. $\mu_0 I/6$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $\vec B = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}\hat\phi$, so $\vec B\cdot\vec{dl} = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}\, r\,d\phi = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi}d\phi$: the integral depends only on the angle swept.
    Start $(-\sqrt3a, a)$: $\phi = 150^\circ$. End $(a, a)$: $\phi = 45^\circ$. The line ($y = a$) does not cross the wire, so $|\Delta\phi| = 105^\circ = \dfrac{7\pi}{12}$.
    $\left|\int\vec B\cdot\vec{dl}\right| = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi}\cdot\dfrac{7\pi}{12} = \dfrac{7\mu_0 I}{24}$ → (A).

  5. Question 35 · Chemistry · Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry
    A closed vessel contains 10 g of an ideal gas X at 300 K, which exerts 2 atm pressure. At the same temperature, 80 g of another ideal gas Y is added to it and the pressure becomes 6 atm. The ratio of root mean square velocities of X and Y at 300 K is

    1. $2\sqrt{2} : \sqrt{3}$
    2. $2\sqrt{2} : 1$
    3. $1 : 2$
    4. $2 : 1$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Same $V$ and $T$: partial pressures are proportional to moles. $p_X = 2$ atm, $p_Y = 6 - 2 = 4$ atm, so $n_Y = 2n_X$.
    $\dfrac{80}{M_Y} = 2\cdot\dfrac{10}{M_X} \Rightarrow M_Y = 4M_X$.
    $\dfrac{u_X}{u_Y} = \sqrt{\dfrac{M_Y}{M_X}} = 2$, i.e. $2 : 1$ → (D).

  6. Question 36 · Chemistry · Redox Reactions
    At room temperature, disproportionation of an aqueous solution of in situ generated nitrous acid ($\mathrm{HNO_2}$) gives the species

    1. $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$, $\mathrm{NO_3^-}$ and $\mathrm{NO}$
    2. $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$, $\mathrm{NO_3^-}$ and $\mathrm{NO_2}$
    3. $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$, $\mathrm{NO^-}$ and $\mathrm{NO_2}$
    4. $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$, $\mathrm{NO_3^-}$ and $\mathrm{N_2O}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Nitrous acid (N in +3) disproportionates to N(+5) and N(+2): $3\mathrm{HNO_2} \to \mathrm{HNO_3} + 2\mathrm{NO} + \mathrm{H_2O}$.
    In water $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ is ionised: $\mathrm{H_3O^+} + \mathrm{NO_3^-}$.
    Species: $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$, $\mathrm{NO_3^-}$ and NO → (A).
