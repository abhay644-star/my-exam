Solved questions from this paper

A few questions with their official answers and worked solutions. All 51 are in the online test.

  1. Question 1 · Mathematics · Inverse Trigonometric Functions
    Considering only the principal values of the inverse trigonometric functions, the value of

    $\displaystyle \tan\left(\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{3}{5}\right)-2\cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{2}{\sqrt5}\right)\right)$
    is

    1. $\dfrac{7}{24}$
    2. $\dfrac{-7}{24}$
    3. $\dfrac{-5}{24}$
    4. $\dfrac{5}{24}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Let $a=\sin^{-1}\dfrac35$, so $\tan a=\dfrac34$; let $b=\cos^{-1}\dfrac{2}{\sqrt5}$, so $\tan b=\dfrac12$.
    $\tan 2b=\dfrac{2\cdot\frac12}{1-\frac14}=\dfrac43$.
    $\tan(a-2b)=\dfrac{\frac34-\frac43}{1+\frac34\cdot\frac43}=\dfrac{-\frac{7}{12}}{2}=-\dfrac{7}{24}$ → (B).

  2. Question 2 · Mathematics · Application of Integrals
    Let $S=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R} : x\ge 0,\ y\ge 0,\ y^2\le 4x,\ y^2\le 12-2x \text{ and } 3y+\sqrt8\,x\le 5\sqrt8\right\}$. If the area of the region $S$ is $\alpha\sqrt2$, then $\alpha$ is equal to

    1. $\dfrac{17}{2}$
    2. $\dfrac{17}{3}$
    3. $\dfrac{17}{4}$
    4. $\dfrac{17}{5}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $y^2=4x$ and $y^2=12-2x$ meet at $(2,2\sqrt2)$; the line $3y+2\sqrt2\,x=10\sqrt2$ also passes through $(2,2\sqrt2)$ and meets $y=0$ at $x=5$ (while $y^2=12-2x$ meets it at $x=6$).
    So for $0\le y\le 2\sqrt2$ the region runs from the parabola $x=\dfrac{y^2}{4}$ to the line $x=\dfrac{10\sqrt2-3y}{2\sqrt2}=5-\dfrac{3y}{2\sqrt2}$.
    Area $=\displaystyle\int_0^{2\sqrt2}\left(5-\dfrac{3y}{2\sqrt2}-\dfrac{y^2}{4}\right)dy=10\sqrt2-3\sqrt2-\dfrac{4\sqrt2}{3}=\dfrac{17}{3}\sqrt2$.
    $\alpha=\dfrac{17}{3}$ → (B).

  3. Question 20 · Physics · Atoms
    A metal target with atomic number $Z=46$ is bombarded with a high energy electron beam. The emission of X-rays from the target is analyzed. The ratio $r$ of the wavelengths of the $K_\alpha$-line and the cut-off is found to be $r=2$. If the same electron beam bombards another metal target with $Z=41$, the value of $r$ will be

    1. $2.53$
    2. $1.27$
    3. $2.24$
    4. $1.58$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    The cut-off wavelength $\lambda_c=\dfrac{hc}{eV}$ depends only on the beam, so it is unchanged.
    Moseley: $\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{K\alpha}}\propto(Z-1)^2$, so $r\propto\dfrac{1}{(Z-1)^2}$.
    $r'=2\times\left(\dfrac{45}{40}\right)^2=2\times1.2656=2.53$ → (A).

  4. Question 23 · Physics · Mechanical Properties of Fluids
    A table tennis ball has radius $(3/2)\times10^{-2}$ m and mass $(22/7)\times10^{-3}$ kg. It is slowly pushed down into a swimming pool to a depth of $d=0.7$ m below the water surface and then released from rest. It emerges from the water surface at speed $v$, without getting wet, and rises up to a height $H$. Which of the following option(s) is(are) correct?
    [Given: $\pi=22/7$, $g=10\ \text{m s}^{-2}$, density of water $=1\times10^3\ \text{kg m}^{-3}$, viscosity of water $=1\times10^{-3}$ Pa-s.]

    1. The work done in pushing the ball to the depth $d$ is $0.077$ J.
    2. If we neglect the viscous force in water, then the speed $v=7$ m/s.
    3. If we neglect the viscous force in water, then the height $H=1.4$ m.
    4. The ratio of the magnitudes of the net force excluding the viscous force to the maximum viscous force in water is $500/9$.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (b), (d)

    $V=\dfrac43\cdot\dfrac{22}{7}\cdot(1.5\times10^{-2})^3=1.4143\times10^{-5}\ \text{m}^3$; buoyancy $=\rho Vg=0.14143$ N; weight $=0.03143$ N.
    Net upward force (excluding viscosity) $F=0.11$ N. Work to push it slowly to $0.7$ m: $0.11\times0.7=0.077$ J → (A) true.
    Without viscosity: $a=\dfrac{0.11}{(22/7)\times10^{-3}}=35\ \text{m s}^{-2}$, $v^2=2(35)(0.7)=49$, $v=7$ m/s → (B) true.
    $H=\dfrac{v^2}{2g}=2.45$ m → (C) false.
    Maximum viscous force (at $v=7$ m/s): $6\pi\eta rv=6\cdot\dfrac{22}{7}\cdot10^{-3}\cdot1.5\times10^{-2}\cdot7=1.98\times10^{-3}$ N; ratio $=\dfrac{0.11}{1.98\times10^{-3}}=\dfrac{500}{9}$ → (D) true.
    Answer: (A), (B), (D).

  5. Question 35 · Chemistry · Structure of Atom
    According to Bohr’s model, the highest kinetic energy is associated with the electron in the

    1. first orbit of H atom
    2. first orbit of $\mathrm{He^+}$
    3. second orbit of $\mathrm{He^+}$
    4. second orbit of $\mathrm{Li^{2+}}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    In Bohr’s model $\text{KE}=13.6\,\dfrac{Z^2}{n^2}$ eV, so compare $\dfrac{Z^2}{n^2}$.
    H, $n=1$: $1$; $\mathrm{He^+}$, $n=1$: $4$; $\mathrm{He^+}$, $n=2$: $1$; $\mathrm{Li^{2+}}$, $n=2$: $\dfrac94$.
    Highest: first orbit of $\mathrm{He^+}$ → (B).

  6. Question 36 · Chemistry · Redox Reactions
    In a metal deficient oxide sample, $\mathbf{M_XY_2O_4}$ ($\mathbf{M}$ and $\mathbf{Y}$ are metals), $\mathbf{M}$ is present in both +2 and +3 oxidation states and $\mathbf{Y}$ is in +3 oxidation state. If the fraction of $\mathbf{M^{2+}}$ ions present in $\mathbf{M}$ is $\dfrac13$, the value of $\mathbf{X}$ is _____.

    1. $0.25$
    2. $0.33$
    3. $0.67$
    4. $0.75$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Charge balance: $2(+3)+X\cdot(\text{average charge of M})=8$, so $X\times(\text{average charge})=2$.
    Average charge of M $=\dfrac13(2)+\dfrac23(3)=\dfrac83$.
    $X=\dfrac{2}{8/3}=0.75$ → (D).
