  1. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 1 · Question 2
    Let $P$ be the point on the parabola $y = x^2$ such that the slope of the tangent to the parabola at the point $P$ is $4$ . Let $Q$ be the point in the first quadrant lying on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 2$ such that the slope of the tangent to the circle at the point $Q$ is $-1$ . Let $R$ be the point in the first quadrant lying on the ellipse $x^2 + 4y^2 = 8$ such that the slope of the tangent to the ellipse at the point $R$ is $-\frac{1}{2}$. Then the radius of the circle passing through the points $P, Q$ and $R$ is

    1. $\sqrt{10}$
    2. $\sqrt{5}$
    3. $\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}}$
    4. $2\sqrt{5}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Parabola: $2x = 4 \Rightarrow P = (2, 4)$.
    Circle: slope $-x/y = -1 \Rightarrow x = y = 1$, so $Q = (1, 1)$.
    Ellipse: slope $-\dfrac{x}{4y} = -\dfrac12 \Rightarrow x = 2y$; $8y^2 = 8 \Rightarrow R = (2, 1)$.
    $RQ$ is horizontal and $RP$ is vertical, so $\angle PRQ = 90^\circ$ and $PQ$ is a diameter.
    $PQ = \sqrt{1 + 9} = \sqrt{10}$, radius $= \dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2} = \sqrt{\dfrac52}$. Answer (C).

  3. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 2
    Let $T$ be the tangent to the parabola $y^2 = 16x$ at the point $(64, 32)$. Let $L$ be the tangent to the same parabola at another point $(x_1 , y_1)$ on the parabola. If $L$ and $T$ are perpendicular to each other, then the distance between the point $(x_1 , y_1)$ and the focus of the parabola, is

    1. $\dfrac{15}{4}$
    2. $4$
    3. $\dfrac{17}{4}$
    4. $5$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Here $a=4$; points are $(4t^2, 8t)$. $(64,32)$ gives $t_1=4$.
    Tangents at $t_1,t_2$ are perpendicular iff $t_1t_2=-1$, so $t_2=-\tfrac14$.
    Point: $(x_1,y_1)=(\tfrac14,-2)$.
    Focal distance $=x_1+a=\tfrac14+4=\dfrac{17}{4}$.

  4. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 13
    Consider the ellipse $E$ given by $\frac{x^2}{18} + \frac{y^2}{12} = 1$ . Let $H$ be the hyperbola whose eccentricity is the reciprocal of the eccentricity of $E$ and whose foci are the same as that of $E$ . Let $P$ and $Q$ be the points of intersection of $H$ and the parabola $\sqrt{5}\,y = x^2$ in the first quadrant. Let $d$ be the distance between $P$ and $Q$.

    If $a$ and $b$ are the integers such that $d^2 = a + b\sqrt{5}$ , then the value of $a - b$ is ________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 18

    For $E$: $e^2=1-\frac{12}{18}=\frac13$, foci $(\pm\sqrt6,0)$.
    $H$: $e=\sqrt3$, $Ae=\sqrt6\Rightarrow A^2=2$, $B^2=A^2(e^2-1)=4$: $\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{y^2}{4}=1$.
    With $x^2=\sqrt5y$: $y^2-2\sqrt5y+4=0$, $y=\sqrt5\pm1$, $x^2=5\pm\sqrt5$.
    $(y_1-y_2)^2=4$; $(x_1-x_2)^2=10-2\sqrt{20}=10-4\sqrt5$.
    $d^2=14-4\sqrt5$, so $a-b=14+4=18$.

  5. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 2 · Question 4
    Let $S$ denote the locus of the point of intersection of the pair of lines

    $\displaystyle 4x-3y=12\alpha,\qquad 4\alpha x+3\alpha y=12,$
    where $\alpha$ varies over the set of non-zero real numbers. Let $T$ be the tangent to $S$ passing through the points $(p,0)$ and $(0,q)$, $q>0$, and parallel to the line $4x-\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{2}}y=0$.

    Then the value of $pq$ is

    1. $-6\sqrt{2}$
    2. $-3\sqrt{2}$
    3. $-9\sqrt{2}$
    4. $-12\sqrt{2}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    The second line is $4x+3y=\dfrac{12}{\alpha}$. Multiplying with $4x-3y=12\alpha$: $16x^2-9y^2=144$, i.e. $S:\dfrac{x^2}{9}-\dfrac{y^2}{16}=1$.
    Slope of $T$: $m=\dfrac{4\sqrt2}{3}$. Tangent: $y=mx\pm\sqrt{a^2m^2-b^2}=mx\pm\sqrt{9\cdot\dfrac{32}{9}-16}=mx\pm4$.
    $q>0\Rightarrow q=4$, and $p=-\dfrac{4}{m}=-\dfrac{3}{\sqrt2}$.
    $pq=-\dfrac{12}{\sqrt2}=-6\sqrt2$. Answer (A).

  6. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 2 · Question 7
    Let $P(x_1,y_1)$ and $Q(x_2,y_2)$ be two distinct points on the ellipse

    $\displaystyle \dfrac{x^2}{9}+\dfrac{y^2}{4}=1$
    such that $y_1>0$, and $y_2>0$. Let $C$ denote the circle $x^2+y^2=9$, and $M$ be the point $(3,0)$.

    Suppose the line $x=x_1$ intersects $C$ at $R$, and the line $x=x_2$ intersects $C$ at $S$, such that the $y$-coordinates of $R$ and $S$ are positive. Let $\angle ROM=\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\angle SOM=\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, where $O$ denotes the origin $(0,0)$. Let $|XY|$ denote the length of the line segment $XY$.

    Then which of the following statements is (are) TRUE?

    1. The equation of the line joining $P$ and $Q$ is $2x+3y=3(1+\sqrt{3})$
    2. The equation of the line joining $P$ and $Q$ is $2x+y=3(1+\sqrt{3})$
    3. If $N_2=(x_2,0)$, then $3|N_2Q|=2|N_2S|$
    4. If $N_1=(x_1,0)$, then $9|N_1P|=4|N_1R|$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (c)

    $C$ is the auxiliary circle, so $P$ and $Q$ have eccentric angles $\dfrac\pi6$ and $\dfrac\pi3$.
    $R=\left(\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2},\dfrac32\right)$, $P=\left(\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2},1\right)$; $S=\left(\dfrac32,\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}\right)$, $Q=\left(\dfrac32,\sqrt3\right)$.
    Slope $PQ=\dfrac{\sqrt3-1}{\frac32-\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}}=-\dfrac23$; line: $y-1=-\dfrac23\left(x-\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}\right)\Rightarrow2x+3y=3(1+\sqrt3)$ (A true, B false).
    $|N_2Q|=\sqrt3$, $|N_2S|=\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}$: $3\sqrt3=2\cdot\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}$ (C true).
    $|N_1P|=1$, $|N_1R|=\dfrac32$: $9\ne6$ (D false).
    Answer (A), (C).

  7. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 1 · Question 4
    Consider the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{9} + \dfrac{y^2}{4} = 1$. Let $S(p, q)$ be a point in the first quadrant such that $\dfrac{p^2}{9} + \dfrac{q^2}{4} > 1$. Two tangents are drawn from $S$ to the ellipse, of which one meets the ellipse at one end point of the minor axis and the other meets the ellipse at a point $T$ in the fourth quadrant. Let $R$ be the vertex of the ellipse with positive $x$-coordinate and $O$ be the center of the ellipse. If the area of the triangle $\Delta ORT$ is $\dfrac{3}{2}$, then which of the following options is correct?

    1. $q = 2,\ p = 3\sqrt{3}$
    2. $q = 2,\ p = 4\sqrt{3}$
    3. $q = 1,\ p = 5\sqrt{3}$
    4. $q = 1,\ p = 6\sqrt{3}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $S$ is in the first quadrant, so the tangent at an end of the minor axis is the one at $(0, 2)$, namely $y = 2$; hence $q = 2$.
    $R = (3, 0)$, so area $ORT = \dfrac12 \cdot 3 \cdot |y_T| = \dfrac32 \Rightarrow y_T = -1$ (fourth quadrant), and $x_T = 3\sqrt{1 - \dfrac14} = \dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}$.
    Tangent at $T$: $\dfrac{x}{9}\cdot\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2} - \dfrac{y}{4} = 1$, i.e. $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{6}x - \dfrac{y}{4} = 1$.
    It passes through $S(p, 2)$: $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{6}p = \dfrac32 \Rightarrow p = 3\sqrt3$ → (A).

    Let the straight line $y = 2x$ touch a circle with center $(0, \alpha)$, $\alpha > 0$, and radius $r$ at a point $A_1$. Let $B_1$ be the point on the circle such that the line segment $A_1B_1$ is a diameter of the circle. Let $\alpha + r = 5 + \sqrt{5}$.
    Match each entry in List-I to the correct entry in List-II.

    List-IList-II
    (P) $\alpha$ equals(1) $(-2, 4)$
    (Q) $r$ equals(2) $\sqrt{5}$
    (R) $A_1$ equals(3) $(-2, 6)$
    (S) $B_1$ equals(4) $5$
    (5) $(2, 4)$

    1. P→4, Q→2, R→1, S→3
    2. P→2, Q→4, R→1, S→3
    3. P→4, Q→2, R→5, S→3
    4. P→2, Q→4, R→3, S→5
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Tangency: $r$ = distance of $(0, \alpha)$ from $2x - y = 0$ $= \dfrac{\alpha}{\sqrt5}$.

  9. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 2 · Question 7
    Let $A_1,B_1,C_1$ be three points in the $xy$-plane. Suppose that the lines $A_1C_1$ and $B_1C_1$ are tangents to the curve $y^2=8x$ at $A_1$ and $B_1$, respectively. If $O=(0,0)$ and $C_1=(-4,0)$, then which of the following statements is (are) TRUE?

    1. The length of the line segment $OA_1$ is $4\sqrt3$
    2. The length of the line segment $A_1B_1$ is $16$
    3. The orthocenter of the triangle $A_1B_1C_1$ is $(0,0)$
    4. The orthocenter of the triangle $A_1B_1C_1$ is $(1,0)$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (c)

    For $y^2=8x$ ($a=2$), the tangent at $(2t^2,4t)$ is $ty=x+2t^2$. Through $(-4,0)$: $2t^2=4$, $t=\pm\sqrt2$.
    So $A_1=(4,4\sqrt2)$, $B_1=(4,-4\sqrt2)$. $OA_1=\sqrt{16+32}=4\sqrt3$ → (A) true; $A_1B_1=8\sqrt2\ne16$ → (B) false.
    By symmetry the altitude from $C_1$ is the $x$-axis. Slope of $B_1C_1=\dfrac{-4\sqrt2}{8}=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$, so the altitude from $A_1$ has slope $\sqrt2$: $y-4\sqrt2=\sqrt2(x-4)$, which meets $y=0$ at $x=0$.
    Orthocentre $=(0,0)$ → (C) true, (D) false.
    Answer: (A), (C).

  10. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 2 · Question 12
    A normal with slope $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt6}$ is drawn from the point $(0,-\alpha)$ to the parabola $x^2=-4ay$, where $a>0$. Let $L$ be the line passing through $(0,-\alpha)$ and parallel to the directrix of the parabola. Suppose that $L$ intersects the parabola at two points $A$ and $B$. Let $r$ denote the length of the latus rectum and $s$ denote the square of the length of the line segment $AB$. If $r:s=1:16$, then the value of $24a$ is __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 12

    $L$ is $y=-\alpha$; it meets $x^2=-4ay$ at $x=\pm2\sqrt{a\alpha}$, so $s=AB^2=16a\alpha$; $r=4a$.
    $r:s=4a:16a\alpha=1:16\Rightarrow\alpha=4$.
    Point $(2at,-at^2)$: slope of tangent $=-t$, so slope of normal $=\dfrac1t=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\Rightarrow t=\sqrt6$; normal: $y+6a=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\left(x-2\sqrt6\,a\right)$.
    Through $(0,-4)$: $-4+6a=-2a\Rightarrow a=\dfrac12$.
    $24a=12$.

    Let $T_1$ and $T_2$ be two distinct common tangents to the ellipse $E : \dfrac{x^2}{6}+\dfrac{y^2}{3}=1$ and the parabola $P : y^2 = 12x$. Suppose that the tangent $T_1$ touches $P$ and $E$ at the points $A_1$ and $A_2$, respectively and the tangent $T_2$ touches $P$ and $E$ at the points $A_4$ and $A_3$, respectively. Then which of the following statements is(are) true?

    1. The area of the quadrilateral $A_1A_2A_3A_4$ is $35$ square units
    2. The area of the quadrilateral $A_1A_2A_3A_4$ is $36$ square units
    3. The tangents $T_1$ and $T_2$ meet the $x$-axis at the point $(-3,0)$
    4. The tangents $T_1$ and $T_2$ meet the $x$-axis at the point $(-6,0)$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (c)

    A tangent to $y^2=12x$ is $y = mx + \dfrac{3}{m}$. It touches the ellipse if $c^2 = 6m^2 + 3$: $\dfrac{9}{m^2} = 6m^2+3 \Rightarrow 2m^4 + m^2 - 3 = 0 \Rightarrow m^2 = 1$.

    $A_1A_2A_3A_4$ is a trapezium with parallel vertical sides $12$ and $2$ at distance $5$: area $= \dfrac{12+2}{2}\times 5 = 35$. (A) true, (B) false.
    Answer (A), (C).

  12. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 1 · Question 7
    Let $P$ be a point on the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$, where $a > 0$. The normal to the parabola at $P$ meets the $x$-axis at a point $Q$. The area of the triangle $PFQ$, where $F$ is the focus of the parabola, is $120$. If the slope $m$ of the normal and $a$ are both positive integers, then the pair $(a,m)$ is

    1. $(2,3)$
    2. $(1,3)$
    3. $(2,4)$
    4. $(3,4)$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Take $P = (at^2, 2at)$; the normal there has slope $-t$, so $t = -m$.
    Normal: $y - 2at = -t(x - at^2)$; with $y = 0$, $Q = (2a + at^2, 0)$. With $F = (a,0)$, $FQ = a(1+t^2)$.
    Area $= \dfrac12 \cdot a(1+m^2)\cdot 2am = a^2 m(1+m^2) = 120$.
    $m = 3$: $30a^2 = 120 \Rightarrow a = 2$ (other options fail: $(1,3)$ gives $30$, $(2,4)$ gives $272$, $(3,4)$ gives $612$).
    Answer (A).

  13. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 2 · Question 13
    Let $C_1$ be the circle of radius 1 with center at the origin. Let $C_2$ be the circle of radius $r$ with center at the point $A = (4, 1)$, where $1 < r < 3$. Two distinct common tangents $PQ$ and $ST$ of $C_1$ and $C_2$ are drawn. The tangent $PQ$ touches $C_1$ at $P$ and $C_2$ at $Q$. The tangent $ST$ touches $C_1$ at $S$ and $C_2$ at $T$. Mid points of the line segments $PQ$ and $ST$ are joined to form a line which meets the $x$-axis at a point $B$. If $AB = \sqrt{5}$, then the value of $r^2$ is ________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 2

    The midpoint of a common tangent segment has equal tangent lengths to both circles, so it lies on the radical axis.
    Radical axis: $x^2 + y^2 - 1 = (x-4)^2 + (y-1)^2 - r^2 \Rightarrow 8x + 2y = 18 - r^2$.
    It meets the $x$-axis at $B = \left(\dfrac{18 - r^2}{8}, 0\right)$.
    $AB^2 = \left(4 - \dfrac{18 - r^2}{8}\right)^2 + 1 = 5 \Rightarrow \dfrac{14 + r^2}{8} = 2$ (the value $-2$ is impossible).
    $r^2 = 2$.

  17. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 2 · Question 7
    Consider the hyperbola

    $\displaystyle \frac{x^2}{100} - \frac{y^2}{64} = 1$
    with foci at $S$ and $S_1$, where $S$ lies on the positive $x$-axis. Let $P$ be a point on the hyperbola, in the first quadrant. Let $\angle SPS_1 = \alpha$, with $\alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The straight line passing through the point $S$ and having the same slope as that of the tangent at $P$ to the hyperbola, intersects the straight line $S_1P$ at $P_1$. Let $\delta$ be the distance of $P$ from the straight line $SP_1$, and $\beta = S_1P$. Then the greatest integer less than or equal to $\dfrac{\beta\delta}{9}\sin\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$ is ________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 7

    $a = 10$, $b = 8$, $c = \sqrt{164}$. Let $SP = r$; then $\beta - r = 2a = 20$.
    The tangent at $P$ bisects $\angle SPS_1$. The line through $S$ parallel to it meets $S_1P$ (extended) at $P_1$ with $PP_1 = PS = r$ and $\angle SPP_1 = \pi - \alpha$.
    So $\delta$ = distance from the apex of this isosceles triangle to its base $= r\cos\dfrac{\pi-\alpha}{2} = r\sin\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$.
    Required value $= \dfrac{\beta r}{9}\sin^2\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$.
    Cosine rule in $\triangle SPS_1$: $4c^2 = \beta^2 + r^2 - 2\beta r\cos\alpha = (\beta - r)^2 + 4\beta r\sin^2\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$.
    $656 = 400 + 4\beta r\sin^2\dfrac\alpha2 \Rightarrow \beta r\sin^2\dfrac\alpha2 = 64$.
    Value $= \dfrac{64}{9} \approx 7.11$, greatest integer $= 7$.

  18. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 2 · Question 12
    Let $G$ be a circle of radius $R > 0$. Let $G_1, G_2, \ldots, G_n$ be $n$ circles of equal radius $r > 0$. Suppose each of the $n$ circles $G_1, G_2, \ldots, G_n$ touches the circle $G$ externally. Also, for $i = 1, 2, \ldots, n-1$, the circle $G_i$ touches $G_{i+1}$ externally, and $G_n$ touches $G_1$ externally. Then, which of the following statements is/are TRUE ?

    1. If $n = 4$, then $(\sqrt{2} - 1)r < R$
    2. If $n = 5$, then $r < R$
    3. If $n = 8$, then $(\sqrt{2} - 1)r < R$
    4. If $n = 12$, then $\sqrt{2}(\sqrt{3} + 1)r > R$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c), (d)

    The centres of the small circles lie on a circle of radius $R + r$, adjacent centres $2r$ apart: $(R + r)\sin\dfrac{\pi}{n} = r$, i.e. $R = r\left(\dfrac{1}{\sin(\pi/n)} - 1\right)$.
    $n = 4$: $R = (\sqrt2 - 1)r$, so (A) (strict inequality) is false.
    $n = 5$: $R = r(1/\sin36^\circ - 1) \approx 0.70r$, so $r < R$ is false.
    $n = 8$: $R = r(1/\sin22.5^\circ - 1) \approx 1.61r > (\sqrt2 - 1)r$: (C) true.
    $n = 12$: $\dfrac1{\sin15^\circ} = \sqrt2(\sqrt3 + 1)$, so $R = \sqrt2(\sqrt3+1)r - r < \sqrt2(\sqrt3+1)r$: (D) true.
    Answer (C), (D).

  19. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 1
    Consider a triangle $\Delta$ whose two sides lie on the $x$-axis and the line $x + y + 1 = 0$. If the orthocenter of $\Delta$ is $(1, 1)$, then the equation of the circle passing through the vertices of the triangle $\Delta$ is

    1. $x^2 + y^2 - 3x + y = 0$
    2. $x^2 + y^2 + x + 3y = 0$
    3. $x^2 + y^2 + 2y - 1 = 0$
    4. $x^2 + y^2 + x + y = 0$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    The two given sides meet at $A = (-1, 0)$. Let $B = (b, 0)$ lie on the $x$-axis and $C$ on $x + y + 1 = 0$.
    The altitude from $C$ is perpendicular to the $x$-axis and passes through $H(1, 1)$, so $C$ has $x = 1$: $C = (1, -2)$.
    The altitude from $B$ is perpendicular to $x + y + 1 = 0$ (slope $1$) and passes through $H$: it is $y = x$, which meets the $x$-axis at $B = (0, 0)$.
    Circle $x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0$ through $(0,0)$: $F = 0$; through $(-1, 0)$: $1 - D = 0$, $D = 1$; through $(1, -2)$: $5 + 1 - 2E = 0$, $E = 3$.
    So the circle is $x^2 + y^2 + x + 3y = 0$ → (B).

  20. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 9
    Consider the lines $L_1$ and $L_2$ defined by

    $\displaystyle L_1 : x\sqrt2 + y - 1 = 0 \quad\text{and}\quad L_2 : x\sqrt2 - y + 1 = 0$
    For a fixed constant $\lambda$, let $C$ be the locus of a point $P$ such that the product of the distance of $P$ from $L_1$ and the distance of $P$ from $L_2$ is $\lambda^2$. The line $y = 2x + 1$ meets $C$ at two points $R$ and $S$, where the distance between $R$ and $S$ is $\sqrt{270}$.

    Let the perpendicular bisector of $RS$ meet $C$ at two distinct points $R'$ and $S'$. Let $D$ be the square of the distance between $R'$ and $S'$.

    The value of $\lambda^2$ is ___ .

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 8.95 to 9.05

    Product of distances: $\dfrac{|(\sqrt2x + y - 1)(\sqrt2x - y + 1)|}{3} = \lambda^2$, so $C : |2x^2 - (y - 1)^2| = 3\lambda^2$.
    On $y = 2x + 1$: $y - 1 = 2x$, so $|2x^2 - 4x^2| = 2x^2 = 3\lambda^2$, $x = \pm\sqrt{\tfrac32}\,\lambda$.
    $RS^2 = (\Delta x)^2(1 + 2^2) = 5 \cdot 4 \cdot \tfrac32\lambda^2 = 30\lambda^2 = 270$.
    $\lambda^2 = 9$.

  21. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 10
    Consider the lines $L_1$ and $L_2$ defined by

    $\displaystyle L_1 : x\sqrt2 + y - 1 = 0 \quad\text{and}\quad L_2 : x\sqrt2 - y + 1 = 0$
    For a fixed constant $\lambda$, let $C$ be the locus of a point $P$ such that the product of the distance of $P$ from $L_1$ and the distance of $P$ from $L_2$ is $\lambda^2$. The line $y = 2x + 1$ meets $C$ at two points $R$ and $S$, where the distance between $R$ and $S$ is $\sqrt{270}$.

    Let the perpendicular bisector of $RS$ meet $C$ at two distinct points $R'$ and $S'$. Let $D$ be the square of the distance between $R'$ and $S'$.

    The value of $D$ is ___ .

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 77.1 to 77.18

    With $\lambda^2 = 9$, $C$ is the pair of hyperbolas $2x^2 - (y - 1)^2 = \pm 27$; $R, S$ lie on $(y - 1)^2 - 2x^2 = 27$.
    Midpoint of $RS$ is $(0, 1)$; the perpendicular bisector is $y - 1 = -\dfrac{x}{2}$.
    On $(y-1)^2 - 2x^2 = 27$ it gives $-\dfrac{7x^2}{4} = 27$ (no points); on $2x^2 - (y - 1)^2 = 27$ it gives $\dfrac{7x^2}{4} = 27$, $x^2 = \dfrac{108}{7}$.
    $D = (2x)^2\left(1 + \dfrac14\right) = 5x^2 = \dfrac{540}{7} \approx 77.14$.

  22. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 2 · Question 6
    Let $E$ denote the parabola $y^2 = 8x$. Let $P = (-2, 4)$, and let $Q$ and $Q'$ be two distinct points on $E$ such that the lines $PQ$ and $PQ'$ are tangents to $E$. Let $F$ be the focus of $E$. Then which of the following statements is (are) TRUE ?

    1. The triangle $PFQ$ is a right-angled triangle
    2. The triangle $QPQ'$ is a right-angled triangle
    3. The distance between $P$ and $F$ is $5\sqrt2$
    4. $F$ lies on the line joining $Q$ and $Q'$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (b), (d)

    $y^2 = 8x$: $a = 2$, focus $F = (2, 0)$, directrix $x = -2$. So $P$ lies on the directrix.
    Tangents from a point on the directrix are perpendicular → $\angle QPQ' = 90^\circ$ → (B) true.
    The chord of contact from a point on the directrix is a focal chord → $F$ lies on $QQ'$ → (D) true.
    Check: chord of contact of $(-2, 4)$ is $4y = 4(x - 2)$, i.e. $y = x - 2$, through $(2, 0)$; slope of $PF = \dfrac{0 - 4}{2 + 2} = -1$, so $PF \perp QQ'$ and $\angle PFQ = 90^\circ$ → (A) true.
    $PF = \sqrt{16 + 16} = 4\sqrt2 \ne 5\sqrt2$ → (C) false.
    Answer (A), (B), (D).

  23. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 2 · Question 7
    Consider the region $R = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R} : x \ge 0 \text{ and } y^2 \le 4 - x\}$. Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the family of all circles that are contained in $R$ and have centers on the $x$-axis. Let $C$ be the circle that has largest radius among the circles in $\mathcal{F}$. Let $(\alpha, \beta)$ be a point where the circle $C$ meets the curve $y^2 = 4 - x$.

    The radius of the circle $C$ is ___ .

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 1.49 to 1.51

    Let the centre be $(h, 0)$ and radius $\rho$. Staying in $x \ge 0$ needs $\rho \le h$.
    Squared distance from $(h, 0)$ to a point $(4 - u, \pm\sqrt u)$ of the parabola ($u = y^2$): $(4 - h - u)^2 + u$, least at $u = \dfrac72 - h$, value $\dfrac{15}{4} - h$.
    So $\rho^2 \le \dfrac{15}{4} - h$ and $\rho \le h$; the largest $\rho$ has $h^2 = \dfrac{15}{4} - h$, i.e. $h = \dfrac32$, $\rho = \dfrac32$.
    Radius of $C$ $= 1.5$.

  24. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 2 · Question 8
    Consider the region $R = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R} : x \ge 0 \text{ and } y^2 \le 4 - x\}$. Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the family of all circles that are contained in $R$ and have centers on the $x$-axis. Let $C$ be the circle that has largest radius among the circles in $\mathcal{F}$. Let $(\alpha, \beta)$ be a point where the circle $C$ meets the curve $y^2 = 4 - x$.

    The value of $\alpha$ is ___ .

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 1.95 to 2.05

    Let the centre be $(h, 0)$ and radius $\rho$. Staying in $x \ge 0$ needs $\rho \le h$.
    Squared distance from $(h, 0)$ to a point $(4 - u, \pm\sqrt u)$ of the parabola ($u = y^2$): $(4 - h - u)^2 + u$, least at $u = \dfrac72 - h$, value $\dfrac{15}{4} - h$.
    So $\rho^2 \le \dfrac{15}{4} - h$ and $\rho \le h$; the largest $\rho$ has $h^2 = \dfrac{15}{4} - h$, i.e. $h = \dfrac32$, $\rho = \dfrac32$.
    The contact point has $y^2 = u = \dfrac72 - \dfrac32 = 2$, so $\alpha = 4 - y^2 = 2$.

  25. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 2 · Question 18
    Let $E$ be the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{16} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$. For any three distinct points $P, Q$ and $Q'$ on $E$, let $M(P, Q)$ be the mid-point of the line segment joining $P$ and $Q$, and $M(P, Q')$ be the mid-point of the line segment joining $P$ and $Q'$. Then the maximum possible value of the distance between $M(P, Q)$ and $M(P, Q')$, as $P, Q$ and $Q'$ vary on $E$, is ___ .

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 4

    $M(P, Q) - M(P, Q') = \dfrac{Q - Q'}{2}$, so the distance is half the chord $QQ'$.
    The longest chord of the ellipse is the major axis, $2a = 8$ (take $Q, Q' = (\pm4, 0)$ and $P$ any other point).
    Maximum distance $= \dfrac82 = 4$.
