  1. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 1 · Question 5
    Suppose that Box I contains 6 red balls and 9 green balls, and Box II contains 8 red balls and 12 green balls. All the balls of Box I and Box II are mixed together and a ball is chosen at random from them. Let $E_1$ be the event that the ball chosen belonged to Box I and let $E_2$ be the event that the ball chosen belonged to Box II. Let $F_1$ be the event that the ball chosen is red and let $F_2$ be the event that the ball chosen is green.

    Then which of the following statements is (are) TRUE ?

    1. The events $E_1$ and $F_1$ are independent
    2. The events $E_2$ and $F_2$ are dependent
    3. The conditional probability $P(F_1 \mid E_1)$ is equal to the conditional probability $P(F_1 \mid E_2)$
    4. The conditional probability $P(F_1 \mid E_1)$ is greater than the conditional probability $P(F_2 \mid E_2)$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (c)

    Total 35 balls: Box I has 15, Box II has 20; 14 red, 21 green.
    $P(E_1) = \frac{15}{35} = \frac37$, $P(F_1) = \frac{14}{35} = \frac25$, $P(E_1 \cap F_1) = \frac{6}{35} = \frac37\cdot\frac25$: independent, so (A) is true.
    Complements of independent events are independent, so $E_2, F_2$ are independent: (B) false.
    $P(F_1\mid E_1) = \frac{6}{15} = \frac25$, $P(F_1 \mid E_2) = \frac{8}{20} = \frac25$: (C) true.
    $P(F_2 \mid E_2) = \frac{12}{20} = \frac35 > \frac25$: (D) false. Answer (A), (C).

  2. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 11
    A bookshelf contains $6$ distinct books of Mathematics and $5$ distinct books of Physics. From these $11$ books, $6$ books are chosen at random. Let $X$ be the absolute value of the difference between the number of Mathematics books chosen and the number of Physics books chosen. If $\alpha$ is the mean of the random variable $X$ , then the value of $77\alpha$ is ________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 99 to 101

    With $k$ Mathematics books, $X=|2k-6|$ and the count is $\binom6k\binom5{6-k}$, total $\binom{11}6=462$.
    $k=1..6$: counts $6,75,200,150,30,1$ with $X=4,2,0,2,4,6$.
    $\sum X\cdot\text{count}=24+150+0+300+120+6=600$.
    $\alpha=\dfrac{600}{462}$, $77\alpha=\dfrac{600}{6}=100$.

  3. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 1 · Question 2
    Three students $S_1, S_2$, and $S_3$ are given a problem to solve. Consider the following events:
    $U$: At least one of $S_1, S_2$, and $S_3$ can solve the problem,
    $V$: $S_1$ can solve the problem, given that neither $S_2$ nor $S_3$ can solve the problem,
    $W$: $S_2$ can solve the problem and $S_3$ cannot solve the problem,
    $T$: $S_3$ can solve the problem.

    For any event $E$, let $P(E)$ denote the probability of $E$. If

    $\displaystyle P(U) = \dfrac{1}{2}, \quad P(V) = \dfrac{1}{10}, \quad \text{and} \quad P(W) = \dfrac{1}{12},$
    then $P(T)$ is equal to

    1. $\dfrac{13}{36}$
    2. $\dfrac{1}{3}$
    3. $\dfrac{19}{60}$
    4. $\dfrac{1}{4}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Let $p_1, p_2, p_3$ be the (independent) probabilities that $S_1, S_2, S_3$ solve the problem.
    $P(V) = p_1 = \dfrac{1}{10}$ (by independence).
    $P(U) = 1 - (1-p_1)(1-p_2)(1-p_3) = \dfrac12 \Rightarrow (1-p_2)(1-p_3) = \dfrac{1/2}{9/10} = \dfrac59$.
    $P(W) = p_2(1-p_3) = \dfrac{1}{12}$.
    Adding: $(1-p_3) = \dfrac59 + \dfrac1{12} = \dfrac{23}{36}$, so $P(T) = p_3 = \dfrac{13}{36}$ → (A).

  4. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 2 · Question 11
    A factory has a total of three manufacturing units, $M_1$, $M_2$, and $M_3$, which produce bulbs independent of each other. The units $M_1$, $M_2$, and $M_3$ produce bulbs in the proportions of $2:2:1$, respectively. It is known that $20\%$ of the bulbs produced in the factory are defective. It is also known that, of all the bulbs produced by $M_1$, $15\%$ are defective. Suppose that, if a randomly chosen bulb produced in the factory is found to be defective, the probability that it was produced by $M_2$ is $\dfrac{2}{5}$.

    If a bulb is chosen randomly from the bulbs produced by $M_3$, then the probability that it is defective is ________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 0.27 to 0.33

    $P(M_1)=P(M_2)=0.4$, $P(M_3)=0.2$, $P(D)=0.2$.
    $P(D\cap M_1)=0.4\times0.15=0.06$; $P(D\cap M_2)=P(M_2\mid D)P(D)=\dfrac25\times0.2=0.08$.
    $P(D\cap M_3)=0.2-0.06-0.08=0.06$.
    $P(D\mid M_3)=\dfrac{0.06}{0.2}=0.3$.

  5. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 1 · Question 2
    A student appears for a quiz consisting of only true-false type questions and answers all the questions. The student knows the answers of some questions and guesses the answers for the remaining questions. Whenever the student knows the answer of a question, he gives the correct answer. Assume that the probability of the student giving the correct answer for a question, given that he has guessed it, is $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Also assume that the probability of the answer for a question being guessed, given that the student's answer is correct, is $\dfrac{1}{6}$. Then the probability that the student knows the answer of a randomly chosen question is

    1. $\dfrac{1}{12}$
    2. $\dfrac{1}{7}$
    3. $\dfrac{5}{7}$
    4. $\dfrac{5}{12}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Let $p$ = P(knows). Then P(guesses) $= 1-p$.
    P(correct) $= p\cdot1 + (1-p)\cdot\dfrac12 = \dfrac{1+p}{2}$.
    P(guessed $\mid$ correct) $= \dfrac{(1-p)/2}{(1+p)/2} = \dfrac{1-p}{1+p} = \dfrac16$.
    $6 - 6p = 1 + p \Rightarrow p = \dfrac57$ → (C).

  6. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 1 · Question 13
    Let $X$ be a random variable, and let $P(X = x)$ denote the probability that $X$ takes the value $x$. Suppose that the points $(x, P(X = x))$, $x = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$, lie on a fixed straight line in the $xy$-plane, and $P(X = x) = 0$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R} - \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$. If the mean of $X$ is $\dfrac{5}{2}$, and the variance of $X$ is $\alpha$, then the value of $24\alpha$ is ______.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 42

    Let $P(X = x) = mx + k$ for $x = 0, \ldots, 4$.
    Total probability: $10m + 5k = 1$. Mean: $\sum x(mx + k) = 30m + 10k = \dfrac52$.
    From the first, $10k = 2 - 20m$, so $10m + 2 = \dfrac52 \Rightarrow m = \dfrac{1}{20}$, $k = \dfrac{1}{10}$.
    $E(X^2) = m\sum x^3 + k\sum x^2 = \dfrac{100}{20} + \dfrac{30}{10} = 8$.
    $\alpha = 8 - \dfrac{25}{4} = \dfrac74$, so $24\alpha = 42$.

  7. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 2 · Question 9
    A bag contains $N$ balls out of which $3$ balls are white, $6$ balls are green, and the remaining balls are blue. Assume that the balls are identical otherwise. Three balls are drawn randomly one after the other without replacement. For $i=1,2,3$, let $W_i, G_i$, and $B_i$ denote the events that the ball drawn in the $i^{\text{th}}$ draw is a white ball, green ball, and blue ball, respectively. If the probability $P(W_1\cap G_2\cap B_3)=\dfrac{2}{5N}$ and the conditional probability $P(B_3\mid W_1\cap G_2)=\dfrac29$, then $N$ equals ______.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 11

    $P(B_3\mid W_1\cap G_2)=\dfrac{N-9}{N-2}=\dfrac29\Rightarrow 9N-81=2N-4\Rightarrow N=11$.
    Check: $P(W_1\cap G_2\cap B_3)=\dfrac{3}{11}\cdot\dfrac{6}{10}\cdot\dfrac{2}{9}=\dfrac{2}{55}=\dfrac{2}{5N}$ ✓.
    $N=11$.

  8. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 2 · Question 2
    Consider an experiment of tossing a coin repeatedly until the outcomes of two consecutive tosses are same. If the probability of a random toss resulting in head is $\dfrac{1}{3}$, then the probability that the experiment stops with head is

    1. $\dfrac{1}{3}$
    2. $\dfrac{5}{21}$
    3. $\dfrac{4}{21}$
    4. $\dfrac{2}{7}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Let $x_H$ ($x_T$) be the probability of finishing with HH given that the last toss was H (T), with $P(H) = \dfrac13$, $P(T) = \dfrac23$.
    $x_H = \dfrac13 + \dfrac23 x_T$ and $x_T = \dfrac13 x_H$.
    So $x_H = \dfrac13 + \dfrac29 x_H \Rightarrow x_H = \dfrac37$, $x_T = \dfrac17$.
    Required probability $= \dfrac13\cdot\dfrac37 + \dfrac23\cdot\dfrac17 = \dfrac17 + \dfrac{2}{21} = \dfrac{5}{21}$ → (B).

  9. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 2 · Question 10
    Let $X$ be the set of all five digit numbers formed using 1, 2, 2, 2, 4, 4, 0. For example, 22240 is in $X$ while 02244 and 44422 are not in $X$. Suppose that each element of $X$ has an equal chance of being chosen. Let $p$ be the conditional probability that an element chosen at random is a multiple of 20 given that it is a multiple of 5. Then the value of $38p$ is equal to ________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 31

    A multiple of 5 must end in 0; the first four digits come from $\{1, 2, 2, 2, 4, 4\}$.
    Count 4-digit arrangements by (number of 1s, 2s, 4s): without 1: $(3,1)$: 4, $(2,2)$: 6; with 1: $(3,0)$: 4, $(2,1)$: 12, $(1,2)$: 12. Total $= 38$.
    Multiple of 20 ⇔ ends in 0 and the tens digit is even, i.e. the fourth digit is not 1.
    Arrangements with 1 in the fourth place: the first three from $\{2,2,2,4,4\}$: $1 + 3 + 3 = 7$.
    $p = \dfrac{38 - 7}{38} = \dfrac{31}{38}$, so $38p = 31$.

  10. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 2 · Question 16
    Consider the $6 \times 6$ square in the figure. Let $A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_{49}$ be the points of intersections (dots in the picture) in some order. We say that $A_i$ and $A_j$ are friends if they are adjacent along a row or along a column. Assume that each point $A_i$ has an equal chance of being chosen.

    Let $p_i$ be the probability that a randomly chosen point has $i$ many friends, $i = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$. Let $X$ be a random variable such that for $i = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$, the probability $P(X = i) = p_i$. Then the value of $7E(X)$ is ________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 23.99 to 24.01

    The 49 points form a $7 \times 7$ lattice.
    4 corners have 2 friends, $4 \times 5 = 20$ other boundary points have 3, and $5 \times 5 = 25$ interior points have 4 (none has 0 or 1).
    $E(X) = \dfrac{4\cdot2 + 20\cdot3 + 25\cdot4}{49} = \dfrac{168}{49} = \dfrac{24}{7}$.
    $7E(X) = 24$.

  11. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 2 · Question 17
    Consider the $6 \times 6$ square in the figure. Let $A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_{49}$ be the points of intersections (dots in the picture) in some order. We say that $A_i$ and $A_j$ are friends if they are adjacent along a row or along a column. Assume that each point $A_i$ has an equal chance of being chosen.

    Two distinct points are chosen randomly out of the points $A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_{49}$. Let $p$ be the probability that they are friends. Then the value of $7p$ is ________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 0.49 to 0.51

    Friend pairs are the unit segments of the grid: $7$ rows $\times\ 6$ + $7$ columns $\times\ 6$ $= 84$.
    Total pairs: $\binom{49}{2} = 1176$.
    $p = \dfrac{84}{1176} = \dfrac{1}{14}$, so $7p = 0.5$.

  12. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 1 · Question 16
    Two players, $P_1$ and $P_2$, play a game against each other. In every round of the game, each player rolls a fair die once, where the six faces of the die have six distinct numbers. Let $x$ and $y$ denote the readings on the die rolled by $P_1$ and $P_2$, respectively. If $x>y$, then $P_1$ scores 5 points and $P_2$ scores 0 point. If $x=y$, then each player scores 2 points. If $x<y$, then $P_1$ scores 0 point and $P_2$ scores 5 points. Let $X_i$ and $Y_i$ be the total scores of $P_1$ and $P_2$, respectively, after playing the $i^{th}$ round.

    List-IList-II
    (I) Probability of $(X_2\ge Y_2)$ is(P) $\frac{3}{8}$
    (II) Probability of $(X_2>Y_2)$ is(Q) $\frac{11}{16}$
    (III) Probability of $(X_3=Y_3)$ is(R) $\frac{5}{16}$
    (IV) Probability of $(X_3>Y_3)$ is(S) $\frac{355}{864}$
    (T) $\frac{77}{432}$
    The correct option is:

    1. (I) → (Q); (II) → (R); (III) → (T); (IV) → (S)
    2. (I) → (Q); (II) → (R); (III) → (T); (IV) → (T)
    3. (I) → (P); (II) → (R); (III) → (Q); (IV) → (S)
    4. (I) → (P); (II) → (R); (III) → (Q); (IV) → (T)
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Each round: $P_1$ wins with probability $\frac{15}{36}=\frac5{12}$, loses with $\frac5{12}$, ties with $\frac16$. A tie adds the same to both, so the comparison depends only on wins and losses.
    (II) $X_2>Y_2$: (win, win), or one win and one tie: $\left(\frac5{12}\right)^2+2\cdot\frac5{12}\cdot\frac16=\frac{25+20}{144}=\frac5{16}$ (R).
    (I) By symmetry $P(X_2<Y_2)=\frac5{16}$, so $P(X_2\ge Y_2)=\frac{11}{16}$ (Q).
    (III) $X_3=Y_3$: three ties, or one win, one loss, one tie: $\frac1{216}+6\cdot\frac{25}{144}\cdot\frac16=\frac{4+150}{864}=\frac{77}{432}$ (T).
    (IV) $P(X_3>Y_3)=\frac12\left(1-\frac{77}{432}\right)=\frac{355}{864}$ (S).
    Answer (A).

  13. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 2 · Question 17
    Suppose that
    Box-I contains 8 red, 3 blue and 5 green balls,
    Box-II contains 24 red, 9 blue and 15 green balls,
    Box-III contains 1 blue, 12 green and 3 yellow balls,
    Box-IV contains 10 green, 16 orange and 6 white balls.
    A ball is chosen randomly from Box-I; call this ball $b$. If $b$ is red then a ball is chosen randomly from Box-II, if $b$ is blue then a ball is chosen randomly from Box-III, and if $b$ is green then a ball is chosen randomly from Box-IV. The conditional probability of the event ‘one of the chosen balls is white’ given that the event ‘at least one of the chosen balls is green’ has happened, is equal to

    1. $\dfrac{15}{256}$
    2. $\dfrac{3}{16}$
    3. $\dfrac{5}{52}$
    4. $\dfrac{1}{8}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $P(\text{at least one green}) = \dfrac5{16} + \dfrac8{16}\cdot\dfrac{15}{48} + \dfrac3{16}\cdot\dfrac{12}{16} = \dfrac{20}{64} + \dfrac{10}{64} + \dfrac9{64} = \dfrac{39}{64}$.
    White is possible only from Box-IV, i.e. when $b$ is green (so the green condition holds): $P(\text{white} \cap \text{green}) = \dfrac5{16}\cdot\dfrac6{32} = \dfrac{15}{256}$.
    Required probability $= \dfrac{15/256}{39/64} = \dfrac{15}{156} = \dfrac5{52}$ → (C).

  14. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 3
    Consider three sets $E_1 = \{1, 2, 3\}$, $F_1 = \{1, 3, 4\}$ and $G_1 = \{2, 3, 4, 5\}$. Two elements are chosen at random, without replacement, from the set $E_1$, and let $S_1$ denote the set of these chosen elements. Let $E_2 = E_1 - S_1$ and $F_2 = F_1 \cup S_1$. Now two elements are chosen at random, without replacement, from the set $F_2$ and let $S_2$ denote the set of these chosen elements.

    Let $G_2 = G_1 \cup S_2$. Finally, two elements are chosen at random, without replacement, from the set $G_2$ and let $S_3$ denote the set of these chosen elements.
    Let $E_3 = E_2 \cup S_3$. Given that $E_1 = E_3$, let $p$ be the conditional probability of the event $S_1 = \{1, 2\}$. Then the value of $p$ is

    1. $\dfrac{1}{5}$
    2. $\dfrac{3}{5}$
    3. $\dfrac{1}{2}$
    4. $\dfrac{2}{5}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $E_2$ has one element, so $E_3 = E_1$ forces $S_3 = S_1$.
    $S_1 = \{1,2\}$ (prob. $\frac13$): $F_2 = \{1,2,3,4\}$; $S_3 = \{1,2\}$ needs $1 \in G_2$, i.e. $1 \in S_2$ (prob. $\frac36$), then $|G_2| = 5$ and $P(S_3 = \{1,2\}) = \frac1{10}$. Probability $= \frac13\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac1{10} = \frac1{60}$.
    $S_1 = \{1,3\}$: $F_2 = \{1,3,4\}$; need $1 \in S_2$ (prob. $\frac23$), then $\frac1{10}$. Probability $= \frac13\cdot\frac23\cdot\frac1{10} = \frac1{45}$.
    $S_1 = \{2,3\}$: $F_2 = \{1,2,3,4\}$; if $1 \in S_2$ (prob. $\frac12$), $|G_2| = 5$, chance $\frac1{10}$; otherwise $G_2 = \{2,3,4,5\}$, chance $\frac16$. Probability $= \frac13\left(\frac1{20} + \frac1{12}\right) = \frac2{45}$.
    $P(E_3 = E_1) = \frac1{60} + \frac1{45} + \frac2{45} = \frac1{12}$, so $p = \dfrac{1/60}{1/12} = \dfrac15$ → (A).

  15. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 5
    Three numbers are chosen at random, one after another with replacement, from the set $S = \{1, 2, 3, \ldots, 100\}$. Let $p_1$ be the probability that the maximum of chosen numbers is at least 81 and $p_2$ be the probability that the minimum of chosen numbers is at most 40.

    The value of $\dfrac{625}{4}p_1$ is ___ .

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 76.1 to 76.4

    Maximum at least 81 is the complement of all three numbers $\le 80$: $p_1 = 1 - (0.8)^3 = 0.488$.
    $\dfrac{625}{4}p_1 = 156.25 \times 0.488 = 76.25$.

  16. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 6
    Three numbers are chosen at random, one after another with replacement, from the set $S = \{1, 2, 3, \ldots, 100\}$. Let $p_1$ be the probability that the maximum of chosen numbers is at least 81 and $p_2$ be the probability that the minimum of chosen numbers is at most 40.

    The value of $\dfrac{125}{4}p_2$ is ___ .

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 24.4 to 24.6

    Minimum at most 40 is the complement of all three numbers $\ge 41$: $p_2 = 1 - (0.6)^3 = 0.784$.
    $\dfrac{125}{4}p_2 = 31.25 \times 0.784 = 24.5$.

  17. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 13
    Let $E, F$ and $G$ be three events having probabilities

    $\displaystyle P(E) = \dfrac18,\ P(F) = \dfrac16 \text{ and } P(G) = \dfrac14, \text{ and let } P(E \cap F \cap G) = \dfrac{1}{10}.$
    For any event $H$, if $H^c$ denotes its complement, then which of the following statements is (are) TRUE ?

    1. $P(E \cap F \cap G^c) \le \dfrac{1}{40}$
    2. $P(E^c \cap F \cap G) \le \dfrac{1}{15}$
    3. $P(E \cup F \cup G) \le \dfrac{13}{24}$
    4. $P(E^c \cap F^c \cap G^c) \le \dfrac{5}{12}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (b), (c)

    (A) $P(E \cap F \cap G^c) = P(E \cap F) - P(E \cap F \cap G) \le P(E) - \dfrac1{10} = \dfrac18 - \dfrac1{10} = \dfrac1{40}$: true.
    (B) $P(E^c \cap F \cap G) = P(F \cap G) - P(E \cap F \cap G) \le \dfrac16 - \dfrac1{10} = \dfrac1{15}$: true.
    (C) $P(E \cup F \cup G) \le P(E) + P(F) + P(G) = \dfrac{3 + 4 + 6}{24} = \dfrac{13}{24}$: true.
    (D) $P(E^c \cap F^c \cap G^c) = 1 - P(E \cup F \cup G) \ge 1 - \dfrac{13}{24} = \dfrac{11}{24} > \dfrac5{12}$: false.
    Answer (A), (B), (C).
