  1. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 19
    A metal wire of cross-sectional area $0.5\ \mathrm{mm^2}$ and length $100\ \mathrm{m}$ is connected across a battery of e.m.f. $2\ \mathrm{V}$ and internal resistance $1\ \Omega$. The density, atomic mass and electrical conductivity of the metal are $6.35 \times 10^3\ \mathrm{kg\ m^{-3}}$, $63.5$ gm/mole and $2 \times 10^8\ \mathrm{mho\ m^{-1}}$, respectively. Assuming one conduction electron per atom of the metal, the drift velocity (in $\mathrm{mm\ s^{-1}}$) of the electrons in the wire is:
    [Take Avogadro’s number as $6 \times 10^{23}$ and charge of the electron as $1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ C.]

    1. $0.052$
    2. $0.104$
    3. $0.208$
    4. $0.156$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Wire resistance $R = \dfrac{L}{\sigma A} = \dfrac{100}{(2\times10^{8})(0.5\times10^{-6})} = 1\ \Omega$.
    Current $I = \dfrac{2}{1+1} = 1\ \mathrm{A}$.
    Electron density $n = \dfrac{\rho N_A}{M} = \dfrac{6.35\times10^{3}}{0.0635}\times 6\times10^{23} = 6\times10^{28}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$.
    $v_d = \dfrac{I}{neA} = \dfrac{1}{(6\times10^{28})(1.6\times10^{-19})(0.5\times10^{-6})} = 2.08\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m/s} = 0.208\ \mathrm{mm/s}$.

  2. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 31
    As shown in the figure, the resistance of a galvanometer $G$ can be found by the half-deflection method. Here the resistance $R_2$ is adjusted such that when the key $K$ is closed the deflection in the galvanometer becomes half of the value as compared to when $K$ is open. Half-deflection is obtained at $R_2 = 4\ \Omega$ and thus the galvanometer resistance is found to be $6\ \Omega$. In this half-deflection condition the current (in mA) through the resistor $R_1$ is:

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 690 to 710

    $K$ open: $I_G = \dfrac{10}{R_1+6}$.
    $K$ closed: $G\parallel R_2 = 2.4\ \Omega$, total $I = \dfrac{10}{R_1+2.4}$, $I_G = \dfrac{4}{10}I$.
    Half deflection: $\dfrac{0.4\cdot10}{R_1+2.4} = \dfrac{0.5\cdot 10}{R_1+6} \Rightarrow R_1 = 12\ \Omega$.
    Current through $R_1$ $= \dfrac{10}{14.4} = 0.694$ A $\approx 694$ mA.

  3. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 1 · Question 20
    In a circuit shown in the figure, the capacitor $C$ is initially uncharged and the key $K$ is open. In this condition, a current of $1$ A flows through the $1\,\Omega$ resistor. The key is closed at time $t = t_0$. Which of the following statement(s) is(are) correct?
    [Given: $e^{-1} = 0.36$]

    1. The value of the resistance $R$ is $3\,\Omega$.
    2. For $t < t_0$, the value of current $I_1$ is $2$ A.
    3. At $t = t_0 + 7.2\ \mu$s, the current in the capacitor is $0.6$ A.
    4. For $t \to \infty$, the charge on the capacitor is $12\ \mu$C.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (b), (c), (d)

    Let $V$ be the potential difference between the two vertical rails. In the $5$ V branch $|V - 5| = 1$; the $15$ V cell drives current back through the $5$ V cell, so $V = 6$ V.
    Then $I_1 = \dfrac{6}{3} = 2$ A (B true), and the $15$ V branch carries $I_1 + 1 = 3$ A: $\dfrac{15 - 6}{R} = 3 \Rightarrow R = 3\,\Omega$ (A true).
    Seen from the capacitor branch, the rest is a source of $6$ V with internal resistance $3 \parallel 1 \parallel 3 = \dfrac37\,\Omega$.
    Total resistance $3 + \dfrac37 = \dfrac{24}{7}\,\Omega$, $\tau = \dfrac{24}{7}\times2\ \mu\text{s} \approx 6.9\ \mu$s, initial current $\dfrac{6}{24/7} = 1.75$ A.
    At $7.2\ \mu$s ($\approx\tau$): $i \approx 1.75\,e^{-1.05} \approx 0.6$ A (C true). Finally $Q = CV = 2\times6 = 12\ \mu$C (D true).
    Answer (A), (B), (C), (D).

  4. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 1 · Question 28
    The figure shows a circuit having eight resistances of $1\ \Omega$ each, labelled $R_1$ to $R_8$, and two ideal batteries with voltages $\mathcal{E}_1=12$ V and $\mathcal{E}_2=6$ V.
    Which of the following statement(s) is(are) correct?

    1. The magnitude of current flowing through $R_1$ is $7.2$ A.
    2. The magnitude of current flowing through $R_2$ is $1.2$ A.
    3. The magnitude of current flowing through $R_3$ is $4.8$ A.
    4. The magnitude of current flowing through $R_5$ is $2.4$ A.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (b), (c), (d)

    Label the corners Top, Left, Right, Bottom and the centre O. The circuit is symmetric about the horizontal line, so Top and Bottom are at the same potential; take it as 0.
    Let $I_3$ flow Left → O through $\mathcal E_2,R_3$ and $I_1$ flow O → Right through $\mathcal E_1,R_1$ (both batteries drive current to the right).
    Node Left: $I_3=2(0-V_L)$; node Right: $I_1=2V_R$; node O: $I_3+2(0-V_O)=I_1$.
    Branches: $V_O=V_L+6-I_3$, $V_R=V_O+12-I_1$.
    Solving: $I_1=12-I_3$ and $5I_3=24$, so $I_3=4.8$ A, $I_1=7.2$ A, $V_O=-1.2$ V, $V_L=-2.4$ V.
    $I_{R_2}=I_{R_4}=1.2$ A and $I_{R_5}=I_{R_6}=2.4$ A. All four statements are correct.

  5. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 2 · Question 21
    Two resistances $R_1 = X\ \Omega$ and $R_2 = 1\ \Omega$ are connected to a wire $AB$ of uniform resistivity, as shown in the figure. The radius of the wire varies linearly along its axis from $0.2$ mm at $A$ to $1$ mm at $B$. A galvanometer (G) connected to the center of the wire, $50$ cm from each end along its axis, shows zero deflection when $A$ and $B$ are connected to a battery. The value of $X$ is ________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 5

    For radius varying linearly, $r = r_1 + \dfrac{(r_2 - r_1)x}{L}$: $\displaystyle R = \int\frac{\rho\,dx}{\pi r^2} = \frac{\rho L}{\pi(r_2 - r_1)}\left(\frac1{r_1} - \frac1{r_2}\right)$, so $R \propto \dfrac1{r_\text{start}} - \dfrac1{r_\text{end}}$ for equal slope.
    The middle has radius $0.6$ mm. $R_{AM} \propto \dfrac1{0.2} - \dfrac1{0.6} = \dfrac{10}3$; $R_{MB} \propto \dfrac1{0.6} - \dfrac11 = \dfrac23$.
    Balanced bridge: $\dfrac{R_1}{R_2} = \dfrac{R_{AM}}{R_{MB}} = 5$, so $X = 5$.

  6. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 2 · Question 28
    In Circuit-1 and Circuit-2 shown in the figures, $R_1 = 1\ \Omega$, $R_2 = 2\ \Omega$ and $R_3 = 3\ \Omega$.
    $P_1$ and $P_2$ are the power dissipations in Circuit-1 and Circuit-2 when the switches S$_1$ and S$_2$ are in open conditions, respectively.
    $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ are the power dissipations in Circuit-1 and Circuit-2 when the switches S$_1$ and S$_2$ are in closed conditions, respectively.
    Which of the following statement(s) is(are) correct?

    1. When a voltage source of $6$ V is connected across A and B in both circuits, $P_1 < P_2$.
    2. When a constant current source of $2$ Amp is connected across A and B in both circuits, $P_1 > P_2$.
    3. When a voltage source of $6$ V is connected across A and B in Circuit-1, $Q_1 > P_1$.
    4. When a constant current source of $2$ Amp is connected across A and B in both circuits, $Q_2 < Q_1$.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (b), (c)

    Circuit-1: $R_1$ in series with $(R_2 + R_3) \parallel \dfrac{R_1}{2} = 5 \parallel 0.5 = \dfrac5{11}\ \Omega$. Open: $\dfrac{16}{11}\ \Omega$; closed (S$_1$ shorts $R_1$): $\dfrac5{11}\ \Omega$.
    Circuit-2: $1 \parallel 2 \parallel 3 = \dfrac6{11}\ \Omega$ open; closed, add $2R_3 = 6\ \Omega$ in parallel: $\dfrac12\ \Omega$.
    (A) 6 V: $P = V^2/R$, smaller $R$ dissipates more; $\dfrac{16}{11} > \dfrac6{11}$, so $P_1 < P_2$: true.
    (B) 2 A: $P = I^2R$, so $P_1 > P_2$: true.
    (C) 6 V on Circuit-1: $\dfrac5{11} < \dfrac{16}{11}$, so $Q_1 > P_1$: true.
    (D) 2 A: $Q_2 = 4\cdot\dfrac12 = 2$ W, $Q_1 = 4\cdot\dfrac5{11} \approx 1.82$ W, so $Q_2 > Q_1$: false.
    Answer (A), (B), (C).

  7. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 26
    In the circuit shown below, the switch S is connected to position P for a long time so that the charge on the capacitor becomes $q_1$ $\mu$C. Then S is switched to position Q. After a long time, the charge on the capacitor is $q_2$ $\mu$C.

    The magnitude of $q_1$ is ___ .

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 1.3 to 1.4

    In the steady state no current flows into the capacitor. With S at P, the $1$ V and $2$ V cells (positive terminals upward) oppose each other in the loop through $1\ \Omega$ and $2\ \Omega$.
    Current $I = \dfrac{2 - 1}{1 + 2} = \dfrac13$ A, flowing from the $2$ V cell towards the $1$ V cell.
    Capacitor voltage $= 2 - 2 \times \dfrac13 = \dfrac43$ V (equivalently $1 + 1 \times \dfrac13$).
    $q_1 = 1\ \mu\text{F} \times \dfrac43\text{ V} \approx 1.33\ \mu$C.

  8. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 27
    In the circuit shown below, the switch S is connected to position P for a long time so that the charge on the capacitor becomes $q_1$ $\mu$C. Then S is switched to position Q. After a long time, the charge on the capacitor is $q_2$ $\mu$C.

    The magnitude of $q_2$ is ___ .

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 0.6 to 0.72

    With S at Q, the left end of the $1\ \Omega$ resistor is joined directly to the bottom wire (potential $0$).
    Steady current $I = \dfrac{2}{1 + 2} = \dfrac23$ A through $1\ \Omega$ and $2\ \Omega$.
    Capacitor voltage $= 1 \times \dfrac23 = \dfrac23$ V.
    $q_2 = 1\ \mu\text{F} \times \dfrac23\text{ V} \approx 0.67\ \mu$C.

  9. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 2 · Question 36
    In order to measure the internal resistance $r_1$ of a cell of emf $E$, a meter bridge of wire resistance $R_0 = 50\ \Omega$, a resistance $R_0/2$, another cell of emf $E/2$ (internal resistance $r$) and a galvanometer G are used in a circuit, as shown in the figure. If the null point is found at $l = 72$ cm, then the value of $r_1 =$ ___ $\Omega$.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 3

    At null no current flows in the galvanometer branch, so the main current is $I = \dfrac{E}{r_1 + 25 + 50}$.
    The $E/2$ cell balances the p.d. between the junction and the null point: across $R_0/2$ plus the remaining $28$ cm of wire, $25 + 0.28 \times 50 = 39\ \Omega$.
    $\dfrac{E}{2} = 39I = \dfrac{39E}{r_1 + 75} \Rightarrow r_1 + 75 = 78 \Rightarrow r_1 = 3\ \Omega$.
