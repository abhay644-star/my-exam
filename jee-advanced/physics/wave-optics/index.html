  1. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 30
    As shown in the figure, a ray $AB$ of unpolarized light enters from water of refractive index $n_w = 4/3$ into a medium of refractive index $n_p = 4/\sqrt{3}$ after passing through a glass plate of refractive index $n_g = 1.5$ and a layer of water. At a particular incident angle $i$ the reflected ray $CD$ is polarized in the direction as shown in the figure. The value of $i$ (in degrees) is:

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 60

    The reflected ray is plane polarized, so reflection at the water–$n_p$ interface occurs at Brewster's angle.
    $\tan\theta_B = \dfrac{n_p}{n_w} = \dfrac{4/\sqrt3}{4/3} = \sqrt3 \Rightarrow \theta_B = 60^\circ$ in the upper water layer.
    Across parallel layers $n\sin\theta$ is constant: $n_w\sin i = n_w\sin 60^\circ$.
    Hence $i = 60^\circ$.

  2. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 1 · Question 27
    A solid glass sphere of refractive index $n = \sqrt{3}$ and radius $R$ contains a spherical air cavity of radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$, as shown in the figure. A very thin glass layer is present at the point O so that the air cavity (refractive index $n = 1$) remains inside the glass sphere. An unpolarized, unidirectional and monochromatic light source $S$ emits a light ray from a point inside the glass sphere towards the periphery of the glass sphere. If the light is reflected from the point O and is fully polarized, then the angle of incidence at the inner surface of the glass sphere is $\theta$. The value of $\sin\theta$ is ______

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 0.5 or 0.75

    The ray reflects at the outer surface (angle $\theta$) and then travels towards O. Reflected light is fully polarized when it meets O at Brewster's angle.
    Taking the cavity into account: just before O the ray is in the air of the cavity, so at O (air → glass) $\tan\theta_B = \sqrt3$, $\theta_B = 60^\circ$.
    Put O at the origin, normal along $+y$; cavity centre $(0, R/2)$, sphere centre $(0, R)$. The chord of the cavity ending at O at $60^\circ$ starts at $B = \left(\dfrac{\sqrt3R}{4}, \dfrac R4\right)$, where the ray also meets the cavity at $60^\circ$.
    At B (glass → air): $\sqrt3\sin\beta = \sin60^\circ \Rightarrow \beta = 30^\circ$; working out the direction, the ray in the glass was travelling horizontally, along $y = \dfrac R4$.
    This line is at distance $R - \dfrac R4 = \dfrac{3R}{4}$ from the sphere centre, so at the outer surface $\sin\theta = \dfrac34 = 0.75$.
    If the cavity is ignored and Brewster's angle is taken for glass → air at O ($\tan\theta_B = 1/\sqrt3$, $\theta_B = 30^\circ$), the chord in the sphere gives $\theta = 30^\circ$, $\sin\theta = 0.5$.
    The official final key accepts both: $0.5$ or $0.75$.

    Note: Official final key: 0.5 or 0.75 (either value is awarded marks).

  3. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 2 · Question 30
    In a Young's double slit experiment, a combination of two glass wedges $A$ and $B$, having refractive indices 1.7 and 1.5, respectively, are placed in front of the slits, as shown in the figure. The separation between the slits is $d=2$ mm and the shortest distance between the slits and the screen is $D=2$ m. Thickness of the combination of the wedges is $t=12\ \mu$m. The value of $l$ as shown in the figure is 1 mm. Neglect any refraction effect at the slanted interface of the wedges. Due to the combination of the wedges, the central maximum shifts (in mm) with respect to O by ________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 1.2

    The combination has height $d+2l=4$ mm; the thickness of $A$ falls linearly from $t$ at the top to $0$ at the bottom, and $B$ makes up the rest.
    At the upper slit (1 mm from the top): $A=\dfrac34t$, $B=\dfrac14t$. At the lower slit: $A=\dfrac14t$, $B=\dfrac34t$.
    Extra optical paths: upper $=0.7\cdot\dfrac34t+0.5\cdot\dfrac14t=0.65t$; lower $=0.7\cdot\dfrac14t+0.5\cdot\dfrac34t=0.55t$.
    Path difference $=0.1t=1.2\ \mu$m.
    Shift $=\dfrac{\Delta D}{d}=\dfrac{1.2\times10^{-6}\times2}{2\times10^{-3}}=1.2\times10^{-3}$ m $=1.2$ mm (towards the upper slit).

  4. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 1 · Question 27
    A point source S emits unpolarized light uniformly in all directions. At two points A and B, the ratio $r = I_A/I_B$ of the intensities of light is 2. If a set of two polaroids having $45^\circ$ angle between their pass-axes is placed just before point B, then the new value of $r$ will be _____.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 8

    First polaroid: unpolarized light is halved, $I_B/2$.
    Second polaroid (Malus): $\dfrac{I_B}{2}\cos^2 45^\circ = \dfrac{I_B}{4}$.
    New $r = \dfrac{I_A}{I_B/4} = 4 \times 2 = 8$.

  5. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 2 · Question 31
    In a Young’s double slit experiment, each of the two slits A and B, as shown in the figure, are oscillating about their fixed center and with a mean separation of $0.8$ mm. The distance between the slits at time $t$ is given by $d=(0.8+0.04\sin\omega t)$ mm, where $\omega=0.08\ \text{rad s}^{-1}$. The distance of the screen from the slits is $1$ m and the wavelength of the light used to illuminate the slits is $6000$ Å. The interference pattern on the screen changes with time, while the central bright fringe (zeroth fringe) remains fixed at point O.

    The $8^{\text{th}}$ bright fringe above the point O oscillates with time between two extreme positions. The separation between these two extreme positions, in micrometer ($\mu$m), is ______.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 598 to 602

    $y_8=\dfrac{8\lambda D}{d}=\dfrac{8(6\times10^{-7})(1)}{d}=\dfrac{4.8\times10^{-6}}{d}$ m.
    $d$ varies between $0.76$ mm and $0.84$ mm.
    $y_{\max}=\dfrac{4.8\times10^{-6}}{0.76\times10^{-3}}=6.3158$ mm, $y_{\min}=\dfrac{4.8\times10^{-6}}{0.84\times10^{-3}}=5.7143$ mm.
    Separation $=0.6015$ mm $\approx601.5\ \mu$m (within $598$ to $602$).

  6. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 2 · Question 32
    In a Young’s double slit experiment, each of the two slits A and B, as shown in the figure, are oscillating about their fixed center and with a mean separation of $0.8$ mm. The distance between the slits at time $t$ is given by $d=(0.8+0.04\sin\omega t)$ mm, where $\omega=0.08\ \text{rad s}^{-1}$. The distance of the screen from the slits is $1$ m and the wavelength of the light used to illuminate the slits is $6000$ Å. The interference pattern on the screen changes with time, while the central bright fringe (zeroth fringe) remains fixed at point O.

    The maximum speed in $\mu$m/s at which the $8^{\text{th}}$ bright fringe will move is __________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 23.5 to 24.5

    $y_8=\dfrac{8\lambda D}{d}$, so $\left|\dfrac{dy_8}{dt}\right|=\dfrac{8\lambda D}{d^2}\left|\dfrac{dd}{dt}\right|$, with $\dfrac{dd}{dt}=0.04\omega\cos\omega t$ mm/s.
    The speed is greatest near $\cos\omega t=\pm1$, where $d=0.8$ mm and $\left|\dfrac{dd}{dt}\right|=0.04\times0.08=3.2\times10^{-3}$ mm/s $=3.2\times10^{-6}$ m/s.
    $v_{\max}\approx\dfrac{4.8\times10^{-6}\times3.2\times10^{-6}}{(0.8\times10^{-3})^2}=2.4\times10^{-5}$ m/s $=24\ \mu$m/s (the small variation of $d$ shifts this only to about $24.1$).

  7. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 2 · Question 22
    A monochromatic light wave is incident normally on a glass slab of thickness $d$, as shown in the figure. The refractive index of the slab increases linearly from $n_1$ to $n_2$ over the height $h$. Which of the following statement(s) is(are) true about the light wave emerging out of the slab?

    1. It will deflect up by an angle $\tan^{-1}\left[\dfrac{(n_2^2 - n_1^2)d}{2h}\right]$.
    2. It will deflect up by an angle $\tan^{-1}\left[\dfrac{(n_2 - n_1)d}{h}\right]$.
    3. It will not deflect.
    4. The deflection angle depends only on $(n_2 - n_1)$ and not on the individual values of $n_1$ and $n_2$.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b), (d)

    Across the slab the optical path at the top exceeds that at the bottom by $(n_2 - n_1)d$, over a height $h$.
    The emerging wavefront is therefore tilted: the upper part lags, so the wave turns towards the region of higher index, i.e. upwards.
    Tilt angle: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{(n_2 - n_1)d}{h}$, so $\theta = \tan^{-1}\left[\dfrac{(n_2 - n_1)d}{h}\right]$ → (B); (A) and (C) are false.
    The angle involves only $n_2 - n_1$ → (D).
    Answer (B), (D).

  8. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 2 · Question 31
    A double slit setup is shown in the figure. One of the slits is in medium 2 of refractive index $n_2$. The other slit is at the interface of this medium with another medium 1 of refractive index $n_1\ (\ne n_2)$. The line joining the slits is perpendicular to the interface and the distance between the slits is $d$. The slit widths are much smaller than $d$. A monochromatic parallel beam of light is incident on the slits from medium 1. A detector is placed in medium 2 at a large distance from the slits, and at an angle $\theta$ from the line joining them, so that $\theta$ equals the angle of refraction of the beam. Consider two approximately parallel rays from the slits received by the detector.
    Which of the following statement(s) is(are) correct?

    1. The phase difference between the two rays is independent of $d$.
    2. The two rays interfere constructively at the detector.
    3. The phase difference between the two rays depends on $n_1$ but is independent of $n_2$.
    4. The phase difference between the two rays vanishes only for certain values of $d$ and the angle of incidence of the beam, with $\theta$ being the corresponding angle of refraction.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (b)

    Both slits are reached by the refracted beam, which travels in medium 2 at angle $\theta$ to the line joining the slits.
    To reach the lower slit the light travels an extra $d\cos\theta$ in medium 2 (beyond the wavefront through the upper slit).
    Towards the detector, at the same angle $\theta$, the ray from the upper slit travels an extra $d\cos\theta$ in medium 2.
    The two extra paths cancel, so the phase difference is zero for every $d$, $n_1$, $n_2$ and angle of incidence.
    (A) true, (B) true (constructive), (C) false, (D) false.
