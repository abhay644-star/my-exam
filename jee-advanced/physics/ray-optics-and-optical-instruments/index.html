  1. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 1 · Question 20
    A double convex lens made of glass of refractive index 1.5 and radii of curvature of the curved surfaces 20 cm each is immersed in a liquid of refractive index $n_L$. The correct plot showing the variation of the power, in the units of diopter ($D$), as a function of $n_L$ is:

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a) or (b) (either is accepted)

    Lens-maker: $P = \left(\dfrac{1.5}{n_L} - 1\right)\left(\dfrac{2}{0.2\ \text{m}}\right) = 10\left(\dfrac{1.5}{n_L} - 1\right)$ D.
    $n_L = 1$: $P = 5$ D; $n_L = 1.5$: $P = 0$; $n_L = 2$: $P = -2.5$ D.
    $P$ is finite everywhere (rules out C, D), decreasing and convex in $n_L$, matching (A).
    The official final key accepts (A) or (B).

    Note: Official final key: A or B (either option is awarded marks).

  3. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 21
    A beam of polychromatic light passes through a thin prism of prism angle $6^\circ$. The refractive index of the material of the prism varies with wavelength ($\lambda$) as $n(\lambda) = \alpha\lambda + \frac{\beta}{\lambda^2}$, where $\alpha = 3\ \mu\mathrm{m}^{-1}$ and $\beta = 0.096\ \mu\mathrm{m}^2$. If $\lambda_{\min}$ is the wavelength at which the angle of minimum deviation $D_m$ is smallest, then the correct value of $D_m$ at $\lambda_{\min}$ is

    1. $6.4^\circ$
    2. $4.8^\circ$
    3. $3.2^\circ$
    4. $2.4^\circ$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Thin prism: $D_m = (n-1)A$, so $D_m$ is smallest where $n$ is smallest.
    $\dfrac{dn}{d\lambda} = \alpha - \dfrac{2\beta}{\lambda^3} = 0 \Rightarrow \lambda^3 = \dfrac{2(0.096)}{3} = 0.064 \Rightarrow \lambda_{\min} = 0.4\ \mu\mathrm{m}$.
    $n = 3(0.4) + \dfrac{0.096}{0.16} = 1.2 + 0.6 = 1.8$.
    $D_m = 0.8 \times 6^\circ = 4.8^\circ$.

  4. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 23
    Consider two isosceles prisms 1 and 2 with prism angles $A_1$ and $A_2$ and refractive indices $n_1$ and $n_2$, respectively, as shown in the figure. The faces $a_1b_1$ and $a_2b_2$ are parallel to each other and perpendicular to the mirror $M$. If a ray of light is incident on the face $a_1c_1$ and emerges from the face $a_2c_2$, then the correct statement(s) is/are:

    1. If both the prisms are at minimum deviation condition, then $\frac{n_2}{n_1} = \sin\left(\frac{A_1}{2}\right) / \sin\left(\frac{A_2}{2}\right)$.
    2. If prism 2 is at minimum deviation condition, then $\sin i_1 = n_2 \sin\left(\frac{A_2}{2}\right)$ is always true.
    3. If both the prisms 1 and 2 are thin and are at minimum deviation condition with angles of deviation $\delta_{m1}$ and $\delta_{m2}$, respectively, then $\theta = \frac{\delta_{m1}}{2(n_1-1)} + \frac{\delta_{m2}}{2(n_2-1)}$.
    4. If prism 1 is at minimum deviation condition, then $\sin i_2 = n_1 \sin\left(\frac{A_1}{2}\right)$ is always true.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (c), (d)

    Normals to $a_1b_1$ and $a_2b_2$ are parallel to the mirror, and reflection at $M$ is symmetric, so the ray meets $a_2b_2$ with $i_2 = e_1$ always.
    At minimum deviation in an isosceles prism, $\sin i = \sin e = n\sin(A/2)$.
    Prism 1 at minimum deviation: $\sin i_2 = \sin e_1 = n_1\sin(A_1/2)$ — (D) true.
    Prism 2 at minimum deviation: $\sin i_2 = n_2\sin(A_2/2)$, but this equals $\sin e_1$, not $\sin i_1$ in general — (B) false.
    Both at minimum deviation: $n_1\sin(A_1/2) = n_2\sin(A_2/2) \Rightarrow \dfrac{n_2}{n_1} = \dfrac{\sin(A_1/2)}{\sin(A_2/2)}$ — (A) true.
    The dashed lines meeting at $\theta$ are the angle bisectors from $a_1$, $a_2$, each tilted $A/2$ from the vertical faces, so $\theta = \dfrac{A_1}{2}+\dfrac{A_2}{2}$; thin prism $A = \dfrac{\delta_m}{n-1}$ gives (C) true.

  5. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 2 · Question 22
    Two identical concave mirrors each of focal length $f$ are facing each other as shown in the schematic diagram. The focal length $f$ is much larger than the size of the mirrors. A glass slab of thickness $t$ and refractive index $n_0$ is kept equidistant from the mirrors and perpendicular to their common principal axis. A monochromatic point light source $S$ is embedded at the center of the slab on the principal axis, as shown in the schematic diagram. For the image to be formed on $S$ itself, which of the following distances between the two mirrors is/are correct:

    1. $4f+\left(1-\dfrac{1}{n_0}\right)t$
    2. $2f+\left(1-\dfrac{1}{n_0}\right)t$
    3. $4f+(n_0-1)t$
    4. $2f+(n_0-1)t$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (b)

    Let the mirror separation be $D$. Seen from either mirror, $S$ lies behind glass of thickness $t/2$, so its apparent distance from the mirror is $\dfrac D2-\dfrac t2\left(1-\dfrac1{n_0}\right)$.
    Case 1: $S$ appears at the centre of curvature of each mirror; light returns on itself to $S$: $\dfrac D2-\dfrac t2\left(1-\dfrac1{n_0}\right)=2f\Rightarrow D=4f+\left(1-\dfrac1{n_0}\right)t$.
    Case 2: $S$ appears at the focus of each mirror; light from $S$ leaves mirror 1 parallel and mirror 2 focuses it back on $S$: $\dfrac D2-\dfrac t2\left(1-\dfrac1{n_0}\right)=f\Rightarrow D=2f+\left(1-\dfrac1{n_0}\right)t$.
    Answer (A), (B).

  6. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 1 · Question 24
    A glass beaker has a solid, plano-convex base of refractive index 1.60, as shown in the figure. The radius of curvature of the convex surface (SPU) is 9 cm, while the planar surface (STU) acts as a mirror. This beaker is filled with a liquid of refractive index $n$ up to the level QPR. If the image of a point object O at a height of $h$ (OT in the figure) is formed onto itself, then, which of the following option(s) is(are) correct?

    1. For $n = 1.42$, $h = 50$ cm.
    2. For $n = 1.35$, $h = 36$ cm.
    3. For $n = 1.45$, $h = 65$ cm.
    4. For $n = 1.48$, $h = 85$ cm.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (b)

    The image coincides with the object when the rays strike the plane mirror STU normally, i.e. travel parallel to the axis inside the glass.
    On the axis the liquid layer has zero thickness at P (the liquid level touches the vertex), so the flat air–liquid surface adds no power; only the liquid–glass curved surface ($R = 9$ cm) refracts.

    $n = 1.42$: $h = 50$ cm ✓. $n = 1.35$: $h = 36$ cm ✓. $n = 1.45$: $h = 60$ cm (not 65). $n = 1.48$: $h = 75$ cm (not 85).
    Answer: (A), (B).

  7. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 1 · Question 33
    A light ray is incident on the surface of a sphere of refractive index $n$ at an angle of incidence $\theta_0$. The ray partially refracts into the sphere with angle of refraction $\phi_0$ and then partly reflects from the back surface. The reflected ray then emerges out of the sphere after a partial refraction. The total angle of deviation of the emergent ray with respect to the incident ray is $\alpha$. Match the quantities mentioned in List-I with their values in List-II and choose the correct option.

    List-IList-II
    (P) If $n = 2$ and $\alpha = 180^\circ$, then all the possible values of $\theta_0$ will be(1) $30^\circ$ and $0^\circ$
    (Q) If $n = \sqrt{3}$ and $\alpha = 180^\circ$, then all the possible values of $\theta_0$ will be(2) $60^\circ$ and $0^\circ$
    (R) If $n = \sqrt{3}$ and $\alpha = 180^\circ$, then all the possible values of $\phi_0$ will be(3) $45^\circ$ and $0^\circ$
    (S) If $n = \sqrt{2}$ and $\theta_0 = 45^\circ$, then all the possible values of $\alpha$ will be(4) $150^\circ$
    (5) $0^\circ$

    1. P→5, Q→2, R→1, S→4
    2. P→5, Q→1, R→2, S→4
    3. P→3, Q→2, R→1, S→4
    4. P→3, Q→1, R→2, S→5
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Deviation: $(\theta_0 - \phi_0)$ at entry, $(180^\circ - 2\phi_0)$ at the reflection, $(\theta_0 - \phi_0)$ at exit: $\alpha = 180^\circ + 2\theta_0 - 4\phi_0$.
    $\alpha = 180^\circ \Rightarrow \theta_0 = 2\phi_0$; with $\sin\theta_0 = n\sin\phi_0$: $2\sin\phi_0\cos\phi_0 = n\sin\phi_0$, so $\phi_0 = 0$ or $\cos\phi_0 = \dfrac n2$.
    (P) $n = 2$: $\cos\phi_0 = 1$ gives $\phi_0 = 0$ only, so $\theta_0 = 0^\circ$ → (5).
    (Q), (R) $n = \sqrt3$: $\phi_0 = 0^\circ$ or $30^\circ$, so $\theta_0 = 0^\circ$ or $60^\circ$. Q → (2), R → (1).
    (S) $n = \sqrt2$, $\theta_0 = 45^\circ$: $\sin\phi_0 = \dfrac12$, $\phi_0 = 30^\circ$; $\alpha = 180^\circ + 90^\circ - 120^\circ = 150^\circ$ → (4).
    P→5, Q→2, R→1, S→4 → (A).

  8. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 2 · Question 28
    Two equilateral-triangular prisms $\mathrm{P_1}$ and $\mathrm{P_2}$ are kept with their sides parallel to each other, in vacuum, as shown in the figure. A light ray enters prism $\mathrm{P_1}$ at an angle of incidence $\theta$ such that the outgoing ray undergoes minimum deviation in prism $\mathrm{P_2}$. If the respective refractive indices of $\mathrm{P_1}$ and $\mathrm{P_2}$ are $\sqrt{\dfrac32}$ and $\sqrt3$, then $\theta=\sin^{-1}\left[\sqrt{\dfrac32}\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{\beta}\right)\right]$, where the value of $\beta$ is ______.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 12

    Minimum deviation in $\mathrm{P_2}$ ($A=60^\circ$): internal angle $30^\circ$, so the incidence on $\mathrm{P_2}$ satisfies $\sin i=\sqrt3\sin30^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, $i=60^\circ$.
    The facing faces are parallel, so the ray leaves $\mathrm{P_1}$ at $60^\circ$: $\sin r_2=\dfrac{\sin60^\circ}{\sqrt{3/2}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$, $r_2=45^\circ$.
    $r_1=60^\circ-45^\circ=15^\circ$, so $\sin\theta=\sqrt{\dfrac32}\sin15^\circ=\sqrt{\dfrac32}\sin\dfrac{\pi}{12}$.
    $\beta=12$.

  9. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 1 · Question 19
    A plane polarized blue light ray is incident on a prism such that there is no reflection from the surface of the prism. The angle of deviation of the emergent ray is $\delta = 60^{\circ}$ (see Figure-1). The angle of minimum deviation for red light from the same prism is $\delta_{\min} = 30^{\circ}$ (see Figure-2). The refractive index of the prism material for blue light is $\sqrt3$. Which of the following statement(s) is(are) correct?

    1. The blue light is polarized in the plane of incidence.
    2. The angle of the prism is $45^{\circ}$.
    3. The refractive index of the material of the prism for red light is $\sqrt2$.
    4. The angle of refraction for blue light in air at the exit plane of the prism is $60^{\circ}$.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (c), (d)

    No reflection means incidence at Brewster’s angle with the light polarized in the plane of incidence. (A) true.
    $\tan i = \sqrt3 \Rightarrow i = 60^{\circ}$, and then $r_1 = 90^{\circ} - 60^{\circ} = 30^{\circ}$.
    $\delta = i + e - A = 60^{\circ} \Rightarrow e = A$, and $r_2 = A - 30^{\circ}$. Snell at the exit face: $\sin A = \sqrt3\sin(A - 30^{\circ}) = \dfrac32\sin A - \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\cos A \Rightarrow \tan A = \sqrt3$.
    So $A = 60^{\circ}$ (B false) and the angle of refraction in air at the exit face is $e = 60^{\circ}$ (D true).
    Red light: $n = \dfrac{\sin\frac{A + \delta_{\min}}{2}}{\sin\frac A2} = \dfrac{\sin45^{\circ}}{\sin30^{\circ}} = \sqrt2$. (C) true.
    Answer (A), (C), (D).

  10. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 1 · Question 26
    An optical arrangement consists of two concave mirrors $\mathrm{M_1}$ and $\mathrm{M_2}$, and a convex lens L with a common principal axis, as shown in the figure. The focal length of L is $10$ cm. The radii of curvature of $\mathrm{M_1}$ and $\mathrm{M_2}$ are $20$ cm and $24$ cm, respectively. The distance between L and $\mathrm{M_2}$ is $20$ cm. A point object S is placed at the mid-point between L and $\mathrm{M_2}$ on the axis. When the distance between L and $\mathrm{M_1}$ is $n/7$ cm, one of the images coincides with S. The value of $n$ is ______.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 80 or 150 or 220

    S is $10$ cm from L (its focus) and $10$ cm from $\mathrm{M_2}$ ($f = 12$ cm).
    Reflection at $\mathrm{M_2}$: $\dfrac1v = \dfrac1{10} - \dfrac1{12} = \dfrac1{60}$: virtual image $60$ cm behind $\mathrm{M_2}$, i.e. $80$ cm to the right of L.
    Through L (object $80$ cm away): $\dfrac1v = \dfrac1{10} - \dfrac1{80} = \dfrac{7}{80}$, image $\dfrac{80}{7}$ cm to the left of L.
    The light retraces its path (and the final image falls on S) if this image is at the pole of $\mathrm{M_1}$: $x = \dfrac{80}{7}$, or at its centre of curvature: $x = \dfrac{80}{7} + 20 = \dfrac{220}{7}$.
    Path S → L → $\mathrm{M_1}$ → L → $\mathrm{M_2}$: S is at the focus of L, so the beam is parallel and $\mathrm{M_1}$ focuses it $10$ cm in front of it. For the final image at S, L must image this point to $80$ cm on its right, i.e. it must be $\dfrac{80}{7}$ cm from L: $x - 10 = \dfrac{80}{7} \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{150}{7}$.
    So $n = 80$, $150$ or $220$; the official key accepts all three.

    Note: Official final key: 80 or 150 or 220 (any of these is awarded marks).

  11. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 1 · Question 27
    In an experiment for determination of the focal length of a thin convex lens, the distance of the object from the lens is $10 \pm 0.1$ cm and the distance of its real image from the lens is $20 \pm 0.2$ cm. The error in the determination of focal length of the lens is $n$ %. The value of $n$ is ______.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 1

    For a real image, $\dfrac1f = \dfrac1u + \dfrac1v$ (magnitudes): $f = \dfrac{10\times20}{30} = \dfrac{20}{3}$ cm.
    $\dfrac{\Delta f}{f^2} = \dfrac{\Delta u}{u^2} + \dfrac{\Delta v}{v^2} = \dfrac{0.1}{100} + \dfrac{0.2}{400} = 0.0015$.
    $\dfrac{\Delta f}{f} = 0.0015\times\dfrac{20}{3} = 0.01 = 1\%$. So $n = 1$.

    A rod of length $2$ cm makes an angle $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ rad with the principal axis of a thin convex lens. The lens has a focal length of $10$ cm and is placed at a distance of $\dfrac{40}{3}$ cm from the object as shown in the figure. The height of the image is $\dfrac{30\sqrt3}{13}$ cm and the angle made by it with respect to the principal axis is $\alpha$ rad. The value of $\alpha$ is $\dfrac{\pi}{n}$ rad, where $n$ is ______.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 5.95 to 6.05

    The lower end of the rod is on the axis at $u=-\dfrac{40}{3}$ cm; the upper end is $2\cos60^\circ=1$ cm further left and $2\sin60^\circ=\sqrt3$ cm high, at $u=-\dfrac{43}{3}$ cm.

    Horizontal extent of the image: $40-\dfrac{430}{13}=\dfrac{90}{13}$ cm.
    $\tan\alpha=\dfrac{30\sqrt3/13}{90/13}=\dfrac1{\sqrt3}\Rightarrow\alpha=\dfrac\pi6$, so $n=6$.

  13. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 1 · Question 30
    Three plane mirrors form an equilateral triangle with each side of length $L$. There is a small hole at a distance $l>0$ from one of the corners as shown in the figure. A ray of light is passed through the hole at an angle $\theta$ and can only come out through the same hole. The cross section of the mirror configuration and the ray of light lie on the same plane.
    Which of the following statement(s) is(are) correct?

    1. The ray of light will come out for $\theta=30^\circ$, for $0<l<L$.
    2. There is an angle for $l=\dfrac L2$ at which the ray of light will come out after two reflections.
    3. The ray of light will <b>NEVER</b> come out for $\theta=60^\circ$, and $l=\dfrac L3$.
    4. The ray of light will come out for $\theta=60^\circ$, and $0<l<\dfrac L2$ after six reflections.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (b)

    Inside, the ray travels at angle $\theta$ to the base, heading towards the far (left) side, which makes $60^\circ$ with the base.
    (A) $\theta=30^\circ$: the ray meets the left side at $180^\circ-60^\circ-30^\circ=90^\circ$, i.e. normally, so it retraces its path to the hole for every $l$ — correct.
    (B) $l=\dfrac L2$, $\theta=60^\circ$: the ray runs along the medial triangle (midpoint of the left side, midpoint of the right side, back to the hole) — two reflections — correct.
    (C), (D) $\theta=60^\circ$ from a point at $x_0$ on the base: the ray runs parallel to the right side, then parallel to the base, then parallel to the left side and returns to the base at $L-x_0$; three more reflections bring it back to $x_0$.
    So for $l\ne\dfrac L2$ it does come out (after 5 reflections) — (C) wrong and (D) wrong (not six).

    Consider a configuration of $n$ identical units, each consisting of three layers. The first layer is a column of air of height $h = \dfrac13$ cm, and the second and third layers are of equal thickness $d = \dfrac{\sqrt3 - 1}{2}$ cm, and refractive indices $\mu_1 = \sqrt{\dfrac32}$ and $\mu_2 = \sqrt3$, respectively. A light source O is placed on the top of the first unit, as shown in the figure. A ray of light from O is incident on the second layer of the first unit at an angle of $\theta = 60^\circ$ to the normal. For a specific value of $n$, the ray of light emerges from the bottom of the configuration at a distance $l = \dfrac{8}{\sqrt3}$ cm, as shown in the figure. The value of $n$ is ________.

  16. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 2 · Question 26
    An object and a concave mirror of focal length $f = 10$ cm both move along the principal axis of the mirror with constant speeds. The object moves with speed $V_0 = 15$ cm s$^{-1}$ towards the mirror with respect to a laboratory frame. The distance between the object and the mirror at a given moment is denoted by $u$. When $u = 30$ cm, the speed of the mirror $V_m$ is such that the image is instantaneously at rest with respect to the laboratory frame, and the object forms a real image. The magnitude of $V_m$ is ________ cm s$^{-1}$.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 3

    At $u = 30$ cm: $\dfrac1v = \dfrac1{10} - \dfrac1{30}$, so the real image is at $v = 15$ cm in front of the mirror.
    Differentiating $\dfrac1u + \dfrac1v = \dfrac1f$: $\dot v = -\dfrac{v^2}{u^2}\dot u = -\dfrac14\dot u$.
    Take the axis towards the mirror, mirror at $x_m$: object at $x_m - u$, image at $x_m - v$. With mirror velocity $V_m$, $\dot u = V_m - 15$.
    Image velocity $= V_m - \dot v = V_m + \dfrac14(V_m - 15) = 0 \Rightarrow V_m = 3$ cm s$^{-1}$.

  17. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 22
    An extended object is placed at point O, 10 cm in front of a convex lens $\mathrm{L_1}$ and a concave lens $\mathrm{L_2}$ is placed 10 cm behind it, as shown in the figure. The radii of curvature of all the curved surfaces in both the lenses are 20 cm. The refractive index of both the lenses is 1.5. The total magnification of this lens system is

    1. 0.4
    2. 0.8
    3. 1.3
    4. 1.6
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Lens maker: $\dfrac1f = (1.5 - 1)\left(\dfrac{2}{20}\right)$, so $f_1 = +20$ cm and $f_2 = -20$ cm.
    $\mathrm{L_1}$: $u = -10$ cm, $\dfrac1v = \dfrac1{20} - \dfrac1{10} \Rightarrow v = -20$ cm; $m_1 = \dfrac{v}{u} = 2$.
    This virtual image is $20 + 10 = 30$ cm in front of $\mathrm{L_2}$: $\dfrac1v = -\dfrac1{20} - \dfrac1{30} \Rightarrow v = -12$ cm; $m_2 = \dfrac{-12}{-30} = 0.4$.
    Total magnification $= 2 \times 0.4 = 0.8$ → (B).

  18. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 31
    A wide slab consisting of two media of refractive indices $n_1$ and $n_2$ is placed in air as shown in the figure. A ray of light is incident from medium $n_1$ to $n_2$ at an angle $\theta$, where $\sin\theta$ is slightly larger than $1/n_1$. Take refractive index of air as 1. Which of the following statement(s) is(are) correct?

    1. The light ray enters air if $n_2 = n_1$
    2. The light ray is finally reflected back into the medium of refractive index $n_1$ if $n_2 < n_1$
    3. The light ray is finally reflected back into the medium of refractive index $n_1$ if $n_2 > n_1$
    4. The light ray is reflected back into the medium of refractive index $n_1$ if $n_2 = 1$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b), (c), (d); the final key also accepts (c), (d)

    For parallel layers $n\sin(\text{angle})$ is the same in every layer: $n_1\sin\theta > 1$.
    To enter air the ray would need $\sin(\text{angle in air}) = n_1\sin\theta > 1$, which is impossible. So the ray never enters air: (A) is false.
    (B) $n_2 < n_1$: either total internal reflection at the $n_1$–$n_2$ boundary, or the ray enters $n_2$ and is totally reflected at the top; either way it returns to $n_1$: true.
    (C) $n_2 > n_1$: the ray enters $n_2$, is totally reflected at the $n_2$–air surface and comes back into $n_1$: true.
    (D) $n_2 = 1$: $n_1\sin\theta > n_2$, so it is totally reflected at the boundary into $n_1$: true.
    Answer (B), (C), (D); the official final key also accepts (C), (D).

    Note: Official final key: B, C, D or C, D (either set gets full marks).

  19. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 2 · Question 22
    For a prism of prism angle $\theta = 60^\circ$, the refractive indices of the left half and the right half are, respectively, $n_1$ and $n_2$ ($n_2 \ge n_1$) as shown in the figure. The angle of incidence $i$ is chosen such that the incident light rays will have minimum deviation if $n_1 = n_2 = n = 1.5$. For the case of unequal refractive indices, $n_1 = n$ and $n_2 = n + \Delta n$ (where $\Delta n \ll n$), the angle of emergence $e = i + \Delta e$. Which of the following statement(s) is(are) correct?

    1. The value of $\Delta e$ (in radians) is greater than that of $\Delta n$
    2. $\Delta e$ is proportional to $\Delta n$
    3. $\Delta e$ lies between 2.0 and 3.0 milliradians, if $\Delta n = 2.8 \times 10^{-3}$
    4. $\Delta e$ lies between 1.0 and 1.6 milliradians, if $\Delta n = 2.8 \times 10^{-3}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b), (c)

    At minimum deviation the ray inside travels parallel to the base, so it meets the vertical interface between the halves normally and is not bent there.
    It reaches the second face at $r_2 = 30^\circ$ in medium $n_2$: $\sin e = n_2\sin30^\circ = \dfrac{n_2}{2}$.
    Differentiating: $\cos e\,\Delta e = \dfrac{\Delta n}{2}$. With $\sin i = 0.75$, $\cos i = 0.661$: $\Delta e = \dfrac{\Delta n}{2 \times 0.661} = 0.756\,\Delta n$.
    So $\Delta e \propto \Delta n$ (B) and $\Delta e < \Delta n$ ((A) false).
    $\Delta n = 2.8 \times 10^{-3}$: $\Delta e \approx 2.12 \times 10^{-3}$ rad, between 2.0 and 3.0 mrad → (C); (D) false.
    Answer (B), (C).
