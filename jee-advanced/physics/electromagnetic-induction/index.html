  1. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 1 · Question 18
    Consider a circuit consisting of a capacitor of capacitance $C$ and a coil with $N$ turns per unit length, cross sectional area $S$ and length $d$, where $d^2 \gg S$. There is another coil of length $d/2$, cross sectional area $S/2$ and $2N$ turns per unit length completely inside the larger coil, as shown in the figure. The ends of this smaller coil are connected with each other by an insulated conducting wire. The self-inductance of the larger coil is $L$. Neglecting edge effects and all the Ohmic resistances, the resonant frequency of the circuit is:

    1. $\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{15\,LC}}$
    2. $\dfrac{6}{\sqrt{5\,LC}}$
    3. $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3\,LC}}$
    4. $\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{3\,LC}}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $L = \mu_0 N^2 S d$. Mutual inductance $M = \mu_0 N (2N)\dfrac{S}{2}\dfrac{d}{2} = \dfrac{L}{2}$; self-inductance of the small coil $L_2 = \mu_0 (2N)^2 \dfrac{S}{2}\dfrac{d}{2} = L$.
    The shorted resistanceless coil keeps zero flux: $L_2 I_2 + M I = 0 \Rightarrow I_2 = -I/2$.
    Flux of the large coil: $\Phi = L I + M I_2 = LI - LI/4 = \tfrac{3}{4}LI$, so $L_{\text{eff}} = 3L/4$.
    $\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{L_{\text{eff}}C} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3LC}}$ → (C).

  2. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 1 · Question 31
    List-I contains four conducting loops lying in the $XY$ plane, as shown in the figures. The loops are rotating about $Z$ axis passing through the point $O$ with time period $T$ in clockwise direction. The region $x > 0$ contains a uniform magnetic field $B$ in the $+z$ direction. List-II contains the qualitative variation of the induced current $i(t)$ for each of these loops. Choose the option which describes the correct match between the entries in List-I to those in List-II.

    List-IList-II
    (P) Loop (P) in the figure(1) Graph (1) in the figure
    (Q) Loop (Q) in the figure(2) Graph (2) in the figure
    (R) Loop (R) in the figure(3) Graph (3) in the figure
    (S) Loop (S) in the figure(4) Graph (4) in the figure
    (5) Graph (5) in the figure

    1. P → 5, Q → 4, R → 1, S → 3
    2. P → 3, Q → 2, R → 5, S → 4
    3. P → 3, Q → 2, R → 1, S → 4
    4. P → 5, Q → 1, R → 2, S → 3
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    While a circular-arc edge of radius $a$ sweeps across the boundary ($y$-axis), the flux changes at the constant rate $\tfrac12B\omega a^2$, giving a constant emf; no change, no current.
    (P) semicircle: area in the field grows steadily for half a turn, then shrinks for the next half → constant current that reverses at $T/2$ → (3).
    (Q) two $60^\circ$ sectors with $60^\circ$ gaps: flux grows in steps (on/off) over the first half turn, then falls in steps → (2).
    (R) one $60^\circ$ sector: enters during $0$–$T/6$, stays inside, leaves during $T/2$–$2T/3$ → pulses of opposite sign → (1).
    (S) two opposite sectors: one enters while the other leaves, so the flux in the field stays constant → $i = 0$ → (4).
    Match → (C).

  3. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 1 · Question 19
    A conducting square loop initially lies in the $XZ$ plane with its lower edge hinged along the $X$-axis. Only in the region $y \ge 0$, there is a time dependent magnetic field pointing along the $Z$-direction, $\vec{B}(t) = B_0(\cos\omega t)\hat{k}$, where $B_0$ is a constant. The magnetic field is zero everywhere else. At time $t = 0$, the loop starts rotating with constant angular speed $\omega$ about the $X$ axis in the clockwise direction as viewed from the $+X$ axis (as shown in the figure). Ignoring self-inductance of the loop and gravity, which of the following plots correctly represents the induced e.m.f. ($V$) in the loop as a function of time:

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Let the side be $a$. After time $t$ the loop makes angle $\omega t$ with the $Z$ axis and lies over $y \ge 0$ for $0 \le \omega t \le \pi$; its area projected on the $XY$ plane is $a^2\sin\omega t$.
    Flux for $0 \le t \le \pi/\omega$: $\Phi = B_0\cos\omega t\cdot a^2\sin\omega t = \dfrac{B_0a^2}{2}\sin2\omega t$.
    emf $= -\dfrac{d\Phi}{dt} = -B_0a^2\omega\cos2\omega t$: starts at its negative extreme, reaches the positive maximum at $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2\omega}$, and is back at the negative extreme at $t = \dfrac\pi\omega$.
    For $\dfrac\pi\omega \lt t \lt \dfrac{2\pi}\omega$ the loop is in $y \lt 0$ where $B = 0$: emf $= 0$ (sudden jump to zero). The pattern then repeats.
    This is plot (A).

  4. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 1 · Question 21
    A conducting square loop of side $L$, mass $M$ and resistance $R$ is moving in the $XY$ plane with its edges parallel to the $X$ and $Y$ axes. The region $y \ge 0$ has a uniform magnetic field, $\vec{B} = B_0\hat{k}$. The magnetic field is zero everywhere else. At time $t = 0$, the loop starts to enter the magnetic field with an initial velocity $v_0\hat{j}$ m/s, as shown in the figure. Considering the quantity $K = \dfrac{B_0^2L^2}{RM}$ in appropriate units, ignoring self-inductance of the loop and gravity, which of the following statements is/are correct:

    1. If $v_0 = 1.5KL$, the loop will stop before it enters completely inside the region of magnetic field.
    2. When the complete loop is inside the region of magnetic field, the net force acting on the loop is zero.
    3. If $v_0 = \dfrac{KL}{10}$, the loop comes to rest at $t = \left(\dfrac{1}{K}\right)\ln\left(\dfrac{5}{2}\right)$.
    4. If $v_0 = 3KL$, the complete loop enters inside the region of magnetic field at time $t = \left(\dfrac{1}{K}\right)\ln\left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)$.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b), (d)

    While entering: emf $= B_0Lv$, current $= \dfrac{B_0Lv}{R}$, force $= -\dfrac{B_0^2L^2v}{R}$, so $\dfrac{dv}{dt} = -Kv$.
    Hence $v = v_0e^{-Kt}$ and, with $\dfrac{dv}{dx} = -K$, $v = v_0 - Kx$.
    (A) The loop would stop only after $x = \dfrac{v_0}{K} = 1.5L \gt L$, so it enters fully: false.
    (B) Fully inside, the flux is constant, no current, no force: true.
    (C) $v = v_0e^{-Kt}$ never becomes zero in finite time: false.
    (D) $x = \dfrac{v_0}{K}(1 - e^{-Kt}) = 3L(1 - e^{-Kt}) = L \Rightarrow e^{-Kt} = \dfrac23 \Rightarrow t = \dfrac1K\ln\dfrac32$: true.
    Answer: (B), (D).

  5. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 2 · Question 18
    A region in the form of an equilateral triangle (in $x$-$y$ plane) of height $L$ has a uniform magnetic field $\vec B$ pointing in the $+z$-direction. A conducting loop PQR, in the form of an equilateral triangle of the same height $L$, is placed in the $x$-$y$ plane with its vertex P at $x=0$ in the orientation shown in the figure. At $t=0$, the loop starts entering the region of the magnetic field with a uniform velocity $\vec v$ along the $+x$-direction. The plane of the loop and its orientation remain unchanged throughout its motion.
    Which of the following graph best depicts the variation of the induced emf ($E$) in the loop as a function of the distance ($x$) starting from $x=0$?

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Measure depth $s$ from $x=0$ into the field triangle; both triangles have width $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt3}\times$(distance from their apex). With P at distance $x$, the loop occupies $x-L\le s\le x$ and the field $0\le s\le L$.
    $0\le x\le L$: overlap width is $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt3}\min(s,\,x-s)$, area $A=\dfrac{x^2}{2\sqrt3}$, so $|E|=Bv\dfrac{dA}{dx}=\dfrac{Bvx}{\sqrt3}$: grows linearly to $\dfrac{BvL}{\sqrt3}$ at $x=L$.
    $L\le x\le 2L$: $A=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\left[\dfrac{x^2}{2}-2(x-L)^2\right]$, so $\dfrac{dA}{dx}=\dfrac{4L-3x}{\sqrt3}$.
    This falls linearly, is zero at $x=\dfrac{4L}{3}$ (between $L$ and $\dfrac{3L}{2}$), and at $x=2L$ equals $-\dfrac{2L}{\sqrt3}$: the emf reverses and reaches twice the earlier peak. At $x=2L$ the overlap vanishes, so $E=0$ beyond.
    Linear rise to $L$, reversal crossing zero before $\dfrac{3L}{2}$ and a peak of double size at $2L$ → (A).

  6. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 1 · Question 34
    A thin conducting rod MN of mass $20$ gm, length $25$ cm and resistance $10\ \Omega$ is held on frictionless, long, perfectly conducting vertical rails as shown in the figure. There is a uniform magnetic field $B_0 = 4$ T directed perpendicular to the plane of the rod-rail arrangement. The rod is released from rest at time $t = 0$ and it moves down along the rails. Assume air drag is negligible. Match each quantity in List-I with an appropriate value from List-II, and choose the correct option.
    [Given: The acceleration due to gravity $g = 10\ \text{m s}^{-2}$ and $e^{-1} = 0.4$]

    List-IList-II
    (P) At $t = 0.2$ s, the magnitude of the induced emf in Volt(1) $0.07$
    (Q) At $t = 0.2$ s, the magnitude of the magnetic force in Newton(2) $0.14$
    (R) At $t = 0.2$ s, the power dissipated as heat in Watt(3) $1.20$
    (S) The magnitude of terminal velocity of the rod in $\text{m s}^{-1}$(4) $0.12$
    (5) $2.00$

    1. P → 5, Q → 2, R → 3, S → 1
    2. P → 3, Q → 1, R → 4, S → 5
    3. P → 4, Q → 3, R → 1, S → 2
    4. P → 3, Q → 4, R → 2, S → 5
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    $m\dfrac{dv}{dt} = mg - \dfrac{B^2l^2}{R}v$ with $\dfrac{B^2l^2}{R} = \dfrac{16\times0.0625}{10} = 0.1$.
    Terminal velocity $v_T = \dfrac{mg}{0.1} = \dfrac{0.2}{0.1} = 2$ m/s → (S)(5); time constant $\tau = \dfrac{m}{0.1} = 0.2$ s.
    At $t = 0.2$ s: $v = 2(1 - e^{-1}) = 1.2$ m/s.
    (P) emf $= Blv = 4\times0.25\times1.2 = 1.20$ V → (3). (Q) $F = 0.1\times1.2 = 0.12$ N → (4). (R) $P = \dfrac{1.2^2}{10} = 0.144 \approx 0.14$ W → (2).
    Answer (D).

  7. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 2 · Question 26
    A rectangular conducting loop of length 4 cm and width 2 cm is in the $xy$-plane, as shown in the figure. It is being moved away from a thin and long conducting wire along the direction $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\hat x + \dfrac12\hat y$ with a constant speed $\mathrm v$. The wire is carrying a steady current $I = 10$ A in the positive $x$-direction. A current of $10\ \mu$A flows through the loop when it is at a distance $d = 4$ cm from the wire. If the resistance of the loop is $0.1\ \Omega$, then the value of $\mathrm v$ is ________ m s$^{-1}$.
    [Given: The permeability of free space $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}$ N A$^{-2}$]

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 4

    Only the velocity component perpendicular to the wire, $\dfrac{\mathrm v}{2}$ (along $y$), changes the flux.
    The two 2 cm sides parallel to the wire are at $0.04$ m and $0.08$ m: $\varepsilon = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi}\,L\,\dfrac{\mathrm v}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{0.04} - \dfrac{1}{0.08}\right)$ with $L = 0.02$ m.
    $\varepsilon = (2\times10^{-6})(0.02)\dfrac{\mathrm v}{2}(12.5) = 2.5\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm v$ V.
    $i = \dfrac{\varepsilon}{R} = 2.5\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm v = 10\times10^{-6} \Rightarrow \mathrm v = 4$ m/s.

  8. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 1 · Question 25
    Consider an LC circuit, with inductance $L=0.1$ H and capacitance $C=10^{-3}$ F, kept on a plane. The area of the circuit is $1\ \mathrm{m^2}$. It is placed in a constant magnetic field of strength $B_0$ which is perpendicular to the plane of the circuit. At time $t=0$, the magnetic field strength starts increasing linearly as $B=B_0+\beta t$ with $\beta=0.04\ \mathrm{T\,s^{-1}}$. The maximum magnitude of the current in the circuit is ______ mA.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 3.98 to 4.02

    From $t=0$ a constant emf $\varepsilon=A\beta=1\times0.04=0.04$ V acts in the loop.
    $L\dfrac{di}{dt}+\dfrac qC=\varepsilon$ with $q=i=0$ at $t=0$ gives $q=C\varepsilon(1-\cos\omega t)$, $i=C\varepsilon\omega\sin\omega t$.
    $\omega=\dfrac1{\sqrt{LC}}=\dfrac1{\sqrt{10^{-4}}}=100$ rad/s.
    $i_{max}=C\varepsilon\omega=10^{-3}\times0.04\times100=4\times10^{-3}$ A $=4$ mA.

  9. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 1 · Question 33
    A small circular loop of area $A$ and resistance $R$ is fixed on a horizontal $xy$-plane with the center of the loop always on the axis $\hat n$ of a long solenoid. The solenoid has $m$ turns per unit length and carries current $I$ counterclockwise as shown in the figure. The magnetic field due to the solenoid is in $\hat n$ direction. List-I gives time dependences of $\hat n$ in terms of a constant angular frequency $\omega$. List-II gives the torques experienced by the circular loop at time $t=\dfrac{\pi}{6\omega}$. Let $\alpha=\dfrac{A^2\mu_0^2m^2I^2\omega}{2R}$.

    List-IList-II
    (I) $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(\sin\omega t\,\hat\jmath+\cos\omega t\,\hat k\right)$(P) $0$
    (II) $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(\sin\omega t\,\hat\imath+\cos\omega t\,\hat\jmath\right)$(Q) $-\frac{\alpha}{4}\hat\imath$
    (III) $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(\sin\omega t\,\hat\imath+\cos\omega t\,\hat k\right)$(R) $\frac{3\alpha}{4}\hat\imath$
    (IV) $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(\cos\omega t\,\hat\jmath+\sin\omega t\,\hat k\right)$(S) $\frac{\alpha}{4}\hat\jmath$
    (T) $-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\hat\imath$
    Which one of the following options is correct?

    1. I → Q, II → P, III → S, IV → T
    2. I → S, II → T, III → Q, IV → P
    3. I → Q, II → P, III → S, IV → R
    4. I → T, II → Q, III → P, IV → R
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: Dropped: full marks to everyone

    $B=\mu_0mI\,\hat n$ at the loop; flux $\Phi=\mu_0mIA\,(\hat n\cdot\hat k)$; induced current $i=-\dfrac1R\dfrac{d\Phi}{dt}$; moment $\vec\mu=iA\hat k$; torque $\vec\tau=\vec\mu\times\vec B=iA\mu_0mI\,(\hat k\times\hat n)$.
    (I) $\hat n\cdot\hat k=\frac{\cos\omega t}{\sqrt2}$, $i=\dfrac{\mu_0mIA\omega\sin\omega t}{\sqrt2R}$, $\hat k\times\hat n=-\frac{\sin\omega t}{\sqrt2}\hat\imath$: $\vec\tau=-\alpha\sin^2\omega t\,\hat\imath=-\dfrac\alpha4\hat\imath$ (Q).
    (II) $\hat n\cdot\hat k=0$: no flux change, no current, $\vec\tau=0$ (P).
    (III) same current as (I), $\hat k\times\hat n=\frac{\sin\omega t}{\sqrt2}\hat\jmath$: $\vec\tau=\alpha\sin^2\omega t\,\hat\jmath=\dfrac\alpha4\hat\jmath$ (S).
    (IV) $\hat n\cdot\hat k=\frac{\sin\omega t}{\sqrt2}$, $i=-\dfrac{\mu_0mIA\omega\cos\omega t}{\sqrt2R}$, $\hat k\times\hat n=-\frac{\cos\omega t}{\sqrt2}\hat\imath$: $\vec\tau=\alpha\cos^2\omega t\,\hat\imath=\dfrac{3\alpha}4\hat\imath$ (R).
    This working gives option (C); the question was dropped in the official final key.

    Note: Dropped in the official final key: full marks to everyone.

  10. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 34
    A long straight wire carries a current, $I = 2$ ampere. A semi-circular conducting rod is placed beside it on two conducting parallel rails of negligible resistance. Both the rails are parallel to the wire. The wire, the rod and the rails lie in the same horizontal plane, as shown in the figure. Two ends of the semi-circular rod are at distances 1 cm and 4 cm from the wire. At time $t = 0$, the rod starts moving on the rails with a speed $v = 3.0$ m/s (see the figure).
    A resistor $R = 1.4\ \Omega$ and a capacitor $C_o = 5.0\ \mu$F are connected in series between the rails. At time $t = 0$, $C_o$ is uncharged. Which of the following statement(s) is(are) correct? [$\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}$ SI units. Take $\ln 2 = 0.7$]

    1. Maximum current through $R$ is $1.2 \times 10^{-6}$ ampere
    2. Maximum current through $R$ is $3.8 \times 10^{-6}$ ampere
    3. Maximum charge on capacitor $C_o$ is $8.4 \times 10^{-12}$ coulomb
    4. Maximum charge on capacitor $C_o$ is $2.4 \times 10^{-12}$ coulomb
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (c)

    The emf of the curved rod equals that of the straight segment joining its ends (from $r = 1$ cm to $4$ cm):
    $\varepsilon = \displaystyle\int_{1\text{ cm}}^{4\text{ cm}} \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}v\,dr = \dfrac{\mu_0 I v}{2\pi}\ln 4 = 2\times10^{-7} \times 2 \times 3 \times 1.4 = 1.68 \times 10^{-6}$ V.
    Maximum current (at $t = 0$, capacitor uncharged): $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{R} = \dfrac{1.68\times10^{-6}}{1.4} = 1.2 \times 10^{-6}$ A: (A).
    Maximum charge (fully charged): $C_o\varepsilon = 5\times10^{-6} \times 1.68\times10^{-6} = 8.4 \times 10^{-12}$ C: (C).
    Answer (A), (C).

  11. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 2 · Question 32
    A special metal $S$ conducts electricity without any resistance. A closed wire loop, made of $S$, does not allow any change in flux through itself by inducing a suitable current to generate a compensating flux. The induced current in the loop cannot decay due to its zero resistance. This current gives rise to a magnetic moment which in turn repels the source of magnetic field or flux. Consider such a loop, of radius $a$, with its center at the origin. A magnetic dipole of moment $m$ is brought along the axis of this loop from infinity to a point at distance $r\ (\gg a)$ from the center of the loop with its north pole always facing the loop, as shown in the figure below.
    The magnitude of magnetic field of a dipole $m$, at a point on its axis at distance $r$, is $\dfrac{\mu_0}{2\pi}\dfrac{m}{r^3}$, where $\mu_0$ is the permeability of free space. The magnitude of the force between two magnetic dipoles with moments, $m_1$ and $m_2$, separated by a distance $r$ on the common axis, with their north poles facing each other, is $\dfrac{k\,m_1m_2}{r^4}$, where $k$ is a constant of appropriate dimensions. The direction of this force is along the line joining the two dipoles.

    When the dipole $m$ is placed at a distance $r$ from the center of the loop (as shown in the figure), the current induced in the loop will be proportional to

    1. $m/r^3$
    2. $m^2/r^2$
    3. $m/r^2$
    4. $m^2/r$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Since $r \gg a$, the dipole field is nearly uniform over the loop: flux $\Phi = \pi a^2\dfrac{\mu_0}{2\pi}\dfrac{m}{r^3} \propto \dfrac{m}{r^3}$.
    The loop keeps its total flux at zero: $LI = \Phi$, so $I \propto \dfrac{m}{r^3}$ → (A).

  12. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 2 · Question 33
    A special metal $S$ conducts electricity without any resistance. A closed wire loop, made of $S$, does not allow any change in flux through itself by inducing a suitable current to generate a compensating flux. The induced current in the loop cannot decay due to its zero resistance. This current gives rise to a magnetic moment which in turn repels the source of magnetic field or flux. Consider such a loop, of radius $a$, with its center at the origin. A magnetic dipole of moment $m$ is brought along the axis of this loop from infinity to a point at distance $r\ (\gg a)$ from the center of the loop with its north pole always facing the loop, as shown in the figure below.
    The magnitude of magnetic field of a dipole $m$, at a point on its axis at distance $r$, is $\dfrac{\mu_0}{2\pi}\dfrac{m}{r^3}$, where $\mu_0$ is the permeability of free space. The magnitude of the force between two magnetic dipoles with moments, $m_1$ and $m_2$, separated by a distance $r$ on the common axis, with their north poles facing each other, is $\dfrac{k\,m_1m_2}{r^4}$, where $k$ is a constant of appropriate dimensions. The direction of this force is along the line joining the two dipoles.

    The work done in bringing the dipole from infinity to a distance $r$ from the center of the loop by the given process is proportional to

    1. $m/r^5$
    2. $m^2/r^5$
    3. $m^2/r^6$
    4. $m^2/r^7$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Loop moment $m_\text{loop} = I\pi a^2 \propto \dfrac{m}{r^3}$.
    Repulsive force $F = \dfrac{k\,m\,m_\text{loop}}{r^4} \propto \dfrac{m^2}{r^7}$.
    Work against it: $W = \int_\infty^r -F\,dr \propto \dfrac{m^2}{r^6}$ → (C).
