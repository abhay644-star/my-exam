  1. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 20
    A nuclear reactor starts producing a radioactive nuclide $X$ from $t = 0$, at a constant rate of $\alpha$ per second. Each decay of $X$ produces energy $E_0$, which is utilized to heat a liquid of mass $m$ and specific heat $s$. Assuming no heat loss from the liquid and taking $\lambda$ as the decay constant of $X$, the rate of increase in the temperature of the liquid is:

    1. $\dfrac{\alpha E_0}{m\,s}\left(1 - e^{-\lambda t}\right)$
    2. $\dfrac{\alpha E_0}{m\,s}\left(e^{\lambda t} - 1\right)$
    3. $\dfrac{\lambda E_0}{m\,s}\left(1 - e^{-\lambda t}\right)$
    4. $\dfrac{E_0}{m\,s}\left(\alpha - \lambda e^{-\lambda t}\right)$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $\dfrac{dN}{dt} = \alpha - \lambda N$ with $N(0)=0$ gives $N = \dfrac{\alpha}{\lambda}(1-e^{-\lambda t})$.
    Decay rate $= \lambda N = \alpha(1-e^{-\lambda t})$, so heating power $P = \alpha E_0(1-e^{-\lambda t})$.
    $m s \dfrac{dT}{dt} = P \Rightarrow \dfrac{dT}{dt} = \dfrac{\alpha E_0}{m s}(1-e^{-\lambda t})$.

  2. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 1 · Question 32
    List-I shows various functional dependencies of energy ($E$) on the atomic number ($Z$). Energies associated with certain phenomena are given in List-II.
    Choose the option that describes the correct match between the entries in List-I to those in List-II.

    List-IList-II
    (P) $E \propto Z^2$(1) energy of characteristic x-rays
    (Q) $E \propto (Z-1)^2$(2) electrostatic part of the nuclear binding energy for stable nuclei with mass numbers in the range 30 to 170
    (R) $E \propto Z(Z-1)$(3) energy of continuous x-rays
    (S) $E$ is practically independent of $Z$(4) average nuclear binding energy per nucleon for stable nuclei with mass number in the range 30 to 170
    (5) energy of radiation due to electronic transitions from hydrogen-like atoms

    1. P→4, Q→3, R→1, S→2
    2. P→5, Q→2, R→1, S→4
    3. P→5, Q→1, R→2, S→4
    4. P→3, Q→2, R→1, S→5
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    (P) Hydrogen-like atoms: $E_n \propto Z^2$, so photon energies $\propto Z^2$ → (5).
    (Q) Moseley's law for characteristic x-rays: $E \propto (Z-1)^2$ → (1).
    (R) Coulomb (electrostatic) energy of $Z$ protons counts $\dfrac{Z(Z-1)}{2}$ pairs: $\propto Z(Z-1)$ → (2).
    (S) Binding energy per nucleon is nearly constant ($\approx 8$ MeV) for $A = 30$–$170$ → (4).
    (Continuous x-ray energy depends on the accelerating voltage, not on $Z$ in these forms.)
    Match: P→5, Q→1, R→2, S→4 → (C).

  3. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 1 · Question 31
    List-I shows different radioactive decay processes and List-II provides possible emitted particles. Match each entry in List-I with an appropriate entry from List-II, and choose the correct option.

    List-IList-II
    (P) ${}^{238}_{92}U \to {}^{234}_{91}Pa$(1) one $\alpha$ particle and one $\beta^+$ particle
    (Q) ${}^{214}_{82}Pb \to {}^{210}_{82}Pb$(2) three $\beta^-$ particles and one $\alpha$ particle
    (R) ${}^{210}_{81}Tl \to {}^{206}_{82}Pb$(3) two $\beta^-$ particles and one $\alpha$ particle
    (S) ${}^{228}_{91}Pa \to {}^{224}_{88}Ra$(4) one $\alpha$ particle and one $\beta^-$ particle
    (5) one $\alpha$ particle and two $\beta^+$ particles

    1. P → 4, Q → 3, R → 2, S → 1
    2. P → 4, Q → 1, R → 2, S → 5
    3. P → 5, Q → 3, R → 1, S → 4
    4. P → 5, Q → 1, R → 3, S → 2
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Each mass number falls by $4$, so each process has one $\alpha$ (which lowers $Z$ by 2); the remaining change in $Z$ is made by $\beta$ particles.
    (P) $92 \to 90$ by $\alpha$, then $+1$: one $\beta^-$ → (4).
    (Q) $82 \to 80$, then $+2$: two $\beta^-$ → (3).
    (R) $81 \to 79$, then $+3$: three $\beta^-$ → (2).
    (S) $91 \to 89$, then $-1$: one $\beta^+$ → (1).
    Answer (A).

  4. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 2 · Question 29
    In a radioactive decay process, the activity is defined as $A = -\dfrac{dN}{dt}$, where $N(t)$ is the number of radioactive nuclei at time $t$. Two radioactive sources, $S_1$ and $S_2$ have same activity at time $t = 0$. At a later time, the activities of $S_1$ and $S_2$ are $A_1$ and $A_2$, respectively. When $S_1$ and $S_2$ have just completed their 3rd and 7th half-lives, respectively, the ratio $A_1/A_2$ is ________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 16

    Activity halves every half-life: $A_1 = \dfrac{A_0}{2^3}$ and $A_2 = \dfrac{A_0}{2^7}$.
    $\dfrac{A_1}{A_2} = 2^4 = 16$.

  5. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 1 · Question 20
    The minimum kinetic energy needed by an alpha particle to cause the nuclear reaction ${}^{16}_{7}\mathrm{N}+{}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He}\to{}^{1}_{1}\mathrm{H}+{}^{19}_{8}\mathrm{O}$ in a laboratory frame is $n$ (in MeV). Assume that ${}^{16}_{7}\mathrm{N}$ is at rest in the laboratory frame. The masses of ${}^{16}_{7}\mathrm{N}$, ${}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He}$, ${}^{1}_{1}\mathrm{H}$ and ${}^{19}_{8}\mathrm{O}$ can be taken to be $16.006\ u$, $4.003\ u$, $1.008\ u$ and $19.003\ u$, respectively, where $1\ u=930\ \mathrm{MeV}c^{-2}$. The value of $n$ is ______.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 2.3 to 2.35

    $Q=(16.006+4.003-1.008-19.003)\,u\,c^2=-0.002\times930=-1.86$ MeV.
    At threshold the products move together with the centre of mass, so the alpha must supply $|Q|$ plus the KE of the centre of mass: $K_{th}=|Q|\left(1+\dfrac{m_\alpha}{m_N}\right)$.
    $K_{th}=1.86\left(1+\dfrac{4.003}{16.006}\right)=1.86\times1.2501\approx2.33$ MeV.

  6. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 1 · Question 32
    The binding energy of nucleons in a nucleus can be affected by the pairwise Coulomb repulsion. Assume that all nucleons are uniformly distributed inside the nucleus. Let the binding energy of a proton be $E_b^p$ and the binding energy of a neutron be $E_b^n$ in the nucleus.
    Which of the following statement(s) is(are) correct?

    1. $E_b^p-E_b^n$ is proportional to $Z(Z-1)$ where $Z$ is the atomic number of the nucleus.
    2. $E_b^p-E_b^n$ is proportional to $A^{-\frac13}$ where $A$ is the mass number of the nucleus.
    3. $E_b^p-E_b^n$ is positive.
    4. $E_b^p$ increases if the nucleus undergoes a beta decay emitting a positron.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (b), (d)

    Apart from the Coulomb term, protons and neutrons are bound alike; the difference comes from the electrostatic energy of the $Z$ protons in a uniform sphere of radius $R=R_0A^{1/3}$: $E_C=\dfrac35\dfrac{Z(Z-1)e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0R_0A^{1/3}}$.
    This repulsion lowers the binding of the protons: $E_b^p-E_b^n=-E_C\propto Z(Z-1)A^{-1/3}$ — (A) and (B) correct; it is negative — (C) wrong.
    Positron emission lowers $Z$ by 1 with $A$ unchanged, so the Coulomb reduction is smaller and $E_b^p$ increases — (D) correct.

  7. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 2 · Question 20
    In a radioactive decay chain reaction, ${}^{230}_{90}\mathrm{Th}$ nucleus decays into ${}^{214}_{84}\mathrm{Po}$ nucleus. The ratio of the number of $\alpha$ to number of $\beta^-$ particles emitted in this process is ________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 2

    Mass number falls by $230 - 214 = 16$, so there are $4$ $\alpha$ decays.
    These lower $Z$ by $8$, but $Z$ falls only by $90 - 84 = 6$, so there are $2$ $\beta^-$ decays.
    Ratio $= 4 : 2 = 2$.

  8. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 23
    A heavy nucleus $Q$ of half-life 20 minutes undergoes alpha-decay with probability of 60% and beta-decay with probability of 40%. Initially, the number of $Q$ nuclei is 1000. The number of alpha-decays of $Q$ in the first one hour is

    1. 50
    2. 75
    3. 350
    4. 525
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    One hour is three half-lives, so the nuclei left are $1000 \times \left(\dfrac12\right)^3 = 125$.
    Total decays $= 1000 - 125 = 875$.
    Alpha-decays $= 0.6 \times 875 = 525$ → (D).

  9. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 2 · Question 24
    A heavy nucleus $N$, at rest, undergoes fission $N \to P + Q$, where $P$ and $Q$ are two lighter nuclei. Let $\delta = M_N - M_P - M_Q$, where $M_P$, $M_Q$ and $M_N$ are the masses of $P$, $Q$ and $N$, respectively. $E_P$ and $E_Q$ are the kinetic energies of $P$ and $Q$, respectively. The speeds of $P$ and $Q$ are $v_P$ and $v_Q$, respectively. If $c$ is the speed of light, which of the following statement(s) is(are) correct?

    1. $E_P + E_Q = c^2\delta$
    2. $E_P = \left(\dfrac{M_P}{M_P + M_Q}\right)c^2\delta$
    3. $\dfrac{v_P}{v_Q} = \dfrac{M_Q}{M_P}$
    4. The magnitude of momentum for $P$ as well as $Q$ is $c\sqrt{2\mu\delta}$, where $\mu = \dfrac{M_PM_Q}{(M_P + M_Q)}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (c), (d)

    Energy released: $E_P + E_Q = \delta c^2$ → (A) true.
    Momentum: $M_Pv_P = M_Qv_Q = p$, so $\dfrac{v_P}{v_Q} = \dfrac{M_Q}{M_P}$ → (C) true.
    $E = \dfrac{p^2}{2M}$, so $\dfrac{E_P}{E_Q} = \dfrac{M_Q}{M_P}$ and $E_P = \dfrac{M_Q}{M_P + M_Q}\,\delta c^2$ → (B) false.
    $p^2 = 2M_PE_P = \dfrac{2M_PM_Q}{M_P + M_Q}\,\delta c^2 = 2\mu\delta c^2$, so $p = c\sqrt{2\mu\delta}$ → (D) true.
    Answer (A), (C), (D).
