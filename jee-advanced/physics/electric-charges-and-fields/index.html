  1. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 26
    Consider an electric dipole comprising two charges $+q$ and $-q$ each with mass $m$, separated by a fixed distance $d$ and initially at rest with its dipole moment pointing along $\hat{\imath}$. A uniform electric field $E\hat{\jmath}$ is turned on at time $t = 0$ and it is turned off at $t = t_f$, when the dipole moment makes an angle $\theta_f$ with $\hat{\imath}$. Neglecting any sources of energy loss, correct option(s) is/are:

    1. The center of mass of the dipole is deflected towards $\hat{\jmath}$ in the presence of the field.
    2. If the magnitude of the final angular velocity $\omega_f = \sqrt{\frac{2qE}{md}}$, then $\theta_f = \frac{\pi}{6}$.
    3. If $\theta_f = \pi/3$, then the change in kinetic energy of the dipole is given by $2\sqrt{3}\,qEd$.
    4. For $\theta_f = \pi/4$, the dipole rotates around its center of mass with a constant angular velocity after $t > t_f$.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b), (d); the final key also accepts (d)

    Net force on the dipole in a uniform field is zero, so the centre of mass stays at rest — (A) false.
    Work done by the field when $\vec p$ turns from $\hat\imath$ to angle $\theta_f$: $\Delta K = pE\sin\theta_f = qEd\sin\theta_f$.
    For $\theta_f=\pi/3$: $\Delta K = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}qEd \neq 2\sqrt3\,qEd$ — (C) false.
    $I_{cm} = 2m(d/2)^2 = \tfrac{md^2}{2}$; $\tfrac12 I\omega_f^2 = qEd\sin\theta_f \Rightarrow \omega_f^2 = \dfrac{4qE\sin\theta_f}{md}$.
    $\omega_f^2 = \dfrac{2qE}{md} \Rightarrow \sin\theta_f = \tfrac12$, i.e. $\theta_f = \pi/6$ on the first swing — (B).
    After the field is off there is no torque or force, so the dipole spins about its CM with constant $\omega$ — (D) true.

    Note: Official final key: BD or D

  2. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 1 · Question 30
    List-I shows four configurations, each consisting of a pair of ideal electric dipoles. Each dipole has a dipole moment of magnitude $p$, oriented as marked by arrows in the figures. In all the configurations the dipoles are fixed such that they are at a distance $2r$ apart along the $x$ direction. The midpoint of the line joining the two dipoles is $X$. The possible resultant electric fields $\vec{E}$ at $X$ are given in List-II.
    Choose the option that describes the correct match between the entries in List-I to those in List-II.

    List-IList-II
    (P) Configuration (P) in the figure(1) $\vec{E} = 0$
    (Q) Configuration (Q) in the figure(2) $\vec{E} = -\dfrac{p}{2\pi\epsilon_0r^3}\hat{j}$
    (R) Configuration (R) in the figure(3) $\vec{E} = -\dfrac{p}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^3}(\hat{i} - \hat{j})$
    (S) Configuration (S) in the figure(4) $\vec{E} = \dfrac{p}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^3}(2\hat{i} - \hat{j})$
    (5) $\vec{E} = \dfrac{p}{\pi\epsilon_0r^3}\hat{i}$

    1. P→3, Q→1, R→2, S→4
    2. P→4, Q→5, R→3, S→1
    3. P→2, Q→1, R→4, S→5
    4. P→2, Q→1, R→3, S→5
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    At distance $r$: on the axis of a dipole $\vec E = \dfrac{2\vec p}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^3}$; on its equatorial line $\vec E = -\dfrac{\vec p}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^3}$. Let $E_0 = \dfrac{p}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^3}$.
    (P) both along $+\hat j$, X on both equatorial lines: $\vec E = -2E_0\hat j = -\dfrac{p}{2\pi\epsilon_0r^3}\hat j$ → (2).
    (Q) $+\hat j$ and $-\hat j$: the fields cancel, $\vec E = 0$ → (1).
    (R) left dipole along $+\hat j$ (equatorial): $-E_0\hat j$; right dipole along $+\hat i$ (X on its axis): $+2E_0\hat i$. $\vec E = E_0(2\hat i - \hat j)$ → (4).
    (S) both along $+\hat i$, X on both axes: $\vec E = 4E_0\hat i = \dfrac{p}{\pi\epsilon_0r^3}\hat i$ → (5).
    Match: P→2, Q→1, R→4, S→5 → (C).

  3. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 2 · Question 18
    Two co-axial conducting cylinders of same length $\ell$ with radii $\sqrt{2}R$ and $2R$ are kept, as shown in Fig. 1. The charge on the inner cylinder is $Q$ and the outer cylinder is grounded. The annular region between the cylinders is filled with a material of dielectric constant $\kappa=5$. Consider an imaginary plane of the same length $\ell$ at a distance $R$ from the common axis of the cylinders. This plane is parallel to the axis of the cylinders. The cross-sectional view of this arrangement is shown in Fig. 2. Ignoring edge effects, the flux of the electric field through the plane is ($\epsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space):

    1. $\dfrac{Q}{30\epsilon_0}$
    2. $\dfrac{Q}{15\epsilon_0}$
    3. $\dfrac{Q}{60\epsilon_0}$
    4. $\dfrac{Q}{120\epsilon_0}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    The field is non-zero only between the cylinders: $E=\dfrac{Q}{2\pi\kappa\epsilon_0\ell r}$, radial (zero inside the inner conductor and outside the grounded outer one).
    For a radial field of a line charge, the flux through a strip equals $\dfrac{Q}{\kappa\epsilon_0}\times\dfrac{\text{angle subtended at the axis}}{2\pi}$.
    On the plane (distance $R$), $\sqrt2R\le r\le2R$ corresponds to angles from the perpendicular between $45^\circ$ ($\cos^{-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}$) and $60^\circ$ ($\cos^{-1}\frac12$): $15^\circ$ on each side, $30^\circ=\dfrac\pi6$ in total.
    Flux $=\dfrac{Q}{5\epsilon_0}\times\dfrac{1}{12}=\dfrac{Q}{60\epsilon_0}$. Answer (C).

  4. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 2 · Question 23
    Six infinitely large and thin non-conducting sheets are fixed in configurations I and II. As shown in the figure, the sheets carry uniform surface charge densities which are indicated in terms of $\sigma_0$. The separation between any two consecutive sheets is $1\ \mu$m. The various regions between the sheets are denoted as 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5. If $\sigma_0=9\ \mu$C/m$^2$, then which of the following statements is/are correct:
    (Take permittivity of free space $\epsilon_0=9\times10^{-12}$ F/m)

    1. In region 4 of the configuration I, the magnitude of the electric field is zero.
    2. In region 3 of the configuration II, the magnitude of the electric field is $\dfrac{\sigma_0}{\epsilon_0}$.
    3. Potential difference between the first and the last sheets of the configuration I is 5 V.
    4. Potential difference between the first and the last sheets of the configuration II is zero.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Field between sheets $=\dfrac{(\text{charge to the left})-(\text{charge to the right})}{2\epsilon_0}$ (per unit area, taken positive to the right).
    Configuration I: regions 1, 3, 5 have $E=\dfrac{\sigma_0-(-\sigma_0)}{2\epsilon_0}=\dfrac{\sigma_0}{\epsilon_0}$; regions 2, 4 have $E=0$ (A true).
    PD (I) $=3\times\dfrac{\sigma_0}{\epsilon_0}\times10^{-6}=3\times10^6\times10^{-6}=3$ V (C false).
    Configuration II: in every region $|E|=\dfrac{\sigma_0}{2\epsilon_0}$, pointing right in 1, 3, 5 and left in 2, 4. So region 3 has $\dfrac{\sigma_0}{2\epsilon_0}$ (B false).
    PD (II) $=\dfrac{\sigma_0}{2\epsilon_0}\times10^{-6}\times(1-1+1-1+1)=0.5$ V $\ne0$ (D false).
    Answer (A).

  5. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 1 · Question 20
    Two beads, each with charge $q$ and mass $m$, are on a horizontal, frictionless, non-conducting, circular hoop of radius $R$. One of the beads is glued to the hoop at some point, while the other one performs small oscillations about its equilibrium position along the hoop. The square of the angular frequency of the small oscillations is given by
    [$\varepsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space.]

    1. $q^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 R^3 m)$
    2. $q^2/(32\pi\varepsilon_0 R^3 m)$
    3. $q^2/(8\pi\varepsilon_0 R^3 m)$
    4. $q^2/(16\pi\varepsilon_0 R^3 m)$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Equilibrium: the free bead is diametrically opposite the fixed one.
    Displace it by an angle $\theta$ (measured from the centre) along the hoop; the separation becomes $2R\cos\dfrac\theta2$.
    $U(\theta) = \dfrac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\cdot 2R\cos(\theta/2)} \approx \dfrac{q^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 R}\left(1 + \dfrac{\theta^2}{8}\right)$.
    Kinetic energy $\dfrac12 mR^2\dot\theta^2$, so $\omega^2 = \dfrac{U''(0)}{mR^2} = \dfrac{q^2/(32\pi\varepsilon_0 R)}{mR^2} = \dfrac{q^2}{32\pi\varepsilon_0 R^3 m}$ → (B).

  6. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 2 · Question 22
    A small electric dipole $\vec p_0$, having a moment of inertia $I$ about its center, is kept at a distance $r$ from the center of a spherical shell of radius $R$. The surface charge density $\sigma$ is uniformly distributed on the spherical shell. The dipole is initially oriented at a small angle $\theta$ as shown in the figure. While staying at a distance $r$, the dipole is free to rotate about its center.
    If released from rest, then which of the following statement(s) is(are) correct?
    [$\varepsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space.]

    1. The dipole will undergo small oscillations at any finite value of $r$.
    2. The dipole will undergo small oscillations at any finite value of $r>R$.
    3. The dipole will undergo small oscillations with an angular frequency of $\sqrt{\dfrac{2\sigma p_0}{\epsilon_0 I}}$ at $r=2R$.
    4. The dipole will undergo small oscillations with an angular frequency of $\sqrt{\dfrac{\sigma p_0}{100\epsilon_0 I}}$ at $r=10R$.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b), (d)

    Inside the shell ($r<R$) the field is zero, so there is no torque and no oscillation → (A) false.
    Outside, $E=\dfrac{\sigma R^2}{\epsilon_0r^2}$ (radial), and the restoring torque $p_0E\sin\theta\approx p_0E\theta$ gives SHM for any $r>R$ → (B) true.
    $\omega=\sqrt{\dfrac{p_0E}{I}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{\sigma p_0R^2}{\epsilon_0Ir^2}}$.
    $r=2R$: $\omega=\sqrt{\dfrac{\sigma p_0}{4\epsilon_0I}}$ → (C) false. $r=10R$: $\omega=\sqrt{\dfrac{\sigma p_0}{100\epsilon_0I}}$ → (D) true.
    Answer: (B), (D).

  7. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 2 · Question 27
    A charge is kept at the central point P of a cylindrical region. The two edges subtend a half-angle $\theta$ at P, as shown in the figure. When $\theta=30^\circ$, then the electric flux through the curved surface of the cylinder is $\Phi$. If $\theta=60^\circ$, then the electric flux through the curved surface becomes $\Phi/\sqrt n$, where the value of $n$ is______.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 3

    Each flat face subtends a cone of half-angle $\theta$, solid angle $2\pi(1-\cos\theta)$, so it receives a fraction $\dfrac{1-\cos\theta}{2}$ of the total flux $\dfrac{q}{\epsilon_0}$.
    Curved surface: $\dfrac{q}{\epsilon_0}\left[1-2\cdot\dfrac{1-\cos\theta}{2}\right]=\dfrac{q}{\epsilon_0}\cos\theta$.
    $\theta=30^\circ$: $\Phi=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\dfrac{q}{\epsilon_0}$; $\theta=60^\circ$: $\dfrac12\dfrac{q}{\epsilon_0}=\dfrac{\Phi}{\sqrt3}$.
    $n=3$.

  8. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 1 · Question 31
    Six charges are placed around a regular hexagon of side length $a$ as shown in the figure. Five of them have charge $q$, and the remaining one has charge $x$. The perpendicular from each charge to the nearest hexagon side passes through the center O of the hexagon and is bisected by the side.
    Which of the following statement(s) is(are) correct in SI units?

    1. When $x=q$, the magnitude of the electric field at O is zero.
    2. When $x=-q$, the magnitude of the electric field at O is $\dfrac{q}{6\pi\epsilon_0a^2}$.
    3. When $x=2q$, the potential at O is $\dfrac{7q}{4\sqrt3\pi\epsilon_0a}$.
    4. When $x=-3q$, the potential at O is $-\dfrac{3q}{4\sqrt3\pi\epsilon_0a}$.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (b), (c)

    Distance from O to each side is $\dfrac{\sqrt3a}{2}$, so each charge is at $r=\sqrt3a$ from O, the six placed symmetrically.
    (A) Six equal charges symmetric about O: $E=0$ — correct.
    (B) $x=-q$ is $q$ plus $-2q$ at that point: $E=\dfrac{2q}{4\pi\epsilon_0(3a^2)}=\dfrac{q}{6\pi\epsilon_0a^2}$ — correct.
    (C) $V=\dfrac{5q+2q}{4\pi\epsilon_0\sqrt3a}=\dfrac{7q}{4\sqrt3\pi\epsilon_0a}$ — correct.
    (D) $V=\dfrac{5q-3q}{4\pi\epsilon_0\sqrt3a}=+\dfrac{2q}{4\sqrt3\pi\epsilon_0a}$ — wrong.

  9. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 2 · Question 24
    A charge $q$ is surrounded by a closed surface consisting of an inverted cone of height $h$ and base radius $R$, and a hemisphere of radius $R$ as shown in the figure. The electric flux through the conical surface is $\dfrac{nq}{6\epsilon_0}$ (in SI units). The value of $n$ is ________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 3

    The charge sits at the centre of the common circular face, so the hemisphere subtends exactly half of the full solid angle: flux through it $= \dfrac{q}{2\epsilon_0}$.
    Total flux through the closed surface is $\dfrac{q}{\epsilon_0}$ (Gauss), so the cone carries $\dfrac{q}{\epsilon_0} - \dfrac{q}{2\epsilon_0} = \dfrac{q}{2\epsilon_0} = \dfrac{3q}{6\epsilon_0}$.
    $n = 3$.

  10. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 2 · Question 27
    In the figure, the inner (shaded) region $A$ represents a sphere of radius $r_A = 1$, within which the electrostatic charge density varies with the radial distance $r$ from the center as $\rho_A = kr$, where $k$ is positive. In the spherical shell $B$ of outer radius $r_B$, the electrostatic charge density varies as $\rho_B = \dfrac{2k}{r}$. Assume that dimensions are taken care of. All physical quantities are in their SI units.
    Which of the following statement(s) is(are) correct?

    1. If $r_B = \sqrt{\dfrac32}$, then the electric field is zero everywhere outside $B$.
    2. If $r_B = \dfrac32$, then the electric potential just outside $B$ is $\dfrac{k}{\epsilon_0}$.
    3. If $r_B = 2$, then the total charge of the configuration is $15\pi k$.
    4. If $r_B = \dfrac52$, then the magnitude of the electric field just outside $B$ is $\dfrac{13\pi k}{\epsilon_0}$.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $Q_A = \displaystyle\int_0^1 kr\cdot4\pi r^2\,dr = \pi k$; $Q_B = \displaystyle\int_1^{r_B}\frac{2k}{r}4\pi r^2\,dr = 4\pi k(r_B^2 - 1)$.
    (A) Total charge is always positive, so the field outside is never zero: false.
    (B) $r_B = \dfrac32$: $Q = \pi k + 5\pi k = 6\pi k$; $V = \dfrac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r_B} = \dfrac{6\pi k}{6\pi\epsilon_0} = \dfrac{k}{\epsilon_0}$: true.
    (C) $r_B = 2$: $Q = \pi k + 12\pi k = 13\pi k \ne 15\pi k$: false.
    (D) $r_B = \dfrac52$: $Q = \pi k + 21\pi k = 22\pi k$; $E = \dfrac{22\pi k}{4\pi\epsilon_0(25/4)} = \dfrac{22k}{25\epsilon_0} \ne \dfrac{13\pi k}{\epsilon_0}$: false.
    Answer (B).
