  1. Question 4 · Mathematics · Limits and Derivatives
    Let $f : (0,1) \to \mathbb{R}$ be the function defined as $f(x) = \sqrt{n}$ if $x \in \left[\dfrac{1}{n+1}, \dfrac{1}{n}\right)$ where $n \in \mathbb{N}$. Let $g : (0,1) \to \mathbb{R}$ be a function such that $\displaystyle\int_{x^2}^{x} \sqrt{\dfrac{1-t}{t}}\,dt < g(x) < 2\sqrt{x}$ for all $x \in (0,1)$. Then $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0} f(x)g(x)$

    1. does NOT exist
    2. is equal to $1$
    3. is equal to $2$
    4. is equal to $3$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    For $x \in \left[\dfrac{1}{n+1}, \dfrac1n\right)$: $\dfrac{n}{n+1} \le nx < 1$, so $f(x)\sqrt{x} = \sqrt{nx} \to 1$ as $x \to 0$ ($n \to \infty$).
    Upper bound: $f(x)g(x) < 2\sqrt{nx} \to 2$.
    Lower bound: for $t \le x$, $\sqrt{\dfrac{1-t}{t}} \ge \dfrac{\sqrt{1-x}}{\sqrt t}$, so $\displaystyle\int_{x^2}^{x} \ge \sqrt{1-x}\,(2\sqrt x - 2x)$, and $f(x)g(x) > 2\sqrt{nx}\,\sqrt{1-x}\,(1 - \sqrt x) \to 2$.
    By the sandwich theorem the limit is $2$. Answer (C).

  2. Question 5 · Mathematics · Three Dimensional Geometry
    Let $Q$ be the cube with the set of vertices $\left\{(x_1,x_2,x_3) \in \mathbb{R}^3 : x_1, x_2, x_3 \in \{0,1\}\right\}$. Let $F$ be the set of all twelve lines containing the diagonals of the six faces of the cube $Q$. Let $S$ be the set of all four lines containing the main diagonals of the cube $Q$; for instance, the line passing through the vertices $(0,0,0)$ and $(1,1,1)$ is in $S$. For lines $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$, let $d(\ell_1,\ell_2)$ denote the shortest distance between them. Then the maximum value of $d(\ell_1,\ell_2)$, as $\ell_1$ varies over $F$ and $\ell_2$ varies over $S$, is

    1. $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt6}$
    2. $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt8}$
    3. $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$
    4. $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{12}}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    By symmetry take $\ell_2$ through $(0,0,0)$ with direction $(1,1,1)$. A face diagonal that shares a vertex with it meets it (distance $0$).
    The others are skew, e.g. the diagonal from $(1,0,0)$ to $(0,1,0)$, direction $(-1,1,0)$.
    $(1,1,1)\times(-1,1,0) = (-1,-1,2)$, of length $\sqrt6$; joining vector $(1,0,0)$ gives $|(1,0,0)\cdot(-1,-1,2)| = 1$.
    So $d = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt6}$; every skew pair is equivalent to this one by symmetry. Answer (A).

  3. Question 21 · Physics · System of Particles and Rotational Motion
    A bar of mass $M = 1.00$ kg and length $L = 0.20$ m is lying on a horizontal frictionless surface. One end of the bar is pivoted at a point about which it is free to rotate. A small mass $m = 0.10$ kg is moving on the same horizontal surface with $5.00\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ speed on a path perpendicular to the bar. It hits the bar at a distance $L/2$ from the pivoted end and returns back on the same path with speed $v$. After this elastic collision, the bar rotates with an angular velocity $\omega$. Which of the following statement is correct?

    1. $\omega = 6.98\ \text{rad s}^{-1}$ and $v = 4.30\ \text{m s}^{-1}$
    2. $\omega = 3.75\ \text{rad s}^{-1}$ and $v = 4.30\ \text{m s}^{-1}$
    3. $\omega = 3.75\ \text{rad s}^{-1}$ and $v = 10.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}$
    4. $\omega = 6.80\ \text{rad s}^{-1}$ and $v = 4.10\ \text{m s}^{-1}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $I = \dfrac{ML^2}{3} = \dfrac{0.04}{3}\ \text{kg m}^2$; the impact point is at $\dfrac L2 = 0.1$ m.
    Angular momentum about the pivot: $0.1\times5\times0.1 = I\omega - 0.1\,v\times0.1 \Rightarrow 0.05 = \dfrac{0.04}{3}\omega - 0.01v$.
    Elastic (relative speed at the contact point reversed): $\omega\dfrac L2 + v = 5 \Rightarrow 0.1\omega + v = 5$.
    Solving: $\omega = \dfrac{10}{1.4333} = 6.98\ \text{rad s}^{-1}$, $v = 4.30\ \text{m s}^{-1}$. Answer (A).

  4. Question 23 · Physics · Thermodynamics
    One mole of an ideal gas expands adiabatically from an initial state $(T_{\mathrm A}, V_0)$ to final state $(T_{\mathrm f}, 5V_0)$. Another mole of the same gas expands isothermally from a different initial state $(T_{\mathrm B}, V_0)$ to the same final state $(T_{\mathrm f}, 5V_0)$. The ratio of the specific heats at constant pressure and constant volume of this ideal gas is $\gamma$. What is the ratio $T_{\mathrm A}/T_{\mathrm B}$?

    1. $5^{\gamma-1}$
    2. $5^{1-\gamma}$
    3. $5^{\gamma}$
    4. $5^{1+\gamma}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Adiabatic: $T_{\mathrm A}V_0^{\gamma-1} = T_{\mathrm f}(5V_0)^{\gamma-1} \Rightarrow T_{\mathrm A} = 5^{\gamma-1}T_{\mathrm f}$.
    Isothermal: $T_{\mathrm B} = T_{\mathrm f}$.
    $T_{\mathrm A}/T_{\mathrm B} = 5^{\gamma-1}$. Answer (A).

  5. Question 38 · Chemistry · The d- and f-Block Elements
    In the scheme given below, $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{Y}$, respectively, are

    $\displaystyle \text{Metal halide} \xrightarrow{\text{aq. NaOH}} \text{White precipitate }(\mathbf{P}) + \text{Filtrate }(\mathbf{Q})$
    $\displaystyle \mathbf{P} \xrightarrow[\text{heat}]{\text{aq. }\mathrm{H_2SO_4},\ \mathrm{PbO_2}\text{ (excess)}} \mathbf{X}\ \text{(a coloured species in solution)}$
    $\displaystyle \mathbf{Q} \xrightarrow[\text{warm}]{\mathrm{MnO(OH)_2},\ \text{Conc. }\mathrm{H_2SO_4}} \mathbf{Y}\ \text{(gives blue-coloration with KI-starch paper)}$

    1. $\mathrm{CrO_4^{2-}}$ and $\mathrm{Br_2}$
    2. $\mathrm{MnO_4^{2-}}$ and $\mathrm{Cl_2}$
    3. $\mathrm{MnO_4^{-}}$ and $\mathrm{Cl_2}$
    4. $\mathrm{MnSO_4}$ and $\mathrm{HOCl}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    The white precipitate with NaOH is $\mathrm{Mn(OH)_2}$, so the salt is $\mathrm{MnCl_2}$ and the filtrate contains $\mathrm{Cl^-}$.
    $\mathrm{PbO_2}$ in acid oxidises $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$ to purple $\mathrm{MnO_4^-}$: X $= \mathrm{MnO_4^-}$.
    $\mathrm{MnO(OH)_2}$ with conc. $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ oxidises $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ to $\mathrm{Cl_2}$, which liberates $\mathrm{I_2}$ from KI (blue with starch): Y $= \mathrm{Cl_2}$.
    Answer (C).

  6. Question 39 · Chemistry · Electrochemistry
    Plotting $1/\Lambda_{\mathrm m}$ against $c\Lambda_{\mathrm m}$ for aqueous solutions of a monobasic weak acid (HX) resulted in a straight line with y-axis intercept of P and slope of S. The ratio P/S is
    [$\Lambda_{\mathrm m}$ = molar conductivity, $\Lambda_{\mathrm m}^{\mathrm o}$ = limiting molar conductivity, c = molar concentration, $\mathrm{K_a}$ = dissociation constant of HX]

    1. $\mathrm{K_a}\Lambda_{\mathrm m}^{\mathrm o}$
    2. $\mathrm{K_a}\Lambda_{\mathrm m}^{\mathrm o}/2$
    3. $2\,\mathrm{K_a}\Lambda_{\mathrm m}^{\mathrm o}$
    4. $1/(\mathrm{K_a}\Lambda_{\mathrm m}^{\mathrm o})$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    With $\alpha = \dfrac{\Lambda_{\mathrm m}}{\Lambda_{\mathrm m}^{\mathrm o}}$ and $K_a = \dfrac{c\alpha^2}{1-\alpha}$: $K_a(1-\alpha) = c\alpha^2$.
    $K_a\left(1 - \dfrac{\Lambda_{\mathrm m}}{\Lambda_{\mathrm m}^{\mathrm o}}\right) = \dfrac{c\Lambda_{\mathrm m}^2}{(\Lambda_{\mathrm m}^{\mathrm o})^2}$; divide by $K_a\Lambda_{\mathrm m}$: $\dfrac{1}{\Lambda_{\mathrm m}} = \dfrac{1}{\Lambda_{\mathrm m}^{\mathrm o}} + \dfrac{c\Lambda_{\mathrm m}}{K_a(\Lambda_{\mathrm m}^{\mathrm o})^2}$.
    $P = \dfrac{1}{\Lambda_{\mathrm m}^{\mathrm o}}$, $S = \dfrac{1}{K_a(\Lambda_{\mathrm m}^{\mathrm o})^2}$, so $\dfrac PS = K_a\Lambda_{\mathrm m}^{\mathrm o}$. Answer (A).
