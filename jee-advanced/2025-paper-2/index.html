Solved questions from this paper

A few questions with their official answers and worked solutions. All 48 are in the online test.

  1. Question 1 · Mathematics · Limits and Derivatives
    Let $x_0$ be the real number such that $e^{x_0}+x_0=0$. For a given real number $\alpha$, define

    $\displaystyle g(x)=\dfrac{3xe^x+3x-\alpha e^x-\alpha x}{3(e^x+1)}$
    for all real numbers $x$.

    Then which one of the following statements is TRUE?

    1. For $\alpha=2$, $\lim_{x \to x_0}\left|\dfrac{g(x)+e^{x_0}}{x-x_0}\right|=0$
    2. For $\alpha=2$, $\lim_{x \to x_0}\left|\dfrac{g(x)+e^{x_0}}{x-x_0}\right|=1$
    3. For $\alpha=3$, $\lim_{x \to x_0}\left|\dfrac{g(x)+e^{x_0}}{x-x_0}\right|=0$
    4. For $\alpha=3$, $\lim_{x \to x_0}\left|\dfrac{g(x)+e^{x_0}}{x-x_0}\right|=\dfrac{2}{3}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $g(x)=\dfrac{3x(e^x+1)-\alpha(e^x+x)}{3(e^x+1)}=x-\dfrac{\alpha}{3}\cdot\dfrac{e^x+x}{e^x+1}$.
    At $x_0$: $e^{x_0}+x_0=0$, so $g(x_0)=x_0=-e^{x_0}$. Hence $g(x)+e^{x_0}=g(x)-g(x_0)$ and the limit is $|g'(x_0)|$.
    With $h(x)=e^x+x$ (so $h(x_0)=0$): $\dfrac{d}{dx}\dfrac{h}{e^x+1}\Big|_{x_0}=\dfrac{h'(x_0)}{e^{x_0}+1}=\dfrac{e^{x_0}+1}{e^{x_0}+1}=1$.
    So $g'(x_0)=1-\dfrac{\alpha}{3}$.
    $\alpha=2$: limit $=\dfrac13$ (A, B false); $\alpha=3$: limit $=0$ (C true, D false). Answer (C).

  2. Question 2 · Mathematics · Application of Integrals
    Let $\mathbb{R}$ denote the set of all real numbers. Then the area of the region

    $\displaystyle \left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R} : x>0,\ y>\dfrac{1}{x},\ 5x-4y-1>0,\ 4x+4y-17<0\right\}$
    is

    1. $\dfrac{17}{16}-\log_e 4$
    2. $\dfrac{33}{8}-\log_e 4$
    3. $\dfrac{57}{8}-\log_e 4$
    4. $\dfrac{17}{2}-\log_e 4$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    The region lies above $y=\dfrac1x$ and below both lines $y=\dfrac{5x-1}{4}$ and $y=\dfrac{17-4x}{4}$.
    The lines meet at $(2,\tfrac94)$; $y=\dfrac1x$ meets $5x-4y=1$ at $x=1$ ($5x^2-x-4=0$) and $4x+4y=17$ at $x=4$ ($4x^2-17x+4=0$).
    Area $=\displaystyle\int_1^2\dfrac{5x-1}{4}dx+\int_2^4\dfrac{17-4x}{4}dx-\int_1^4\dfrac{dx}{x}$.
    $=\dfrac14\left[\dfrac{5x^2}{2}-x\right]_1^2+\dfrac14\left[17x-2x^2\right]_2^4-\ln4=\dfrac{13}{8}+\dfrac{5}{2}-\ln4$.
    $=\dfrac{33}{8}-\log_e4$. Answer (B).

  3. Question 17 · Physics · Units and Measurements
    A temperature difference can generate e.m.f. in some materials. Let $S$ be the e.m.f. produced per unit temperature difference between the ends of a wire, $\sigma$ the electrical conductivity and $\kappa$ the thermal conductivity of the material of the wire. Taking $M, L, T, I$ and $K$ as dimensions of mass, length, time, current and temperature, respectively, the dimensional formula of the quantity $Z=\dfrac{S^2\sigma}{\kappa}$ is:

    1. $[M^0L^0T^0I^0K^0]$
    2. $[M^0L^0T^0I^0K^{-1}]$
    3. $[M^1L^2T^{-2}I^{-1}K^{-1}]$
    4. $[M^1L^2T^{-4}I^{-1}K^{-1}]$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Voltage: $[ML^2T^{-3}I^{-1}]$, so $[S]=[ML^2T^{-3}I^{-1}K^{-1}]$.
    Resistivity $\rho=\dfrac{RA}{l}$: $[ML^3T^{-3}I^{-2}]$, so $[\sigma]=[M^{-1}L^{-3}T^3I^2]$.
    Thermal conductivity (W m$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$): $[\kappa]=[MLT^{-3}K^{-1}]$.
    $[Z]=\dfrac{[M^2L^4T^{-6}I^{-2}K^{-2}][M^{-1}L^{-3}T^3I^2]}{[MLT^{-3}K^{-1}]}=[M^0L^0T^0I^0K^{-1}]$. Answer (B).

  4. Question 21 · Physics · Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
    A positive point charge of $10^{-8}$ C is kept at a distance of 20 cm from the center of a neutral conducting sphere of radius 10 cm. The sphere is then grounded and the charge on the sphere is measured. The grounding is then removed and subsequently the point charge is moved by a distance of 10 cm further away from the center of the sphere along the radial direction. Taking $\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}=9\times10^9$ Nm$^2$/C$^2$ (where $\epsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space), which of the following statements is/are correct:

    1. Before the grounding, the electrostatic potential of the sphere is 450 V.
    2. Charge flowing from the sphere to the ground because of grounding is $5\times10^{-9}$ C.
    3. After the grounding is removed, the charge on the sphere is $-5\times10^{-9}$ C.
    4. The final electrostatic potential of the sphere is 300 V.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (b), (c)

    The sphere is equipotential, so its potential equals that at its centre, where the induced charges (all at distance $0.1$ m) contribute $\dfrac{kq_{\text{sphere}}}{0.1}$.
    Neutral: $V=\dfrac{kq}{0.2}=\dfrac{9\times10^9\times10^{-8}}{0.2}=450$ V (A true).
    Grounded: $\dfrac{kq}{0.2}+\dfrac{kq'}{0.1}=0\Rightarrow q'=-\dfrac q2=-5\times10^{-9}$ C. So $+5\times10^{-9}$ C flows from the sphere to the ground (B true) and the sphere keeps $-5\times10^{-9}$ C after the ground is removed (C true).
    Charge moved to $0.3$ m: $V=\dfrac{kq}{0.3}+\dfrac{kq'}{0.1}=300-450=-150$ V (D false).
    Answer (A), (B), (C).

  5. Question 33 · Chemistry · Coordination Compounds
    During sodium nitroprusside test of sulphide ion in an aqueous solution, one of the ligands coordinated to the metal ion is converted to

    1. $\mathrm{NOS^-}$
    2. $\mathrm{SCN^-}$
    3. $\mathrm{SNO^-}$
    4. $\mathrm{NCS^-}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Sodium nitroprusside is $\mathrm{Na_2[Fe(CN)_5NO]}$.
    With sulphide: $\mathrm{[Fe(CN)_5NO]^{2-}+S^{2-}\rightarrow[Fe(CN)_5NOS]^{4-}}$ (violet).
    The nitrosyl ligand $\mathrm{NO^+}$ is converted to $\mathrm{NOS^-}$. Answer (A).

  6. Question 34 · Chemistry · Redox Reactions
    The complete hydrolysis of ICl, $\mathrm{ClF_3}$ and $\mathrm{BrF_5}$, respectively, gives

    1. $\mathrm{IO^-}$, $\mathrm{ClO_2^-}$ and $\mathrm{BrO_3^-}$
    2. $\mathrm{IO_3^-}$, $\mathrm{ClO_2^-}$ and $\mathrm{BrO_3^-}$
    3. $\mathrm{IO^-}$, $\mathrm{ClO^-}$ and $\mathrm{BrO_2^-}$
    4. $\mathrm{IO_3^-}$, $\mathrm{ClO_4^-}$ and $\mathrm{BrO_2^-}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    On hydrolysis the central (less electronegative) halogen keeps its oxidation state and forms an oxoacid; the more electronegative halogen gives $\mathrm{HX}$.
    $\mathrm{ICl}$: I is $+1$ → $\mathrm{HOI}$, i.e. $\mathrm{IO^-}$.
    $\mathrm{ClF_3}$: Cl is $+3$ → $\mathrm{HClO_2}$, i.e. $\mathrm{ClO_2^-}$.
    $\mathrm{BrF_5}$: Br is $+5$ → $\mathrm{HBrO_3}$, i.e. $\mathrm{BrO_3^-}$.
    Answer (A).
