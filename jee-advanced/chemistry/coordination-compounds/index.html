  1. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 1 · Question 42
    The total number of all possible isomers for the square planar complex with formula $\mathrm{K[M(NCS)(NO_2)(gly)]}$ is ____.

    (M = metal ion and gly = $\mathrm{NH_2CH_2COO^-}$)

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 8

    The complex is of type $\mathrm{M(AB)cd}$ with an unsymmetrical chelate (gly binds through N and O).
    Square planar $\mathrm{M(AB)cd}$ has 2 geometrical isomers (NCS trans to N or trans to O of gly).
    NCS$^-$ is ambidentate (–NCS / –SCN): ×2 linkage isomers.
    $\mathrm{NO_2^-}$ is ambidentate (–NO$_2$ / –ONO): ×2 linkage isomers.
    Square planar complexes here are not optically active, so total $= 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8$.

  2. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 49
    Consider that the coordinating atoms of the ligands in $cis$-$\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_4Cl_2]Cl}$ and $mer$-$\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_3Cl_3]}$ octahedral complexes are at the vertices of an octahedron. The sum of total number of the triangular faces in both the complexes having one N atom and two Cl atoms at their corners is____.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 6

    A face of an octahedron uses three mutually cis vertices; a face with 2 Cl needs a cis Cl–Cl edge, and each edge belongs to exactly 2 faces.
    cis-$\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_4Cl_2]^+}$: one cis Cl–Cl edge → 2 faces, and the third vertex of each is N → 2.
    mer-$\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_3Cl_3]}$: Cl at $+x, -x, +y$; N at $-y, \pm z$. Cis Cl–Cl edges: $(+x,+y)$ and $(-x,+y)$; each has 2 faces completed by $+z$ or $-z$ (both N) → 4.
    Total $= 2 + 4 = 6$.

  3. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 1 · Question 34
    The correct order of the wavelength maxima of the absorption band in the ultraviolet-visible region for the given complexes is

    1. $[\mathrm{Co(CN)_6}]^{3-} \lt [\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_6}]^{3+} \lt [\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5(H_2O)}]^{3+} \lt [\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5(Cl)}]^{2+}$
    2. $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5(Cl)}]^{2+} \lt [\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5(H_2O)}]^{3+} \lt [\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_6}]^{3+} \lt [\mathrm{Co(CN)_6}]^{3-}$
    3. $[\mathrm{Co(CN)_6}]^{3-} \lt [\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5(Cl)}]^{2+} \lt [\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5(H_2O)}]^{3+} \lt [\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_6}]^{3+}$
    4. $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_6}]^{3+} \lt [\mathrm{Co(CN)_6}]^{3-} \lt [\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5(Cl)}]^{2+} \lt [\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5(H_2O)}]^{3+}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    All are Co(III) complexes; $\lambda_{\max} \propto \dfrac{1}{\Delta_o}$.
    Spectrochemical series: $\mathrm{CN^-} \gt \mathrm{NH_3} \gt \mathrm{H_2O} \gt \mathrm{Cl^-}$, so $\Delta_o$: $[\mathrm{Co(CN)_6}]^{3-} \gt [\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_6}]^{3+} \gt [\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5(H_2O)}]^{3+} \gt [\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5Cl}]^{2+}$.
    $\lambda_{\max}$ follows the reverse order → (A).

  4. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 2 · Question 33
    During sodium nitroprusside test of sulphide ion in an aqueous solution, one of the ligands coordinated to the metal ion is converted to

    1. $\mathrm{NOS^-}$
    2. $\mathrm{SCN^-}$
    3. $\mathrm{SNO^-}$
    4. $\mathrm{NCS^-}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Sodium nitroprusside is $\mathrm{Na_2[Fe(CN)_5NO]}$.
    With sulphide: $\mathrm{[Fe(CN)_5NO]^{2-}+S^{2-}\rightarrow[Fe(CN)_5NOS]^{4-}}$ (violet).
    The nitrosyl ligand $\mathrm{NO^+}$ is converted to $\mathrm{NOS^-}$. Answer (A).

  5. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 2 · Question 47
    The sum of the spin only magnetic moment values (in B.M.) of $\mathrm{[Mn(Br)_6]^{3-}}$ and $\mathrm{[Mn(CN)_6]^{3-}}$ is ________.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 7.5 to 7.8

    Both contain $\mathrm{Mn^{3+}}$, $3d^4$.
    $\mathrm{Br^-}$ is a weak-field ligand: high spin $t_{2g}^3e_g^1$, 4 unpaired, $\mu=\sqrt{4\times6}=4.90$ B.M.
    $\mathrm{CN^-}$ is a strong-field ligand: low spin $t_{2g}^4$, 2 unpaired, $\mu=\sqrt{2\times4}=2.83$ B.M.
    Sum $=4.90+2.83=7.73$ B.M.

  6. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 1 · Question 38
    Among the following options, select the option in which each complex in Set-I shows geometrical isomerism and the two complexes in Set-II are ionization isomers of each other.
    [en = $\mathrm{H_2NCH_2CH_2NH_2}$]

    1. Set-I: $\mathrm{[Ni(CO)_4]}$ and $\mathrm{[PdCl_2(PPh_3)_2]}$
      Set-II: $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_5Cl]SO_4}$ and $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_5(SO_4)]Cl}$
    2. Set-I: $\mathrm{[Co(en)(NH_3)_2Cl_2]}$ and $\mathrm{[PdCl_2(PPh_3)_2]}$
      Set-II: $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_6][Cr(CN)_6]}$ and $\mathrm{[Cr(NH_3)_6][Co(CN)_6]}$
    3. Set-I: $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_3(NO_2)_3]}$ and $\mathrm{[Co(en)_2Cl_2]}$
      Set-II: $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_5Cl]SO_4}$ and $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_5(SO_4)]Cl}$
    4. Set-I: $\mathrm{[Cr(NH_3)_5Cl]Cl_2}$ and $\mathrm{[Co(en)(NH_3)_2Cl_2]}$
      Set-II: $\mathrm{[Cr(H_2O)_6]Cl_3}$ and $\mathrm{[Cr(H_2O)_5Cl]Cl_2\cdot H_2O}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    (A) $\mathrm{[Ni(CO)_4]}$ is tetrahedral: no geometrical isomers. Rejected.
    (B) Set-II differ by exchange of metals between cation and anion: coordination isomers, not ionization isomers. Rejected.
    (D) $\mathrm{[Cr(NH_3)_5Cl]^{2+}}$ ($\mathrm{MA_5B}$) has no geometrical isomers, and Set-II are hydrate isomers. Rejected.
    (C) $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_3(NO_2)_3]}$ has fac/mer isomers and $\mathrm{[Co(en)_2Cl_2]^+}$ has cis/trans isomers; in Set-II, $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ and $\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}$ swap between the coordination sphere and the counter-ion: ionization isomers → (C).

  7. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 1 · Question 45
    Among $\mathrm{V(CO)_6}$, $\mathrm{Cr(CO)_5}$, $\mathrm{Cu(CO)_3}$, $\mathrm{Mn(CO)_5}$, $\mathrm{Fe(CO)_5}$, $\mathrm{[Co(CO)_3]^{3-}}$, $\mathrm{[Cr(CO)_4]^{4-}}$, and $\mathrm{Ir(CO)_3}$, the total number of species isoelectronic with $\mathrm{Ni(CO)_4}$ is ______.
    [Given, atomic number: V = 23, Cr = 24, Mn = 25, Fe = 26, Co = 27, Ni = 28, Cu = 29, Ir = 77]

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 3

    Compare the valence electron count (metal electrons + 2 per CO + negative charge). $\mathrm{Ni(CO)_4}$: $10 + 8 = 18$.
    $\mathrm{V(CO)_6}$: $5 + 12 = 17$. $\mathrm{Cr(CO)_5}$: $6 + 10 = 16$. $\mathrm{Cu(CO)_3}$: $11 + 6 = 17$. $\mathrm{Mn(CO)_5}$: $7 + 10 = 17$.
    $\mathrm{Fe(CO)_5}$: $8 + 10 = 18$ ✓. $\mathrm{[Co(CO)_3]^{3-}}$: $9 + 6 + 3 = 18$ ✓. $\mathrm{[Cr(CO)_4]^{4-}}$: $6 + 8 + 4 = 18$ ✓. $\mathrm{Ir(CO)_3}$: $9 + 6 = 15$.
    Number of species $= 3$.

  8. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 1 · Question 47
    Among the following complexes, the total number of diamagnetic species is ______.
    $\mathrm{[Mn(NH_3)_6]^{3+}}$, $\mathrm{[MnCl_6]^{3-}}$, $\mathrm{[FeF_6]^{3-}}$, $\mathrm{[CoF_6]^{3-}}$, $\mathrm{[Fe(NH_3)_6]^{3+}}$, and $\mathrm{[Co(en)_3]^{3+}}$
    [Given, atomic number: Mn = 25, Fe = 26, Co = 27; en = $\mathrm{H_2NCH_2CH_2NH_2}$]

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 1

    $\mathrm{Mn^{3+}}$ ($d^4$): unpaired electrons in both $\mathrm{[Mn(NH_3)_6]^{3+}}$ and $\mathrm{[MnCl_6]^{3-}}$ (2 or 4, never 0): paramagnetic.
    $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ ($d^5$): always at least one unpaired electron: $\mathrm{[FeF_6]^{3-}}$ and $\mathrm{[Fe(NH_3)_6]^{3+}}$ paramagnetic.
    $\mathrm{Co^{3+}}$ ($d^6$): $\mathrm{F^-}$ is a weak field ligand, high spin $t_{2g}^4e_g^2$: paramagnetic. en is a strong field ligand, low spin $t_{2g}^6$: diamagnetic.
    Only $\mathrm{[Co(en)_3]^{3+}}$ is diamagnetic: answer $1$.

  9. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 2 · Question 46
    Among $\mathrm{[Co(CN)_4]^{4-}}$, $\mathrm{[Co(CO)_3(NO)]}$, $\mathrm{XeF_4}$, $\mathrm{[PCl_4]^+}$, $\mathrm{[PdCl_4]^{2-}}$, $\mathrm{[ICl_4]^-}$, $\mathrm{[Cu(CN)_4]^{3-}}$ and $\mathrm{P_4}$ the total number of species with tetrahedral geometry is _____.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 4 or 5

    Tetrahedral: $\mathrm{[Co(CO)_3(NO)]}$ (18-electron, $sp^3$, like $\mathrm{Ni(CO)_4}$), $\mathrm{[PCl_4]^+}$ ($sp^3$), $\mathrm{[Cu(CN)_4]^{3-}}$ ($\mathrm{Cu^+}$, $d^{10}$, $sp^3$), $\mathrm{P_4}$ (tetrahedron of P atoms): $4$.
    Square planar: $\mathrm{XeF_4}$, $\mathrm{[PdCl_4]^{2-}}$ ($d^8$, $dsp^2$), $\mathrm{[ICl_4]^-}$.
    $\mathrm{[Co(CN)_4]^{4-}}$ (Co(0), $d^9$) is not clearly assigned; counting it as tetrahedral gives $5$.
    Answer: $4$ (the official key also accepts $5$).

    Note: Official final key: 4 or 5 (either value is awarded marks).

  10. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 2 · Question 50
    When potassium iodide is added to an aqueous solution of potassium ferricyanide, a reversible reaction is observed in which a complex $\mathbf{P}$ is formed. In a strong acidic medium, the equilibrium shifts completely towards $\mathbf{P}$. Addition of zinc chloride to $\mathbf{P}$ in a slightly acidic medium results in a sparingly soluble complex $\mathbf{Q}$.

    The number of moles of potassium iodide required to produce two moles of $\mathbf{P}$ is _____.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 2

    $\mathrm{2K_3[Fe(CN)_6]+2KI\rightleftharpoons 2K_4[Fe(CN)_6]+I_2}$; in strong acid the equilibrium shifts fully to the right.
    $\mathbf{P}$ $=\mathrm{K_4[Fe(CN)_6]}$; one KI is needed per $\mathbf{P}$.
    For two moles of $\mathbf{P}$: $2$ moles of KI.

  11. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 2 · Question 51
    When potassium iodide is added to an aqueous solution of potassium ferricyanide, a reversible reaction is observed in which a complex $\mathbf{P}$ is formed. In a strong acidic medium, the equilibrium shifts completely towards $\mathbf{P}$. Addition of zinc chloride to $\mathbf{P}$ in a slightly acidic medium results in a sparingly soluble complex $\mathbf{Q}$.

    The number of zinc ions present in the molecular formula of $\mathbf{Q}$ is _____.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 2 or 3

    $\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}$ precipitates ferrocyanide: $\mathrm{K_4[Fe(CN)_6]+2ZnCl_2\to Zn_2[Fe(CN)_6]\downarrow+4KCl}$, so $\mathbf{Q}$ $=\mathrm{Zn_2[Fe(CN)_6]}$ with $2$ zinc ions.
    With excess ferrocyanide the double salt $\mathrm{K_2Zn_3[Fe(CN)_6]_2}$ (3 zinc ions) forms instead.
    Answer: $2$ (the official key also accepts $3$).

    Note: Official final key: 2 or 3 (either value is awarded marks).

  12. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 1 · Question 49
    Match the electronic configurations in List-I with appropriate metal complex ions in List-II and choose the correct option.
    [Atomic Number: Fe = 26, Mn = 25, Co = 27]

    List-IList-II
    (P) $t_{2g}^6\,e_g^0$(1) $\mathrm{[Fe(H_2O)_6]^{2+}}$
    (Q) $t_{2g}^3\,e_g^2$(2) $\mathrm{[Mn(H_2O)_6]^{2+}}$
    (R) $e^2\,t_2^3$(3) $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+}}$
    (S) $t_{2g}^4\,e_g^2$(4) $\mathrm{[FeCl_4]^-}$
    (5) $\mathrm{[CoCl_4]^{2-}}$

    1. P → 1; Q → 4; R → 2; S → 3
    2. P → 1; Q → 2; R → 4; S → 5
    3. P → 3; Q → 2; R → 5; S → 1
    4. P → 3; Q → 2; R → 4; S → 1
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    (P) Low-spin $d^6$ octahedral: $\mathrm{Co^{3+}}$ with strong-field $\mathrm{NH_3}$, $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+}}$ → (3).
    (Q) High-spin $d^5$ octahedral: $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$ with weak-field water → (2).
    (R) Tetrahedral $d^5$ ($e^2t_2^3$): $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ in $\mathrm{[FeCl_4]^-}$ → (4). ($\mathrm{[CoCl_4]^{2-}}$ is $d^7$: $e^4t_2^3$.)
    (S) High-spin $d^6$ octahedral: $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$ in $\mathrm{[Fe(H_2O)_6]^{2+}}$ → (1).
    Answer (D).

  13. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 2 · Question 39
    The complex(es), which can exhibit the type of isomerism shown by $\mathrm{[Pt(NH_3)_2Br_2]}$, is(are)
    [en $= \mathrm{H_2NCH_2CH_2NH_2}$]

    1. $\mathrm{[Pt(en)(SCN)_2]}$
    2. $\mathrm{[Zn(NH_3)_2Cl_2]}$
    3. $\mathrm{[Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_4]}$
    4. $\mathrm{[Cr(en)_2(H_2O)(SO_4)]^+}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c), (d)

    Square planar $\mathrm{[Pt(NH_3)_2Br_2]}$ shows geometrical (cis–trans) isomerism.
    (A) $\mathrm{[Pt(en)(SCN)_2]}$: en spans cis positions, so the two SCN must be cis; no geometrical isomers.
    (B) $\mathrm{[Zn(NH_3)_2Cl_2]}$: tetrahedral, no geometrical isomers.
    (C) $\mathrm{[Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_4]}$: octahedral $\mathrm{MA_2B_4}$, cis and trans ✓.
    (D) $\mathrm{[Cr(en)_2(H_2O)(SO_4)]^+}$: octahedral $\mathrm{M(AA)_2BC}$, cis and trans ✓.
    Answer (C), (D).

  14. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 1 · Question 53
    LIST-I contains metal species and LIST-II contains their properties.

    LIST-ILIST-II
    (I) $\mathrm{[Cr(CN)_6]^{4-}}$(P) $t_{2g}$ orbitals contain 4 electrons
    (II) $\mathrm{[RuCl_6]^{2-}}$(Q) $\mu$(spin-only) $=4.9$ BM
    (III) $\mathrm{[Cr(H_2O)_6]^{2+}}$(R) low spin complex ion
    (IV) $\mathrm{[Fe(H_2O)_6]^{2+}}$(S) metal ion in $4+$ oxidation state
    (T) $d^4$ species
    [Given: Atomic number of Cr = 24, Ru = 44, Fe = 26]
    Match each metal species in LIST-I with their properties in LIST-II, and choose the correct option

    1. I → R, T; II → P, S; III → Q, T; IV → P, Q
    2. I → R, S; II → P, T; III → P, Q; IV → Q, T
    3. I → P, R; II → R, S; III → R, T; IV → P, T
    4. I → Q, T; II → S, T; III → P, T; IV → Q, R
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    (I) $\mathrm{Cr^{2+}}$, $d^4$; $\mathrm{CN^-}$ is strong field: low spin $t_{2g}^4$ — R, T (and P).
    (II) $\mathrm{Ru^{4+}}$, $4d^4$; a $4d$ ion is low spin: $t_{2g}^4$ — P, S (and R, T).
    (III) $\mathrm{Cr^{2+}}$, $d^4$, high spin $t_{2g}^3e_g^1$: 4 unpaired, $\mu=\sqrt{24}=4.9$ BM — Q, T.
    (IV) $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$, $d^6$, high spin $t_{2g}^4e_g^2$: 4 unpaired — P, Q.
    Only option (A) contains no false pairing.

  15. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 2 · Question 53
    The reaction of $\mathrm{Pb(NO_3)_2}$ and NaCl in water produces a precipitate that dissolves upon the addition of HCl of appropriate concentration. The dissolution of the precipitate is due to the formation of

    1. $\mathrm{PbCl_2}$
    2. $\mathrm{PbCl_4}$
    3. $\mathrm{[PbCl_4]^{2-}}$
    4. $\mathrm{[PbCl_6]^{2-}}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $\mathrm{Pb(NO_3)_2 + 2NaCl \rightarrow PbCl_2\downarrow + 2NaNO_3}$.
    In excess (concentrated) HCl the precipitate dissolves as the complex ion: $\mathrm{PbCl_2 + 2Cl^- \rightarrow [PbCl_4]^{2-}}$ → (C).

  16. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 42
    The calculated spin only magnetic moments of $\mathrm{[Cr(NH_3)_6]^{3+}}$ and $\mathrm{[CuF_6]^{3-}}$ in BM, respectively, are
    (Atomic numbers of Cr and Cu are 24 and 29, respectively)

    1. 3.87 and 2.84
    2. 4.90 and 1.73
    3. 3.87 and 1.73
    4. 4.90 and 2.84
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $\mathrm{Cr^{3+}}$ is $d^3$: three unpaired electrons whatever the ligand; $\mu = \sqrt{3 \times 5} = 3.87$ BM.
    $\mathrm{Cu^{3+}}$ is $d^8$; in the octahedral complex with weak-field $\mathrm{F^-}$ the configuration is $t_{2g}^6e_g^2$, two unpaired electrons; $\mu = \sqrt{2 \times 4} = 2.84$ BM.
    Answer (A).

  17. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 57
    The total number of possible isomers for $\mathrm{[Pt(NH_3)_4Cl_2]Br_2}$ is ___.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 6

    Ionisation isomers: $\mathrm{[Pt(NH_3)_4Cl_2]Br_2}$, $\mathrm{[Pt(NH_3)_4Br_2]Cl_2}$ and $\mathrm{[Pt(NH_3)_4ClBr]ClBr}$.
    Each octahedral $\mathrm{[MA_4B_2]}$ or $\mathrm{[MA_4BC]}$ ion has cis and trans forms (neither is optically active).
    Total $= 3 \times 2 = 6$.

  18. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 2 · Question 43
    The pair(s) of complexes wherein both exhibit tetrahedral geometry is(are)
    (Note: py = pyridine
    Given: Atomic numbers of Fe, Co, Ni and Cu are 26, 27, 28 and 29, respectively)

    1. $\mathrm{[FeCl_4]^-}$ and $\mathrm{[Fe(CO)_4]^{2-}}$
    2. $\mathrm{[Co(CO)_4]^-}$ and $\mathrm{[CoCl_4]^{2-}}$
    3. $\mathrm{[Ni(CO)_4]}$ and $\mathrm{[Ni(CN)_4]^{2-}}$
    4. $\mathrm{[Cu(py)_4]^+}$ and $\mathrm{[Cu(CN)_4]^{3-}}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (b), (d)

    (A) $\mathrm{[FeCl_4]^-}$: Fe(III) $d^5$, weak-field $\mathrm{Cl^-}$, $sp^3$ tetrahedral; $\mathrm{[Fe(CO)_4]^{2-}}$: Fe($-$II) $d^{10}$, tetrahedral ✓.
    (B) $\mathrm{[Co(CO)_4]^-}$: Co($-$I) $d^{10}$, tetrahedral; $\mathrm{[CoCl_4]^{2-}}$: tetrahedral ✓.
    (C) $\mathrm{[Ni(CO)_4]}$ is tetrahedral but $\mathrm{[Ni(CN)_4]^{2-}}$ ($d^8$, strong field) is square planar ✗.
    (D) Cu(I) $d^{10}$ in both $\mathrm{[Cu(py)_4]^+}$ and $\mathrm{[Cu(CN)_4]^{3-}}$: tetrahedral ✓.
    Answer (A), (B), (D).

  19. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 2 · Question 53
    The reaction of $\mathrm{K_3[Fe(CN)_6]}$ with freshly prepared $\mathrm{FeSO_4}$ solution produces a dark blue precipitate called Turnbull's blue. Reaction of $\mathrm{K_4[Fe(CN)_6]}$ with the $\mathrm{FeSO_4}$ solution in complete absence of air produces a white precipitate $\mathbf{X}$, which turns blue in air. Mixing the $\mathrm{FeSO_4}$ solution with $\mathrm{NaNO_3}$, followed by a slow addition of concentrated $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ through the side of the test tube produces a brown ring.

    Precipitate $\mathbf{X}$ is

    1. $\mathrm{Fe_4[Fe(CN)_6]_3}$
    2. $\mathrm{Fe[Fe(CN)_6]}$
    3. $\mathrm{K_2Fe[Fe(CN)_6]}$
    4. $\mathrm{KFe[Fe(CN)_6]}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $\mathrm{K_4[Fe(CN)_6] + FeSO_4 \to K_2Fe[Fe(CN)_6] + K_2SO_4}$ in the absence of air.
    $\mathrm{K_2Fe[Fe(CN)_6]}$ (all iron Fe(II)) is white; air oxidises it to the blue Fe(III)/Fe(II) compound.
    Answer (C).

  20. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 2 · Question 54
    The reaction of $\mathrm{K_3[Fe(CN)_6]}$ with freshly prepared $\mathrm{FeSO_4}$ solution produces a dark blue precipitate called Turnbull's blue. Reaction of $\mathrm{K_4[Fe(CN)_6]}$ with the $\mathrm{FeSO_4}$ solution in complete absence of air produces a white precipitate $\mathbf{X}$, which turns blue in air. Mixing the $\mathrm{FeSO_4}$ solution with $\mathrm{NaNO_3}$, followed by a slow addition of concentrated $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ through the side of the test tube produces a brown ring.

    Among the following, the brown ring is due to the formation of

    1. $\mathrm{[Fe(NO)_2(SO_4)_2]^{2-}}$
    2. $\mathrm{[Fe(NO)_2(H_2O)_4]^{3+}}$
    3. $\mathrm{[Fe(NO)_4(SO_4)_2]}$
    4. $\mathrm{[Fe(NO)(H_2O)_5]^{2+}}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    Nitrate is reduced by $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$ in conc. $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ to NO, which replaces one water ligand of the aqua ion:
    $\mathrm{[Fe(H_2O)_6]^{2+} + NO \to [Fe(NO)(H_2O)_5]^{2+} + H_2O}$ (brown).
    Answer (D).
