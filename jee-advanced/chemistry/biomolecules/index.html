  1. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 40
    A known artificial sweetener X is composed of 4-chloro-4-deoxy-α-D-galactose and 1,6-dichloro-1,6-dideoxy-β-D-fructose joined by a glycosidic linkage. Structure of D-galactose is given below:
    The correct structure of X is

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    X is sucralose: an $\alpha(1\to2)\beta$ link between C-1 of the galactopyranose unit and C-2 (anomeric carbon) of the fructofuranose unit, as in sucrose.
    Fructose part: C-1 and C-6 both carry Cl ($\mathrm{CH_2Cl}$), so the glycosidic O must sit on C-2 itself, not on a $\mathrm{CH_2}$ group. Options (C) and (D) link through a $\mathrm{CH_2}$–O, which would need a free C-1 OH → rejected.
    Galactose part: in D-galactose the C-4 OH is axial in the $^4C_1$ chair (it differs from glucose only at C-4). So the 4-Cl must be axial.
    (B) has Cl equatorial at C-4 (gluco configuration); (A) has Cl axial with an axial (α) C-1–O bond → (A).

  2. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 45
    The correct statement(s) regarding sugars is(are)

    Given: Specific rotations of L-(−)-glucose and L-(+)-fructose are −52.5° and +92.5°, respectively.

    1. On treatment with $\mathrm{HNO_3}$, gluconic acid is oxidized to saccharic acid, whereas glucose is not oxidized to saccharic acid.
    2. Fructose gives a positive Fehling’s test because it isomerises to glucose and another aldohexose in the presence of Fehling’s reagent.
    3. Invert sugar is an equimolar mixture of D-glucose and D-fructose formed after hydrolysis of the corresponding disaccharide.
    4. Specific rotation of invert sugar is −40°.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b), (c)

    (A) $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ oxidises both CHO and the terminal $\mathrm{CH_2OH}$ of glucose, so glucose itself gives saccharic acid → false.
    (B) In alkaline Fehling’s solution fructose undergoes enediol tautomerism (Lobry de Bruyn–van Ekenstein) to glucose and mannose, which reduce $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ → true.
    (C) Hydrolysis of sucrose gives equimolar D-glucose + D-fructose = invert sugar → true.
    (D) D-enantiomers have opposite rotations: D-glucose $+52.5^\circ$, D-fructose $-92.5^\circ$. Equimolar mixture: $(52.5 - 92.5)/2 = -20^\circ$, not $-40^\circ$ → false.

  3. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 50
    In the following reaction sequence, major products X and Y are acyclic monomers.
    500 mol of X completely reacts with 500 mol of Y to give 1 mol of a single biodegradable acyclic copolymer Z as the only product. The amount of Z formed in grams is ____.

    Given:
    Atomic mass (in amu): H : 1, C : 12, N : 14, O : 16, Br : 80

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 85018 to 85138.02

    $\mathrm{CH_3I \xrightarrow{KCN} CH_3CN \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} CH_3COOH \xrightarrow{P/Br_2} BrCH_2COOH \xrightarrow{NH_3} H_2NCH_2COOH}$: X = glycine, $M = 75$.
    Caprolactam hydrolysis: Y = $\mathrm{H_2N(CH_2)_5COOH}$, $M = 131$.
    Z is a single linear chain (nylon-2–nylon-6) from 1000 monomers joined by 999 amide bonds, each releasing one $\mathrm{H_2O}$.
    Mass $= 500(75) + 500(131) - 999(18) = 37500 + 65500 - 17982 = 85018$ g.

  4. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 2 · Question 48
    A linear octasaccharide (molar mass $=1024$ g mol$^{-1}$) on complete hydrolysis produces three monosaccharides: ribose, 2-deoxyribose and glucose. The amount of 2-deoxyribose formed is 58.26 % (w/w) of the total amount of the monosaccharides produced in the hydrolyzed products. The number of ribose unit(s) present in one molecule of octasaccharide is ________.
    Use: Molar mass (in g mol$^{-1}$): ribose $=150$, 2-deoxyribose $=134$, glucose $=180$; Atomic mass (in amu): H $=1$, O $=16$

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 2

    A linear octasaccharide has 7 glycosidic links, so complete hydrolysis takes up 7 $\mathrm{H_2O}$ $=126$ g.
    Mass of monosaccharides $=1024+126=1150$ g per mole.
    2-Deoxyribose: $0.5826\times1150=670$ g $=5\times134$, i.e. 5 units.
    Remaining 3 units weigh $1150-670=480$ g: $150r+180g=480$ with $r+g=3\Rightarrow g=1$, $r=2$.
    Number of ribose units $=2$.

  5. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 1 · Question 37
    Aspartame, an artificial sweetener, is a dipeptide aspartyl phenylalanine methyl ester. The structure of aspartame is
    Structures of phenylalanine and aspartic acid are given below.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Aspartyl-phenylalanine: the $\alpha$-COOH of aspartic acid forms the peptide (amide) bond with the $\mathrm{NH_2}$ of phenylalanine; the N-terminal $\mathrm{NH_2}$ stays on the aspartyl unit.
    Methyl ester: the C-terminal COOH of phenylalanine is esterified ($\mathrm{CO_2Me}$); the side-chain $\mathrm{CH_2COOH}$ of aspartic acid stays free.
    (A) links through the side-chain (β) carboxyl; (C) is Phe–Asp (reversed); (D) has the ester on the aspartyl side chain and a free Phe COOH.
    Only (B) fits, with both residues in the natural L-configuration as drawn.

  6. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 2 · Question 44
    For a double strand DNA, one strand is given below:
    5′ – A G T C A C G T A A G T C – 3′
    The amount of energy required to split the double strand DNA into two single strands is _____ kcal $\mathrm{mol^{-1}}$.
    [Given: Average energy per H-bond for A-T base pair $=1.0$ kcal $\mathrm{mol^{-1}}$, G-C base pair $=1.5$ kcal $\mathrm{mol^{-1}}$, and A-U base pair $=1.25$ kcal $\mathrm{mol^{-1}}$. Ignore electrostatic repulsion between the phosphate groups.]

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 41

    Bases: A = 4, T = 3, G = 3, C = 3, so the duplex has $7$ A-T pairs and $6$ G-C pairs.
    A-T has 2 H-bonds, G-C has 3 H-bonds (A-U is not present in DNA).
    Energy $=7\times2\times1.0+6\times3\times1.5=14+27=41$ kcal $\mathrm{mol^{-1}}$.

  7. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 2 · Question 38
    A disaccharide X cannot be oxidised by bromine water. The acid hydrolysis of X leads to a laevorotatory solution. The disaccharide X is

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    Not oxidised by bromine water → non-reducing: both anomeric carbons are tied up in the glycosidic bond.
    Hydrolysis giving a laevorotatory solution (invert sugar) → glucose + fructose, i.e. sucrose.
    Sucrose is α-D-glucopyranose (C-1) joined to β-D-fructofuranose (C-2).
    (A) shows this glucopyranose–fructofuranose link; (B) and (C) have a free anomeric OH (reducing), and (D) gives no fructose.
    Answer (A).

  8. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 2 · Question 54
    Treatment of D-glucose with aqueous NaOH results in a mixture of monosaccharides, which are

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    In dilute alkali, D-glucose isomerises through its enediol (Lobry de Bruyn–van Ekenstein rearrangement).
    The enediol re-forms D-glucose, gives D-mannose (epimer at C-2) or D-fructose (ketose).
    Option (C) shows D-glucose, D-fructose and D-mannose → (C).

  9. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 49
    Given: D-glucose on reaction with $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ gives $\mathbf{P}$, $[\alpha]_\mathrm{D} = +52.7^\circ$ (see the figure).
    The compound(s), which on reaction with $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ will give the product having degree of rotation, $[\alpha]_\mathrm{D} = -52.7^\circ$ is(are)

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c), (d)

    $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ oxidises both ends to $\mathrm{COOH}$: P is D-glucaric acid. The product with $[\alpha]_\mathrm{D} = -52.7^\circ$ is its enantiomer, L-glucaric acid.
    Reading the OH positions (C-2 to C-5, R = right, L = left): D-glucose R, L, R, R.
    (A) L, L, R, R is D-mannose → D-mannaric acid: no.
    (B) L, L, R, L is L-gulose; its glucaric acid (read from the other end) is D-glucaric acid ($+52.7^\circ$): no.
    (C) L, R, L, L is L-glucose, the mirror image of D-glucose → L-glucaric acid: yes.
    (D) R, R, L, R is D-gulose; swapping the ends of its diacid gives L, R, L, L, i.e. L-glucaric acid: yes.
    Answer (C), (D).
