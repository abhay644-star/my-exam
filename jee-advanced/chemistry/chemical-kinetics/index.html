  1. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 1 · Question 34
    For a reversible reaction $\mathbf{R} \rightleftharpoons \mathbf{P}$, at constant temperature, both the forward and the backward reactions are first order elementary reactions with rate constants $k_\mathrm{f}$ and $k_\mathrm{b}$, respectively. At time zero, the concentration of $\mathbf{R}$ is $[\mathbf{R}]_0$ and the concentration of $\mathbf{P}$ is zero. At any given time, $[\mathbf{R}]$ and $[\mathbf{P}]$ are the concentrations of $\mathbf{R}$ and $\mathbf{P}$, respectively. If $k_\mathrm{b} = 4k_\mathrm{f}$, the correct graphical representation of the reaction is

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    At equilibrium $k_\mathrm{f}[\mathbf{R}] = k_\mathrm{b}[\mathbf{P}]$, so $[\mathbf{P}]/[\mathbf{R}] = k_\mathrm{f}/k_\mathrm{b} = 1/4$.
    With $[\mathbf{R}] + [\mathbf{P}] = [\mathbf{R}]_0$: $[\mathbf{R}]_{eq}/[\mathbf{R}]_0 = 0.8$ and $[\mathbf{P}]_{eq}/[\mathbf{R}]_0 = 0.2$.
    Both curves approach these limits exponentially with rate constant $k_\mathrm{f} + k_\mathrm{b}$ and then stay flat.
    Only graph (C) shows R levelling at 0.8 and P at 0.2.

  2. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 41
    For a first-order reaction R ⟶ P at a given temperature, $k$ is the rate constant. For this reaction, at the given temperature, the concentrations of R and P at a time $t$ are [R] and [P], respectively. The correct graphical representation(s) for this reaction is(are)

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c), (d)

    $[\mathrm R] = [\mathrm R]_0e^{-kt}$ and $[\mathrm P] = [\mathrm R]_0(1-e^{-kt})$.
    (A) [P] rises with decreasing slope and levels off at $[\mathrm R]_0$; the plotted curve is concave upward → wrong.
    (B) $\dfrac{d[\mathrm R]}{dt} = -k[\mathrm R]$ is a line with negative slope; the plot has positive slope → wrong.
    (C) $\dfrac{d[\mathrm P]}{dt} = k[\mathrm R]_0e^{-kt}$ decays exponentially from $k[\mathrm R]_0$ → correct.
    (D) $k$ is constant at fixed temperature → correct.

  3. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 2 · Question 44
    Consider a reaction $A+R\longrightarrow\text{Product}$. The rate of this reaction is measured to be $k[A][R]$. At the start of the reaction, the concentration of $R$, $[R]_0$, is 10-times the concentration of $A$, $[A]_0$. The reaction can be considered to be a pseudo first order reaction with assumption that $k[R]=k'$ is constant. Due to this assumption, the relative error (in %) in the rate when this reaction is 40 % complete, is ________.
    [$k$ and $k'$ represent corresponding rate constants]

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 4 to 4.25

    At 40% completion: $[A]=0.6[A]_0$ and $[R]=10[A]_0-0.4[A]_0=9.6[A]_0$.
    True rate $=k[A](9.6[A]_0)$; pseudo-first-order rate $=k[R]_0[A]=k[A](10[A]_0)$.
    Relative error $=\dfrac{10-9.6}{9.6}\times100\%=4.17\%$.

  4. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 1 · Question 43
    Consider the following reaction,

    $\displaystyle \mathrm{2H_2(g) + 2NO(g) \longrightarrow N_2(g) + 2H_2O(g)}$
    which follows the mechanism given below:
    $\displaystyle \mathrm{2NO(g)} \underset{k_{-1}}{\overset{k_1}{\rightleftharpoons}} \mathrm{N_2O_2(g)} \qquad (\text{fast equilibrium})$
    $\displaystyle \mathrm{N_2O_2(g) + H_2(g)} \xrightarrow{k_2} \mathrm{N_2O(g) + H_2O(g)} \qquad (\text{slow reaction})$
    $\displaystyle \mathrm{N_2O(g) + H_2(g)} \xrightarrow{k_3} \mathrm{N_2(g) + H_2O(g)} \qquad (\text{fast reaction})$
    The order of the reaction is ______.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 3

    Rate is set by the slow step: rate $= k_2[\mathrm{N_2O_2}][\mathrm{H_2}]$.
    Fast equilibrium: $[\mathrm{N_2O_2}] = \dfrac{k_1}{k_{-1}}[\mathrm{NO}]^2$.
    Rate $= \dfrac{k_1k_2}{k_{-1}}[\mathrm{NO}]^2[\mathrm{H_2}]$: order $2 + 1 = 3$.

  5. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 2 · Question 45
    A sample initially contains only U-238 isotope of uranium. With time, some of the U-238 radioactively decays into Pb-206 while the rest of it remains undisintegrated.
    When the age of the sample is $\mathbf{P}$ $\times10^8$ years, the ratio of mass of Pb-206 to that of U-238 in the sample is found to be 7. The value of $\mathbf{P}$ is _____.
    [Given: Half-life of U-238 is $4.5\times10^9$ years; $\log_e2=0.693$]

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: Dropped: full marks to everyone

    Mole ratio $\dfrac{N_{\mathrm{Pb}}}{N_{\mathrm U}}=7\times\dfrac{238}{206}=8.087$, so $\dfrac{N_0}{N}=9.087$.
    $t=\dfrac{t_{1/2}}{0.693}\ln\dfrac{N_0}{N}=\dfrac{4.5\times10^9}{0.693}\times2.207\approx1.43\times10^{10}$ years, i.e. $\mathbf{P}$ $\approx143$ (not an integer).
    If the mass ratio were taken as the atom ratio, $\dfrac{N_0}{N}=8$ (three half-lives) and $\mathbf{P}$ $=135$; the question is ambiguous.
    The official final key dropped this question (marks to all).

    Note: Dropped in the official final key: full marks to everyone.

  6. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 1 · Question 44
    The plot of $\log k_f$ versus $1/T$ for a reversible reaction $\mathrm{A(g) \rightleftharpoons P(g)}$ is shown.
    Pre-exponential factors for the forward and backward reactions are $10^{15}\ \text{s}^{-1}$ and $10^{11}\ \text{s}^{-1}$, respectively. If the value of $\log K$ for the reaction at $500$ K is $6$, the value of $|\log k_b|$ at $250$ K is ______.
    [$K$ = equilibrium constant of the reaction, $k_f$ = rate constant of forward reaction, $k_b$ = rate constant of backward reaction]

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 5

    At $500$ K ($1/T = 0.002$) the graph gives $\log k_f = 9$.
    $K = \dfrac{k_f}{k_b} \Rightarrow \log k_b = 9 - 6 = 3$ at $500$ K.
    $\log k_b = 11 - \dfrac{E_b}{2.303RT}$: $3 = 11 - \dfrac{E_b}{2.303R}(0.002) \Rightarrow \dfrac{E_b}{2.303R} = 4000$ K.
    At $250$ K: $\log k_b = 11 - \dfrac{4000}{250} = -5$, so $|\log k_b| = 5$.

  7. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 1 · Question 46
    The correct option(s) related to adsorption processes is(are)

    1. Chemisorption results in a unimolecular layer.
    2. The enthalpy change during physisorption is in the range of 100 to 140 kJ $\mathrm{mol^{-1}}$.
    3. Chemisorption is an endothermic process.
    4. Lowering the temperature favors physisorption processes.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (d)

    (A) Chemisorption involves chemical bonding with surface sites, so it forms a single (unimolecular) layer — correct.
    (B) Physisorption enthalpy is only about 20–40 kJ mol$^{-1}$; 100–140 kJ mol$^{-1}$ is typical of chemisorption — wrong.
    (C) Chemisorption, like all adsorption, is exothermic — wrong.
    (D) Physisorption is exothermic and weak, so lower temperature favours it — correct.

  8. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 1 · Question 51
    Match the rate expressions in LIST-I for the decomposition of X with the corresponding profiles provided in LIST-II. $\mathrm{X_s}$ and k are constants having appropriate units. (The five profiles of LIST-II are shown in the figure.)

    LIST-ILIST-II
    (I) $\text{rate}=\dfrac{k[\mathrm{X}]}{\mathrm{X_s}+[\mathrm{X}]}$ under all possible initial concentrations of X(P) half life ($t_{1/2}$) vs initial concentration of X: straight line rising from a positive intercept
    (II) $\text{rate}=\dfrac{k[\mathrm{X}]}{\mathrm{X_s}+[\mathrm{X}]}$ where initial concentrations of X are much less than $\mathrm{X_s}$(Q) half life ($t_{1/2}$) vs initial concentration of X: horizontal line
    (III) $\text{rate}=\dfrac{k[\mathrm{X}]}{\mathrm{X_s}+[\mathrm{X}]}$ where initial concentrations of X are much higher than $\mathrm{X_s}$(R) rate vs initial concentration of X: rising curve that levels off
    (IV) $\text{rate}=\dfrac{k[\mathrm{X}]^2}{\mathrm{X_s}+[\mathrm{X}]}$ where initial concentration of X is much higher than $\mathrm{X_s}$(S) [X] vs time: straight line falling
    (T) ln[X] vs time: straight line falling

    1. I → P; II → Q; III → S; IV → T
    2. I → R; II → S; III → S; IV → T
    3. I → P; II → Q; III → Q; IV → R
    4. I → R; II → S; III → Q; IV → R
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    (I) $\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{X_s}}{[\mathrm X]}+1\right)d[\mathrm X]=-k\,dt$ integrates to $\mathrm{X_s}\ln\dfrac{[\mathrm X]_0}{[\mathrm X]}+[\mathrm X]_0-[\mathrm X]=kt$, so $t_{1/2}=\dfrac{\mathrm{X_s}\ln2+[\mathrm X]_0/2}{k}$: linear in $[\mathrm X]_0$ with a positive intercept (P).
    (II) $[\mathrm X]\ll\mathrm{X_s}$: rate $\approx\dfrac{k}{\mathrm{X_s}}[\mathrm X]$, first order, $t_{1/2}$ independent of $[\mathrm X]_0$ (Q).
    (III) $[\mathrm X]\gg\mathrm{X_s}$: rate $\approx k$, zero order, $[\mathrm X]$ falls linearly with time (S).
    (IV) $[\mathrm X]\gg\mathrm{X_s}$: rate $\approx k[\mathrm X]$, first order, $\ln[\mathrm X]$ falls linearly with time (T).
    Answer (A).

  9. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 2 · Question 41
    For the following reaction

    $\displaystyle 2\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{Y} \xrightarrow{k} \mathbf{P}$
    the rate of reaction is $\dfrac{d[\mathbf{P}]}{dt} = k[\mathbf{X}]$. Two moles of $\mathbf{X}$ are mixed with one mole of $\mathbf{Y}$ to make 1.0 L of solution. At 50 s, 0.5 mole of $\mathbf{Y}$ is left in the reaction mixture. The correct statement(s) about the reaction is(are)
    (Use: ln 2 = 0.693)

    1. The rate constant, $k$, of the reaction is $13.86 \times 10^{-4}$ s$^{-1}$.
    2. Half-life of $\mathbf{X}$ is 50 s.
    3. At 50 s, $-\dfrac{d[\mathbf{X}]}{dt} = 13.86 \times 10^{-3}$ mol L$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$.
    4. At 100 s, $-\dfrac{d[\mathbf{Y}]}{dt} = 3.46 \times 10^{-3}$ mol L$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b), (c), (d)

    $-\dfrac{d[\mathbf X]}{dt} = 2k[\mathbf X]$, so $[\mathbf X] = 2e^{-2kt}$.
    At 50 s, 0.5 mol Y has reacted, so 1.0 mol X has reacted: $[\mathbf X]$ has halved → half-life of X $= 50$ s → (B) true.
    $2k = \dfrac{0.693}{50} = 1.386 \times 10^{-2}$, so $k = 6.93 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$ → (A) false.
    (C) At 50 s: $-\dfrac{d[\mathbf X]}{dt} = 2k \times 1 = 13.86 \times 10^{-3}$ mol L$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$ → true.
    (D) At 100 s, $[\mathbf X] = 0.5$: $-\dfrac{d[\mathbf Y]}{dt} = k[\mathbf X] = 3.465 \times 10^{-3}$ mol L$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$ → true.
    Answer (B), (C), (D).
