  1. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 1 · Question 37
    The 2s and the 2p orbital energies of hydrogen atom are $E_{2\mathrm{s}}(\mathrm{H})$ and $E_{2\mathrm{p}}(\mathrm{H})$, respectively. The 2s and the 2p orbital energies of lithium atom are $E_{2\mathrm{s}}(\mathrm{Li})$ and $E_{2\mathrm{p}}(\mathrm{Li})$, respectively. The correct option(s) about the orbital energies is(are)

    1. $E_{2\mathrm{s}}(\mathrm{Li}) \lt E_{2\mathrm{p}}(\mathrm{Li})$
    2. $E_{2\mathrm{s}}(\mathrm{H}) = E_{2\mathrm{p}}(\mathrm{H})$
    3. $E_{2\mathrm{p}}(\mathrm{H}) \lt E_{2\mathrm{s}}(\mathrm{Li})$
    4. $E_{2\mathrm{s}}(\mathrm{H}) \gt E_{2\mathrm{s}}(\mathrm{Li})$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (b), (d)

    In H (one electron) the energy depends only on $n$: $E_{2\mathrm{s}}(\mathrm{H}) = E_{2\mathrm{p}}(\mathrm{H}) = -3.4$ eV, so (B) is true.
    In Li, 2s penetrates the 1s core more than 2p, so it feels a larger effective nuclear charge: $E_{2\mathrm{s}}(\mathrm{Li}) \lt E_{2\mathrm{p}}(\mathrm{Li})$, so (A) is true.
    $E_{2\mathrm{s}}(\mathrm{Li}) \approx -5.4$ eV (minus the ionisation energy of Li), which is lower than $-3.4$ eV.
    Hence $E_{2\mathrm{s}}(\mathrm{H}) \gt E_{2\mathrm{s}}(\mathrm{Li})$, so (D) is true, and $E_{2\mathrm{p}}(\mathrm{H}) \gt E_{2\mathrm{s}}(\mathrm{Li})$, so (C) is false.

  2. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 46
    $\mathbf{X}^{a+}$ and $\mathbf{Y}^{b+}$ are hydrogen-like species. The wavelength of light absorbed during the transition between the states with principal quantum numbers $n = 1$ and $n = 2$ of $\mathbf{X}^{a+}$ is $\lambda$. The wavelength of light absorbed during the transition between the states with principal quantum numbers $n = 2$ and $n = 4$ of $\mathbf{Y}^{b+}$ is $9\lambda$. The lowest possible value of $(\boldsymbol{a}+\boldsymbol{b})$ is ____.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 3

    $\dfrac1\lambda = R_HZ^2\left(\dfrac1{n_1^2}-\dfrac1{n_2^2}\right)$.
    X: $\dfrac1\lambda = R_HZ_X^2\cdot\dfrac34$. Y: $\dfrac1{9\lambda} = R_HZ_Y^2\cdot\dfrac3{16}$.
    Dividing: $9 = \dfrac{4Z_X^2}{Z_Y^2}$ → $Z_Y = \dfrac{2}{3}Z_X$.
    Smallest integers: $Z_X = 3$ (Li$^{2+}$), $Z_Y = 2$ (He$^+$).
    For a one-electron ion the charge is $Z-1$: $a = 2$, $b = 1$, so $a+b = 3$.

  3. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 1 · Question 39
    Among the following, the correct statement(s) for electrons in an atom is(are)

    1. Uncertainty principle rules out the existence of definite paths for electrons.
    2. The energy of an electron in $2s$ orbital of an atom is lower than the energy of an electron that is infinitely far away from the nucleus.
    3. According to Bohr's model, the most negative energy value for an electron is given by n = 1, which corresponds to the most stable orbit.
    4. According to Bohr's model, the magnitude of velocity of electrons increases with increase in values of n.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (b), (c)

    (A) Position and momentum cannot both be exactly known, so a definite trajectory is meaningless. Correct.
    (B) A bound electron has negative energy; a free electron at infinity has zero energy. Correct.
    (C) $E_n \propto -\dfrac{1}{n^2}$ is most negative for $n = 1$, the most stable orbit. Correct.
    (D) $v_n \propto \dfrac{Z}{n}$ decreases as $n$ increases. Wrong.
    Answer: (A), (B), (C).

  4. JEE Advanced 2024, Paper 2 · Question 35
    According to Bohr’s model, the highest kinetic energy is associated with the electron in the

    1. first orbit of H atom
    2. first orbit of $\mathrm{He^+}$
    3. second orbit of $\mathrm{He^+}$
    4. second orbit of $\mathrm{Li^{2+}}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    In Bohr’s model $\text{KE}=13.6\,\dfrac{Z^2}{n^2}$ eV, so compare $\dfrac{Z^2}{n^2}$.
    H, $n=1$: $1$; $\mathrm{He^+}$, $n=1$: $4$; $\mathrm{He^+}$, $n=2$: $1$; $\mathrm{Li^{2+}}$, $n=2$: $\dfrac94$.
    Highest: first orbit of $\mathrm{He^+}$ → (B).

  5. JEE Advanced 2023, Paper 2 · Question 45
    For $\mathrm{He^+}$, a transition takes place from the orbit of radius 105.8 pm to the orbit of radius 26.45 pm. The wavelength (in nm) of the emitted photon during the transition is ________.
    [Use: Bohr radius, a = 52.9 pm; Rydberg constant, $R_\mathrm{H} = 2.2\times10^{-18}$ J; Planck’s constant, h = $6.6\times10^{-34}$ J s; Speed of light, c = $3\times10^8$ m s$^{-1}$]

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 30

    $r_n = \dfrac{52.9\,n^2}{Z}$ pm with $Z = 2$: 105.8 pm → $n = 2$; 26.45 pm → $n = 1$.
    $E = R_\mathrm{H}Z^2\left(1 - \dfrac14\right) = 2.2\times10^{-18}\times4\times\dfrac34 = 6.6\times10^{-18}$ J.
    $\lambda = \dfrac{hc}{E} = \dfrac{6.6\times10^{-34}\times3\times10^8}{6.6\times10^{-18}} = 3\times10^{-8}$ m $= 30$ nm.

  6. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 2 · Question 56
    Consider a helium (He) atom that absorbs a photon of wavelength 330 nm. The change in the velocity (in cm s$^{-1}$) of He atom after the photon absorption is ___.
    (Assume: Momentum is conserved when photon is absorbed.
    Use: Planck constant = $6.6 \times 10^{-34}$ J s, Avogadro number = $6 \times 10^{23}$ mol$^{-1}$, Molar mass of He = 4 g mol$^{-1}$)

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 30

    Photon momentum $p = \dfrac{h}{\lambda} = \dfrac{6.6 \times 10^{-34}}{330 \times 10^{-9}} = 2 \times 10^{-27}$ kg m s$^{-1}$.
    Mass of one He atom $= \dfrac{4 \times 10^{-3}}{6 \times 10^{23}} = \dfrac23 \times 10^{-26}$ kg.
    $\Delta v = \dfrac{p}{m} = \dfrac{2 \times 10^{-27}}{(2/3) \times 10^{-26}} = 0.3$ m s$^{-1}$ $= 30$ cm s$^{-1}$.
