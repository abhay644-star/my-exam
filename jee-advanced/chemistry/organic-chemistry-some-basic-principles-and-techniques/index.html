  1. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 1 · Question 36
    Considering $\mathrm{LiBH_4}$ reduces an ester group to the corresponding alcohol and does not reduce a carboxylic acid group, the correct statement about the major products P, Q, R and S is

    1. P & Q are identical, and R & S are diastereomers.
    2. P & Q are diastereomers, and R & S are identical.
    3. P & Q are diastereomers, and R & S are diastereomers.
    4. P & Q are identical, and R & S are identical.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $\mathrm{LiBH_4}$ turns the $\mathrm{CH_2CO_2Et}$ arm (wedge) into $\mathrm{CH_2CH_2OH}$; $\mathrm{BH_3}$ turns the $\mathrm{CH_2CO_2H}$ arm (dash) into $\mathrm{CH_2CH_2OH}$. So each pair of products differs only in which face carries the $\mathrm{CH_2CH_2OH}$ arm.
    Top substrate (1,2-disubstituted cyclobutane): in P the $\mathrm{CH_2CH_2OH}$ arm is on the wedge side; in Q it is on the dash side. The $\mathrm{CH_3}$-bearing carbon keeps its configuration, so C1 is inverted while C2 is unchanged: P and Q are diastereomers.
    Bottom substrate (1,3-disubstituted): in R the $\mathrm{CH_2CH_2OH}$ arm is cis to $\mathrm{CH_3}$; in S it is trans to $\mathrm{CH_3}$ (the cis arm is $\mathrm{CH_2CO_2H}$).
    These are cis/trans (geometrical) isomers, so R and S are also diastereomers.

  2. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 53
    Consider the following reaction sequence in which J, K, L and M are the major products.
    Given:
    Atomic mass (in amu): H : 1, C : 12, N : 14, O : 16, S : 32, Br : 80, Ba : 137

    The volume of 1 M aqueous $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ required to completely neutralize the ammonia evolved from 5.72 g of L in Kjeldahl’s method of nitrogen estimation is ____ mL.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 10

    Friedel–Crafts acylation of m-xylene at C-4 gives $\mathrm{ArCOCH_2Cl}$ (Ar = 2,4-dimethylphenyl); NaI gives $\mathrm{ArCOCH_2I}$; m-nitrophenoxide gives J = $\mathrm{ArCOCH_2OAr'}$ (Ar′ = 3-nitrophenyl).
    $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ then $\mathrm{PBr_3}$: K = $\mathrm{ArCHBrCH_2OAr'}$; check $M = 105 + 93 + 14 + 16 + 122 = 350$ ✓.
    L = $\mathrm{ArCH(NH_2)CH_2OAr'}$, $M = 350 - 80 + 16 = 286$; 5.72 g = 0.020 mol.
    Kjeldahl's method does not convert nitro N to $\mathrm{NH_3}$, so only the amine N counts: 0.020 mol $\mathrm{NH_3}$.
    $\mathrm{2NH_3 + H_2SO_4}$: 0.010 mol $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ = 10 mL of 1 M.

  3. JEE Advanced 2026, Paper 2 · Question 54
    Consider the following reaction sequence in which J, K, L and M are the major products.
    Given:
    Atomic mass (in amu): H : 1, C : 12, N : 14, O : 16, S : 32, Br : 80, Ba : 137

    In sulphur estimation by Carius method, the amount of $\mathrm{BaSO_4}$ formed from 3.79 g of M is ____ g.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 2.33

    K = $\mathrm{ArCHBrCH_2OAr'}$ ($M = 350$), formed as in Q.17.
    PhSNa replaces Br by SPh: M = $\mathrm{ArCH(SPh)CH_2OAr'}$, $M = 350 - 80 + 109 = 379$.
    3.79 g M = 0.010 mol, containing 0.010 mol S.
    Carius: all S → $\mathrm{BaSO_4}$ ($M = 233$): $0.010\times233 = 2.33$ g.

  4. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 1 · Question 36
    Consider the depicted hydrogen ($\mathbf{H}$) in the hydrocarbons given below. The most acidic hydrogen ($\mathbf{H}$) is

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    Acidity is decided by the stability of the carbanion left after removing the marked H.
    (B): the marked H is on the $\mathrm{CH_2}$ next to the exocyclic C=C of a fulvene. The anion is allylic, and its resonance form puts the negative charge on the ring carbon, giving a ring with two C=C bonds and a lone pair: the aromatic cyclopentadienyl anion ($6\pi$ electrons). It is also a primary carbanion.
    (A), (C): removing the tertiary H gives a tertiary carbanion; the fulvene's exocyclic $=\mathrm{CH_2}$ stays outside the ring, so no resonance form gives an aromatic cyclopentadienyl anion.
    (D): the ring contains an $sp^3$ $\mathrm{CH_2}$, so the anion cannot become an aromatic cyclopentadienyl anion either.
    Most acidic: (B).

  5. JEE Advanced 2025, Paper 1 · Question 44
    The monomer (X) involved in the synthesis of Nylon 6,6 gives positive carbylamine test. If $10$ moles of X are analyzed using Dumas method, the amount (in grams) of nitrogen gas evolved is ______.
    Use: Atomic mass of N (in amu) $= 14$

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 280

    Nylon 6,6 is made from adipic acid and hexamethylenediamine; the one giving the carbylamine test (primary amine) is X $= \mathrm{H_2N(CH_2)_6NH_2}$.
    Each molecule has $2$ N atoms, so $1$ mol X gives $1$ mol $\mathrm{N_2}$ in the Dumas method.
    $10$ mol X → $10$ mol $\mathrm{N_2}$ $= 10 \times 28 = 280$ g.

  6. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 1 · Question 42
    Consider the following reaction.
    On estimation of bromine in 1.00 g of $\mathbf{R}$ using Carius method, the amount of AgBr formed (in g) is ______.
    [Given: Atomic mass of H = 1, C = 12, O = 16, P = 31, Br = 80, Ag = 108]

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 1.49 to 1.51

    Red P/$\mathrm{Br_2}$ (i.e. $\mathrm{PBr_3}$) converts the $\mathrm{CH_2OH}$ group to $\mathrm{CH_2Br}$: R is 4-bromobenzyl bromide, $\mathrm{C_7H_6Br_2}$, $M=84+6+160=250$.
    1.00 g of R $=\dfrac1{250}$ mol, containing $\dfrac2{250}$ mol Br.
    AgBr ($M=188$): $\dfrac{2}{250}\times188=1.504$ g $\approx1.50$ g.

  7. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 1 · Question 54
    Match the compounds in LIST-I with the observations in LIST-II, and choose the correct option.

    LIST-ILIST-II
    (I) Aniline(P) Sodium fusion extract of the compound on boiling with $\mathrm{FeSO_4}$, followed by acidification with conc. $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$, gives Prussian blue color.
    (II) o-Cresol(Q) Sodium fusion extract of the compound on treatment with sodium nitroprusside gives blood red color.
    (III) Cysteine(R) Addition of the compound to a saturated solution of $\mathrm{NaHCO_3}$ results in effervescence.
    (IV) Caprolactam(S) The compound reacts with bromine water to give a white precipitate.
    (T) Treating the compound with neutral $\mathrm{FeCl_3}$ solution produces violet color.

    1. I → P, Q; II → S; III → Q, R; IV → P
    2. I → P; II → R, S; III → R; IV → Q, S
    3. I → Q, S; II → P, T; III → P; IV → S
    4. I → P, S; II → T; III → Q, R; IV → P
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    (I) Aniline contains N (Prussian blue, P) and gives 2,4,6-tribromoaniline as a white precipitate with bromine water (S).
    (II) o-Cresol is a phenol: violet colour with neutral $\mathrm{FeCl_3}$ (T).
    (III) Cysteine, $\mathrm{HSCH_2CH(NH_2)COOH}$, contains sulphur, detected in the sodium fusion extract (Q as given in the paper), and its $-\mathrm{COOH}$ releases $\mathrm{CO_2}$ with $\mathrm{NaHCO_3}$ (R).
    (IV) Caprolactam (a cyclic amide) contains N: Prussian blue (P).
    Answer (D).

  8. JEE Advanced 2022, Paper 2 · Question 50
    Among the following, the correct statement(s) about polymers is(are)

    1. The polymerization of chloroprene gives natural rubber.
    2. Teflon is prepared from tetrafluoroethene by heating it with persulphate catalyst at high pressures.
    3. PVC are thermoplastic polymers.
    4. Ethene at 350-570 K temperature and 1000-2000 atm pressure in the presence of a peroxide initiator yields high density polythene.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b), (c)

    (A) Polymerisation of chloroprene gives neoprene; natural rubber is cis-polyisoprene: false.
    (B) Teflon is made by heating tetrafluoroethene with a persulphate catalyst at high pressure: true.
    (C) PVC is a thermoplastic: true.
    (D) Ethene at 350–570 K and 1000–2000 atm with a peroxide initiator gives low density polythene: false.
    Answer (B), (C).

  9. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 1 · Question 40
    Among the following, the conformation that corresponds to the most stable conformation of meso-butane-2,3-diol is

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    In (A) the two OH groups, the two Me groups and the two H atoms are each anti: this is the anti conformer of the meso diol.
    In (B) the OH groups are gauche (as are the Me groups), which is also a staggered conformer of the meso compound.
    The gauche arrangement allows an intramolecular O–H···O hydrogen bond, which outweighs the gauche Me/Me and OH/OH repulsions.
    So the most stable conformation is (B).

  10. JEE Advanced 2021, Paper 2 · Question 51
    The amount of energy required to break a bond is same as the amount of energy released when the same bond is formed. In gaseous state, the energy required for homolytic cleavage of a bond is called Bond Dissociation Energy (BDE) or Bond Strength. BDE is affected by s-character of the bond and the stability of the radicals formed. Shorter bonds are typically stronger bonds. BDEs for some bonds are given below:

    $\displaystyle \mathrm{H_3C{-}H_{(g)} \longrightarrow H_3C^{\bullet}_{(g)} + H^{\bullet}_{(g)}} \qquad \Delta H^\circ = 105\ \text{kcal mol}^{-1}$
    $\displaystyle \mathrm{Cl{-}Cl_{(g)} \longrightarrow Cl^{\bullet}_{(g)} + Cl^{\bullet}_{(g)}} \qquad \Delta H^\circ = 58\ \text{kcal mol}^{-1}$
    $\displaystyle \mathrm{H_3C{-}Cl_{(g)} \longrightarrow H_3C^{\bullet}_{(g)} + Cl^{\bullet}_{(g)}} \qquad \Delta H^\circ = 85\ \text{kcal mol}^{-1}$
    $\displaystyle \mathrm{H{-}Cl_{(g)} \longrightarrow H^{\bullet}_{(g)} + Cl^{\bullet}_{(g)}} \qquad \Delta H^\circ = 103\ \text{kcal mol}^{-1}$

    Correct match of the $\mathbf{C{-}H}$ bonds (shown in bold) in Column $\mathbf{J}$ with their BDE in Column $\mathbf{K}$ is
    Column J (Molecule)Column K (BDE (kcal mol$^{-1}$))
    (P) $\mathbf{H{-}C}\mathrm{H(CH_3)_2}$(i) 132
    (Q) $\mathbf{H{-}C}\mathrm{H_2Ph}$(ii) 110
    (R) $\mathbf{H{-}C}\mathrm{H{=}CH_2}$(iii) 95
    (S) $\mathbf{H{-}C}\mathrm{{\equiv}CH}$(iv) 88

    1. P – iii, Q – iv, R – ii, S – i
    2. P – i, Q – ii, R – iii, S – iv
    3. P – iii, Q – ii, R – i, S – iv
    4. P – ii, Q – i, R – iv, S – iii
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    BDE rises with s-character of the carbon orbital and falls with radical stability.
    (Q) benzylic C–H gives a resonance-stabilised benzyl radical: weakest, 88 → iv.
    (P) secondary C–H of propane ($sp^3$, gives a 2° radical): 95 → iii.
    (R) vinylic C–H ($sp^2$): 110 → ii.
    (S) alkynyl C–H ($sp$, 50% s-character, shortest): 132 → i.
    P–iii, Q–iv, R–ii, S–i → (A).
