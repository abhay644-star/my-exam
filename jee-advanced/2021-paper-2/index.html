  1. Question 14 · Mathematics · Sequences and Series
    Let

    $\displaystyle M = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R} : x^2 + y^2 \le r^2\},$
    where $r > 0$. Consider the geometric progression $a_n = \dfrac{1}{2^{n-1}}$, $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$. Let $S_0 = 0$ and, for $n \ge 1$, let $S_n$ denote the sum of the first $n$ terms of this progression. For $n \ge 1$, let $C_n$ denote the circle with center $(S_{n-1}, 0)$ and radius $a_n$, and $D_n$ denote the circle with center $(S_{n-1}, S_{n-1})$ and radius $a_n$.

    Consider $M$ with $r = \dfrac{(2^{199} - 1)\sqrt2}{2^{198}}$. The number of all those circles $D_n$ that are inside $M$ is

    1. $198$
    2. $199$
    3. $200$
    4. $201$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $r = \sqrt2\left(2 - 2^{-198}\right)$. The farthest point of $D_n$ from the origin is at $\sqrt2\,S_{n-1} + a_n = \sqrt2\left(2 - 2^{2-n}\right) + 2^{1-n}$.
    Condition: $\sqrt2\left(2 - 2^{2-n}\right) + 2^{1-n} \le \sqrt2\left(2 - 2^{-198}\right)$
    $\iff 2^{1-n}\left(2\sqrt2 - 1\right) \ge \sqrt2 \cdot 2^{-198} \iff 2^{199-n} \ge \dfrac{\sqrt2}{2\sqrt2 - 1} \approx 0.77$.
    This holds for $199 - n \ge 0$ and fails for $199 - n = -1$ ($0.5 < 0.77$). So $n = 1, 2, \ldots, 199$: 199 circles → (B).

  2. Question 15 · Mathematics · Application of Derivatives
    Let $\psi_1 : [0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}$, $\psi_2 : [0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}$, $f : [0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ and $g : [0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ be functions such that $f(0) = g(0) = 0$,

    $\displaystyle \psi_1(x) = e^{-x} + x, \quad x \ge 0,$
    $\displaystyle \psi_2(x) = x^2 - 2x - 2e^{-x} + 2, \quad x \ge 0,$
    $\displaystyle f(x) = \int_{-x}^{x}\left(|t| - t^2\right)e^{-t^2}\,dt, \quad x > 0$
    and
    $\displaystyle g(x) = \int_0^{x^2}\sqrt{t}\,e^{-t}\,dt, \quad x > 0.$
    Which of the following statements is TRUE ?

    1. $f\left(\sqrt{\ln 3}\right) + g\left(\sqrt{\ln 3}\right) = \dfrac13$
    2. For every $x > 1$, there exists an $\alpha \in (1, x)$ such that $\psi_1(x) = 1 + \alpha x$
    3. For every $x > 0$, there exists a $\beta \in (0, x)$ such that $\psi_2(x) = 2x\left(\psi_1(\beta) - 1\right)$
    4. $f$ is an increasing function on the interval $\left[0, \dfrac32\right]$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $f(x) = 2\int_0^x (t - t^2)e^{-t^2}dt$ (even integrand); with $t = u^2$, $g(x) = 2\int_0^x u^2e^{-u^2}du$.
    (A) $f + g = 2\int_0^x te^{-t^2}dt = 1 - e^{-x^2}$; at $x = \sqrt{\ln 3}$ this is $\dfrac23 \ne \dfrac13$ → false.
    (B) MVT on $[0, x]$: $\psi_1(x) = 1 + x\left(1 - e^{-c}\right)$ with $0 < 1 - e^{-c} < 1$, so the required $\alpha$ would be $< 1$, not in $(1, x)$ → false.
    (C) $\psi_2(0) = 0$ and $\psi_2'(x) = 2x - 2 + 2e^{-x} = 2\left(\psi_1(x) - 1\right)$. MVT: $\psi_2(x) = x\,\psi_2'(\beta) = 2x\left(\psi_1(\beta) - 1\right)$ for some $\beta \in (0, x)$ → true.
    (D) $f'(x) = 2(x - x^2)e^{-x^2} < 0$ for $x > 1$ → false.
    Answer (C).

  3. Question 21 · Physics · Waves
    A source, approaching with speed $u$ towards the open end of a stationary pipe of length $L$, is emitting a sound of frequency $f_s$. The farther end of the pipe is closed. The speed of sound in air is $v$ and $f_0$ is the fundamental frequency of the pipe. For which of the following combination(s) of $u$ and $f_s$, will the sound reaching the pipe lead to a resonance?

    1. $u = 0.8v$ and $f_s = f_0$
    2. $u = 0.8v$ and $f_s = 2f_0$
    3. $u = 0.8v$ and $f_s = 0.5f_0$
    4. $u = 0.5v$ and $f_s = 1.5f_0$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (d)

    A closed pipe resonates at odd harmonics: $f_0, 3f_0, 5f_0, \ldots$
    Frequency reaching the pipe: $f = f_s\dfrac{v}{v - u}$.
    $u = 0.8v$: $f = 5f_s$. (A) $5f_0$ → resonance; (B) $10f_0$ → no; (C) $2.5f_0$ → no.
    $u = 0.5v$: $f = 2f_s$. (D) $3f_0$ → resonance.
    Answer (A), (D).

  4. Question 23 · Physics · Units and Measurements
    A physical quantity $\vec{S}$ is defined as $\vec{S} = (\vec{E} \times \vec{B})/\mu_0$, where $\vec{E}$ is electric field, $\vec{B}$ is magnetic field and $\mu_0$ is the permeability of free space. The dimensions of $\vec{S}$ are the same as the dimensions of which of the following quantity(ies) ?

    1. $\dfrac{\text{Energy}}{\text{Charge} \times \text{Current}}$
    2. $\dfrac{\text{Force}}{\text{Length} \times \text{Time}}$
    3. $\dfrac{\text{Energy}}{\text{Volume}}$
    4. $\dfrac{\text{Power}}{\text{Area}}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b), (d)

    $\vec S$ is the Poynting vector: energy flow per unit area per unit time, $[S] = \dfrac{\text{W}}{\text{m}^2} = [\mathrm{MT^{-3}}]$.
    (A) $\dfrac{\mathrm{ML^2T^{-2}}}{\mathrm{AT}\cdot\mathrm{A}} = [\mathrm{ML^2T^{-3}A^{-2}}]$ → no.
    (B) $\dfrac{\mathrm{MLT^{-2}}}{\mathrm{L}\cdot\mathrm{T}} = [\mathrm{MT^{-3}}]$ → yes.
    (C) $\dfrac{\mathrm{ML^2T^{-2}}}{\mathrm{L^3}} = [\mathrm{ML^{-1}T^{-2}}]$ → no.
    (D) $\dfrac{\mathrm{ML^2T^{-3}}}{\mathrm{L^2}} = [\mathrm{MT^{-3}}]$ → yes.
    Answer (B), (D).

  5. Question 41 · Chemistry · Chemical Kinetics
    For the following reaction

    $\displaystyle 2\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{Y} \xrightarrow{k} \mathbf{P}$
    the rate of reaction is $\dfrac{d[\mathbf{P}]}{dt} = k[\mathbf{X}]$. Two moles of $\mathbf{X}$ are mixed with one mole of $\mathbf{Y}$ to make 1.0 L of solution. At 50 s, 0.5 mole of $\mathbf{Y}$ is left in the reaction mixture. The correct statement(s) about the reaction is(are)
    (Use: ln 2 = 0.693)

    1. The rate constant, $k$, of the reaction is $13.86 \times 10^{-4}$ s$^{-1}$.
    2. Half-life of $\mathbf{X}$ is 50 s.
    3. At 50 s, $-\dfrac{d[\mathbf{X}]}{dt} = 13.86 \times 10^{-3}$ mol L$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$.
    4. At 100 s, $-\dfrac{d[\mathbf{Y}]}{dt} = 3.46 \times 10^{-3}$ mol L$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b), (c), (d)

    $-\dfrac{d[\mathbf X]}{dt} = 2k[\mathbf X]$, so $[\mathbf X] = 2e^{-2kt}$.
    At 50 s, 0.5 mol Y has reacted, so 1.0 mol X has reacted: $[\mathbf X]$ has halved → half-life of X $= 50$ s → (B) true.
    $2k = \dfrac{0.693}{50} = 1.386 \times 10^{-2}$, so $k = 6.93 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$ → (A) false.
    (C) At 50 s: $-\dfrac{d[\mathbf X]}{dt} = 2k \times 1 = 13.86 \times 10^{-3}$ mol L$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$ → true.
    (D) At 100 s, $[\mathbf X] = 0.5$: $-\dfrac{d[\mathbf Y]}{dt} = k[\mathbf X] = 3.465 \times 10^{-3}$ mol L$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$ → true.
    Answer (B), (C), (D).

  6. Question 53 · Chemistry · Coordination Compounds
    The reaction of $\mathrm{K_3[Fe(CN)_6]}$ with freshly prepared $\mathrm{FeSO_4}$ solution produces a dark blue precipitate called Turnbull's blue. Reaction of $\mathrm{K_4[Fe(CN)_6]}$ with the $\mathrm{FeSO_4}$ solution in complete absence of air produces a white precipitate $\mathbf{X}$, which turns blue in air. Mixing the $\mathrm{FeSO_4}$ solution with $\mathrm{NaNO_3}$, followed by a slow addition of concentrated $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ through the side of the test tube produces a brown ring.

    Precipitate $\mathbf{X}$ is

    1. $\mathrm{Fe_4[Fe(CN)_6]_3}$
    2. $\mathrm{Fe[Fe(CN)_6]}$
    3. $\mathrm{K_2Fe[Fe(CN)_6]}$
    4. $\mathrm{KFe[Fe(CN)_6]}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    $\mathrm{K_4[Fe(CN)_6] + FeSO_4 \to K_2Fe[Fe(CN)_6] + K_2SO_4}$ in the absence of air.
    $\mathrm{K_2Fe[Fe(CN)_6]}$ (all iron Fe(II)) is white; air oxidises it to the blue Fe(III)/Fe(II) compound.
    Answer (C).
