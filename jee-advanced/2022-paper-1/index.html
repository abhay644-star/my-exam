Solved questions from this paper

A few questions with their official answers and worked solutions. All 54 are in the online test.

  1. Question 1 · Mathematics · Inverse Trigonometric Functions
    Considering only the principal values of the inverse trigonometric functions, the value of

    $\displaystyle \frac{3}{2}\cos^{-1}\sqrt{\frac{2}{2+\pi^2}}+\frac{1}{4}\sin^{-1}\frac{2\sqrt{2}\,\pi}{2+\pi^2}+\tan^{-1}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi}$
    is ______.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 2.35 to 2.37

    Let $\theta=\tan^{-1}\dfrac{\pi}{\sqrt2}$, so $\cos\theta=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{\sqrt{2+\pi^2}}$ and the first angle is $\theta$; note $\theta>\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ since $\dfrac{\pi}{\sqrt2}>1$.
    $\sin2\theta=\dfrac{2\tan\theta}{1+\tan^2\theta}=\dfrac{2\sqrt2\,\pi}{2+\pi^2}$, and $2\theta\in\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2},\pi\right)$, so $\sin^{-1}\dfrac{2\sqrt2\,\pi}{2+\pi^2}=\pi-2\theta$.
    $\tan^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt2}{\pi}=\dfrac{\pi}{2}-\theta$.
    Sum $=\dfrac32\theta+\dfrac{\pi-2\theta}{4}+\dfrac{\pi}{2}-\theta=\dfrac{3\pi}{4}\approx2.36$.

  2. Question 2 · Mathematics · Limits and Derivatives
    Let $\alpha$ be a positive real number. Let $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ and $g:(\alpha,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}$ be the functions defined by

    $\displaystyle f(x)=\sin\left(\frac{\pi x}{12}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad g(x)=\frac{2\log_e\left(\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{\alpha}\right)}{\log_e\left(e^{\sqrt{x}}-e^{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)}.$
    Then the value of $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\alpha^+}f(g(x))$ is ______.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 0.49 to 0.51

    Put $t=\sqrt x-\sqrt\alpha\to0^+$. Then $e^{\sqrt x}-e^{\sqrt\alpha}=e^{\sqrt\alpha}(e^{t}-1)$.
    $g(x)=\dfrac{2\ln t}{\sqrt\alpha+\ln(e^t-1)}=\dfrac{2\ln t}{\sqrt\alpha+\ln t+\ln\dfrac{e^t-1}{t}}$.
    As $t\to0^+$, $\ln t\to-\infty$ and $\ln\dfrac{e^t-1}{t}\to0$, so $g(x)\to2$.
    $f$ is continuous, so the limit is $f(2)=\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6}=0.5$.

  3. Question 20 · Physics · Nuclei
    The minimum kinetic energy needed by an alpha particle to cause the nuclear reaction ${}^{16}_{7}\mathrm{N}+{}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He}\to{}^{1}_{1}\mathrm{H}+{}^{19}_{8}\mathrm{O}$ in a laboratory frame is $n$ (in MeV). Assume that ${}^{16}_{7}\mathrm{N}$ is at rest in the laboratory frame. The masses of ${}^{16}_{7}\mathrm{N}$, ${}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He}$, ${}^{1}_{1}\mathrm{H}$ and ${}^{19}_{8}\mathrm{O}$ can be taken to be $16.006\ u$, $4.003\ u$, $1.008\ u$ and $19.003\ u$, respectively, where $1\ u=930\ \mathrm{MeV}c^{-2}$. The value of $n$ is ______.

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 2.3 to 2.35

    $Q=(16.006+4.003-1.008-19.003)\,u\,c^2=-0.002\times930=-1.86$ MeV.
    At threshold the products move together with the centre of mass, so the alpha must supply $|Q|$ plus the KE of the centre of mass: $K_{th}=|Q|\left(1+\dfrac{m_\alpha}{m_N}\right)$.
    $K_{th}=1.86\left(1+\dfrac{4.003}{16.006}\right)=1.86\times1.2501\approx2.33$ MeV.

  4. Question 23 · Physics · System of Particles and Rotational Motion
    At time $t=0$, a disk of radius $1$ m starts to roll without slipping on a horizontal plane with an angular acceleration of $\alpha=\dfrac23\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$. A small stone is stuck to the disk. At $t=0$, it is at the contact point of the disk and the plane. Later, at time $t=\sqrt\pi$ s, the stone detaches itself and flies off tangentially from the disk. The maximum height (in m) reached by the stone measured from the plane is $\dfrac12+\dfrac{x}{10}$. The value of $x$ is ______. [Take $g=10\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.]

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 0.48 to 0.56

    At $t=\sqrt\pi$: $\omega=\alpha t=\dfrac{2\sqrt\pi}{3}$ rad/s and the disk has turned $\theta=\dfrac12\alpha t^2=\dfrac\pi3$.
    The stone is then at angle $\dfrac\pi3$ from the lowest point: height $R-R\cos\dfrac\pi3=\dfrac12$ m above the plane.
    Its velocity is $\omega\times$(distance from contact point), perpendicular to the line joining it to the contact point; this gives components $\omega R(1-\cos\theta)$ horizontally and $\omega R\sin\theta$ vertically.
    $v_y=\dfrac{2\sqrt\pi}{3}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}=\sqrt{\dfrac\pi3}$, so the extra rise is $\dfrac{v_y^2}{2g}=\dfrac{\pi/3}{20}=\dfrac{\pi}{60}$.
    $H=\dfrac12+\dfrac{\pi}{60}=\dfrac12+\dfrac{x}{10}\Rightarrow x=\dfrac\pi6\approx0.52$.

  5. Question 37 · Chemistry · Thermodynamics
    2 mol of $\mathrm{Hg(g)}$ is combusted in a fixed volume bomb calorimeter with excess of $\mathrm{O_2}$ at 298 K and 1 atm into $\mathrm{HgO(s)}$. During the reaction, temperature increases from 298.0 K to 312.8 K. If heat capacity of the bomb calorimeter and enthalpy of formation of $\mathrm{Hg(g)}$ are $20.00\ \mathrm{kJ\,K^{-1}}$ and $61.32\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ at 298 K, respectively, the calculated standard molar enthalpy of formation of $\mathrm{HgO(s)}$ at 298 K is $X\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. The value of $|X|$ is ______.
    [Given: Gas constant $R=8.3\ \mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}$]

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 89 to 91

    Heat released at constant volume: $20.00\times(312.8-298.0)=296$ kJ, so for $\mathrm{2Hg(g)+O_2(g)\to2HgO(s)}$, $\Delta U=-296$ kJ.
    $\Delta n_g=0-3=-3$: $\Delta H=\Delta U+\Delta n_gRT=-296-3\times8.3\times10^{-3}\times298=-303.42$ kJ.
    $\Delta H=2\Delta_fH(\mathrm{HgO})-2\Delta_fH(\mathrm{Hg,g})\Rightarrow2\Delta_fH(\mathrm{HgO})=-303.42+2(61.32)=-180.78$ kJ.
    $\Delta_fH(\mathrm{HgO})=-90.39\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, so $|X|\approx90.39$.

  6. Question 38 · Chemistry · Electrochemistry
    The reduction potential ($E^0$, in V) of $\mathrm{MnO_4^-(aq)/Mn(s)}$ is ______.
    [Given: $E^0_{\left(\mathrm{MnO_4^-(aq)/MnO_2(s)}\right)}=1.68$ V; $E^0_{\left(\mathrm{MnO_2(s)/Mn^{2+}(aq)}\right)}=1.21$ V; $E^0_{\left(\mathrm{Mn^{2+}(aq)/Mn(s)}\right)}=-1.03$ V]

    Show answer and solution

    Answer: 0.74 to 0.8

    Add the free energies ($\Delta G^0=-nFE^0$) of the three steps: $\mathrm{MnO_4^-\to MnO_2}$ ($n=3$), $\mathrm{MnO_2\to Mn^{2+}}$ ($n=2$), $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}\to Mn}$ ($n=2$); overall $n=7$.
    $E^0=\dfrac{3(1.68)+2(1.21)+2(-1.03)}{7}=\dfrac{5.04+2.42-2.06}{7}=\dfrac{5.40}{7}\approx0.77$ V.
