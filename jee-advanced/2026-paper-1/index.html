  1. Question 1 · Mathematics · Application of Derivatives
    Consider the function $f : (0, \infty) \to (-\infty, \infty)$ given by

    $\displaystyle f(x) = \sqrt{x}\,\log_e(x) - x + 1 .$
    Then which one of the following statements is TRUE ?

    1. The derivative of the function $f$ is decreasing in the interval $(0, 1)$
    2. The function $f$ has a local maximum at some point $a \in (0, \infty)$
    3. The function $f$ has a local minimum at some point $b \in (0, \infty)$
    4. The function $f$ has NEITHER a point of local maximum NOR a point of local minimum in the interval $(0, \infty)$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (d)

    $f'(x) = \dfrac{\ln x}{2\sqrt{x}} + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}} - 1 = \dfrac{\ln x + 2 - 2\sqrt{x}}{2\sqrt{x}}$.
    Let $g(x) = \ln x + 2 - 2\sqrt{x}$; then $g'(x) = \dfrac{1}{x} - \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}} = \dfrac{1-\sqrt{x}}{x}$, so $g$ increases on $(0,1)$ and decreases on $(1,\infty)$.
    Hence $g(x) \le g(1) = 0$, with equality only at $x = 1$.
    So $f'(x) \le 0$ with equality only at $x=1$: $f$ is strictly decreasing and has no local extremum. (Also $f'(x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to 0^+$ and $f'(1)=0$, so $f'$ is not decreasing on $(0,1)$.) Answer (D).

  2. Question 2 · Mathematics · Conic Sections
    Let $P$ be the point on the parabola $y = x^2$ such that the slope of the tangent to the parabola at the point $P$ is $4$ . Let $Q$ be the point in the first quadrant lying on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 2$ such that the slope of the tangent to the circle at the point $Q$ is $-1$ . Let $R$ be the point in the first quadrant lying on the ellipse $x^2 + 4y^2 = 8$ such that the slope of the tangent to the ellipse at the point $R$ is $-\frac{1}{2}$. Then the radius of the circle passing through the points $P, Q$ and $R$ is

    1. $\sqrt{10}$
    2. $\sqrt{5}$
    3. $\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}}$
    4. $2\sqrt{5}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (c)

    Parabola: $2x = 4 \Rightarrow P = (2, 4)$.
    Circle: slope $-x/y = -1 \Rightarrow x = y = 1$, so $Q = (1, 1)$.
    Ellipse: slope $-\dfrac{x}{4y} = -\dfrac12 \Rightarrow x = 2y$; $8y^2 = 8 \Rightarrow R = (2, 1)$.
    $RQ$ is horizontal and $RP$ is vertical, so $\angle PRQ = 90^\circ$ and $PQ$ is a diameter.
    $PQ = \sqrt{1 + 9} = \sqrt{10}$, radius $= \dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2} = \sqrt{\dfrac52}$. Answer (C).

  3. Question 21 · Physics · Atoms
    Consider a hydrogen atom with $v_k, r_k$, and $K_k$ denoting the velocity, orbital radius and kinetic energy of the electron in the $k^{\text{th}}$ orbit, respectively. The electron undergoes a transition from the $n^{\text{th}}$ orbit, emitting radiation corresponding to the Lyman series. Considering $h$ to be the Planck's constant and $\epsilon_0$ the permittivity of the free space, the correct statement(s) is/are:

    1. Magnitude of change in kinetic energy of electron can be expressed as $\dfrac{h}{4\pi}\left|\dfrac{nv_n}{r_n} - \dfrac{v_1}{r_1}\right|$.
    2. Magnitude of change in de Broglie wavelength of the electron can be expressed as $\dfrac{e^2}{4\epsilon_0}\left|\dfrac{1}{K_n} - \dfrac{1}{K_1}\right|$.
    3. Frequency of the radiation emitted can be expressed as $\dfrac{e^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0 h}\left(\dfrac{1}{r_1} - \dfrac{1}{r_n}\right)$.
    4. Magnitude of change in total energy of the electron can be expressed as $\dfrac{h}{2\pi}\left|\dfrac{v_1}{r_1} - \dfrac{nv_n}{r_n}\right|$.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (c)

    Bohr: $mv_kr_k = kh/2\pi \Rightarrow K_k = \tfrac12 mv_k^2 = \dfrac{h}{4\pi}\dfrac{kv_k}{r_k}$. So $|\Delta K| = \dfrac{h}{4\pi}\left|\dfrac{nv_n}{r_n} - \dfrac{v_1}{r_1}\right|$: (A) true.
    $|\Delta E| = |\Delta K|$ (since $E = -K$), which has $h/4\pi$, not $h/2\pi$: (D) false.
    $E_k = -\dfrac{e^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0 r_k}$, so $\nu = \dfrac{E_n - E_1}{h} = \dfrac{e^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0 h}\left(\dfrac{1}{r_1} - \dfrac{1}{r_n}\right)$: (C) true.
    $\lambda_k = 2\pi r_k/k$ and $r_k = \dfrac{e^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0 K_k}$ give $\lambda_k = \dfrac{e^2}{4\epsilon_0 kK_k}$; the change is $\dfrac{e^2}{4\epsilon_0}\left|\dfrac{1}{nK_n} - \dfrac{1}{K_1}\right|$, so (B) false.

  4. Question 24 · Physics · Electromagnetic Waves
    The electric field associated with an electromagnetic wave travelling in vacuum is given by $E_0\sin(3y + 4z + \omega t)\hat{\imath}$, where $\omega$ is the angular frequency. All quantities are in SI units. The correct statement(s) about this wave is/are:
    [Given: speed of light in vacuum $c = 3 \times 10^8$ ms$^{-1}$.]

    1. The wave is travelling in $-\frac{1}{5}(3\hat{\jmath} + 4\hat{k})$ direction.
    2. The magnitude of the wave vector is $0.5$ m$^{-1}$.
    3. The value of $\omega$ is $1.5 \times 10^9$ rad s$^{-1}$.
    4. The magnetic field associated with this wave is given by $\frac{E_0}{c}\sin(3y + 4z + \omega t)(4\hat{\jmath} - 3\hat{k})$.
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a), (c)

    Phase $\vec k\cdot\vec r + \omega t$ with $\vec k = 3\hat\jmath + 4\hat k$ means propagation along $-\vec k$, unit vector $-\tfrac15(3\hat\jmath + 4\hat k)$: (A) true.
    $|\vec k| = 5$ m$^{-1}$: (B) false.
    $\omega = c|\vec k| = 1.5\times10^9$ rad s$^{-1}$: (C) true.
    $\vec B = \tfrac1c\,\hat n \times \vec E$ with $\hat n = -\tfrac15(3\hat\jmath + 4\hat k)$: $\hat n \times \hat\imath = \tfrac15(3\hat k - 4\hat\jmath)$, so $\vec B = \tfrac{E_0}{5c}\sin(\dots)(3\hat k - 4\hat\jmath)$ — (D) has the wrong sign and magnitude: false.

  5. Question 33 · Chemistry · Thermodynamics
    An ideal gas (0.5 mol), initially at 2 bar pressure, is compressed at a constant temperature of 600 K in two steps: first, against a constant external pressure of $P$ bar ($2 \lt P \lt 8$), and then against constant external pressure of 8 bar. At each step, the compression is stopped only when the pressure of the gas becomes equal to the external pressure. The total work done on the gas in these steps is $W$. Considering all possible values of $P$ ($2 \lt P \lt 8$) and taking the gas constant as $R$ (in J K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$), the minimum value of $|W|$ (in J) is

    1. $207R$
    2. $600R$
    3. $630R$
    4. $900R$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (b)

    $nRT = 0.5 \times R \times 600 = 300R$.
    Step 1 (against $P$): $W_1 = P\left(\dfrac{nRT}{2} - \dfrac{nRT}{P}\right) = nRT\left(\dfrac{P}{2} - 1\right)$.
    Step 2 (against 8 bar): $W_2 = 8\left(\dfrac{nRT}{P} - \dfrac{nRT}{8}\right) = nRT\left(\dfrac{8}{P} - 1\right)$.
    $|W| = nRT\left(\dfrac{P}{2} + \dfrac{8}{P} - 2\right)$, minimised when $\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{8}{P^2}$, i.e. $P = 4$ bar.
    $|W|_{\min} = 300R\,(2 + 2 - 2) = 600R$.

  6. Question 35 · Chemistry · Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure
    The correct order of dipole moments for the given species is

    1. $\mathrm{BF_3} = \mathrm{NH_4^+} \lt \mathrm{NF_3} \lt \mathrm{NH_3}$
    2. $\mathrm{BF_3} \lt \mathrm{NH_4^+} \lt \mathrm{NF_3} \lt \mathrm{NH_3}$
    3. $\mathrm{NH_4^+} \lt \mathrm{BF_3} \lt \mathrm{NH_3} \lt \mathrm{NF_3}$
    4. $\mathrm{BF_3} \lt \mathrm{NH_4^+} \lt \mathrm{NH_3} \lt \mathrm{NF_3}$
    Show answer and solution

    Answer: (a)

    $\mathrm{BF_3}$ (trigonal planar) and $\mathrm{NH_4^+}$ (regular tetrahedral) are symmetric, so their dipole moments are both zero.
    In $\mathrm{NH_3}$ the N–H bond moments point towards N and add to the lone-pair moment: about 1.47 D.
    In $\mathrm{NF_3}$ the N–F bond moments point away from N and partly cancel the lone-pair moment: about 0.23 D.
    Order: $\mathrm{BF_3} = \mathrm{NH_4^+} \lt \mathrm{NF_3} \lt \mathrm{NH_3}$.
